# 1. SOURCES ACTUALLY USED

## R1 — David Gerrold, *The Galactic Whirlpool*
**REFERENCE ONLY**

Useful mechanisms:
- an orbital settlement becomes a city because it develops jobs, courts, taxation/customs, voting, recreation, logistics and an economy;
- nominal legal status can lag behind operational autonomy;
- construction/energy/transport contracts shape employment and political power;
- inhabitants resist being treated as temporary workers once the station becomes home;
- a habitat needs recreational environments and civic life or it remains a work camp;
- isolated/structured environments can create different spatial habits and identity.

Do NOT import:
- L5 independence plot;
- military leverage;
- moving-habitat escape;
- exact politics/history.

## R2 — *The Cambridge Companion to Science Fiction*
**REFERENCE ONLY**

Useful mechanisms:
- station/habitat should function as a multi-layered city, not a single corridor;
- class, markets, zoning, parks, slums/privileged zones, privacy and political conflict can exist inside advanced technical environments;
- engineering seeks logical optimization, while politics requires compromise between legitimate incompatible values;
- an apparently efficient utopia becomes oppressive if civic disagreement has no forum and all conflict is reduced to administration;
- the closed habitat increases interdependence without eliminating human politics.

Do NOT import:
- any specific fictional polity;
- ideological program from cited novels.

## R3 — Andy Weir, *Project Hail Mary*
**REFERENCE ONLY**

Useful mechanisms:
- artificial gravity changes bodily action, orientation, work and risk;
- effective gravity depends on rotational geometry;
- transitions between gravity states should be felt physically, not merely named;
- a system design should follow what must reliably work, not what looks futuristic;
- mature legacy equipment may be preferable to exotic equipment if operated in the environment for which it was perfected;
- dangerous work becomes convincing through concrete handholds, tethering, friction, access, timing and failure modes;
- exterior work should often be robotic before humans are casually sent EVA.

Do NOT import:
- Hail Mary spacecraft design;
- beetles;
- Rocky;
- specific engineering set pieces.

## R4 — Pierce Brown, *Red Rising*
**REFERENCE ONLY / NEGATIVE MODEL**

Useful mechanisms:
- gravity-well logistics can make orbital/lunar infrastructure economically and politically important;
- city scenes feel large when transport, leisure, architecture, labor and status are visible in one image/scene;
- closed-resource civilization can rationalize dangerous labor hierarchies.

Use the last point as an **anti-model**:
IVDIVO must show pressure toward utility/status stratification without creating a copied Color/caste system.

## R5 — Ursula K. Le Guin, “On the Frontier”
**REFERENCE ONLY**

Useful mechanism:
- a frontier has two sides;
- what visitors experience as “new territory” is already an ordinary meaningful home to residents;
- the arriving observer can erase local meaning by treating people as material for expansion, science, media or tourism.

Book-Two application:
Earth cohort is not discovering Vela.
They are entering somebody else’s commute, bedroom, job, sport team and civic argument.

## R6 — Edward James / SF history and critical context
**REFERENCE ONLY**

Useful mechanisms:
- sense of wonder can coexist with incomplete understanding;
- built futures work when technology, society and narrative pressure are inseparable;
- utopian/heterotopian settings become dramatically alive through contradictions rather than perfection.

# 2. MASTER REFERENCE SYNTHESIS

The most important combined lesson is:

> **An orbital habitat is not a machine containing a city. The machine and the city have become one coupled social organism — physically, economically, politically and emotionally.**

This strengthens the current Ollie engine rather than replacing it.

Current story already asks:
“What counts as part of the habitat system?”

Reference synthesis expands the answer:
- resident bodies;
- routes;
- shops;
- shifts;
- housing;
- maintenance closures;
- gravity habits;
- public disagreement;
- private life;
- local identity;
- economic dependence;
- institutional legitimacy;
are all causal variables.

# 3. TEN REFERENCE-DRIVEN LAWS FOR BOOK TWO

## LAW 1 — VELA IS ACCRETED, NOT DESIGNED ALL AT ONCE
Different generations of construction must remain visible.

## LAW 2 — GRAVITY IS CULTURE
Local-born people move, play, flirt, work and navigate differently because rotation is embodied habit.

## LAW 3 — INFRASTRUCTURE IS SOCIAL GEOGRAPHY
A closed route can ruin a date, shift, school day, sleepover, delivery or family schedule.

## LAW 4 — THE STATION HAS AN ECONOMY
Construction, maintenance, fabrication, transit, imports and service work affect real people.

## LAW 5 — SCARCITY SURVIVES ABUNDANCE
Life support baseline can be secure while private space, views, commute, compatible housing, fabrication time and event access remain scarce.

## LAW 6 — MAINTENANCE STATUS ≠ HUMAN WORTH
Closed habitats naturally reward usefulness. The novel must resist turning “productive” into “deserving to exist.”

This is especially important for Ollie.

## LAW 7 — POLITICS CANNOT BE REPLACED BY OPTIMIZATION
Safety engineers, residents, housing staff, workers and young people need a legitimate place to disagree.

## LAW 8 — LOCALS ARE NOT GUIDES
Every local character needs a life that continues when the Earth cohort leaves the scene.

## LAW 9 — FUTURE BODY DIFFERENCE IS ORDINARY BEFORE IT IS PHILOSOPHICAL
Food, seating, gravity, climate, touch, dating and sport reveal embodiment before ontology.

## LAW 10 — WONDER MUST HAVE A BACK SIDE
A spectacular view has a booking queue.
A beautiful ring has waste lines.
A zero-g performance has cleaning staff afterward.
A new home has an allocation argument.

This does not kill wonder. It makes it inhabitable.

- radial environmental gradients;
- dynamic partition/airlock sequencing;
- crowd route substitution;
- personal environmental regulators;
- control feedback lag.

## Strengthen ACT III

It must emerge from:
**engineering evidence + resident priorities + housing constraints + maintenance reality + civic compromise.**

## Strengthen RESOLUTION
The ring becomes successful when it becomes boring:
laundry, deliveries, youth noise, maintenance tickets, local arguments, meals, missed connections.
