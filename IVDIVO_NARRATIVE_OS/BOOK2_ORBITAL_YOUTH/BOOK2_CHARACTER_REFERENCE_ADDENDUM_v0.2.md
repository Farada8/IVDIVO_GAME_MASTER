# REFERENCE-ENHANCED CHARACTER ADDENDUM v0.2

This addendum supplements Character/Relationship Bible v0.1.

---

# 23. OLLIE — VALUE vs USEFULNESS

Fresh orbital-reference pass sharpens Ollie's deepest emotional danger.

## New private distortion
Ollie does not only want to be competent.

He is vulnerable to believing:
> **If I am not useful, why am I here?**

This belief fits a closed habitat where maintenance competence visibly matters, but the novel must not endorse it.

## Required beats
1. He envies Ethan's visible centrality.
2. He envies local apprentices whose place in Vela feels earned through work.
3. He experiences being replaced by his Earth maker team.
4. After midpoint he works ordinary maintenance where nobody needs his “special” intelligence.
5. At the end he sees a non-urgent alert and does **not** prove his worth by abandoning friends.

## End-state
“I can contribute” replaces “I must be indispensable.”

Never articulate this as a lesson speech.

# 24. AOIFE — VISITOR / FRONTIER BLIND SPOT

Aoife's media arc gains a second layer.

Her danger is not only privacy.

She can still frame Vela as a place the Earth audience is “discovering.”

## Required correction
Her best documentary choices treat locals as people whose world continues off-camera.

Add:
- ordinary scenes she records because locals care, not because Earth viewers find them exotic;
- at least one important event she deliberately does not frame as “first” or “frontier”;
- a source who rejects being translated into an Earth political argument.

This strengthens the inference breach: she has learned not to steal words, but still underestimates what Earth audiences can turn a life into.

---

# 25. MAYA — BODY CONFIDENCE vs LOCAL HABIT

Maya's early “I can handle this” attitude should meet orbital body practice.

Local teens are not braver; they have embodied habits:
- spin transitions;
- handholds;
- route timing;
- radial movement;
- reading crowd flow.

Maya can become physically competent quickly, but she should remain socially wrong sometimes.

Her independence arc improves if her local friendships are built through:
- failing visibly;
- laughing;
- being corrected by peers;
- later being trusted with real route knowledge.

---

# 26. SAMIR — SPORT AS BELONGING, NOT CLUE FACTORY

Sport must remain valuable even if it produced zero plot information.

Reference-enhanced rule:
- one practice/game result matters socially but not technically;
- Samir wants playing time/status/team belonging;
- he can be benched, teased or wrong;
- his later timing observation comes from genuine participation.

Do not make the sport a disguised sensor laboratory.

---

# 27. IRI — LOCAL WITHOUT BEING AN EXPOSITION GUIDE

Iri's local authority is embodied, not encyclopedic.

Iri may know:
- routes;
- maintenance habit;
- work culture;
- local social consequences.

Iri may **not** automatically know:
- citywide politics;
- every species;
- Confederation policy;
- full engineering theory;
- the correct moral interpretation.

Required ordinary-life facts before prose:
1. one household/family fact;
2. one hobby/social obligation;
3. one resource/status irritation unrelated to Ollie.

Recommended:
Iri wants a desirable workshop block that Ollie's prestige access could have displaced.

---

# 28. LEA — “THIS IS HOME, NOT FRONTIER”

Lea's narrative function expands.

For the five, Ring Six is extraordinary future civilization.

For Lea, it is:
- where her sibling sleeps;
- how long school takes;
- whether a friend lives two transfers away;
- whether she has to share temporary space;
- whether she can attend a late event and still get home.

This makes her the strongest antidote to tourist/frontier framing.

---

# 29. TOMAS — ECONOMIC CONSEQUENCE THROUGH BODY/TIME

Tomas must never explain the station economy.

Show:
- shift start;
- lost sleep;
- transfer windows;
- caregiving;
- food purchased because there is no time to go home;
- one threatened work opportunity if housing delay continues.

His body carries the economic story through fatigue.

---

# 30. ETHAN — POLITICS vs OPTIMIZATION

Ethan's Book-Two growth gets a sharper institutional test.

At Ch28 he can correctly map consequences.

His error:
thinking that a clear consequence map produces a correct answer.

Reference-enhanced correction:
he must see a civic process where people knowingly choose different weights for:
- safety margin;
- housing urgency;
- equality;
- access;
- work disruption.

He learns that some problems require legitimate compromise, not a better objective function.

---

# 31. RELATIONSHIP / BODY WORLD

Orbital culture should affect attraction without turning romance into exposition.

Possible ordinary pressures:
- two people prefer different gravity bands;
- one social zone is comfortable for one body and annoying for another;
- last transfer home determines whether a date continues;
- a view room requires booking;
- a shared meal needs compatibility negotiation;
- local-born ease in low-g can be attractive/irritating/status-marked.

Use sparingly and specifically.

---

# 32. GROUP ARC — NEW CIVILIZATION SEPARATES THEM

The group's divergence should be driven by actual city structures:
- different ring addresses;
- different commute loads;
- shifts;
- sport;
- source work;
- adult track;
- local friends;
- late transit.

This is stronger than abstract “growing apart.”

By the ending:
friendship is scheduled, chosen and sometimes missed.

That is not failure.
That is adolescence inside orbital civilization.
