# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 31 — THE ADULT DOOR

The placement was better than Ethan had expected.

That was the problem.

He opened the adult-track notice at 08:11 between two migration-support updates and one message from Maya asking whether anyone had seen her blue route clip.

ADULT CIVIC OPERATIONS — ENTRY PLACEMENT

LIVE DEPENDENCY / ROUTE OPERATIONS

START WINDOW: 23 JANUARY

INITIAL TERM: SIX WEEKS

The work summary was short enough to be dangerous.

Real route planning.

Real service dependencies.

Live operational consequences.

Not observation.

Not Youth Cohort simulation.

Not “developmental exposure.”

Ethan read the liability line twice.

Then the start date.

Then the placement title again.

He had already selected LATER after the current Ring Six commitment review.

That had felt like a decision when the option was abstract.

Now the system wanted confirmation because an actual placement had opened.

CONFIRM IMMEDIATE PLACEMENT

or

TRANSFER TO LATER INTAKE POOL

Later intake remained available.

Exact placement did not.

At 08:13, Aoife sent:

AOIFE: alive?

He stared at the message.

Typed:

ETHAN: yes

Deleted it.

Typed:

ETHAN: adult-track thing is real

Deleted that too.

Then closed the message window and opened the placement details again.

This was not hiding information.

Not yet.

It was two minutes.

He could have used the two minutes better.

* * *

The adult-track coordinator was younger than Ethan expected and more tired than any representative of adulthood had a right to look.

She joined the call from a transit office with a service map behind her and a half-finished drink beside one hand.

“You asked whether the current placement could be deferred.”

“Yes.”

“It cannot.”

Ethan had known that from the notice.

He still felt something drop.

“The later intake is still open?”

“Yes.”

“Same division?”

“Possibly.”

“Same assignment class?”

“Likely.”

“Same placement?”

“No guarantee.”

He nodded.

The coordinator waited.

Ethan opened the schedule panel before realizing he had done it.

Ring Six migration support occupied the next eleven days in blocks that were already changing.

The adult placement started inside them.

“What if I start two days late?”

“The first field cycle begins on the twenty-third.”

“One day?”

“No.”

“Remote orientation first?”

“That is not the issue.”

“What is?”

“The placement needs somebody who is available for the placement.”

The sentence was almost offensively simple.

Ethan closed the schedule panel.

“Right.”

The coordinator glanced at his file.

“You are not being penalized for finishing an existing commitment.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

“Does the later pool have fewer live placements?”

“Usually.”

“How many fewer?”

She gave him last cycle’s range.

He did the comparison automatically.

The immediate cohort was better.

Not dramatically.

Enough.

“And if I transfer later, can I request dependency operations again?”

“Yes.”

“Priority?”

“No.”

“Because I declined this one.”

“Because later intake is a new allocation set.”

“Same effect.”

“Different cause.”

Ethan almost smiled.

The coordinator did not.

He liked her slightly more.

“What would you do?” he asked.

She leaned back.

“No.”

“That was fast.”

“You are asking me to convert an administrative explanation into permission.”

“I asked what you would do.”

“With my commitments, my finances and my career?”

“Yes.”

“That information would be useless to you.”

Ethan looked at the placement again.

“I selected later already.”

“I can see that.”

“So this is just confirmation.”

“If it feels like just confirmation, confirm it.”

It did not.

The coordinator took a drink.

“You have until twenty-one hundred.”

“The message said twenty-three fifty-nine.”

“That is system closure. I go home at twenty-one hundred.”

“Important distinction.”

“To me.”

The call ended without advice.

Ethan sat with the placement open.

He could take it.

Nobody had told him he could not.

The Youth Cohort commitment was not a legal barrier.

Ring Six would continue without him.

That last part was true.

He disliked it for reasons that had nothing to do with responsibility.

* * *

He found Aoife in a food volume near the relocation transfer, holding a container with one hand and reading something on her device with the other.

Her recorder was not visible.

That was new enough that Ethan noticed.

She looked up.

“You look organized.”

“That is normally a compliment.”

“Not on your face.”

He sat across from her.

“The adult placement came through.”

Aoife put the device down.

“When?”

“This morning.”

“How long have you known?”

“Forty-seven minutes.”

She stared at him.

Ethan checked the time.

“Forty-nine.”

“That helped.”

“I thought accuracy might.”

“It did not.”

“Noted.”

Aoife opened her food.

“What is it?”

“Live dependency and route operations.”

She stopped moving for half a second.

“That is exactly the kind of thing you wanted.”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Starts tomorrow.”

“And Ring Six?”

“Overlaps.”

Aoife nodded slowly.

He waited for the obvious question.

She did not ask it.

That was worse than if she had.

“There is a later intake,” Ethan said.

“Same job?”

“Not guaranteed.”

“So what are you doing?”

“I am staying through the Ring Six commitment.”

Aoife looked at him.

He kept going before he could start explaining the decision into correctness.

“I have not sent it yet.”

“Why are you telling me?”

The answer arrived too fast.

Because you got angry last time.

He did not say it.

“Because I wanted you to know before I sent it.”

“Why?”

Ethan hated that she was good at this.

“Because it matters to me whether you know.”

Aoife’s expression changed slightly.

Not softer.

More careful.

“Do you want my opinion?”

“Yes.”

“That sounds dangerous.”

“I am not asking you to decide.”

“Better.”

She took a bite, chewed, and made him wait.

Ethan did not fill the silence.

That took effort.

Finally she said, “Do you want the placement?”

“Yes.”

“More than the later unknown one?”

“Yes.”

“Then why stay?”

He looked through the open side of the food volume toward the transfer line.

Two move carts passed, followed by three people carrying school cases.

“Because I said I would finish this.”

“That is the official answer.”

“No.”

Aoife waited.

Ethan looked back at her.

“The five matter. You matter. The people here matter. I like being part of this more than I expected.”

Aoife said nothing.

“And I still want the adult work.”

“Good.”

“That sounded like approval.”

“It was approval of not pretending you suddenly stopped wanting it.”

He looked down at his hands.

“I also do not want every commitment to become temporary the moment a better one appears.”

Aoife’s eyes narrowed.

“That sounded prepared.”

“It was not.”

“It sounded like a line.”

“I hate that you do this.”

“No, you don’t.”

He almost laughed.

Then did.

Aoife leaned back.

“So you are staying.”

“Yes.”

“For the ring.”

“Yes.”

“For the group.”

“Partly.”

“For me.”

Ethan looked at her.

“Partly.”

She held his eyes for a second.

He could feel the next sentence trying to become important.

He stopped it.

Aoife said, “Good.”

Ethan blinked.

“That is all?”

“What were you expecting?”

“I do not know.”

“You absolutely do.”

He did.

Some version of: you chose us.

Some version of: I am glad.

Something that turned losing the placement into a clean exchange.

Aoife took another bite.

“I am glad you told me before.”

That was all she gave him.

It was enough.

He opened the adult-track response.

Aoife immediately looked away.

“You can look.”

“I do not need to supervise the button.”

“I did not say supervise.”

“You thought it loudly.”

Ethan selected:

TRANSFER TO LATER INTAKE POOL.

A confirmation opened.

CURRENT PLACEMENT WILL BE RELEASED.

LATER PLACEMENT TYPE / LOCATION NOT GUARANTEED.

CONFIRM?

His finger stopped.

Aoife did not look back.

He pressed CONFIRM.

The placement vanished.

Not faded.

Not moved to an archive.

Gone.

The empty space on the screen was rude.

Ethan locked the device.

Aoife looked at him now.

“Done?”

“Yes.”

“How do you feel?”

“Annoyed.”

She smiled.

“That I believe.”

* * *

At 16:20, one of the other adult-track entrants posted a placement update to the cohort channel.

CIVIC OPERATIONS — ENTRY CONFIRMED.

Different assignment.

Same intake.

Ethan looked at it too long.

The person had done nothing wrong.

He disliked them for almost thirty seconds anyway.

Then he disliked himself for disliking them, which was less useful.

He closed the channel.

A Ring Six migration-support request arrived.

Three households needed revised route windows because one service corridor had moved into a restricted period.

Ethan opened the dependency map.

His first thought was that he could rebuild the entire sequence and recover seven minutes.

His second was that nobody had asked him to rebuild the entire sequence.

He checked the request.

MAP CONFLICTS / RETURN OPTIONS.

No recommendation.

He smiled despite himself.

Nadia had probably written it that way on purpose.

He mapped the conflicts.

Two clean options.

One ugly one.

He left all three visible.

A message from Maya appeared while he was working.

MAYA: host meeting tonight

Then:

MAYA: if they try to put live tracking back i am moving into a duct

Ethan typed:

ETHAN: do not move into a duct

Deleted it.

Typed:

ETHAN: what are you proposing instead?

He looked at that.

Still managing.

Deleted it too.

Finally:

ETHAN: good luck

MAYA: suspiciously normal response

ETHAN: i contain multitudes

MAYA: no you contain schedules

He put the device away.

The immediate placement was still gone.

He still wanted it.

Nothing had replaced that.

Across the migration map, three households still needed routes.

Ethan returned to the work he had chosen to finish.
