ORBITAL YOUTH
Chapter 18 — EIGHTEEN

Ethan turned eighteen at 00:00 Vela local and discovered adulthood came with seven automated notices, three legal acknowledgements and one message from Maya containing a badly drawn crown.

The crown had teeth.

ETHAN: why does it have teeth

MAYA: authority

ETHAN: concerning

SAMIR: happy birthday

AOIFE: condolences on your administrative transformation

OLLIE: did the legal status update finish

MAYA: do not answer him

ETHAN: it did

MAYA: both of you are blocked

His family called eleven minutes later.

His host household produced food at a time when nobody should have wanted food and everybody ate it anyway.

At 00:34 Ethan went to bed legally different and physically identical.

At 07:10 his band reminded him that he could now accept certain contracts independently.

At 07:11 another notice informed him that his Youth Cohort obligations were unchanged.

He appreciated the sequencing.

By breakfast, the group had arranged to meet in one of the market volumes before Ollie’s shift, Samir’s practice, Aoife’s damaged documentary schedule and Maya’s temporarily reduced late-event freedom made another full gathering impossible.

Ethan arrived first.

He did not mention that.

Aoife arrived second carrying a small package.

“If you thank me like a committee chair, I’m taking it back.”

“That seems premature.”

“It is preventative.”

She put the package on the table.

“Open it.”

Ethan did.

Inside was a printed route card.

Not digital. A physical card.

It showed one of the first impossible routes he had proposed during their arrival week, with every mistake marked in red.

At the bottom Aoife had written:

FASTEST ROUTE: ASK SOMEONE.

Ethan looked at her.

“This is hostile.”

“It cost fabrication allowance.”

“You spent resources insulting me.”

“I wanted permanence.”

He laughed.

“Thank you.”

Aoife nodded once and sat down before he could expand it.

Maya arrived carrying a food container and wearing a shirt Ethan had never seen.

“Adult.”

“Still Ethan.”

“Temporary title until I find something worse.”

She put the food down.

“Your gift is that I came despite restrictions.”

Ethan answered too quickly. “You are restricted because you lied about where you slept.”

Maya froze.

Aoife looked at Ethan.

He heard the sentence after it was gone.

Maya opened the food container.

“Excellent birthday behavior.”

“Sorry.”

“Put it beside the crown. I’ll review later.”

Inside were six pieces of something baked, each cut into a different geometric shape.

“One is yours,” Maya said.

“Which one?”

“You have contractual independence now. Use it.”

Samir arrived at the same time as Ollie.

Samir carried a narrow strip of grip material used in his sport volume.

Ollie carried nothing.

“Your gift is pending,” Ollie said before Ethan could ask.

Maya made a noise.

“I had work.”

“You had advance notice of birthdays as a concept.”

“I did not know him for most of those.”

“Administrative failure.”

Samir handed Ethan the grip strip.

“What is this?”

“Use it on low-band transfers.”

“I already use the rail.”

“Wrong side.”

Aoife glanced at Ethan’s hand. “He does.”

Maya said, “I have witnessed it.”

Ollie reached for one of the baked pieces instead of adding evidence.

Ethan looked at all of them.

“This gathering has hostile intent.”

Samir sat.

“Birthday.”

They ate.

For fifteen minutes nobody discussed Ring Six, Aoife’s inference breach, Maya’s host conditions, Ethan’s adult track or anything large enough to deserve a meeting.

Maya tried to convince Samir to bring her to practice under a false name. Samir let her reach the third version of the name, then moved his grip strip out of reach.

Aoife complained that one documentary gap now lasted ninety-two seconds and looked like a deliberate artistic choice.

Ollie said gaps could be useful.

Aoife told him never to say that to an editor again.

Ethan cut the wrong baked shape and discovered Maya had filled one with something aggressively sour.

She looked innocent badly.

At 08:02 his band vibrated.

ADULT PROFESSIONAL PATHWAY — INFORMATION SESSION AVAILABLE.

He did not open it.

Aoife saw the header anyway.

Her eyes moved from the band to him and then back to her food.

Ethan hated how relieved that made him.

Maya was trying to steal Samir’s grip strip.

Nobody else noticed.

The notification disappeared.

Then returned with a second line.

OPTIONAL INTRODUCTORY MEETING — TODAY 16:30.

Ethan locked the screen.

Aoife kept eating.

* * *

The adult-track meeting took place in a civic operations office that was less impressive than Ethan had hoped and therefore more convincing.

No panoramic windows. No ceremonial table. No wall of famous names.

Four work surfaces, three people, one badly placed storage unit and a route-status display large enough to dominate the room.

The person running the meeting was a woman in her thirties who introduced herself as Tessa and immediately said, “This is not recruitment.”

Ethan sat.

“Then I have lowered my expectations at the correct time.”

“Useful skill.”

She opened his pathway record.

“You requested information before majority. Today the system stopped requiring a guardian layer for this pathway.”

“And began sending me documents at breakfast.”

“That part never improves.”

She showed him the adult civic-coordination pathway.

It was not command.

That became clear fast.

Adults on the route carried more liability, not more general permission. They could sign certain commitments directly and accept responsibility for work windows that Youth Cohort participants could only observe or assist under supervision.

They also lost protections Ethan had not realized he liked.

A missed deadline did not automatically become an educational event. A bad decision attached to a real work record. A professional conflict did not wait for a coordinator to interpret it into growth.

Schedules were less flexible. Some work began earlier. Some ended when the work ended.

“Do I get broader access?” Ethan asked.

“To what?”

“Operational decision work.”

“Sometimes. Not by turning eighteen.”

“Civic simulation?”

Tessa slid a finger down his record.

“You already know simulation is not operational work. Ask me what you actually want.”

Ethan stopped.

“How soon could I work on a real dependency problem?”

“Better.”

She opened a current project.

Not Ring Six.

A mature-ring service transition was being reorganized because one freight timing change had created conflicts between school routes, care schedules and two maintenance shifts.

No one was in danger.

Everyone was annoyed.

The task was to map dependencies and produce three viable timing options for the actual decision group.

“Can I work on that?”

“Not today.”

“After intake?”

“If accepted and scheduled.”

“How soon?”

“Sooner than the Youth Cohort route.”

The phrase stayed on the screen.

Ethan stopped the thought before it turned into a sentence.

Tessa continued.

“You would map consequences. The decision group chooses among them.”

“That part is clear.”

“Good. I’m less interested in whether you can repeat the distinction than whether you remember it when your map looks obviously correct.”

That was annoyingly specific.

The work looked exactly like the kind of problem his mind wanted.

Routes. Timing. Dependencies. Tradeoffs visible enough to map.

And no one asking him to pretend the mapping itself was the decision.

That last part irritated him slightly.

Which probably meant it mattered.

Tessa closed the example.

“Your current Youth Cohort commitment runs through the Ring Six window. You can begin adult-path orientation now. Full transfer to project work would normally wait until that closes.”

“Normally.”

“If both programmes agree, it can overlap.”

The opening arrived without him having to dig for it.

Adult project work. Real deliverables. No youth label in the title.

He could start before the others.

Before Samir’s sport became serious. Before Maya negotiated more independence. Before Aoife rebuilt her project. Before Ollie finished whatever Ring Six turned into.

The thought felt good for the wrong reason.

Tessa watched him long enough to notice.

“You do not have to schedule the next step today.”

“I understand.”

She sent him the continuation packet.

“Read it. My recommendation is after your current commitment review.”

“Recommendation, not requirement.”

“Correct.”

“Why?”

“Because you are already inside one live commitment with people depending on your participation. Starting a second path before you know what the first one costs is bad scheduling.”

“That sounds suspiciously like a moral answer.”

“I work in civic coordination. We hide most morality inside calendars.”

Ethan smiled.

She did not tell him what to choose.

Tessa slid the continuation packet across the table and waited for him to take it.

* * *

Aoife found out because Ethan left his adult-track continuation packet open on a shared study display.

Not public. Not private enough.

He had connected his tablet to the table while showing Samir a route conflict for the next day’s practice transfer.

The adult-path file appeared in the recent-documents rail for three seconds.

Aoife saw it.

Ethan saw her see it.

Samir saw both of them and stood.

“I am getting food.”

“You have food,” Ethan said.

Samir picked up the container. “Then I am relocating it.”

He left.

Aoife kept looking at the blank table rail where the file had been.

Ethan disconnected his tablet.

“That was not how I planned to tell you.”

Aoife folded her arms. “Did you go to the meeting?”

“Yes.”

“Today?”

“Yes.”

“How long have you had the notice?”

“Eight days.”

Aoife stared at him.

Ethan felt the structure of the argument forming and wanted to get ahead of it.

“I didn’t know what it meant yet.”

Aoife’s expression changed.

“You knew enough to keep it separate.”

“I wanted one thing that wasn’t immediately a group discussion.”

“That part is allowed.”

“Then what are we fighting about?”

“You already decided what telling me would do.”

Ethan frowned.

Aoife continued before he could clean up her sentence for her.

“You assumed I would have an opinion. You assumed the group would turn it into a decision. So you waited until you could explain it without letting anybody react while you were still uncertain.”

“I was trying to know what I wanted.”

“That would have been useful information eight days ago.”

Ethan leaned back.

The study volume around them continued without respect for the argument.

A student across the aisle swore at a failed simulation. Two people fought quietly over a charging surface. A transit notice moved one late route by four minutes.

Samir returned with the same food in a different container.

He sat without asking what stage they had reached.

Ethan said, “I didn’t know if I wanted it.”

Aoife asked, “Do you now?”

He looked at the continuation packet.

“Yes.”

The word arrived fast enough to surprise him.

Aoife noticed.

“Okay.”

“That’s all?”

“That is the part you get to decide.”

Ethan looked at her.

“I’m not leaving the cohort tomorrow.”

Aoife picked up her recorder bag. “I know. And I didn’t ask you to stay.”

He almost explained that he understood. The explanation would have been for him.

“So what happens now?”

“I have work.”

“Source?”

He heard the mistake immediately.

Aoife’s face closed.

“Sorry. Wrong question.”

She adjusted the recorder strap.

“Yes.”

Then she left.

Samir opened the replacement container.

Ethan said, “You can say whatever you have been saving.”

“I was trying to eat through it.”

“Did it work?”

“No.”

Ethan waited.

Samir tapped the continuation packet with the end of his fork.

“You keep asking whether people permit the thing after they have already told you what hurt.”

Ethan looked at the packet.

“That is not the same as deciding whether I take it.”

“Exactly.”

That was more useful than Ethan wanted it to be.

He opened the response field.

CONTINUE ADULT-PATH PROCESS?

YES / LATER / DECLINE

His thumb hovered over YES.

The adult path was still real. Attractive. His.

He moved to LATER.

Not decline.

Not surrender.

LATER — AFTER CURRENT RING SIX COMMITMENT REVIEW.

The system accepted it.

Samir glanced at the screen.

“She didn’t ask you to do that.”

“I know.”

Samir took another bite.

This time Ethan heard what the sentence was for.

“She didn’t,” he said again, and locked the tablet.

“You are very irritating at eighteen.”

Samir looked at the time.

“You have been eighteen for less than a day.”

“Strong sample.”
