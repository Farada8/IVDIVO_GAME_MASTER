# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 24 — WHERE MAYA IS

Maya knew the late-event agreement was real because it was annoying in exactly three places.

The first was the return window.

23:20 VELA LOCAL.

The second was the named contact.

AREL — EVENT CONTACT ONLY.

The third was the sentence her host had added after their last argument.

IF A ROUTE OR ENVIRONMENT CHANGE ALTERS YOUR RETURN OR SLEEP PLAN, UPDATE BEFORE YOU CHANGE THE PLAN.

Under it:

LIVE LOCATION: NOT REQUIRED.

Maya had read that line four times when the agreement arrived.

The first three because she liked it.

The fourth because she wanted to make sure there was not another line underneath saying *unless we become nervous*.

There was not.

She had a zone.
A contact.
A return condition.

Nobody was watching a dot move across a map.

She could live with that.

At 18:42 she entered the late-market level in Zone Four wearing a jacket she had borrowed from Aoife and absolutely had not asked whether Aoife wanted back that night.

Arel was already there with Davi and two people Maya knew well enough to insult but not well enough to borrow money from.

Lea arrived seven minutes later.

Maya noticed because she had been pretending not to watch the entrance.

Lea had changed after school and looked like someone who had done it in a temporary housing washroom with bad lighting and won anyway.

“Your jacket is Earth,” Lea said.

Maya looked down.

“It is fabric.”

“It is aggressively Earth fabric.”

“Aoife owns it.”

“That explains the pockets.”

“What is wrong with the pockets?”

“Too many. Visitors think every object needs a documentary backup.”

Maya put both hands into two different pockets.

“Jealous.”

“Deeply.”

The exchange was almost normal.

Almost mattered too much.

Davi handed Maya something fried on a stick.

“What is it?”

“Food.”

“That is not an answer.”

“Ollie contamination.”

Maya bit it.

It was hot enough to punish curiosity.

She refused to react.

Arel watched her.

“You burned your mouth.”

“No.”

“You stopped breathing.”

“Strategic.”

Lea said, “Very planet.”

Maya swallowed with dignity she had not earned.

The late-market level occupied the inside curve of an older public ring. During the day it was mostly food counters, repair kiosks and transit spillover. At night half the counters folded into benches, projection walls came down over service shutters, and the central floor changed its gravity profile just enough for local teenagers to turn walking into an unnecessary competitive activity.

Maya had become less terrible at it.

This was not the same as good.

At 19:10 she lost a balance game to a twelve-year-old.

At 19:22 she won against Davi only because Davi looked away to insult someone.

At 19:31 Arel told her the win was invalid.

At 19:32 Maya demanded a formal review.

At 19:33 everyone left to buy drinks.

Nobody discussed Ring Six for almost an hour.

That was the best part.

Lea complained about the temporary unit’s shower because the temperature control had two settings: reasonable and betrayal.

Davi had missed a school assessment because his alarm followed an old local schedule after a route shift.

Arel was trying to trade one workshop block for a later one and had discovered that three other people wanted exactly the same trade.

Maya complained that her host had made her rewrite the late-event agreement twice because “somewhere around there” did not qualify as a zone.

Lea looked at her.

“You wrote somewhere around there?”

“It was accurate.”

“It was language pretending not to be information.”

Maya pointed at her.

“That sounded like Aoife.”

“Take it back.”

“Never.”

For a few minutes the old argument between them disappeared under ordinary ones.

Maya knew better than to touch it.

At 21:46 the person she had hoped would show up finally did.

Maya had not told anyone she was hoping.

This protected her from absolutely nothing.

Lea noticed first.

“Oh,” she said.

Maya kept looking at the drink menu.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Davi followed Lea’s gaze.

“Oh.”

Maya turned on both of them.

“If either of you becomes interesting, I am leaving.”

“You have a return window,” Lea said.

“Exactly. I can weaponize it.”

The person who had arrived crossed toward a group near the projection wall, saw Maya and raised a hand.

Maya raised hers back casually enough to injure several muscles.

Arel said, “Very smooth.”

“Die.”

“Can’t. Named contact.”

That ruined everyone for thirty seconds.

Maya hated them and stayed.

* * *

The route closure arrived at 22:08.

It appeared on every local board at once.

NORTH TRANSFER — CLOSE 22:20–00:10.

RING SIX ROLLBACK / RELOCATION SERVICE PRIORITY.

PASSENGER ALTERNATES: EAST TWO / LOWER LOOP / VERIFIED SERVICE SHUTTLES WHERE POSTED.

For three seconds nobody at Maya’s table reacted.

Then everyone checked their own route.

The social level changed around them.

Not panic.

Calculation.

People who had planned to leave later stood up.
Two groups combined transport.
Someone shouted across the floor asking who was going toward the lower loop.
A counter worker changed the staff-exit notice.

Maya opened her return plan.

NORTH TRANSFER was the first leg.

CLOSED.

The suggested East Two alternative arrived at her host sector at 00:03 if every connection held.

Her return window was 23:20.

The lower loop required leaving Zone Four and crossing a sector her agreement did not include.

A verified service shuttle showed one departure at 22:37, limited capacity, priority to displaced households and workers.

Davi looked over her shoulder.

“You can make East Two.”

“After midnight.”

“So?”

“So my agreement says 23:20.”

Davi stared.

“You have a curfew.”

“I have a return condition.”

“That is a curfew with branding.”

Maya shoved his shoulder.

Arel said, “Stay at mine.”

The sentence was simple.

Too simple.

That was what she had done last time.

Stayed at Arel’s.

Let the host continue believing Lea’s household was the backup.

Got home safe and treated safety as the end of the equation.

Maya looked at her agreement.

IF A ROUTE OR ENVIRONMENT CHANGE ALTERS YOUR RETURN OR SLEEP PLAN, UPDATE BEFORE YOU CHANGE THE PLAN.

Davi said, “Just tell them you’re with Lea again.”

He meant it as a joke.

Maya answered before Lea could.

“No.”

Davi blinked.

Maya locked her screen.

“Lea is not my backup.”

The table went quiet for half a second.

Lea looked at Maya.

Maya did not look back.

Arel said, “Mine is actually possible.”

“I know.”

“You already survived it once.”

“Strong recommendation.”

“Five stars. One functioning shower.”

Lea said, “Luxury.”

The conversation restarted, but something had shifted.

Maya opened her host thread.

Her thumb hovered.

She could wait.

The closure had just happened.

Nobody knew whether it would reopen early.

She could stay at the market another hour, see what happened, then message when she had an actual solution.

That sounded reasonable.

It also sounded exactly like waiting until the truth became convenient to report.

Maya hated that resemblance.

She typed:

MAYA: North Transfer closure removes planned return. Still in Zone Four with agreed event contact Arel. I have not changed plan yet. Options here are East Two after return window or overnight at Arel’s if approved. No route move yet.

She read it.

Davi leaned over.

“You write like Ethan now.”

Maya covered the screen.

“Read my messages again and I will throw you into a lower gravity band.”

“Threat recorded.”

“No recorder.”

“Wrong friend.”

Maya sent the message.

Her stomach tightened immediately.

Nothing bad had happened.

That was annoying too.

The host replied ninety seconds later.

HOST: received. Stay within current agreed zone until plan confirmed. Is Arel willing to be overnight household/contact if needed?

Maya looked at Arel.

“Are you actually offering or socially offering?”

Arel frowned.

“What is socially offering?”

“Offering because it is easy to say while assuming I will solve something else.”

“I am offering because we have a spare sleep panel and my household already knows you.”

“Do they want me?”

“They survived last time.”

“That is not consent.”

Arel stared at her.

Lea started laughing.

Maya turned.

“What?”

“Nothing. Keep going.”

Arel opened a household thread.

“My mother says yes if you stop leaving cups beside the service shelf.”

“That happened once.”

“She has evidence.”

“Hostile household.”

“Do you want it or not?”

“Yes.”

Maya wrote:

MAYA: Arel confirms household okay with overnight. Request sleep-plan change to Arel household, same zone. Contact remains Arel. Return tomorrow by 09:00 unless routes change again.

She stopped before sending.

Lea was watching the projection wall, not Maya.

Maya sent it.

HOST: received. Need direct household confirmation through contact channel. Sending request to Arel. No live location required. Stay current zone until confirmed.

Davi said, “This is extremely romantic.”

Maya kicked his chair.

“It is an administrative sleep panel.”

“The foundation of all great love.”

Arel’s band vibrated.

He opened the confirmation request.

“Your host asks whether my household address is current.”

“It better be.”

“I moved in the last twelve minutes.”

“Confirm.”

Arel did.

A minute later:

HOST: overnight change approved. Arel household/contact. No tracking. If you leave Zone Four or contact changes, update before moving. Return condition 09:00. If relocation closure expands, contact me before choosing a new route.

Maya stared at the message.

That was it.

No demand for a live map.
No call.
No interrogation.

Davi looked at her.

“Disappointed?”

“No.”

“You look disappointed.”

“I expected more resistance.”

Lea said, “You told them before changing anything.”

Maya turned toward her.

Lea’s face gave nothing away.

“Yes.”

“That is why.”

Maya wanted to take the sentence as praise.

Lea picked up her drink before Maya could.

“Do not make it a ceremony.”

“Wasn’t going to.”

“You were.”

“Maybe a small ceremony.”

“No.”

Maya put the band away.

The person she had hoped would arrive was still on the other side of the room.

They looked over.

Maya smiled back.

Davi made a noise.

Maya kicked his chair again.

Her night continued.

* * *

At 23:04 the late-market level began thinning out.

The North Transfer closure had scattered people into solutions the route planner had never listed as one plan.

Two students left early for East Two.

Three stayed with friends nearby.

A worker joined a verified shuttle after an allocation family released a seat.

One group crossed to the lower loop because they were already authorized for both zones.

Davi convinced someone’s older sibling to collect him at a maintenance-adjacent transfer.

The same closure produced six different nights.

Maya noticed because she had spent the last hour arguing about everybody’s choices.

She did not turn it into a theory.

At 23:10 Lea stood.

“My temporary route is still open.”

“Miracle,” Davi said.

“Do not say that near transport.”

She collected her jacket.

Maya stood too.

“I’ll walk to your connector.”

Lea looked at her.

“You’re staying here.”

“The connector is inside Zone Four.”

“That sounded defensive.”

“It was geographic.”

Lea started walking.

Maya followed.

The corridor outside the social level was quieter. Route boards still carried the North Transfer closure and a growing list of relocation service moves.

For a while they walked without speaking.

Maya let the silence exist for almost thirty seconds.

This was personal growth and she wanted credit.

She did not ask for it.

At the connector Lea said, “You didn’t use me.”

Maya looked at her.

“No.”

“Good.”

The word arrived flatter than Maya wanted.

“I corrected the host after last time.”

“I know.”

“And I told them tonight before changing the plan.”

“I know.”

Maya felt irritation rise.

“You could sound slightly less angry about me doing the thing you wanted.”

Lea turned toward her.

“That is not what I wanted.”

“What did you want?”

“I wanted not to be put in it the first time.”

Maya stopped.

Lea continued.

“You fixed the host record. Good. You didn’t use my household tonight. Good. That doesn’t turn the old night into nothing.”

“I didn’t say it did.”

“You are looking like it should count toward something.”

“It should count toward me not doing it again.”

“Yes.”

“Then what?”

Lea looked at the route board above them.

“You got to be private that night. I got to remember what I was supposed to say if someone asked.”

Maya’s first response died before she said it.

Nobody asked.

That was true.

It was also not the point Lea had made.

Maya leaned against the wall.

“I didn’t think you were carrying it after the night.”

“I know.”

“That is worse when you say it like that.”

“Yes.”

Maya looked down the corridor.

A relocation cart crossed the far junction with two folded panels strapped to it.

“Did you have to lie?” Maya asked.

“No.”

“Did anyone contact you?”

“Not then.”

“Then what were you carrying?”

Lea stared at her.

Maya almost apologized for the question.

Lea answered.

“The possibility. You had already decided that if it became a problem, I was part of the answer.”

Maya looked at the floor.

That was specific enough to hurt.

“I’m sorry.”

Lea did not rescue her from the silence.

Maya added nothing.

After a while Lea said, “I saw you say no to Davi.”

Maya looked up.

“Good.”

“Do not become unbearable.”

“Trying.”

“I remain unconvinced.”

The transfer arrived.

Lea stepped toward it.

Maya said, “Are we okay?”

Lea stopped with one foot inside.

“No.”

Maya’s face fell before she could stop it.

Lea looked almost sorry and then did not give her the easy version.

“We’re better than yesterday,” she said. “That is not the same thing.”

The doors warned.

Lea stepped fully inside.

Maya said, “Fine.”

“Very mature.”

“I can retract it.”

The doors closed on Lea’s smile before Maya could decide whether it counted.

* * *

Arel’s household was thirteen minutes away from the connector.

Maya walked there without sending another location update because she remained inside the agreed zone, with the agreed contact, under the approved sleep change.

That felt almost rebellious now.

At the door, Arel’s mother looked at Maya, then at the band in her hand.

“Your host knows this time?”

Maya froze.

Arel said, “Mother.”

“What? She left a cup beside the service shelf and appeared without paperwork last time.”

“It was not paperwork,” Maya said.

“It was definitely paperwork.”

Maya held up the approval message.

“Confirmed before arrival.”

Arel’s mother glanced at it.

“Good.”

Maya waited.

“That’s all?”

“What do you want, applause?”

“No.”

“Then put your cup in the correct place.”

Arel started laughing before the door closed.

Maya shoved him toward the corridor.

“Your whole household is hostile.”

“You requested it.”

Inside, the spare sleep panel was already down.

Maya put her bag beside it.

Her band showed one new host message.

HOST: agreement worked under route change. keep same conditions for next authorized late event; we can review return window separately tomorrow. no live tracking proposed.

Maya read it twice.

Not because she was looking for a hidden condition this time.

Because the sentence was good.

She typed:

MAYA: understood

Then deleted it.

Typed:

MAYA: okay. want later return window next time.

HOST: unsurprising. discuss tomorrow.

Maya locked the band.

She had stayed out late.

She had changed where she was sleeping.

Nobody had tracked her.

Nobody had been turned into her cover.

Lea was still angry.

All four things were true at once.

Arel threw a clean blanket at her from the doorway.

Maya caught it with one hand.

“Light off in ten,” he said.

“Your hospitality remains poor.”

“You have a confirmed overnight slot. Do not abuse your power.”

Maya threw the blanket back at him.

He ducked.

She had to pick it up herself.
