# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 32 — THE LATE ROUTE

Lea did not want another apology.

Maya discovered this while holding one end of a storage case that was too wide for the corridor and too important, apparently, to have been packed in anything sensible.

“I was going to say something,” Maya said.

“I know.”

Lea pulled the case sideways.

It hit the wall.

“Not that way.”

“You are on the wall side.”

“You are steering.”

“I was unaware this had leadership structure.”

“It does now. Lift.”

Maya lifted.

The case cleared the corner by two centimeters.

Lea kept walking.

They were moving school equipment from the temporary household unit to a handoff shelf near the first-phase relocation route. Lea's household was not moving together. Two members could enter the first safe segment. Two could not. The family had chosen not to split yet.

The school case still had to move because one of Lea's younger siblings had an assessment kit that belonged near the new route hub, not in the temporary unit forty minutes away.

This was the kind of detail Maya had not understood when Ring Six was just a place people were waiting to inhabit.

Now it had handles.

Bad ones.

They reached the handoff shelf.

Lea said, “Down.”

Maya lowered the case.

“About before—”

“No.”

Maya straightened.

“That is an aggressive conversational policy.”

“I heard the apology.”

“That was not the whole thing.”

“It usually isn’t with you.”

Maya opened her mouth.

Lea pointed at the storage case.

“You wanted to help. You helped.”

“That is not relationship repair.”

“It is a box.”

“Case.”

Lea stared at her.

Maya looked at the case.

“Fine.”

A relocation worker scanned the handoff tag.

“Wrong shelf.”

Lea closed her eyes.

Maya said, “Excellent.”

The worker pointed across the junction.

“School-transfer staging moved to Four-C because the freight window shifted.”

“That was changed this morning,” Lea said.

“Changed again.”

“When?”

“Twenty minutes ago.”

Lea picked up one end of the case.

Maya took the other without being asked.

The worker said, “Four-C, yellow marker, not orange. Orange is temporary resident bedding.”

Maya looked across the junction.

Three marker colors overlapped under the transfer display.

“Who designed that?”

“Someone who is not carrying the case.”

“Coward.”

Lea pulled.

Maya followed.

They made it twelve meters before a service cart blocked the direct crossing.

The cart operator was unloading sealed household support units into a temporary bay. Two more carts waited behind it.

Lea stopped.

Maya looked at the route board.

Four-C was visible on the other side.

Direct route: blocked.

Normal alternative: seven minutes around the residential access loop.

Late service cut: open for another eleven minutes.

Maya smiled.

Lea saw it.

“No.”

“You do not know what I am going to say.”

“You got the route face.”

“I do not have a route face.”

“You absolutely do.”

Maya pulled up the late-service path.

“It is open.”

“To us?”

“To registered support movement.”

“We are carrying school equipment.”

“Exactly.”

Lea looked at the case.

Then at Maya.

“This is the task.”

“Yes.”

“Move this case to Four-C.”

“Yes.”

“Not redesign my family.”

Maya frowned.

“I did not redesign your family.”

“Not yet.”

They took the service cut.

It was faster.

Maya enjoyed this more than was socially appropriate.

At Four-C, the receiving worker scanned the case and said, “This household is not moving today.”

“Correct,” Lea said.

“Then why is school equipment staged here?”

“Because the school route changes before the room does.”

The worker checked the household profile.

“Right. Sorry.”

Maya looked over Lea's shoulder.

The profile showed the same problem she already knew about: the family could reduce its temporary commute if it accepted a split move for three nights, then reunified when the fourth segment cleared or a different compatible room opened.

Three nights.

Maya read the route consequence.

Then read it again.

“You could do this.”

Lea did not turn.

Maya tapped the temporary split option.

“Three nights. Your mother and brother move first. You and—”

“No.”

“You have not looked.”

“I looked yesterday.”

“The late transfer changed.”

Lea turned now.

“What?”

“The old version gave you the twenty-three-minute school route if you stayed temporary. But Four-C is live now. If your mother moves first, your brother can use the short route, and you can stay with the current host unit until—”

“Maya.”

“It removes one of the bad parts.”

“It adds another move.”

“Only for three nights.”

“For you.”

Maya stopped.

Lea folded her arms.

“My mother has already moved twice.”

“I know.”

“My brother has slept in three rooms this month.”

“I know.”

“We said no split.”

“I know, but this version—”

“No.”

The word was quiet enough that Maya finally heard it.

Lea looked at the household profile, not at her.

“We said no split because we do not want another temporary arrangement just because somebody found one that looks efficient.”

“It is not just efficient. It fixes the school route.”

“For him.”

“Yes.”

“And gives my mother another move.”

Maya looked at the profile.

“And gives you another night transfer.”

“Yes.”

“And makes us decide again after we already decided.”

Maya closed the option.

The receiving worker was pretending not to listen.

Badly.

Maya lowered her voice.

“I was trying to help.”

Lea's expression did not change.

“I asked you to carry the case.”

That hurt more than it should have.

Maya looked at the case.

It was on the correct shelf now.

Task completed.

No one had requested household optimization.

“What do you actually want me to do?” Maya asked.

Lea blinked.

Maya disliked that the question sounded unfamiliar in her own mouth.

Lea looked toward the transfer board.

“Find out whether the school handoff stays at Four-C tomorrow morning.”

“That is all?”

“Yes.”

Maya almost asked whether Lea wanted the fastest route too.

Did not.

“Okay.”

“And if it changes, message me. Do not change anything for us.”

“Understood.”

Lea picked up her empty strap.

Maya said, “You could sound less surprised.”

“I am trying.”

“That was almost friendly.”

“It was not.”

“Better than yesterday?”

Lea looked at her.

“Do not push it.”

Maya smiled anyway.

* * *

The Four-C handoff stayed valid until 18:26.

At 18:27 it moved.

Maya was on her way to meet Davi and two others in Zone Three when the route notice changed.

SCHOOL TRANSFER STAGING — TEMPORARY SHIFT

FOUR-C → THREE-F

EFFECTIVE 05:40–09:10

She stopped in the middle of a low-gravity connector.

A woman behind her said, “Move or become architecture.”

Maya moved.

She sent Lea the update.

MAYA: school staging moved to 3-F tomorrow 05:40–09:10. I did not change your family.

Three dots appeared.

LEA: astonishing restraint

MAYA: painful

LEA: route from temp unit?

Maya checked.

The most obvious path used Transfer B.

The best late path used D, but that did not matter for morning.

She sent only the morning route.

LEA: thanks

Maya looked at the word.

One practical request.

One practical answer.

No declaration that they were fine.

She put the device away.

At 18:41 Davi messaged:

DAVI: changed plan. Zone 3 food place full. going 4 low band instead

Maya checked her host agreement.

Her declared zone for the evening was Three.

Zone Four was also permitted under the standing trial, but the plan had materially changed.

She sent the minimum update.

MAYA → HOST: plan changed to Zone 4 low band. Davi + Nera. return condition unchanged, 22:54 E/C.

HOST: received

No question.

No permission request.

Maya continued toward Four.

The process took nineteen seconds.

She resented every one of them on principle.

At 19:03 the transfer timing shifted by four minutes.

Her return condition did not change.

She sent nothing.

That felt even better.

* * *

The food place in Four had six tables designed for four people and approximately forty people refusing to accept this information.

Maya found Davi sitting on the edge of a structural step with Nera and two boys she knew badly enough to still remember their names carefully.

“You are late,” Davi said.

“Your plan moved rings.”

“Zone.”

“Do not become technical at me.”

He handed her a drink.

Maya took it.

For twenty-two minutes she did nothing useful.

It was excellent.

They argued about music. Nera complained about a teacher who had rescheduled an assessment during migration week. Davi claimed he could tell which Earth arrivals still thought in floor plans because they kept asking what level things were on.

Maya accused him of thinking in railings.

He accused her of fighting furniture.

She was winning when her device buzzed.

LEA: problem

Maya opened it immediately.

LEA: 3-F staging marker still shows old temporary-unit approach. school case is there but morning support route points kids through closed connector

A photo followed.

The official route marker was technically correct from the residential side.

From the temporary unit side it sent people toward a connector that would be restricted during the morning freight release.

Maya stood.

Davi looked at her.

“Where?”

“Three-F.”

“You just got here.”

“I know.”

Nera said, “Do you have to?”

Maya looked at the message again.

Lea had asked her.

Not because Maya had forced a solution into the household profile.

Because the route marker was wrong for the people actually using it.

“I said I would check the school handoff.”

Davi said, “That sounds suspiciously like responsibility.”

“Do not say disgusting things.”

He grinned.

“Come back?”

Maya checked the route.

“If it is quick.”

“Dangerous sentence.”

She left.

On the way to Three-F she sent her host another update.

MAYA: still permitted zones. going 3-F for school-route marker, then back 4 if time. return condition unchanged.

HOST: received

That was all.

At Three-F, Lea was waiting beside two relocation volunteers and the bad marker.

Maya approached from the temporary-unit direction.

The arrow looked perfect.

It pointed directly toward the closed connector.

“That is terrible,” Maya said.

“I know.”

“From the residential side it works.”

“I know.”

Maya stopped herself before the third repetition.

One of the volunteers said, “Operations already has the closure. We need a temporary traveler-facing route until the morning board refresh.”

Maya opened the local late/morning route map.

“Three-F to E, E to school transfer, but the first sign has to be before this junction or people commit to the wrong corridor.”

The volunteer nodded.

“Can you walk it once from the temporary-unit approach?”

“Yes.”

“Mark where the instruction becomes too late.”

“Do I get to insult the sign?”

“Privately.”

“No professional freedom.”

“None.”

Maya walked the route.

At the second turn, the alternative still made sense.

At the third, a family with two school bags would already have crossed the easiest return point.

She marked the earlier junction.

The volunteer checked it against operations restrictions.

“Good. We can place a temporary notice here.”

Maya said, “And at the low-band exit.”

“Why?”

“Because local people will use the inside rail and see this. New arrivals will stay wide and miss it behind the crowd.”

The volunteer looked from Maya to the junction.

Then walked the approach.

“Fair.”

They added the second notice.

Lea watched without helping Maya turn that into anything larger.

When they finished, she said, “Can you do the same check on the late relocation path tomorrow?”

Maya looked at her.

“Are you asking me?”

“Yes.”

“Specifically me?”

Lea's expression became dangerous.

Maya lifted both hands.

“Stopping.”

“Good.”

The relocation volunteer said, “We actually need young-route checks during peak. Not authorization. Walk-through and signage reality check. Staff confirms changes.”

Maya said, “Paid?”

“No.”

“Food?”

“Yes.”

“Better system.”

“06:30.”

Maya's face changed.

Lea smiled for the first time that evening.

“That is the cost of freedom.”

“Do not become philosophical.”

“06:30.”

Maya checked the time.

She still had twenty-eight minutes before she needed to start toward her planned return.

Davi had sent:

DAVI: alive?

Then:

DAVI: nera says responsibility killed you

Maya typed:

MAYA: temporary death. back in 6

She paused.

Her route back to Four was still inside the declared zones. Return condition still unchanged.

No host update required.

She sent the message to Davi and put the device away.

Lea picked up one end of the empty marker case.

Maya took the other.

This time she did not ask where they should optimize it.

“Where?” she asked.

Lea pointed.

They carried it there.

At 22:54 Maya took E/C home as declared.

At 06:30 the next morning, she was standing beside a relocation cart with bad coffee, three temporary route markers and no authority over anything important.

Move day began around her.
