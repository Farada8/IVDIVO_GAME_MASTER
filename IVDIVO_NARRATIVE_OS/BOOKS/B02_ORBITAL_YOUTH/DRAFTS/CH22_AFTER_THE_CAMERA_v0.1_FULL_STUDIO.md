ORBITAL YOUTH
Chapter 22 — AFTER THE CAMERA

Aoife checked that the clip was gone three times before she admitted she was not checking the clip.

The original page returned the same message every time.

REMOVED BY PUBLISHER.

Below it sat the archival accountability notice she had left in place after the takedown.

SOURCE REQUESTED REMOVAL.
ORIGINAL PUBLICATION WITHDRAWN.
DERIVATIVE COPIES MAY EXIST OUTSIDE PUBLISHER CONTROL.

She had written the notice herself and hated how competent it looked.

The original had been down for four days.

The copied frames had not.

Aoife opened the incident-discussion feed and found the same image again: the blurred service wall, the corner of the humidity-buffer marker, the red circle somebody else had drawn around it.

The caption above it had changed.

RING SIX FAILURE RAISES NEW QUESTIONS ABOUT HIDDEN ACCOMMODATION INFRASTRUCTURE.

Hidden.

As if a thing became secret because the person filming it had failed to understand what another viewer could infer.

She closed the post.

A second account had isolated the faint coupling tone.

A third had combined the old transfer-board timestamp with public housing-delay data and produced a new speculation thread about which resident class had been waiting for Ring Six.

Most of it was wrong.

Enough of it was close.

Aoife put both hands flat on the table.

Her editing interface was open in front of her.

The project still existed locally because deleting the project would have destroyed the record of what had actually been published, approved and removed. She had reduced it to the minimum evidence package the source agreement required: approval version, publication version, takedown record, timestamps, correspondence about removal.

No public render.
No working social cut.
No discovery copy.

The sequence itself was still beautiful.

That was irritating.

The food machine opened the piece cleanly. The shift from transit noise to the cramped table made the next conversation feel intimate without announcing intimacy. The small joke about the cupboard still landed exactly where she had wanted it to land.

Aoife closed the timeline.

An interview request appeared before the window disappeared.

EARTH YOUTH CURRENT — REQUEST FOR COMMENT

We are preparing a segment on orbital youth, hidden adaptation systems and the Ring Six shutdown. Your original documentary appears to have captured early evidence relevant to current concerns. Would you discuss what you knew, what you were asked to remove, and whether local authorities discouraged disclosure?

Aoife read the sentence twice.

There were at least four false assumptions in it.

Her first response formed immediately.

She had not known.
Nobody had discouraged disclosure.
The source had requested privacy for a personal accommodation.
The Ring Six shutdown did not prove the clip had captured anything causal.

All true.

All useful to the interviewer.

All guaranteed to put the source back into circulation.

Aoife pressed decline.

A field opened.

REASON OPTIONAL.

She left it blank.

The next request came from a smaller account asking only for permission to reproduce three frames in a media-ethics thread.

Decline.

The third wanted an audio statement about de-identification failure.

Decline.

The fourth offered to blur the original blurred frame more heavily.

Aoife almost laughed.

Decline.

Ethan sat down opposite her with a drink and did not look at the screen.

That was deliberate enough that she noticed.

“You can look,” she said.

“I know.”

“You are making not looking noticeable.”

“I was trying not to interfere.”

“You are interfering visually.”

He moved the drink two centimeters.

“Better?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Aoife closed the requests.

Ethan waited.

He was getting better at waiting and she resented that this made him more useful.

“The original is still down,” she said.

“Okay.”

“There are more copies.”

“I assumed.”

“People are tying it to Ring Six now.”

Ethan looked at her then.

“Do you want help?”

Aoife thought about the last time he had offered to help frame a response.

“No.”

“Okay.”

He opened his own work.

That was all.

Aoife looked at him.

“What?” he asked.

“Nothing.”

“You were expecting me to continue.”

“No.”

“You have an expectation face.”

“Stop learning local nonsense.”

He looked back down.

Her band vibrated.

S1: I will meet

Aoife stopped breathing for one second.

Then another message arrived.

S1: no recorder

S1: no notes

S1: no transcript

S1: no summary system

Aoife read the four lines.

She typed:

AOIFE: yes

Deleted it.

Typed:

AOIFE: agreed

Deleted that too.

Finally:

AOIFE: okay

S1: your band off

AOIFE: yes

S1: not silent. off

Aoife looked at the band on her wrist.

AOIFE: okay

The meeting location arrived.

Ethan looked up because she had gone still.

She turned the display toward him only far enough to show the location, not the thread.

“I’m going out.”

He read the location.

“Do you need—”

“No.”

He stopped.

Aoife stood.

Ethan said, “Aoife.”

She looked back.

“Nothing,” he said. “Go.”

She hated that this was exactly right.

* * *

The meeting place was a narrow public food terrace attached to a residential connector, private enough that nobody crossed it by accident and public enough that neither of them needed to pretend this was friendship.

Aoife arrived six minutes early.

She powered her band down.

The dark screen immediately made her feel less prepared.

She put it inside her bag.

Then took it out again and switched the bag to manual lock so the band could not wake for transit or emergency summaries.

The source arrived carrying a food container and a folded work jacket.

They looked at Aoife’s wrist first.

“Off?”

“Yes.”

“Show me.”

Aoife took the band out.

Black screen.

The source held out a hand.

Aoife gave it over.

They pressed the side control.

Nothing happened.

“Fine.”

They handed it back.

Aoife put it into the bag and locked the bag again.

The source sat.

Aoife sat opposite.

For several seconds neither spoke.

There was a strong frame in it.

Aoife noticed that and immediately became angry with herself.

The source opened the food container.

“You can eat,” Aoife said.

“I know.”

“Sorry.”

The source looked up.

Aoife almost added for everything.

That would have made the first word do too much work.

She started again.

“I’m sorry for what the piece made possible.”

The source stopped opening the container.

Aoife continued before she could retreat into accuracy.

“I kept the words and cuts we agreed. I removed what you asked me to remove. And I still published enough context for other people to work backward into something you told me not to make public.”

The source looked at her.

Aoife had another sentence ready.

The background marker was ordinary.
The tone was ordinary.
The route board was public.
No single element identified anyone.

All true.

She did not say any of it.

The silence extended.

Aoife let it.

The source opened the food container fully.

“You checked the agreement?”

“Yes.”

“After?”

“Yes.”

“To see if you technically broke it.”

Aoife looked down.

“Yes.”

“What did it say?”

“That I didn’t explicitly publish the protected detail.”

The source took one bite of food.

Aoife waited.

“And?”

“And that was not enough.”

The source’s expression tightened.

“Do not turn this into the good version of your lesson.”

Aoife felt the reply arrive.

I am not.

She stopped it.

“Okay.”

The source looked toward the connector traffic.

A delivery worker passed behind Aoife carrying two sealed containers and complaining to someone on a call about a late route window.

When the worker was gone, the source said, “I agreed to those words. I did not agree to become legible in that way.”

Aoife nodded once.

“That is fair.”

“No.”

Aoife looked up.

“It is not about fair,” the source said. “You keep trying to put it in a category where you can answer correctly.”

Aoife closed her mouth.

The source ate again.

Aoife waited.

It became physically difficult not to fill the silence.

She could feel sentences accumulating behind her teeth.

She could explain what she had taken down.
She could explain the derivative copies.
She could explain that she had declined four interview requests that morning.
She could explain that she had preserved only the minimum accountability archive.

She could present evidence of improved behavior.

The source had not asked for any of it.

Aoife picked at a small scratch in the table surface instead.

After a while the source said, “Did you answer the Earth interview people?”

“No.”

“How many?”

“Four requests today. More before.”

“What did you tell them?”

“Nothing.”

The source looked at her.

Aoife said, “I thought any explanation from me would put the inference back into circulation.”

“That was probably right.”

Probably.

Not good.
Not thank you.
Not forgiven.

Aoife found that she preferred it.

The source looked down at the food.

“People at work know.”

Aoife’s stomach tightened.

“Because of the repost?”

“Some because of that. Some because people asked questions until they found someone who knew someone.”

Aoife did not say I’m sorry again.

The source continued.

“One person asked if my support setting was why I got moved into the Ring Six queue.”

“Was it?”

The source looked at her sharply.

Aoife felt herself flinch.

“That was a question,” she said.

“Yes.”

“I shouldn’t have asked it.”

“No.”

Aoife nodded.

The source closed the food container.

“You still do it.”

“What?”

“You hear a fact and immediately look for the next fact.”

Aoife almost defended journalism as a sequence of questions.

She did not.

The source put both hands on the table.

“Ring Six review contacted our household.”

Aoife looked up.

“For the incident?”

“Yes.”

“Because of the resident reports?”

The source’s eyes narrowed.

Aoife stopped.

“Sorry.”

“Better.”

They sat for another few seconds.

Then the source said, “The timeline they sent me is wrong.”

Aoife’s body reacted before her mind did.

She wanted the band.

Not to publish.

To capture the exact sentence.
The exact time.
The exact distinction.

Her bag sat beside her chair with the band powered off inside it.

She kept both hands on the table.

“How wrong?”

“The first warning time.”

Aoife waited.

The source watched her, perhaps checking whether she would reach for anything.

She did not.

“They start my report when the interface logged the adaptation event,” the source said. “That is later than when I noticed the change.”

“How much later?”

“I don’t know exactly.”

Aoife felt a small internal collapse.

The journalist part of her wanted exact numbers.

The source continued.

“I was already noticing the pressure change before I opened the report path. Then the support correction started. Then I logged it.”

Aoife held the order in her head.

Notice.
Support correction.
Report.

She did not repeat it aloud yet.

The source said, “It is not a huge secret. I told the review contact. I am telling you because the clip discussion has people acting like the support tone proves some hidden machine condition. It doesn’t. The support corrected because I felt something first.”

Aoife said, “Do you know what you felt?”

“No.”

“Same as the later Ring Six incident?”

“I wasn’t in the later incident.”

“Right.”

“Do not build the bridge for me.”

Aoife breathed out.

“Okay.”

The source looked at her bag.

“You want to write this down.”

“Yes.”

“You can’t.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

Aoife almost smiled.

The source did not.

She stopped.

The source stood.

Aoife stood too.

“Wait,” she said.

The source’s face closed slightly.

Aoife chose the next sentence slowly.

“May I pass one thing to the incident review?”

The source did not answer immediately.

“The timing order only,” Aoife said. “That your experienced onset began before the interface log. No recording, no old clip, no location, no accommodation class, no support tone. Just that the current reconstruction may start your warning window too late.”

The source looked at her.

“No publication?”

“No publication.”

“No documentary update?”

“No.”

“No telling Ollie directly.”

Aoife paused.

“No.”

“He is on review hold.”

“I know.”

“And you do not get to call this your correction.”

Aoife felt that one.

“No.”

The source adjusted the folded work jacket under one arm.

“Then yes.”

Aoife nodded.

“Thank you.”

“That is not forgiveness.”

“I know.”

The source looked unconvinced that she knew.

Aoife accepted that too.

They left in different directions.

* * *

Aoife did not power her band on until she reached a public transit landing two connectors away.

The device woke into thirteen notifications.

Two new interview requests.
One derivative repost alert.
Three group messages.
A school deadline.
A message from Maya containing only:

MAYA: are you alive

Aoife ignored all of them long enough to open the incident-reporting portal.

The portal was not part of her media workspace.

That mattered more than she expected.

INCIDENT REVIEW — EXTERNAL FACTUAL CORRECTION

The first field asked for source identity.

Aoife selected:

WITHHELD / REVIEW-VERIFIED CONTACT AVAILABLE THROUGH AUTHORIZED LIAISON.

The next asked whether the source had consented to submission.

YES.

Scope of consent.

Aoife typed carefully.

DE-IDENTIFIED TIMING STATEMENT ONLY. NO ORIGINAL RECORDING. NO PUBLICATION USE. NO RESIDENTIAL / MEDICAL / ACCOMMODATION CONTEXT. NO DIRECT RELEASE TO PERSONNEL UNDER REVIEW HOLD.

The next field asked for the correction.

Aoife stopped.

She could hear the source’s wording in her head.

The timing order.

Notice.
Support correction.
Report.

She wrote:

CURRENT RECONSTRUCTION MAY PLACE FIRST WARNING AT INTERFACE LOG TIME. SOURCE STATES EXPERIENCED ENVIRONMENTAL ONSET PRECEDED AUTOMATED/PERSONAL-INTERFACE ADAPTATION LOGGING. EXACT LEAD INTERVAL UNKNOWN. SOURCE DOES NOT CLAIM CAUSE OR EQUIVALENCE WITH LATER INCIDENT.

She read it twice.

There was a button to attach supporting media.

She left it empty.

There was a field marked publication provenance.

She selected:

NOT APPLICABLE TO REVIEW USE.

There was a checkbox:

ALLOW CONTRIBUTOR TO RECEIVE DERIVED REVIEW FINDINGS.

Aoife left it unchecked.

Another:

ALLOW CONTRIBUTOR TO USE SUBMISSION IN FUTURE PUBLICATION.

Unchecked.

She submitted.

The system returned a receipt number and a warning.

SUBMISSION DOES NOT CONFER INCIDENT-REVIEW ACCESS.

Good.

Aoife saved the receipt only to the accountability folder.

Then she opened her media workspace.

The old documentary project sat at the top because she had accessed it that morning.

She could have added an internal note.

New timing correction submitted to Ring Six review.

She could have preserved the fact for a future version.

She did not.

Instead she opened the source-permissions panel.

PUBLICATION ACCESS: WITHDRAWN.
FUTURE CONTACT: SOURCE INITIATED ONLY.
DERIVATIVE RESPONSE RIGHTS: NONE.

She added nothing.

Maya’s message was still waiting.

AOIFE: alive

MAYA: suspiciously brief

AOIFE: busy

MAYA: worse

Aoife almost typed I met S1.

She stopped.

AOIFE: later

MAYA: okay

Aoife closed the thread.

A new incident-portal notice arrived.

CORRECTION ACCEPTED FOR TIMELINE REVIEW.
SOURCE MATERIAL REMAINS RESTRICTED.
NO ACCESS CHANGE TO PERSONNEL UNDER REVIEW.

Aoife read the last line once.

Ollie would not see what she had seen.

He would see whatever the review team later decided he was allowed to see.

For the first time that did not feel like information being wasted.

Another interview request appeared.

Aoife declined it without opening the details.

Then she powered down the media workspace and went to class.
