# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 25 — SPORT ISN'T DATA

Samir wanted forty-two minutes of Crossband and got a consent problem before the first horn.

Kesh was tightening the grip limiter on a ball rack when Samir said, “I need the transition logs from last week.”

Kesh did not look up.

“No.”

Samir waited.

Kesh tightened the second limiter.

“That was quick.”

“You asked badly.”

“I asked directly.”

“Same thing.”

Around them, the recreation volume was waking into practice. The outer band carried most of the noise: shoes on the heavier surface, equipment lockers, two players arguing about a booking swap. Higher in the curve, Nera was checking the shoulder marker on the synthetic body they used for play.

The central display read:

CROSSBAND PRACTICE — 42:00

Samir looked at the clock.

“Can we have this argument after practice?”

“You started it before practice.”

“I need the logs for the Ring Six review.”

That got Kesh’s attention.

Not agreement.

Attention.

“What logs?”

“Movement and adaptation timing. Crossings between bands. Local support corrections if they are in the training export.”

Kesh stared at him.

“Raw?”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“You said that already.”

“It remains correct.”

Samir lowered his voice.

“They are already recorded.”

Kesh put the limiter tool down.

“So are missed grips, recovery delays, injury holds, body-class settings and half the reasons someone loses a position.”

“I do not need names.”

“You asked for raw logs.”

“I can remove names.”

“After you see them.”

Samir stopped.

Kesh pointed toward the inner band.

“Nera knows exactly how long their forearm limiter takes to settle after a hard inner launch. I know which player on this team is still protecting a bad shoulder. Two people here are trying for a higher booking tier next month. You do not get all of that because your friend has a question.”

“It is not Ollie’s private question.”

“I did not say it was.”

Samir hated how often people in orbit used that sentence on him.

Nera dropped from the middle band and caught the rail beside them.

“What are we refusing?”

“Samir wants everybody’s raw training logs,” Kesh said.

Nera looked at Samir.

“No.”

“You too?”

“My bad decisions are proprietary.”

“They are visible during practice.”

“Not with timestamps.”

The horn sounded once.

BOOKING ACTIVE.

Kesh picked up the ball.

“Practice.”

Samir stayed where he was.

Kesh threw the ball at his chest.

Samir caught it automatically.

“Practice,” Kesh repeated.

Samir went.

* * *

He played badly for nine minutes because he was still designing the argument.

The first mistake cost a gate.

The second cost him a shoulder impact with the recovery mesh.

The third made Nera say, “Your brain is somewhere else.”

“My brain is here.”

“Then this is worse.”

Samir took the return pass and cut toward the middle band.

Crossband punished people who treated gravity as background.

The outer band gave him enough weight to brake hard.

The middle band took that away in degrees.

The inner lane turned every bad prediction into distance.

He watched Kesh move inward and sent the ball before Kesh looked open.

Kesh caught it on the transition.

Gate one.

Nera took the next handoff, hit the inner rail, redirected and missed gate two by centimeters.

The horn marked loss of possession.

Nera swore.

Samir saw the shoulder marker flash a short adaptation pulse.

He saw it because he was looking for it.

That irritated him.

On the next run, nothing flashed.

Then again nothing.

A player named Jori crossed from outer to middle and rubbed one ear afterward.

No marker.

Kesh crossed the same line two seconds later and did not react at all.

Samir nearly missed another pass.

“Stop researching us with your face,” Nera said.

“I am playing.”

“You are counting.”

“I can do both.”

The ball hit him in the hip.

Nera pointed at it.

“Evidence.”

Samir picked up the ball.

“Again.”

They played.

By the end of the booking, his shirt was damp, his lungs hurt properly and he had stopped trying to turn every movement into a clue.

His team lost by one gate.

Samir blamed the first nine minutes.

Kesh blamed Samir.

This was the same thing.

The clock expired.

Players started returning straps and markers to the equipment wall.

Samir waited until Kesh had finished the booking closeout.

Then he tried again.

“Not raw logs.”

Kesh looked at him.

“Improvement.”

“I want to ask people to opt in.”

“To what?”

“Event order only.”

Kesh folded his arms.

Samir opened a blank export request rather than the team archive.

“Transition type. Relative timing of an adaptation or performance marker. No names. No injury fields. No training scores. No grip-force values. No tactical path. Include times when nothing happened.”

Nera came closer.

“Body class?”

Samir hesitated.

“If the person agrees and the category is broad enough not to identify them.”

“Why do you need it?”

“To check whether the early markers are just one body type.”

Nera’s head tilted.

“Bad wording.”

Samir heard it after they said it.

“Right. Whether response timing differs by body/adaptation class.”

“Better.”

Kesh said, “What happens to the export?”

“Bounded comparison. No raw logs.”

“Who sees it?”

“Me first, if people consent. Then only aggregate result through the review channel.”

“Your friend?”

“Whatever the review is allowed to give him.”

Kesh looked at Nera.

Nera said, “I would opt in.”

A player packing a strap nearby said, “I would not.”

Nobody had asked them yet.

Samir turned.

“Okay.”

The player frowned as if expecting the argument.

Samir waited.

They said, “That is all?”

“What do you want me to say?”

“I don’t know. People usually ask why.”

“You can tell me why if you want.”

“I don’t.”

“Okay.”

They left with their bag.

Kesh watched them go.

Then looked at Samir.

“You can ask. Team channel. Individual opt-in. No one sees who refused except the permission system.”

Samir nodded.

“And,” Kesh said, “you do not make it a team decision. No vote. No ‘most of us agreed.’ Each person decides.”

“I know.”

“You asked for raw logs five minutes ago.”

“I know now.”

Nera said, “Growth. Disturbing.”

Samir ignored them.

Kesh authorized the consent request template.

The system generated twelve invitations.

Samir stared at SEND.

Crossband suddenly felt less like a dataset than it had fifteen minutes earlier.

He sent them.

* * *

Seven people opted in.

Three refused.

Two did nothing.

Samir discovered that silence was also a data-access state.

He had expected that academically.

He disliked it operationally.

The system built the export without showing him whose entries were absent.

OPT-IN COMPARISON SET: 7 ATHLETES
NEGATIVE EVENTS INCLUDED: YES
IDENTITY: REMOVED
BODY / ADAPTATION CLASS: COARSE GROUPING ONLY
MEDICAL / INJURY / PERFORMANCE DETAIL: EXCLUDED

Kesh let them use the equipment review bench after the next booking group entered the Crossband volume.

The sport continued behind the glass while Samir and Nera looked at the export.

Samir expected a pattern in under five minutes.

This was his first mistake after practice.

Event one:

OUTER → MIDDLE TRANSITION.

Athlete marker change: +1.8 seconds after transition.

Fixed environment correction: +0.7 seconds after transition.

The building moved first.

Event two:

MIDDLE → INNER.

Athlete marker: none.

Fixed environment correction: none meaningful.

Event three:

INNER → MIDDLE after high-load play.

Athlete adaptation marker: 2.1 seconds before fixed local correction.

Samir leaned closer.

Nera said, “Do not fall in love.”

“I am not.”

“You made the face.”

“Everyone has invented a face for me.”

“That one is real.”

Event four:

Same transition class.

No early marker.

Event five:

Early marker.

No fixed correction followed.

Samir sat back.

“Useless.”

Nera said, “Rude.”

“The set.”

“I know.”

They kept going.

The coarse body categories did not save him.

Two biological-local entries responded differently under the same transition class.

One recent-arrival biological entry showed a long correction after a sequence that produced nothing in another recent arrival.

Nera’s synthetic-body entries were clean in some high-load transitions and noisy in others.

One athlete with no support marker at all produced the earliest performance change in the set: a missed grip before the local environment trace moved.

Samir looked at it.

“That could just be a missed grip.”

“Yes,” Nera said.

“It probably is.”

“Yes.”

“Stop agreeing.”

“No.”

Samir sorted by body class.

Nothing clean.

Sorted by transition direction.

Weak.

Sorted by gravity delta.

Worse.

Nera said, “Try load.”

Samir looked at them.

“What?”

“Your third event. High-load play before the transition.”

“That field is excluded.”

“Exact performance is excluded. The export still has coarse state because safety timing needs it.”

Samir opened the allowed field.

PRE-EVENT LOAD STATE:
REST / WARM / HIGH / RECOVERY.

He sorted.

The early markers moved.

Not into a perfect line.

Into something less random.

Most of the early-marker cases sat under HIGH or RECOVERY.

Most REST cases did not.

But there were exceptions immediately.

Nera pointed at one.

“Warm.”

“I see it.”

“And no building correction after.”

“I see that too.”

“Good.”

Samir opened the training context.

Coarse hydration state was unavailable.

Stress unavailable.

Recent exertion available only as load band.

Warm-up phase available.

He added warm-up.

The set changed again.

Nera said, “You are making more categories than people.”

“I have seven people.”

“Exactly.”

Samir stopped.

Seven opt-in athletes.

A handful of usable transition events.

Too many variables.

He could make the pattern look impressive by choosing the right subset.

That realization made him angry.

Not because the data were bad.

Because the temptation was obvious.

He closed the filters.

Nera looked at him.

“What?”

“If I keep slicing this, I can get anything.”

“Yes.”

“You could have said that earlier.”

“I wanted to see when you noticed.”

“That is terrible science.”

“It is excellent entertainment.”

Kesh returned from the corridor carrying a drink.

“Well?”

Samir said, “Not one body.”

Kesh waited.

“Not one response,” Samir corrected. “Body class does not explain it. Transition direction does not explain it. Load state changes the pattern, but the set is too small and there are too many exceptions.”

Kesh drank.

“So sport remains difficult.”

“That was already known.”

“What did you learn that we did not?”

Samir looked through the glass.

A player entered the middle band after a hard outer sprint and misjudged a grip.

Another crossed behind them cleanly.

Same space.

Different body state.

He said, “Maybe the question is not which body notices first.”

Nera said, “Good.”

Kesh looked at them both.

“What is the question?”

Samir opened a blank summary.

“Whether transition order interacts with current adaptation or load state. Not enough to predict anything. Enough to test.”

Kesh said, “That sounds less exciting.”

“It is.”

“Probably better.”

Samir typed.

* * *

The review form rejected his first summary because he had used the phrase BODY RESPONSE DATA.

The recreation privacy layer flagged it.

CATEGORY TOO BROAD / MAY IMPLY MEDICAL OR PHYSIOLOGICAL CONTENT NOT INCLUDED IN EXPORT.

Samir stared.

Nera leaned over.

“The form is smarter than you.”

“Temporary condition.”

He rewrote it.

OPT-IN SPORT EVENT-ORDER COMPARISON.

Then:

Seven consenting athletes; de-identified event-order export; negative cases included. No identity, injury, medical, tactical or raw performance values. No universal early marker observed. Coarse body/adaptation class did not explain timing pattern. Early adaptation/performance markers occurred in a subset, including non-escalating cases. Preliminary association may depend on transition sequence plus current load/adaptation state. Sample too small for causal claim.

He added:

Do not infer that athletes or body systems are reliable infrastructure sensors.

Nera read it.

“Harsh.”

“Necessary.”

Kesh read it next.

“Where does it go?”

“Authorized incident comparison channel.”

“Do you keep the export?”

“Until the review intake confirms receipt, then local copy expires under the consent terms.”

Kesh looked at the consent screen.

“Good.”

Samir submitted.

The system returned:

RECEIVED — AGGREGATE COMPARISON ONLY.
RAW ATHLETE RECORDS NOT TRANSFERRED.

No congratulations appeared.

Samir preferred that.

Mostly.

Behind the glass, the next Crossband booking ended.

The horn sounded.

Nera stood.

“Extra run?”

Samir looked at the clock.

They had twelve minutes before the equipment wall reset for the next group.

Kesh said, “You complained the review was eating practice.”

“It did.”

“So?”

Samir took the ball from the rack.

“So it owes me twelve minutes.”

“That is not how booking works.”

Nera was already climbing toward the middle band.

Samir followed.

They used no recording mode beyond the normal safety minimum.

On the first pass, Samir moved too early.

The ball went behind him.

Nera shouted, “Researcher!”

Samir caught the rail.

“Again.”

The second pass came while he was still recovering.

He watched Nera’s shoulders instead of the ball.

Waited half a count.

Caught it clean.

For the next eleven minutes, nobody was data.
