ORBITAL YOUTH
Chapter 23 — NO ACCESS

By midday, Ollie had calibrated three door sensors, replaced one filter seal and discovered that a mature residential ring could contain four different screws designed to look exactly the same until they failed to fit.

He held the fourth screw beside the third.

“They are identical.”

Iri did not look up from the panel inventory.

“They are not.”

“They are the same diameter.”

“Yes.”

“Same head.”

“Yes.”

“Same length.”

“No.”

Ollie aligned them against the edge of the service tray.

One was shorter by less than a millimeter.

He stared at it.

Iri said, “Historic day.”

“I hate this ring.”

“You said that about Ring Six.”

“Ring Six had better screws.”

“That is growth.”

Ollie put the correct screw into the panel.

The corridor stayed occupied around them.

A resident stepped over the open tool case without slowing. A child came past dragging a school bag that kept striking the wall every third step. Somewhere behind the service partition, someone was arguing about laundry allocation.

Ring Six was shut.

This ring was not.

That difference kept appearing in small ways.

No incident command strip.
No review observers.
No sealed evidence zone.

Just repairs that had to be finished before people came home, left for shifts or tried to open doors that currently hated them.

Ollie tightened the panel and checked the work ticket.

TRACK SENSOR E-17 — COMPLETE.

Below it sat the note from yesterday’s older environmental interface.

RELATED OBSERVATION — INCIDENT REVIEW ACKNOWLEDGED.

He had checked that line nine times.

The wording had not changed.

Acknowledged.

Not accepted.
Not important.
Not assigned.

Acknowledged.

His Ring Six credential still said:

REVIEW HOLD.

Iri closed the inventory panel.

“You are doing it again.”

“What?”

“Looking at a word until it becomes a different word.”

“It could change status.”

“It has not.”

“It might.”

Iri picked up the tool case.

“Then it will be very exciting when it does.”

Ollie followed.

At the next service junction, a duty planner intercepted them.

“E-seventeen done?”

“Yes,” Iri said.

“Good. Take C-twelve after break. We lost one manual pair to injury restriction and two people are still reassigned to Ring Six review support.”

Ollie looked at the staffing board.

One line read:

TOMAS — MANUAL SERVICE RESTRICTION / ARM INJURY.

TEMPORARY ASSIGNMENT — LIGHT DUTY / REASSIGNED ROUTE.

The manual slot Tomas would normally have filled had been covered by someone from another ring whose shift now ended forty minutes later.

The city had already turned injury into scheduling.

Ollie disliked how normal that looked.

The planner handed Iri another ticket.

Iri glanced at it.

“Filter housing.”

Ollie said, “Again?”

The planner looked at him.

“Do you want it to stop filtering?”

“No.”

“Then again.”

They went to break.

* * *

Mara was eating when Ollie found her.

That was unusual enough that he almost left.

She saw him anyway.

“No.”

“I have not asked anything.”

“You came here with a file open.”

Ollie looked at his band.

The file was open.

“That is circumstantial.”

“Sit if this will take less than five minutes.”

He sat.

Mara kept eating.

Ollie opened the old-module observation.

“I want access to the complaint archive.”

“No.”

He had expected that.

“All mature occupied modules. Environmental comfort and interface events, last five years.”

“No.”

“Three years.”

Mara looked at him.

Ollie stopped.

“That was not the real request,” she said.

“It was a request.”

“It was you reducing a bad request until I become tired.”

“I have a different one.”

“Good.”

He closed the archive request entirely.

That felt worse than it should have.

Then he opened a blank scope form.

Mara paused eating.

Ollie said, “I do not need complaint text.”

“Continue.”

“I do not need names, household, accommodation class, medical data or raw personal-interface values.”

“Continue.”

“I need event order.”

Mara put the food container down.

Ollie showed her the fields.

“Small sample. Mature occupied modules only. De-identified. Service-door state changes. Return or environmental correction start. Coarse personal/interface event timestamp if one exists. Fixed-system update timestamp.”

“Ring Six?”

“No raw Ring Six.”

“Incident source data?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because I am not allowed to have it.”

Mara waited.

Ollie added, “And because I am trying to test the older-module pattern without building the answer out of the incident first.”

Mara looked at the scope form.

“What is the question?”

Ollie had written three versions.

The first said:

DO HUMAN/INTERFACE REPORTS PREDICT FIXED ENVIRONMENTAL RESPONSE?

He deleted it.

Too broad.

The second said:

DO PERSONAL ADAPTATION EVENTS LEAD FIXED RESPONSE DURING SHARED-LOOP CORRECTIONS?

He deleted that too.

Too causal.

Mara said nothing.

Ollie typed the third.

IN MATURE OCCUPIED MODULES, HOW OFTEN DOES A RESIDENT/PERSONAL-INTERFACE EVENT PRECEDE A FIXED ENVIRONMENTAL UPDATE WITHIN A SHORT WINDOW, COMPARED WITH GENERIC SERVICE/RETURN EVENTS?

Mara read it.

“Better.”

“What is wrong with it?”

“You are assuming the event classes are comparable.”

“They may not be.”

“Then say that in the scope.”

Ollie added:

EVENT CLASS HETEROGENEITY EXPECTED. COMPARISON IS ORDER/TIMING ONLY; NO CLAIM OF COMMON PHYSIOLOGY OR CAUSE.

Mara picked up her food again.

“Sample size?”

“Twenty modules.”

“No.”

“Twelve.”

“No bargaining.”

Ollie stopped.

Mara said, “What can you justify with the staff time available?”

He checked the maintenance-analysis allocation.

Not much.

“Six modules. Two generations. Thirty days each.”

“Why thirty?”

“Enough to catch ordinary service variation without turning this into a historical archive project.”

“Fields?”

Ollie read them back.

“Door/service state, return-correction start, coarse resident/interface event timestamp, fixed environmental update. No text content. No identity. No personal values.”

“Negative cases?”

“Yes.”

“What counts as negative?”

Ollie hesitated.

Mara waited.

“Resident/interface event with no fixed update inside the comparison window. Fixed update with no preceding resident/interface event. Service correction with neither.”

Mara nodded once.

“Comparison window?”

“Start with fifteen seconds. Report distribution, not one cutoff.”

“Why fifteen?”

“Long enough to include the old complaints’ qualitative ‘before wall update’ timing without pretending we know the exact lead interval.”

Mara looked at him.

“You said old complaints plural.”

“Yes.”

“How many?”

“Four in the maintenance ticket I was authorized to read.”

“Good.”

The word arrived with no warmth in it.

Ollie still wanted more from it.

He hated that.

Mara signed the request as:

ROUTE FOR PRIVACY / INCIDENT REVIEW APPROVAL — DE-IDENTIFIED COMPARISON ONLY.

Ollie looked at the line.

“That is not approval.”

“No.”

“How long?”

“I do not know.”

He looked at the clock.

Mara picked up her food.

“You have filter housing C-twelve.”

“I know.”

“Then know it there.”

Ollie stood.

At the door Mara said, “If they approve it, your access state does not change.”

“I know.”

“Say the useful version.”

Ollie looked back.

“The dataset is not Ring Six access.”

“Correct.”

He left.

* * *

Filter housing C-twelve contained a dead insect.

Ollie stared at it.

Iri looked over his shoulder.

“What?”

“There are insects here?”

“There are people here.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“People bring things.”

“Why is it in the filter?”

“Because it made a poor life choice.”

Ollie removed it with a service strip.

The filter itself was fine.

The seal was not.

He replaced the seal, reseated the housing and ran the pressure check.

Normal.

The work took eleven minutes.

He checked the scope request.

PENDING.

Iri saw the screen.

“You asked.”

“Yes.”

“How much?”

“Six modules.”

Iri stopped packing the old seal.

“That is less than I expected.”

“I am being restrained.”

“Are you ill?”

“Possibly.”

“What data?”

Ollie told them.

Iri listened without interrupting until he reached coarse personal/interface event timestamps.

“No values?”

“No.”

“No type?”

“Coarse event class only.”

“No complaint words?”

“No.”

Iri nodded.

“What are you comparing?”

“Order.”

“Only order?”

“And service/return state.”

Iri considered that.

“Better.”

Ollie looked at them.

“You have become very efficient at saying one word and making me want a report.”

“Practice.”

His band vibrated.

He opened it too quickly.

REQUEST APPROVED — BOUNDED DE-IDENTIFIED EXTRACT.

LIMITS:
6 MODULES.
2 CONTROL GENERATIONS.
30-DAY WINDOW.
EVENT ORDER / TIMESTAMPS ONLY.
NO CONTENT TEXT.
NO IDENTITY / HOUSEHOLD / MEDICAL / ACCOMMODATION DATA.
NO RAW PERSONAL-INTERFACE VALUES.
NO RING SIX INCIDENT PARTITION.
OUTPUT REVIEWABLE BEFORE RETENTION.

Ollie read the last restriction.

“They can take it back.”

Iri read over his shoulder.

“They can review whether you keep the derived file.”

“That is what I said.”

“You said it emotionally.”

“I did not.”

“You have a retention face.”

Ollie closed the message.

“Everyone needs new jokes.”

They finished C-twelve before Ollie opened the approval again.

* * *

The extract arrived after shift.

Not immediately.

Ollie had expected immediately and spent twenty-eight minutes pretending he had not.

When it came, the dataset was smaller than the empty table he had prepared for it.

Six modules.
Thirty days.
Two control generations.

No names.
No complaint text.
No physiological values.

Each row contained only event class, local service state and timestamp.

Iri sat across from him in a study/service pocket because their rehearsal started later and because neither of them called this working together.

Ollie sorted by resident/interface events first.

Iri said, “You are already biasing it.”

“I am locating the events.”

“You are looking at the thing you want.”

Ollie stopped.

Then he duplicated the table.

One view sorted by all fixed environmental updates.

One by service/return corrections.

One by resident/interface events.

“Better?”

“Yes.”

He disliked how often Iri was right lately.

The first module damaged the hypothesis immediately.

Eleven resident/interface events.

Nine had no fixed environmental update within fifteen seconds.

One had a fixed update first.

One preceded a small return correction by three seconds.

Ollie stared at it.

Iri said, “Good.”

“That is mostly negative.”

“Yes.”

“You are pleased.”

“I am pleased the data is not trying to impress you.”

Module two was worse.

Four fixed environmental updates clustered around service-door activity.

No resident/interface events at all.

Module three contained sixteen personal/interface events, most associated with ordinary household transitions and none followed by a fixed update in the short window.

Ollie leaned back.

“The old complaints may be nothing.”

Iri said, “Maybe.”

“You say that when you are happy.”

“Very.”

Module four changed the shape.

It was the older of the two control generations.

Ollie saw three sequences where a personal/interface event came first by between two and six seconds, then a local return correction changed fixed state.

All three occurred during service-door or shared-return activity.

He highlighted them.

Then unhighlighted them.

Iri noticed.

“What?”

“I want the negative cases visible.”

He added the other rows back into the comparison.

Module five had one similar sequence and seven non-matches.

Module six had two fixed-first cases, five event-only cases and two lead-timing cases under return corrections.

Ollie built the first crude table.

PERSON/INTERFACE FIRST + FIXED UPDATE WITHIN 15s: 7
FIXED FIRST + PERSON/INTERFACE EVENT WITHIN 15s: 3
PERSON/INTERFACE EVENT / NO FIXED UPDATE: 37
FIXED UPDATE / NO PERSON EVENT: 19

Iri read it.

“That does not look dramatic.”

“It is not.”

Ollie filtered by generic occupancy movement.

The lead cases did not cluster there.

He filtered by service-door state alone.

Still weak.

Then by return correction active plus service-door transition.

Five of the seven lead cases remained.

He sat forward.

Iri said nothing.

Ollie widened the window to thirty seconds.

More noise entered immediately.

He narrowed it to ten.

Four of the five remained.

“Do not optimize the window around the answer,” Iri said.

“I know.”

“You are doing the face.”

Ollie saved all three windows.

Ten.
Fifteen.
Thirty.

Then he added a baseline comparison for fixed updates after the same shared-loop service state without any resident/interface event.

The effect shrank.

Not disappeared.

Shrank.

Ollie stared at the output.

“That is worse.”

Iri leaned closer.

“For your theory.”

“Yes.”

“Better for the work.”

He looked at them.

“Please stop developing Mara’s personality.”

“No.”

Ollie recalculated without module four.

The lead cluster nearly vanished.

He put module four back.

It returned.

“Generation effect?” Iri asked.

“Maybe.”

“Module-specific?”

“Maybe.”

“Service configuration?”

“Maybe.”

Iri smiled for the first time that evening.

Ollie pointed at them.

“Do not enjoy this.”

“I am not.”

“You are.”

“Yes.”

He built a summary that did not use the word predictor.

PRELIMINARY OBSERVATION:
A SMALL SUBSET OF DE-IDENTIFIED RESIDENT/PERSONAL-INTERFACE EVENTS PRECEDE FIXED ENVIRONMENTAL UPDATES WITHIN SHORT WINDOWS. THESE CASES ARE DISPROPORTIONATELY ASSOCIATED WITH COMBINED RETURN-CORRECTION + SERVICE-TRANSITION STATES IN PART OF THE SAMPLE. MOST RESIDENT/PERSONAL-INTERFACE EVENTS DO NOT SHOW THIS RELATIONSHIP. CONTROL-GENERATION / MODULE EFFECTS REMAIN LARGE. NO CLAIM OF CAUSE.

He read it once.

Then added:

NEGATIVE CASES DOMINATE ABSOLUTE COUNT.

Iri nodded.

“Keep that.”

“I was going to.”

“I know.”

Ollie submitted the derived summary for retention review.

The system did not congratulate him.

It returned:

DERIVED FILE — REVIEW PENDING.

His Ring Six credential remained:

REVIEW HOLD.

He checked it anyway.

Iri saw.

“Still?”

“Yes.”

“Terrible injustice.”

Ollie closed the credential display.

“No. Correct.”

Iri looked at him.

Ollie opened a blank model page.

Not the old route categories.

He wrote three headings.

EVENT ORDER.
SHARED LOOP STATE.
CONTROL GENERATION.

Then stopped.

The dataset did not justify more yet.

He saved the page empty under the headings.

SECOND MODEL — QUESTIONS ONLY.

Iri picked up their rehearsal bag.

“You are done?”

“For today.”

“That sounded painful.”

“It was.”

They left the study pocket together.

Ollie checked the credential once more before closing it.

REVIEW HOLD.

He closed it.

The blank model page stayed open beside it:

EVENT ORDER.
SHARED LOOP STATE.
CONTROL GENERATION.
