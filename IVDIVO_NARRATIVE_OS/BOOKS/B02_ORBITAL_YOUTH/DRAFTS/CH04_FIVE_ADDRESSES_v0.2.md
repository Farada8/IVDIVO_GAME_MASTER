# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 4 — FIVE ADDRESSES

Ethan’s host room had a better desk than his room at home and nowhere sensible to put a backpack.

The desk folded out of the wall under a narrow shelf. The chair locked itself against the floor when he sat down. The lighting changed temperature when his tablet opened a study screen. A shallow storage recess accepted clothes, shoes, toiletries, and approximately nothing else.

His backpack remained beside the door.

Ethan had moved it three times.

Each location interfered with something.

The host family had told him not to worry about it.

This was not useful.

He sat at the desk and opened the group schedule.

Dinner at nineteen hundred should have worked.

It was not a complicated proposal.

The five of them were in the same city.

Technically.

He entered 19:00 into the shared planner.

The system considered this.

Then it produced five different warnings.

OLLIE — MAINTENANCE HANDOVER / OVERLAP

SAMIR — SPORT ORIENTATION / TRANSFER CONFLICT

MAYA — HOST-ZONE COMMITMENT / RETURN WINDOW

AOIFE — MEDIA ACCESS / EDIT BLOCK

ETHAN — YOUTH COHORT STUDY BLOCK / FIXED

Ethan stared at his own line.

The study block had appeared after arrival.

Of course it had.

The Youth Cohort residency had a recurring local learning period that apparently existed to remind them that being allowed to work in orbit did not cancel school.

He opened the block.

ATTENDANCE REQUIRED
REMOTE EARTH COURSEWORK WINDOW FOLLOWS
RESCHEDULING: NOT AVAILABLE

He tried dragging dinner to 19:30.

The planner improved exactly nothing.

20:00.

Samir cleared.

Maya worsened.

20:30.

Aoife cleared, then Ollie failed entirely.

Ethan opened Ollie’s schedule.

FIRST MAINTENANCE HANDOVER
OBSERVATION + TOOL CUSTODY
ATTENDANCE REQUIRED FOR PLACEMENT

He stopped.

There were situations in which an hour could be moved.

This was not one of them.

The group chat lit.

MAYA: Why does dinner require a systems model

SAMIR: because Ethan

ETHAN: I am trying to find one time.

AOIFE: dangerous beginning

ETHAN: We have not eaten together since Earth.

MAYA: We ate on the transfer.

ETHAN: That was nutrient paste and one of Samir’s crackers.

SAMIR: You ate three.

ETHAN: The number is not the issue.

SAMIR: It was my issue.

Aoife sent a picture.

It took Ethan a second to understand what he was looking at.

A bowl.

Five spoons.

None matched.

Underneath:

AOIFE: proof of concept

Ethan smiled before he could decide not to.

ETHAN: That proves a host owns bad cutlery.

AOIFE: It proves five objects can occupy one system without central coordination.

ETHAN: They are spoons.

AOIFE: Start small.

He looked at the photo again.

The joke was stupid.

It also belonged to them in a way the entire city did not yet.

He sent:

ETHAN: 19:30 central food zone?

OLLIE: can’t

No explanation.

Ethan knew the explanation.

That made it worse, because a part of him immediately began calculating whether Ollie could leave the handover ten minutes early and still make the next ringline.

He could.

Probably.

Ethan opened the maintenance route.

Then closed it.

He typed:

ETHAN: after?

OLLIE: not sure how long first shift runs

Ethan’s fingers hovered.

It is the first night.

He could write that.

Ollie would understand what he meant.

That was exactly why Ethan did not write it.

AOIFE: I can do 20:20 for thirty minutes

MAYA: I can do 20:20 if someone wants me to spend my whole evening in transit

SAMIR: practice ends 20:10

MAYA: You live eight minutes from the food zone

SAMIR: correct

MAYA: I hate you

SAMIR: not relevant to transit

Ethan pulled up the city map.

Five host addresses appeared as separate markers.

The map made them look close.

That was the first lie.

He switched from geometry to travel time.

The city changed shape.

His own host zone sat twenty-two minutes from the proposed food area.

Aoife: thirty-one.

Samir: eleven.

Maya: thirty-seven at current transfer load.

Ollie: eighteen from work, forty-two from home.

Five people.

Five addresses.

One dinner.

No time.

His host, a woman named Celia, passed the open doorway carrying a bag of folded laundry.

“You planning an evacuation?”

“No.”

She glanced at his screens.

“Worse?”

“Dinner.”

“Ah.”

Ethan looked at her.

She kept walking.

“That was not helpful.”

“It was accurate.”

He heard her laugh from the next room.

The planner refreshed.

One of the transfer windows shifted by six minutes.

Maya’s route turned red.

DINNER AT 20:20 now produced:

MAYA — ARRIVAL 20:44

Ethan leaned back.

The chair remained politely attached to the floor.

He hated how much he respected that.

* * *

The Youth Cohort study block ended at sixteen hundred in a room that could have been in a school, office, library, or particularly ambitious community center.

That was probably intentional.

There were no floating walls.

No alien interface.

No adaptive test.

Eight young people from different exchange/residency tracks sat around two ordinary tables while a human instructor talked them through local hazard reporting, coursework credit, and the fact that “I was at work” was not an automatic extension for an Earth assignment.

Maya had already tested that last point.

“What if the work is the assignment?”

“It is not.”

“What if it should be?”

“Then submit a curriculum change request.”

Maya considered this.

The instructor pointed at her.

“No.”

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You were about to.”

Ethan looked across the table at Aoife.

She had her lips pressed together.

He tapped one finger against his tablet.

Aoife looked down at it.

Then at him.

The corner of her mouth moved.

It was an old joke now.

Apparently everyone could tell when Maya was about to turn a rule into a project.

After the block, Aoife waited while Ethan packed his tablet.

“You survived education.”

“Barely.”

“You asked three questions.”

“They were relevant.”

“One was about whether orbital placement hours could satisfy Earth civics credit.”

“That was relevant to all of us.”

“You made eye contact with the instructor until she checked.”

“She had the answer.”

“She had fear.”

Ethan put the tablet away.

Aoife handed him a wrapped piece of flatbread.

“What’s this?”

“You didn’t eat during the break.”

“I was reading the housing allocation notes.”

“I know.”

“You brought food into the study block?”

“My host brought food into my life. I redistributed it.”

Ethan took the flatbread.

“You realize that sounds like theft.”

“It is very difficult to steal a sandwich someone put in your hand.”

“That depends on jurisdiction.”

Aoife stopped walking.

“Please don’t make the sandwich orbital law.”

“I wasn’t.”

“You were emotionally close.”

They stepped into the transfer corridor.

The floor curved almost imperceptibly.

Ethan still noticed every time.

Aoife did not look down anymore.

That annoyed him slightly, which made no sense.

“Your place is this way?” he asked.

“For three transfers.”

“You said you had a direct route.”

“I had a direct route when I arrived.”

“What happened?”

“Maintenance closure.”

“Already?”

“Yes, Ethan. The city began maintenance without checking whether it fit our week.”

“Hostile.”

“Targeted.”

They reached a junction.

Aoife’s route indicator pointed left.

Ethan’s pointed right.

They both stopped.

“You’re twenty-nine minutes from me,” Ethan said.

“Thirty-four now.”

“That is worse.”

“Thank you.”

“No, I mean—”

“I know what you mean.”

She adjusted the strap on her recorder bag.

Ethan looked at the group map again.

“You have the longest route to the study block.”

“Maya does tomorrow.”

“Because of the host zone change?”

“Because the route is phased differently in the morning.”

Ethan opened the housing layer.

Aoife watched him do it.

“You are doing something.”

“I’m looking.”

“At what?”

“Why they assigned everyone like this.”

“Because they hate dinner.”

“Obviously.”

She laughed.

It was quick and unguarded.

Ethan liked it too much.

He looked at the map instead.

Aoife’s host zone sat in a mature residential ring with a high-demand view corridor and terrible connection to Ollie’s work area.

Samir was near two sport volumes and the study block, but his host housing was smaller and shared more common space.

Maya had a larger private room in a mixed-environment sector with good late-event access and a miserable daytime commute to central training.

Ollie’s host assignment was close to Ring Six service transfers and nowhere near anyone else.

Ethan’s own room had the easiest access to study and the worst access to Vela Night’s main social routes.

He moved the housing criteria into a list.

Gravity band.

Environmental compatibility.

Host availability.

Work location.

Study access.

Transit load.

Household type.

Private space.

Aoife watched the list grow.

“You’re redesigning housing.”

“I’m trying to understand the assignments.”

“With columns?”

“No columns.”

“Yet.”

He closed the list.

Aoife looked disappointed.

“You have a better view than I do,” Ethan said.

“I do.”

“But your commute is worse.”

“Yes.”

“Samir has the best commute.”

“And the least privacy.”

“Maya has—”

“Ethan.”

“What?”

“None of us won.”

He looked at her.

She gestured at the map.

“You keep looking for which assignment is best.”

“I’m comparing tradeoffs.”

“You’re ranking tradeoffs.”

“That is what comparing means.”

“No. It is what you do when comparing gets bored.”

He almost argued.

Then a family came through the junction behind them with two children, three bags, and a compact environmental carrier humming softly between the adults.

Aoife stepped aside.

Ethan did too.

The family split at the next marker.

One adult and one child continued toward Aoife’s ring.

The other adult took the carrier and second child toward a different route.

Nobody appeared surprised.

The smaller child waved.

The adult waved back.

The doors closed between them.

Ethan looked at the housing map again.

Not best.

Compatible.

Available.

Near enough to some things.

Far from others.

He still wanted to improve it.

That was probably not illegal.

Aoife’s transfer arrived.

She stepped toward the door.

“Twenty-thirty?” Ethan asked.

“For dinner?”

“Yes.”

“You know Ollie still can’t make it.”

“I know.”

“And Maya arrives after twenty-forty.”

“I know.”

“So no.”

The door opened.

Aoife backed into it.

“Congratulations.”

“For what?”

“You have discovered that five people are more difficult than five spoons.”

The door started to close.

Ethan said, “That is not a fair comparison.”

“Write an appeal.”

The door shut.

He stood there with the flatbread in one hand.

His own route indicator blinked behind him.

He had missed the first car.

* * *

By nineteen hundred, dinner had become four separate meals and one unresolved suggestion.

Ethan ate with Celia and her partner at a narrow table that folded out from a wall beside the kitchen.

The food was good.

He barely noticed.

The group chat kept changing.

OLLIE: still at handover

MAYA: host dinner

SAMIR: practice ran over

AOIFE: editing

ETHAN: tomorrow?

No one answered for forty seconds.

Celia asked him something about the study block.

Ethan put his tablet face down.

“Sorry.”

“You don’t have to be.”

“I was reading.”

“I noticed.”

Her partner passed him a dish.

“You’ll stop trying to do two things at once after about a week.”

Celia said, “No, he won’t.”

“You met me yesterday,” Ethan said.

“Yes.”

“That feels statistically weak.”

“It is enough.”

His tablet buzzed.

He ignored it.

For eleven seconds.

Then he checked.

MAYA had sent a picture of her host’s dining area.

There were six people around the table, two of them nonhuman, one child under the table, and something in a bowl that Ethan could not identify.

MAYA: I have been told this is food

SAMIR: brave

MAYA: if I die tell people I understood the transit map eventually

OLLIE: no

MAYA: why are you here

OLLIE: break

A photo followed from Ollie.

Not food.

A set of labeled tool lockers.

Of course.

AOIFE: compelling social life

OLLIE: these labels are wrong

ETHAN: Are you allowed to fix them?

OLLIE: you have learned nothing

Ethan smiled.

Then:

OLLIE: handover may run late tomorrow too

Ethan looked at the planner.

Tomorrow had been his backup.

He deleted the proposed dinner block before anyone could reject it.

SAMIR: practice starts earlier tomorrow

MAYA: I have mobility orientation

AOIFE: source meeting

ETHAN: okay

The word sat there.

Too small.

He added:

ETHAN: Friday?

Three dots appeared.

Disappeared.

Appeared under Aoife.

Disappeared again.

Maya sent:

MAYA: maybe

Samir:

SAMIR: check after practice schedule

Ollie:

OLLIE: depends shift

Aoife did not answer in the group chat.

A private message appeared instead.

AOIFE: You can let one meal fail.

Ethan read it twice.

He typed:

ETHAN: I know.

Deleted it.

Typed:

ETHAN: It isn’t about the meal.

Deleted that too.

The problem with Aoife was that she had an unpleasant habit of being close enough to the real question that even disagreeing required more honesty than he wanted to provide.

He finally sent:

ETHAN: The spoons made this look easier.

Her reply came immediately.

AOIFE: rookie mistake

Then nothing.

He waited.

No second message.

The group chat moved on without either of them.

Maya posted a complaint about the temperature in one corridor.

Samir corrected the name of the corridor.

Maya told him nobody cared.

Ollie asked whether anyone knew if local fabrication access covered replacement charger housings.

Aoife appeared long enough to say no one should let Ollie spend his first orbital allowance on a charger.

Then she vanished again.

Her status changed to EDIT SESSION.

Samir’s changed to SPORT.

Ollie’s to WORK.

Maya’s remained visible for another minute, then went dark.

Ethan looked at the five names.

Yesterday, if he wanted to talk to them, he had turned his head.

He opened the map.

Five markers appeared in five different parts of Vela.

The route estimates started loading.

He closed it before they finished.

Celia’s partner asked if he wanted more rice.

Ethan looked up.

“Yes, please.”

The tablet buzzed again.

He left it face down.

For the rest of dinner, that was enough.
