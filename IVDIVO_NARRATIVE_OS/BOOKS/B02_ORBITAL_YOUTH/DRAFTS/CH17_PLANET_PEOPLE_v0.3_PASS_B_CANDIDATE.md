# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 17 — PLANET PEOPLE

Maya decided Aoife needed defending before Aoife asked for it.

This was already a bad sign.

They were in a food volume near one of the older residential rings, at a table designed for six and occupied by nine because Davi had decided chairs were a conservative idea.

Two people sat on the floor. One leaned against a service cabinet. Lea had taken the end nearest the route display because her household had three different return possibilities and she checked them often enough to pretend she did not.

Aoife’s inference thread was open on somebody’s screen.

Not Aoife’s original clip. That was gone.

The repost had survived.

Circles. Route map. Placement-band guesses. Comments from Earth accounts that had never met the person in the clip and now possessed several confident theories about them.

A local boy Maya had met once at Vela Night looked at the thread.

“This is why I don’t talk to visitors with cameras.”

Maya put down her drink.

“She asked permission.”

He glanced at her.

“The person still got narrowed down.”

“Not identified.”

Davi, from the floor, said, “Placement band narrowed.”

Maya turned on him.

“Whose side are you on?”

“I was reading.”

“Traitor.”

“Devastating.”

Maya pointed at the repost.

“Aoife removed what they asked her to remove. She didn’t secretly record somebody’s support system and dump it online.”

The local boy closed the thread.

“Nobody said she did.”

“You’re acting like intent means nothing.”

Lea said, “It means something.”

Maya looked at her, ready for support.

Lea kept checking the route board.

“It just doesn’t make the repost disappear.”

Maya felt the opening and took it anyway.

“So what exactly was Aoife supposed to do? Predict every person who might combine public details later?”

The local boy shrugged.

“I don’t know. Maybe visitors stop assuming local life is theirs to explain.”

“That is not fair.”

“So is leaving in three weeks.”

The table changed.

Not loudly. Enough.

Maya leaned back.

“We didn’t make the Earth account.”

“No.”

“We didn’t tell them to turn one support marker into a political claim.”

“No.”

Maya opened her mouth for the third item.

Lea stood.

Her route had changed from green to amber.

“I have to go.”

Davi looked up at the board.

“You said you were staying.”

“Direct transfer moved.”

“Lower connection.”

“Twenty-three minutes later.”

Maya looked from Lea to the repost still floating above the table.

Lea slung her bag over one shoulder.

“Another Ring Six delay does more to my evening than this argument.”

That was not a rebuttal Maya could win.

“I’ll come.”

Lea stopped.

“You don’t live there.”

“I noticed.”

“You were defending your friend.”

“I can resume the trial later.”

Davi lifted his cup.

“Heroic restraint.”

Maya pointed at him.

“Do not become interesting while I’m gone.”

“Impossible.”

She followed Lea out.

* * *

Lea’s temporary housing was farther away than Maya remembered.

Not geographically.

Operationally.

They missed one transfer because a freight-priority window expanded by three minutes.

The next arrived on time and was full enough that Maya spent half the ride standing badly and pretending the wall had chosen her position.

Lea did not correct her.

At the second junction Lea bought food from a late counter.

“You just ate,” Maya said.

“That was social food.”

“This?”

“Dinner for my brother.”

Maya took the second container before Lea could object.

“Which one?”

“The brother?”

“You have several?”

Lea looked at her.

Maya sighed.

“Fine. I continue discovering that people have families when I am not present.”

“Progress.”

The temporary unit was in a mature ring Maya had passed through twice without noticing.

Nothing in the corridor announced temporary housing.

The door did.

Lea opened it into a room designed for one household arrangement and currently performing three.

A sleeping surface folded down over what should have been a work table. Two storage walls were open because closing either blocked the other. Someone had hung a privacy sheet through the middle of the common volume.

A younger boy sat behind it doing schoolwork with headphones on.

An older adult slept in a chair reclined almost flat.

Another person was taking a call in the corridor because there was nowhere inside to take it privately.

Lea stepped over a bag and put the food beside the boy.

“You ate?”

He removed one headphone.

“No.”

“There’s an empty container.”

“That was earlier.”

“When?”

“Recently.”

He replaced the headphone.

Lea looked at Maya.

“Family.”

“Earth remains competitive.”

The boy looked up again.

“You’re Planet.”

Maya stopped.

Lea almost smiled.

“Absolutely not.”

“Lea said.”

“I said one word.”

“Historically how propaganda starts.”

The boy considered Maya.

“Ireland?”

“No.”

“America?”

“Yes.”

“Why do you say Earth?”

Lea answered for her.

“She likes suffering.”

The boy accepted this and returned to schoolwork.

Maya looked around.

The unit was clean. Safe. Warm. Functioning.

Nothing in it looked poor.

That made the crowding harder to ignore.

A wall display carried household schedules stacked on top of one another.

School.
Service shift.
Care appointment.
Sleep.
Transfers.

One route began at 05:40 and ended at 07:02 before the first work shift even started.

Maya pointed at it.

“Your mother?”

Lea nodded and opened a storage panel.

Half clothes. Half household equipment that clearly belonged somewhere else.

“If she moves the shift, she loses the care overlap with my aunt. If my aunt moves hers, my brother’s pickup breaks. If we put him in the school nearest this unit, he changes again when Ring Six opens.”

The boy did not look up.

“I care about everybody stopping conversations about me while I’m in the room.”

Lea shut the panel.

“Also that.”

Maya sat on the edge of the folded sleeping surface.

It shifted under her.

Lea said, “Up.”

Maya stood immediately.

“The release is bad,” Lea said.

“You sleep on that?”

“My aunt does.”

“Why isn’t it fixed?”

“It is fixed.”

Maya stared.

Lea shrugged.

“It is also bad.”

Maya moved out of the way while Lea lowered the sleeping surface properly.

“So this is why you want Ring Six open now.”

Lea fitted the food container into a warming recess.

“I want them to decide.”

“What if waiting is actually safer?”

Lea turned around.

“For another month? Fine, if another month buys a real safety margin.”

“And if it doesn’t?”

“Then I would like my family to stop paying rent in hours because nobody wants to sign the next step.”

Maya looked at the schedule wall again.

05:40.
07:02.
School pickup.
Care overlap.

“You think they’re just scared?”

Lea pushed the warming recess closed.

“I don’t know what they are.”

Maya looked at her.

Lea noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing. You sounded uncertain.”

“I am uncertain about things I don’t know.”

“At the food table you had a different system.”

“That was social.”

Maya laughed.

Lea did too, briefly.

The household display changed.

RING SIX COMMISSIONING — GO / NO-GO REVIEW SCHEDULED.

Lea saw it first.

The younger boy took off one headphone.

The sleeping adult opened her eyes.

“Review?”

“Yes,” Lea said.

“Good.”

Maya looked at her.

“Good means open?”

The woman focused on Maya as if she had appeared from the wall.

“Who are you?”

“Planet,” the boy said.

Maya closed her eyes.

Lea’s mother looked at Lea.

“Friend?”

Lea hesitated just long enough to be irritating.

“Yes.”

Maya opened her eyes again.

That word worked better than Planet.

Lea’s mother returned to the notice.

“Good means decision. I’m tired of estimates that become new estimates.”

Lea said, “She trusts numbers more than people.”

Her mother closed her eyes again.

“Numbers do not ask for money after moving out.”

The boy said, “I am still here.”

“Temporarily.”

Headphone back on.

Behind Maya, the sleeping surface needed to come down. She moved without being asked.

* * *

Twenty minutes later Maya’s band vibrated.

HOST: next late-event authorization form is open. system carried forward Lea household as used backup from last time. confirm still valid?

Maya read it twice.

Lea stood beside her in the corridor outside the unit waiting for a laundry slot.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Maya shifted the band away from view.

Lea went back to the laundry display.

The host had not accused Maya of anything. The system had simply carried forward the last arrangement as if it had happened exactly as understood.

LEA HOUSEHOLD — USED BACKUP / PREVIOUS EVENT.

Maya’s stomach tightened.

She could correct it later.

The next late event was not even approved yet.

The record had caused no problem.

All three sentences arrived ready-made.

Lea glanced over and caught the household label before Maya closed it.

Her face changed.

“What is that?”

Maya locked the band.

“Old form.”

“Why does it say my household was used?”

Maya hated how fast the corridor became too narrow.

“It carried the backup forward.”

“You slept at Arel’s.”

“Yes.”

“And your host still thinks my house was the backup you used.”

“They never asked where I slept after the plan changed.”

Lea stared at her.

Maya heard herself and tried another direction.

“I was safe.”

Lea laughed once.

Not kindly.

A laundry door opened behind them.

Lea missed the slot.

Someone else stepped in.

“Great.”

“Sorry.”

Lea pointed at the locked band.

“Not about the laundry.”

Maya folded her arms.

“I never asked you to lie to anybody.”

“You asked me to be the reason your host’s version worked.”

“I was with you.”

“At Arel’s.”

Maya started assembling the exact wording of the old message.

Still with Lea.
Inside Zone Four.
No route or environment change.

Every piece had been true.

Through the open household door she could see the privacy sheet, the folded bed, the schedule wall and Lea’s brother at the improvised work surface.

Lea had not merely been a name on a backup field.

Maya unlocked the band.

“What are you doing?” Lea asked.

“Correcting it.”

She typed:

Correction from last late event: I did not sleep at Lea’s household. I stayed at Arel’s in Zone Four. Lea was with me, but the approved backup location was not used. I should have told you when the plan changed.

Her thumb stopped over SEND.

Lea watched the screen.

Maya said, “I’m going to send it.”

“Then send it.”

She did.

The reply came almost immediately.

HOST: understood. thanks for correcting. next late-event authorization paused until we review backup/contact conditions together.

Maya stared at paused.

“No late event.”

Lea said nothing.

“That is ridiculous.”

Lea turned her head.

Maya lasted one second.

“Fine. It is extremely irritating for reasons I have contributed to.”

“Closer.”

“I hate you.”

“I know.”

Maya locked the band.

Then unlocked it again.

She typed before she could optimize the wording:

MAYA: okay. I still want no live tracking. can we use truthful zone/contact + return condition instead of fixed sleep backup next time?

HOST: yes. we can discuss.

No speech. No restored authorization.

The pause remained.

Maya looked at Lea.

Lea was watching the laundry queue.

“Are we okay?” Maya asked.

“No.”

Maya’s face fell before she could stop it.

Lea noticed and did not rescue her.

“We are waiting for laundry together,” she said. “That is what I have.”

The next slot showed eleven minutes.

Maya leaned against the wall.

“I can wait.”

“You don’t have laundry.”

“No.”

“Why stay?”

Maya looked through the open door at the household schedule, then at Lea.

“Because leaving would be very efficient.”

Lea snorted.

They waited for the next slot.
