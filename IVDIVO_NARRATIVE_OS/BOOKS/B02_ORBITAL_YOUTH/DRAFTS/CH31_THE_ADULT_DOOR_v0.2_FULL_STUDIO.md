# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 31 — THE ADULT DOOR

The offer arrived with a start time.

That made it real.

Ethan had been looking at versions of the adult professional route for almost two years.

Before Contact changed everything.

Before Lesson Zero.

Before the cohort.

Before being seventeen had become a fact large enough to reorganize his future around.

Most of those versions had been descriptions.

Eligibility windows.

Future pathways.

Conditional placements.

Training maps.

This one had a person waiting on the other side of the call and a start time in thirty-six hours.

SYSTEMS OPERATIONS — INITIAL PROFESSIONAL PLACEMENT

INTAKE: CURRENT

START: 07:00 / TWO DAYS

ASSIGNMENT: TRANSIT / SERVICE COORDINATION

STATUS: AVAILABLE IF ACCEPTED BEFORE 20:00

Ethan read the assignment again.

Transit / service coordination.

Not observation.

Not youth support.

Not a preparatory block designed to resemble work.

The real thing.

His first thought was yes.

His second was Ring Six.

He disliked the order.

The coordinator appeared on screen at exactly seventeen thirty.

Just a woman in a work office with three open schedules behind her and a drink she had forgotten to finish.

“Ethan Cole?”

“Yes.”

“Thanks for joining. You received the placement packet?”

“Yes.”

“Questions before I summarize what you already read?”

He had nine.

He asked the first.

“Is the start fixed?”

“For this placement, yes.”

“Could I start after the migration window?”

“Not in this slot.”

“Why?”

“The receiving team has an intake cycle. If you miss it, they fill the place.”

“From the same applicant pool?”

“Usually.”

Ethan nodded.

The answer was reasonable.

That was annoying.

The coordinator continued.

“You are also eligible for the next adult intake.”

“When?”

She sent the window.

Three weeks later.

“Same placement?”

“No guarantee.”

“Same systems track?”

“Likely. Not guaranteed.”

“Transit/service coordination specifically?”

“No.”

“Could be lower preference?”

“Yes.”

“Could be better?”

“Yes.”

“That is not useful.”

The coordinator smiled.

“It is accurate.”

Ethan looked at the current offer.

“Can I accept this and delay the start?”

“No.”

“Can I preserve priority in the later intake if I decline because of an existing cohort commitment?”

“You preserve eligibility. Not placement priority.”

“Does declining count against future review?”

“No.”

“Does accepting mean leaving the youth placement completely?”

“Your current youth residency remains, but this work placement becomes your primary professional commitment during scheduled hours. In practice, yes, you would leave most Ring Six support activity.”

Ethan looked at the migration schedule on the side of his screen.

The first major move window started the morning after his proposed adult start.

He had agreed to support route coordination and resident movement prep.

Not command.

Not anything irreplaceable.

Someone else could do it.

That fact should have made the decision easier.

It did not.

The coordinator watched him reading.

“You do not have to decide on the call.”

“I know.”

“You have until twenty hundred.”

“I know.”

“You said that quickly.”

“People keep telling me that.”

“I will add myself to the list.”

Ethan almost smiled.

She said, “One thing before we end.”

He waited.

“This is not a test of commitment to your current program.”

“I understand.”

“Declining does not prove maturity. Accepting does not prove ambition. They are different choices with different costs.”

Ethan looked at her.

“That sounds like something people need to be told often.”

“Yes.”

“Do you have a preferred answer?”

“No.”

“Your team?”

“They prefer someone starts in the slot. They do not care whether that person is you enough to make this moral.”

The answer stripped away the easiest excuse.

She closed one of the schedules.

“Anything else?”

Ethan had one question left.

It was the question he did not want to ask because the answer would make the choice simpler in the wrong direction.

“If I take the later intake, how likely is it that I lose this exact assignment?”

The coordinator thought before answering.

“Meaningfully likely.”

She did not turn it into a percentage.

“Meaningfully likely.”
Ethan nodded.

“Okay.”

“I will send the acceptance and later-intake links separately.”

“Thanks.”

“Good luck with whichever problem you choose.”

The call ended.

Ethan sat alone in his host room.

The adult offer remained open.

So did the migration schedule.

He moved them side by side.

Then stopped.

That was exactly how this became a spreadsheet.

He closed both.

* * *

The group chat was active.

That was unfortunate.

MAYA: does anyone have two spare route straps

SAMIR: no

MAYA: you did not check

SAMIR: correct

OLLIE: I have one

MAYA: useful child

OLLIE: never call me that again

AOIFE: screenshot saved

ETHAN: I need to tell you something

He stared at the line.

Deleted it.

The five did not need a meeting.

They did not need a vote.

He had no right to turn his decision into a test of how much everyone wanted him around.

Ethan put the band down.

Picked it up again.

Opened Aoife.

Closed it.

Opened the adult placement packet.

Closed that.

Celia passed the host-room doorway.

“You are doing excellent work.”

Ethan looked up.

“I have done nothing.”

“That is what I meant.”

“Was that sarcasm?”

“Yes.”

“Helpful.”

She stepped back into the doorway.

“Problem?”

“Choice.”

“Same thing sometimes.”

He did not explain.

Celia did not ask.

“Dinner in twenty.”

“Okay.”

She left.

Ethan looked at the clock.

18:02.

One hour fifty-eight minutes.

He opened Aoife again.

ETHAN: Are you free for ten minutes?

The reply came after thirty seconds.

AOIFE: define free

ETHAN: not carrying someone’s wall

AOIFE: suspiciously specific

ETHAN: I need to tell you something before 20:00.

The typing indicator appeared.

Stopped.

AOIFE: where

He sent a route.

She sent a different one.

AOIFE: yours is 14 min

ETHAN: yours is 19

AOIFE: mine has food

ETHAN: yours

He got his jacket.

* * *

Aoife was sitting on the floor outside a closed community room with a food container balanced between her knees.

There was a roll of relocation tape stuck to one sleeve.

Ethan looked at it.

“You know that’s there?”

“Yes.”

“Okay.”

“Good start.”

He sat beside her.

The corridor traffic moved around them.

Not crowded.

Enough that nobody had privacy by default.

Aoife handed him a fork.

“What?”

“You asked for ten minutes. I have food.”

“I’m not hungry.”

“Then hold it.”

He held the fork.

Aoife ate.

Ethan said, “I got the adult-track placement.”

She stopped chewing.

Then continued.

“The deadline thing.”

“Yes.”

“Tonight.”

“Yes.”

“You got the assignment you wanted?”

“Close.”

“What does close mean?”

“Systems operations. Transit and service coordination.”

Aoife looked at him.

“That sounds exactly like you.”

“I know.”

“That sounded defensive.”

“It wasn’t.”

“It was.”

He took a breath.

“Start is in two days.”

She looked toward the move-prep room behind the closed door.

“Migration.”

“Yes.”

“So?”

“I can take it and leave most Ring Six support work.”

Aoife waited.

“Or?”

“Later intake.”

“Same placement?”

“No guarantee.”

“Same track?”

“Likely.”

“How much do you want this one?”

Ethan hated the question because she did not soften it.

“A lot.”

“Okay.”

He looked at her.

“That’s all?”

“What do you want me to say?”

“I don’t know.”

“That is a terrible thing to ask me to fix.”

“I didn’t ask you to fix it.”

“You looked like you were about to.”

Ethan looked away.

A family passed carrying two folded room panels.

One child was asleep against an adult’s shoulder.

Aoife took another bite.

“You could go,” she said.

“Yes.”

“Would they replace you here?”

“Yes.”

“Would that hurt the migration?”

“Not structurally.”

“Would Ollie survive?”

“Probably.”

“That weakens the argument.”

“I am aware.”

Aoife wiped sauce from the edge of the container.

Ethan said, “I told them I would finish the Ring Six commitment.”

“You told who?”

“Cohort. Housing support. Mara’s team.”

“People change plans.”

“I know.”

“Adult-track start is a real reason.”

“I know.”

She looked at him.

“You are doing the thing.”

“What thing?”

“Answering every argument before I finish making it.”

“I’m not—”

“You are.”

He stopped.

Aoife waited.

Ethan put the fork down.

“I don’t know how to talk about this without making it sound like I already decided.”

“Have you?”

“No.”

“You asked me here because?”

“I didn’t want you to find out after.”

Aoife's expression shifted before she looked back down at the food.
“Okay.”

Ethan looked at the floor.

“I did that before.”

“What?”

“Held information until I had arranged what it meant.”

Aoife’s mouth tightened.

“Yes.”

“I don’t want to do that again.”

“Good.”

The word was not warm.

It was also not nothing.

He said, “I think I’m staying.”

Aoife looked at him for several seconds.

“For us?”

Aoife's question exposed the version he had been avoiding. He could say no and make the choice sound clean, independent and mature. It would also be false.

“Partly.”

Aoife’s eyebrows moved.

“The group matters.”

“And?”

“And you matter.”

She did not move.

Ethan kept going before the sentence could become the wrong kind of declaration.

“But that is not enough reason.”

Aoife looked relieved and annoyed at the same time.

“That was almost horrible.”

“I know.”

“You do not get credit for stopping one sentence before disaster.”

“I wasn’t asking.”

“You looked like—”

“I know.”

She pointed at him with the fork.

“Do not use my line.”

He almost laughed.

Then did.

Aoife did too, briefly.

Ethan said, “I said I would finish this.”

“That is a reason.”

“Yes.”

“Not the only one.”

“No.”

“Do you think you will resent us?”

He had not wanted that question.

“Maybe.”

Aoife looked down at her food.

“Good.”

“That is not reassuring.”

“It isn’t meant to be.”

He waited.

She said, “If you stay and later act like everyone owes you because you stayed, I will make your life unbearable.”

“That seems fair.”

“It is not a joke.”

“I know.”

“Also, if you go, I will be angry.”

Ethan blinked.

“That seems less fair.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Aoife looked at him.

“Because I am a person, Ethan.”

He smiled despite himself.

“That is inconvenient.”

“Very.”

The corridor announcement changed a transfer route.

Neither moved.

Ethan said, “I don’t want this decision to become about whether you and I are—”

“Do not finish that sentence.”

“Why?”

“Because I am eating.”

“That is not a reason.”

“It is tonight.”

He looked at her.

Aoife looked back.

The question remained between them without being converted into an answer they had not earned.

Ethan said, “I’m going to take the later intake.”

Aoife nodded once and left it there.

“Tell them before the deadline.”

“I will.”

“And then eat.”

“I said I’m not hungry.”

“You have been holding my fork for eight minutes.”

He looked down.

He still had it.

Aoife took it back.

“Administrative theft.”

“That is not a thing.”

“It is now.”

* * *

At 19:16, Ethan opened the adult-track response page.

He was back in his host room.

Celia and her partner were arguing in the kitchen about whether something counted as soup.

The argument had survived three minutes and appeared healthy.

On Ethan’s screen:

CURRENT PLACEMENT

SYSTEMS OPERATIONS — TRANSIT / SERVICE COORDINATION

START: TWO DAYS / 07:00

ACCEPT

DECLINE CURRENT / ENTER LATER INTAKE

He selected the second.

A warning opened.

LATER INTAKE DOES NOT GUARANTEE:

— IDENTICAL ASSIGNMENT
— IDENTICAL TEAM
— IDENTICAL START PRIORITY
— CURRENT PLACEMENT AVAILABILITY

CONFIRM?

He did not confirm.

Not yet.

He opened the group chat.

MAYA: found straps

OLLIE: good

MAYA: not yours

OLLIE: better

SAMIR: practice moved again

AOIFE: relocation owns the city

ETHAN looked at the text field.

He could tell them now.

He could say:

I turned down my preferred adult slot to stay through migration.

That sentence made him sound noble.

He hated it.

He could say nothing.

That made the choice secret.

Also bad.

He typed:

ETHAN: adult-track offered me an immediate placement. I’m taking the later intake so I can finish migration week here.

He looked at it.

Added:

ETHAN: later assignment isn’t guaranteed.

Sent.

The replies arrived in the wrong order.

OLLIE: which placement

SAMIR: okay

AOIFE: he knows

Then:

ETHAN: transit/service coordination

OLLIE: oh

Ethan smiled.

Then Maya:

ETHAN: no

AOIFE: she had several things

MAYA: classified

Ollie sent:

OLLIE: if you complain during migration i’m saving this message

ETHAN: fair

Samir sent a picture of a Crossband ball.

No explanation.

Ethan looked at it.

Then at the confirmation page.

He pressed CONFIRM.

The current placement disappeared immediately from ACTIVE OFFERS instead of fading into a declined state.

A new line appeared:

LATER ADULT INTAKE — ELIGIBLE

PLACEMENT: PENDING

PRIORITY ASSIGNMENT: NOT SET

He stared at the empty space where Transit / Service Coordination had been.

He knew this was what would happen.

Knowing did not make the absence smaller.

Another notification appeared.

PROFESSIONAL ROUTE GROUP

Three names he recognized from Vela Night had confirmed current placements.

Marin — systems analysis.

Tae — transit operations.

Luca — service coordination.

Luca.

Service coordination.

Ethan opened the profile before he could stop himself.

CURRENT INTAKE — CONFIRMED.

For half a second, resentment arrived with perfect clarity.

It was not about Luca personally. The resentment reached backward instead—toward timing, toward seventeen, toward the months already lost, toward the irritating fact that even without anyone blocking him, choosing one future still removed another.
His hand moved toward the group chat.

He stopped.

What was he going to write?

Never mind, this feels bad, everyone please make me feel correct?

He closed the chat.

Closing the group chat did not reduce the resentment, and Ethan did not reopen it to ask anyone else to carry it.
He opened the migration schedule instead, then closed that too. The work he had chosen was easier to face than the opportunity he had released.

Celia called from the kitchen.

“Soup decision.”

“What?”

“You are required.”

“Why?”

“You have opinions.”

“That seems like a poor qualification.”

“It has never stopped you.”

Ethan stood.

His band buzzed once more.

AOIFE: still want it?

He looked at the question.

Typed:

ETHAN: yes

A pause.

AOIFE: okay

Another pause.

AOIFE: dinner tomorrow if neither of us gets turned into housing equipment

He smiled.

ETHAN: what time

He stopped before sending.

Deleted it.

Typed:

ETHAN: when are you free

Aoife sent three windows.

Ethan sent two of his.

They found one overlap.

They found twenty-seven minutes the next evening without opening the group schedule.
He accepted.

Then went to decide whether the thing in the kitchen was soup.

* * *

The next morning, the immediate placement was still gone.

Ethan checked once.

Only once.

The later intake remained.

PENDING.

He closed it.

At the migration-support desk, Nadia handed him a stack of route cards.

“You are early.”

“Yes.”

“You look unhappy about it.”

“I am experimenting with adulthood.”

“That sounds exhausting.”

“It is.”

She pointed toward a staging table.

“Those go there. Do not reorder them.”

Ethan looked at the cards.

They were not sorted.

“By household?”

“No.”

“By route?”

“No.”

“Then—”

Nadia looked at him.

Ethan stopped.

“Staging table.”

“Yes.”

He carried the cards to the table exactly as given.

Behind him, families were arriving with boxes.

Some were moving.

Some were still waiting.

His preferred adult placement existed now in somebody else’s schedule.

The route cards in his hands did not become more important because he had stayed.

He put them down.

Nadia called, “Cole.”

He turned.

“Wrong table.”

Ethan looked.

There were two identical staging tables.

“Which one?”

“The one with the ugly tape.”

He moved the cards.

Nadia was already arguing with someone else by the time he set the cards down.
