# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 18 — EIGHTEEN

At 06:02 Ethan received twelve messages telling him he was legally different.

His mother was first.

HAPPY BIRTHDAY. EIGHTEEN. LOVE YOU. CALL WHEN YOU ARE AWAKE AND NOT PRETENDING TO BE BUSY.

His father sent a shorter message and a transfer receipt.

His sister sent a picture of him at eleven wearing a helmet that was too large and holding a project board upside down.

Under it:

YOU HAVE SHOWN NO IMPROVEMENT

The Book One group thread was worse.

MAYA: adult

MAYA: disgusting

SAMIR: happy birthday

AOIFE: administrative transformation complete

OLLIE: legal status update accepted

ETHAN: I hate all of you.

MAYA: confirmed

Ethan smiled before he had fully opened his eyes.

His host had left a small wrapped package outside the sleeping-room door.

Inside was a local transit clip with his name printed correctly and a note:

FOR ALL THE ROUTES YOU WILL NOW ORGANIZE WITHOUT PERMISSION

Ethan read the note twice.

Then checked whether the clip included new access.

It did not.

He respected that.

* * *

The Youth Cohort study block began at 08:00.

At 07:53 Maya arrived carrying something that looked like food and behaved like structural foam.

“You brought a cake.”

“I brought a material.”

“What is it?”

“Celebration density.”

“That is not food.”

“You have not tried it.”

“That is visible.”

Aoife came in behind her with paper cups, which were still a thing in Vela because apparently no civilization escaped the need for cheap disposable objects during birthdays.

Samir had nothing visible.

Ollie was late by four minutes because a maintenance handover overran.

Ethan noticed all of this before deciding he should probably stop inventorying his own birthday.

The instructor entered, saw the food, checked the schedule, and said, “You have twenty minutes before we do anything educational.”

Maya pointed at Ethan.

“He aged.”

“I was informed.”

“Legally.”

“I was not informed of that detail.”

The instructor looked at Ethan.

“Congratulations. Your coursework remains due.”

“Thank you.”

“Also, because you are eighteen, your local civic status changes at midnight tonight. Do not assume this means every placement restriction disappears.”

Maya turned slowly toward Ethan.

Ethan closed his eyes.

“Do not.”

“You get new menus.”

“I know.”

“Adult menus.”

“Maya.”

“Perhaps taxes.”

“This is a post-scarcity residency.”

“Someone will invent taxes for you personally.”

Samir said, “Likely.”

Ethan pointed at him.

“You were supposed to be the stable one.”

“Birthday exemption.”

Aoife handed Ethan a cup.

“You should enjoy this. They will use your new status against you by lunchtime.”

“That is reassuring.”

“You are welcome.”

They cut the celebration density.

It was edible.

Barely.

Maya watched Ethan take the first bite.

“Well?”

“It has texture.”

“Positive.”

“No.”

Ollie arrived carrying a tool pouch, saw the food and stopped.

“What happened?”

Ethan stared at him.

“Birthday.”

“Right.”

“You forgot.”

“No.”

“You absolutely forgot.”

Ollie put the tool pouch under the table.

“I knew the date.”

“That is not the same as remembering.”

Aoife slid a cup toward him.

“You can recover socially if you have a gift.”

Ollie went still.

Maya laughed so hard she nearly dropped her plate.

Ethan decided the morning was good.

* * *

The first new civic menu appeared at 11:17.

Ethan was in a route-planning workshop when his band displayed:

STATUS UPDATE: AGE OF MAJORITY — 18

LOCAL CIVIC ACCESS REVIEW COMPLETE

New options unfolded beneath it.

INDEPENDENT HOUSING APPLICATION — ELIGIBLE

ADULT WORK / TRAINING PATHWAY — ELIGIBLE

VOLUNTARY CIVIC COMMITTEE PARTICIPATION — ELIGIBLE

LOCAL CONTRACT AUTHORITY — STANDARD

HEALTH / PRIVACY CONSENT — SELF-DIRECTED

Ethan stared.

The workshop facilitator continued talking about service closures.

Nobody else noticed.

He opened ADULT WORK / TRAINING PATHWAY.

The system asked whether he wanted information.

YES.

A second page appeared.

CURRENT YOUTH COHORT PLACEMENT MAY CONTINUE UNCHANGED.

ADULT PATHWAY APPLICATION DOES NOT CANCEL EXISTING YOUTH STATUS.

EARLY TRANSITION REQUIRES:

SUPERVISOR REVIEW

CURRENT COMMITMENT IMPACT REVIEW

PLACEMENT AVAILABILITY

NO GUARANTEE OF PREFERRED ROLE

Ethan read the last line twice.

Then opened independent housing.

WAITLIST ESTIMATE: 4–11 MONTHS DEPENDING ON SECTOR / HOUSEHOLD PROFILE / ACCESS NEED

He closed it.

Maya would find that funny.

He did not tell her.

He went back to the route workshop.

* * *

After lunch, Ethan met Tessa from the adult pathway office.

The office looked disappointingly normal.

Two desks.

One wall showing current training routes.

A plant that might have been real.

No threshold.

No ceremony.

Tessa looked at his file.

“Eighteen today.”

“Yes.”

“You applied for information at eleven seventeen.”

“Yes.”

“Fast.”

“I wanted to know what changed.”

“Mostly the number of decisions you are allowed to make badly without somebody else signing first.”

Ethan smiled.

“That sounds official.”

“It should not.”

She opened his current placement profile.

Youth Cohort residency.

Study block.

Route-planning support.

Ring Six consequence work.

Temporary local project permissions.

“Adult pathway does not mean ‘same work, no supervision,’” Tessa said.

“I assumed not.”

“It means you can apply for responsibilities attached to adult legal status, including contracts, longer placements and some project roles that youth tracks cannot hold.”

“Broader access?”

“Sometimes. Where the responsibility matches.”

She opened a sample project.

A mature-ring service corridor needed a six-hour closure for replacement work. The closure moved a school connector. That shifted a care pickup. The care pickup collided with a service shift. The service shift pushed freight into a new window.

Ethan leaned forward before he meant to.

Tessa noticed.

“Dependency mapping.”

“Yes.”

“Real?”

“Real enough that if you map it badly, somebody gets to explain to a family why their child has no route to school that morning.”

Ethan read the packet again.

The pathway was not glamorous.

That made it worse.

He wanted it.

“When could I start?”

“You can apply now. Placement depends on intake and current commitments.”

“Could I transition before the Youth Cohort ends?”

“Yes.”

The answer landed before he was ready.

Tessa opened his current commitment list.

Ring Six consequence mapping sat near the top.

“Your supervisor can release you early,” she said. “The placement office can accept you early. Those are separate decisions.”

“So both could agree.”

“They could.”

Ethan looked at the Ring Six block.

“And if they do?”

“You choose.”

No ceremony.

Again.

“What would you recommend?”

Tessa leaned back.

“For a random eighteen-year-old? Finish the commitment you are already inside unless the new placement is time-sensitive.”

“And for me?”

“I do not know you well enough to replace your judgment with mine.”

“That sounds like a policy answer.”

“It is also true.”

Ethan looked at the adult pathway packet.

It had more interesting work in it than he wanted it to have.

Tessa sent the information to his account.

“Read it. Do not treat eligibility as instruction.”

“I won’t.”

She gave him a look that suggested she had met eighteen-year-olds before.

* * *

Ethan did not tell the group immediately.

This was not a decision.

It was information.

That distinction remained convincing until 16:40.

He spent forty-seven minutes reading adult placement descriptions while pretending to review Ring Six route dependencies.

At 17:02 Aoife sat down opposite him.

“You missed the message.”

“What message?”

“Food.”

He checked the group thread.

MAYA: eat at 17:00

SAMIR: training until 17:20

OLLIE: shift

AOIFE: where is birthday administration

MAYA: adult abandoned us already

Ethan locked the thread.

“I’m here.”

“Yes. Alone. With forms.”

“They are not forms.”

Aoife looked at the adult pathway packet open beside him.

“Those are aggressively forms.”

Ethan moved it aside too quickly.

Aoife’s eyes followed.

“What is that?”

“Adult pathway information.”

“Since when?”

“This afternoon.”

“You met somebody?”

“Yes.”

Aoife was quiet.

Ethan felt the conversation moving before he understood why.

“It was informational.”

“Okay.”

“I haven’t applied for a placement.”

“Okay.”

He stopped.

Aoife looked at him.

“Why are you explaining it like I accused you?”

“I’m not.”

“You moved the file when I saw it.”

“Because it is mine.”

The sentence came out sharper than he intended.

Aoife leaned back.

“Right.”

Ethan rubbed one thumb against the edge of the table.

“I didn’t tell the group because nothing has happened.”

“Why did you decide it was a group question?”

“It affects the cohort if I move early.”

“That is not what I asked.”

He knew that.

Aoife let him sit with it.

“I wanted one thing not to become a meeting immediately,” Ethan said.

“Then why tell me now?”

“Because you saw it.”

That hurt her.

He saw it before she looked away.

Samir arrived carrying two food packs.

He stopped beside the table.

“Bad time?”

Aoife stood.

“No. I’m going to eat somewhere else.”

Samir looked at Ethan, then at Aoife.

“I can leave.”

“I’m already leaving.”

She picked up her device.

Ethan said, “Aoife.”

She stopped.

He did not have a sentence that improved anything.

Aoife waited anyway.

“I should have told you before you found it.”

“Why?”

The question was not rhetorical.

Ethan looked at the closed group thread.

“Because you matter to me.”

Aoife’s expression changed.

Then became guarded again.

“That is not the same as telling me because you thought I should know.”

“No.”

“That is the part I am angry about.”

She left.

Samir put one food pack on Ethan’s side of the table.

Ethan stared at it.

“You don’t have to say anything.”

“Excellent,” Samir said.

He opened his own food.

They ate for almost a minute.

Ethan opened the adult pathway packet again.

Samir kept eating.

“I do want it,” Ethan said.

Samir nodded.

“That wasn’t a question.”

“I know.”

Ethan looked at the Ring Six commitment block.

Then at the option:

EARLY TRANSITION REQUEST.

He closed it.

Below it:

APPLY FOR LATER INTAKE.

He selected that instead.

The system asked:

PREFERRED TRANSITION WINDOW:

AFTER CURRENT YOUTH COHORT COMMITMENT REVIEW

AFTER RING SIX DECISION CYCLE

CUSTOM

Ethan selected:

AFTER CURRENT YOUTH COHORT COMMITMENT REVIEW.

Not because Aoife was angry.

Not because Maya would make a speech.

Not because Samir was sitting there.

Because Ring Six was still his commitment.

He submitted the information request.

Not a placement application.

A request to be notified when the later intake opened.

Samir looked at the screen.

“Later?”

“Yes.”

“Okay.”

Ethan almost laughed.

“That is all?”

“You told me not to say anything.”

“That was before I did something.”

Samir took another bite.

“Do you want analysis now?”

“No.”

“Good.”

They finished eating.

* * *

At 22:10 Ethan called home.

His mother answered from a kitchen that still looked exactly like home even after Vela.

“You are eighteen.”

“So people keep saying.”

“Did you have cake?”

“Something in that category.”

“Friends?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

She did not ask whether adulthood felt different.

Ethan was grateful.

Halfway through the call his father joined and asked about work.

His sister appeared only long enough to repeat that he had shown no improvement since eleven.

After the call, Ethan lay down in the host room.

The transit clip sat on the small shelf beside his bed.

His adult status menu remained available.

Nothing about the room looked different.

The desk still folded badly against the storage recess.

His backpack was still in the least inconvenient place.

At 22:41 the group thread lit again.

MAYA: birthday rating

SAMIR: 6

MAYA: out of

SAMIR: unclear

OLLIE: I owe you a gift

AOIFE: goodnight

Ethan read Aoife’s message twice.

He typed:

ETHAN: goodnight

Then:

ETHAN: rating 7.5

MAYA: coward decimal

For the first time that day, nobody mentioned eighteen.
