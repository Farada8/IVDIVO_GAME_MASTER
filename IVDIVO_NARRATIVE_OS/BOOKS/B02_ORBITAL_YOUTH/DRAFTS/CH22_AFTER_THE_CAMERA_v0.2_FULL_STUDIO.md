ORBITAL YOUTH
Chapter 22 — AFTER THE CAMERA

The original clip had been gone for almost two days when Aoife received her fourth request to discuss it on camera.

She declined before reading the second paragraph.

The first request had come from an Earth student channel.

The second from an orbital media class.

The third from an account that described itself as a privacy collective and had attached a release form granting itself permanent redistribution rights.

Aoife had read that one twice to make sure she was not imagining it.

The fourth message began:

WE WANT TO GIVE YOU SPACE TO EXPLAIN WHAT REALLY HAPPENED—

Aoife deleted the draft reply she had started automatically.

No explanation.

No statement about the source.

No correction of the politics attached to the reposts.

No thread showing exactly which details had been inference rather than disclosure, because the most efficient way to prove that would be to assemble the inference again.

She marked the request DECLINED — NO COMMENT and moved to the next item.

The source-privacy process had removed one quoted video segment from the first specialist repost.

Two still images remained under discussion because the account argued that they documented public infrastructure rather than the source.

The Earth politics account had copied one of them before the removal request.

Three smaller accounts had copied that copy.

One had cropped out almost everything except the humidity-buffer marker Aoife wished nobody had noticed in the first place.

Deletion had not traveled outward with the original.

Aoife had known that as a fact before this happened.

Knowing it and watching the copies survive were different activities.

Her working project now contained an empty ninety-two-second gap.

She had not filled it.

The hole made the sequence worse.

She left it there.

A notification appeared from S1.

S1: can meet

Aoife stopped moving.

A second message followed.

S1: no recorder

Then:

S1: no notes

Aoife put the recorder case from her lap into her bag.

She had not planned to bring it.

Apparently some part of her had packed it anyway.

AOIFE: understood

S1: no transcription either

Aoife checked the meeting settings on her band.

Local accessibility transcription was off.

Personal memory-assist capture was off.

The documentary workspace had no permission to open automatically from the meeting location.

She sent:

AOIFE: understood. no recording / notes / transcription / project capture

The reply took longer.

S1: food level 4 near old return lift

Aoife knew the place.

She closed the inference thread, declined the last interview request and left.

Ethan sent a message while she was on the transfer.

ETHAN: you okay?

Aoife typed:

AOIFE: meeting source

Three dots appeared.

Then disappeared.

ETHAN: okay

Nothing else.

She kept the message open for a few seconds.

Then closed it.

* * *

S1 was already eating when Aoife arrived.

That was useful.

It gave Aoife something not to comment on.

The food level sat between a laundry corridor and an old return lift whose doors arrived a fraction of a second before the floor indicator admitted it. People crossed continuously behind the tables: workers carrying sealed packs, two students arguing about an assessment, a parent trying to stop a child from taking the same drink twice.

S1 had chosen a small table against the wall.

Aoife stopped beside it.

“Can I sit?”

S1 looked at the empty chair.

“That is what it is doing there.”

Aoife sat.

She put both hands on the table.

No recorder.

No tablet.

No band interface open between them.

S1 noticed anyway.

“Where is it?”

“In the bag.”

“Off?”

“Yes.”

“Everything?”

“No transcription. No project capture. No notes.”

S1 took another bite.

Aoife waited.

She had rehearsed an apology on the way over.

Every version had begun to sound like evidence for the defense.

She used the shortest one.

“I’m sorry.”

S1 kept chewing.

Aoife waited.

The old return lift opened behind them.

Three people got out.

The indicator changed afterward.

S1 put the fork down.

“For what?”

Aoife had not rehearsed that.

“For publishing something that let people identify things you explicitly did not want turned into the story.”

“I approved the clip.”

“Yes.”

“I checked it.”

“Yes.”

“You sent me the final version.”

“Yes.”

S1 looked at her.

Aoife felt the old answer waiting.

You approved the frame.

You approved the sound.

We both missed it.

She left it alone.

S1 said, “I agreed to those words. I did not agree to become legible in that way.”

Aoife nodded once.

S1’s expression tightened.

“Do not nod like you are taking minutes.”

Aoife stopped.

“Sorry.”

“That too.”

“What?”

“You keep saying sorry every time you do something.”

“I am trying not to argue.”

“I noticed.”

“Is that bad?”

“I did not say that.”

Aoife almost smiled.

S1 did not.

The smile died before it reached her face.

“I took the original down immediately,” Aoife said.

“I know.”

“I declined interviews.”

“I know.”

“The first privacy request removed one video copy.”

“I know.”

Aoife stopped listing things.

S1 picked up the fork again.

“That list is for you.”

Aoife looked at the table.

“Yes.”

S1 ate for a while.

Aoife did not fill the silence.

It became long enough that she could hear a service cart moving behind the wall.

Then S1 said, “Do you know what happened after the Earth account posted it?”

“No.”

“My cousin sent it to me.”

Aoife looked up.

“They didn’t know it was me. They sent it because they thought the argument was funny.”

Aoife said nothing.

“Then my manager asked whether I wanted a different shift entrance for a few days.”

“Did you?”

“No.”

“Okay.”

“Then someone I barely know asked if my support setting means I cannot travel to Earth.”

Aoife felt herself reach for a correction.

S1 saw it.

“Do not.”

Aoife shut her mouth.

“Exactly,” S1 said.

The parent behind them finally won the argument over the second drink.

The child declared the outcome illegal.

Neither Aoife nor S1 reacted.

S1 said, “The worst part is not even that people guessed.”

Aoife waited.

“I live here. People here can usually tell more about me than Earth people can. Sometimes they ask stupid things too.”

Aoife looked at the recorder bag by her foot.

S1 followed her eyes.

“You made it portable.”

Aoife looked back at them.

“The guessing?”

“The context.”

Aoife let the words sit.

S1 finished eating.

They pushed the tray slightly away.

“I am not giving you anything else for the documentary.”

“I understand.”

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

“For how long?”

Aoife almost said until you are comfortable.

That would still have turned the boundary into a waiting period.

“Unless you decide differently later.”

S1 watched her.

“Better.”

It was not forgiveness.

Aoife felt the difference immediately.

She did not ask for more.

S1 picked up the drink.

Then stopped.

“There is one thing.”

Aoife kept her hands on the table.

“Not for your film.”

“Okay.”

“About the ring review.”

Aoife’s attention changed before she could stop it.

S1 noticed.

“See?”

“What?”

“You have the face too.”

Aoife exhaled through her nose.

“I hate this city.”

“No, you don’t.”

“No.”

For the first time S1 almost smiled.

Almost.

They looked past Aoife toward the old return lift.

“The timing they are using for my warning is wrong.”

Aoife did not move.

“How do you know?”

“I was there.”

Aoife waited.

S1 continued because they chose to.

“The official message I got yesterday asked whether my report started before or after the local wall correction. It started before.”

“How much before?”

“I do not know exactly.”

Aoife felt the interview instinct rise.

What were you doing?
Where were you standing?
What did you feel first?
What did your support system do?

She asked only one question.

“Do you want that corrected in the incident review?”

S1 looked at her.

“Yes.”

“Can I pass it?”

“Yes.”

“In your name?”

“No.”

“With the recording?”

“No.”

“There is no recording here.”

“I mean the old one.”

“Understood.”

S1 leaned back.

“My personal support corrected before the wall indicator changed. That part I remember because I thought the support was being annoying again.”

Aoife did not reach for her band.

“How precise can you make the timing?”

S1 thought.

“Not seconds.”

“That is fine.”

“Maybe one action.”

“What action?”

S1 hesitated.

Aoife said, “You do not have to tell me if the action identifies you.”

“It probably does.”

“Then don’t.”

S1 looked annoyed by the answer.

Aoife waited.

“Can the review ask me through the protected source channel?”

“Yes.”

“Then tell them I can correct event order, not exact seconds.”

“Only that?”

“And that my personal support adjusted before the fixed wall response.”

Aoife repeated it once, slowly, without opening any device.

“Event order can be corrected. Personal support adjusted before fixed wall response. No exact seconds.”

“Yes.”

“May I tell them the correction comes from an affected participant with direct experience?”

S1 considered.

“Yes. Not food service. Not corridor class. Not body-support type.”

“Okay.”

“No publication.”

“No publication.”

“No documentary.”

“No documentary.”

“No telling Ollie because you know him.”

Aoife looked at them.

“I wouldn’t.”

S1 waited.

Aoife corrected herself.

“I won’t.”

“Good.”

The meeting ended without either of them saying it was over.

S1 stood, took the tray and stopped beside the table.

Aoife thought they might say something softer.

They said, “Your bag is open.”

Aoife looked down.

The recorder case was visible inside.

She closed the bag.

When she looked up, S1 was already walking away.

* * *

Aoife did not write anything down until she had permission to write the permitted thing down.

That turned out to be more complicated than it sounded.

The Youth Cohort incident channel was not the documentary channel.

Her media permissions did not cross into safety review.

The ordinary public incident form did not allow anonymous participant timing corrections without a source path.

She could have sent a message to Ollie.

She did not.

She could have asked Mara directly.

She did not know whether that would expose the source relationship unnecessarily.

Instead she opened the protected-source reporting interface she had used originally for the documentary consent record.

It offered three routing options.

PUBLICATION CORRECTION.

SOURCE PRIVACY COMPLAINT.

SAFETY / INCIDENT FACTUAL CORRECTION.

Aoife selected the third.

The form changed.

SOURCE IDENTITY REQUIRED TO REVIEW TEAM?

She selected NO.

The system warned:

DE-IDENTIFIED SUBMISSION MAY LIMIT VALIDATION.
SOURCE MAY BE CONTACTED THROUGH PROTECTED CHANNEL IF PERMISSION EXISTS.

Aoife stopped.

She had not asked whether the source could be contacted.

She closed the form without submitting.

For three seconds she considered being irritated at the system.

Then she messaged S1.

AOIFE: one more permission question only. review form says they may need to contact you through protected channel to validate correction. allow that? yes/no

The response came six minutes later.

S1: yes through protected channel

Then:

S1: not you sitting in the middle

Aoife looked at that line.

AOIFE: agreed

She reopened the form.

CONTACT SOURCE THROUGH PROTECTED CHANNEL: YES.

PUBLICATION RIGHTS ATTACHED: NO.

MEDIA WORKSPACE ACCESS: NO.

CORRECTION:

Affected participant reports event-order correction. Personal/local support adjustment began before fixed wall response. Exact time interval not asserted. Source permits review-team validation through protected channel. No original recording or contextual identity markers authorized for transfer.

Aoife read it twice.

The old version of herself would have improved the sentence.

She submitted it as written.

The incident system returned:

RECEIVED — VALIDATION PENDING.

No copy appeared in her documentary workspace.

No clip unlocked.

No source status changed from SUSPENDED.

A minute later Ethan messaged.

ETHAN: how did it go

Aoife typed:

AOIFE: not forgiven

She sent it before making the answer prettier.

Ethan replied:

ETHAN: okay

Aoife waited.

Nothing else came.

She put the band away.

Across the food level, somebody was recording a birthday message beside the old return lift.

They asked three times for the background crowd to be quieter.

Nobody listened.
