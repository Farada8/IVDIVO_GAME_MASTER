# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 14 — ALMOST LOCAL

Ethan’s eighteenth birthday arrived eight days early as a permissions notice.

He was eating breakfast at a standing counter because the two seats nearest his host’s kitchen had been claimed by a laundry frame and a child who was trying to finish a geometry exercise before school.

The geometry exercise was losing.

The child had constructed a triangle, rotated it, accused the software of changing the answer and started again.

Ethan’s host was somewhere behind a partition arguing with a delivery service about whether three storage modules counted as one household order or three separate objects.

The kitchen wall displayed the morning allocation queue, two transit delays and a polite reminder that somebody had left a wet garment in the shared drying channel overnight.

The reminder had already acquired four anonymous comments.

Ethan was reading them when his band vibrated once against his wrist.

Not a message.

A system notice.

LEGAL ACCESS TRANSITION — ADVANCE INFORMATION

He opened it.

His birthday was listed first.

22 JANUARY 2033.

Below it, the system had assembled the things that would change when he turned eighteen while resident in Vela under the Youth Cohort agreement.

Some were boring.

Expanded contract authority.
Different liability rules for tools and restricted workspaces.
Independent accommodation eligibility under conditions he did not currently meet.
Direct acceptance of certain service obligations without host countersignature.

Then came the line he read twice.

ADULT PROFESSIONAL PATHWAYS — INFORMATIONAL ACCESS AVAILABLE NOW. ACTIVATION ELIGIBLE ON LEGAL MAJORITY, SUBJECT TO EXISTING COHORT COMMITMENTS.

He stopped chewing.

The child at the table said, “You look like you swallowed something technical.”

Ethan looked over.

“What?”

“You do that face.”

“I don’t have a technical face.”

The child turned the geometry display toward him.

“Then fix this.”

Ethan looked at the triangle.

“You rotated the reference plane.”

“I did not.”

“You did.”

“The plane moved.”

“You moved it.”

The child narrowed their eyes.

“You’re very confident for a visitor.”

Ethan rotated the reference plane back.

The answer changed.

The child stared at it.

“Traitor software.”

Ethan returned to his band.

The notice had opened a list of pathways available to residents and temporary participants once legally eligible. Most were irrelevant to him. Habitat fabrication. Environmental service. Mediation. Transport operations. Civic coordination. Systems logistics.

He selected the last two because he knew better than to select the one that sounded most impressive first.

The description for systems logistics lasted five lines.

The description for civic coordination lasted seven.

Neither said leadership.

That helped.

They described real work instead: dependency mapping, resource timing, incident support, cross-team scheduling, public-service coordination, supervised participation in live operational decisions.

There was an information session three days after his birthday.

There was also a smaller pre-eligibility orientation available now.

Ethan read the entry requirement.

Age transition pending.
Current Youth Cohort participation in good standing.
No unresolved access sanction.
Sponsor or host acknowledgement not required for information-only attendance.

He touched the orientation link.

A time appeared.

He could attend.

Not eventually.

Not after an appeal.

Not after somebody reconsidered whether he was ready.

Now.

The denied door from autumn had not reopened dramatically. Nobody had called. Nobody had apologized. The system had simply reached a date at which a different rule applied.

That should have made it smaller.

It did not.

His host came through the partition carrying a storage module under one arm.

“Do not let me order household equipment before coffee again.”

“You ordered three.”

“I ordered one set.”

“The system disagrees.”

“The system is young and can still learn.”

The child pointed at Ethan.

“He agrees with systems.”

“I do not.”

“You defended the triangle.”

“I defended geometry.”

“Same thing.”

His host put the module on the floor.

“Any disasters?”

Ethan closed the adult-path screen.

“No.”

His host looked at him for half a second longer than necessary, then reached for coffee.

Ethan reopened the orientation page beneath the counter where nobody could see it.

He added the time to a private calendar.

Not the group calendar.

Not yet.

For once, he wanted to know what he wanted before everybody else started having opinions about it.

* * *

At 11:06, Ethan attempted to schedule dinner.

By 11:11, dinner had become a systems failure.

ETHAN: tonight 20:00 west market? all five

MAYA: no

ETHAN: useful detail

MAYA: already going out with lea

SAMIR: practice ends 20:10 if coach stops on time

AOIFE: source approval call 19:40. could be ten minutes. could become international law.

OLLIE: test prep

ETHAN: until when

OLLIE: unknown

ETHAN: estimate

OLLIE: 20:20 optimistic

AOIFE: do not encourage him

ETHAN: 20:40

MAYA: still no

ETHAN: you can meet Lea after

Three dots appeared beside Maya’s name.

They vanished.

Then returned.

MAYA: or i can meet her when i said i would

Ethan rested his thumb against the edge of the band.

He had already opened the route map.

Maya’s meeting point was twelve minutes from West Market if the transfer timing held. Samir’s practice volume was eighteen minutes away but only nine if he caught the fast inward connector. Aoife’s source call could be taken from anywhere quiet, which meant—

He stopped.

Anywhere quiet was not the same as movable.

He deleted the sentence he had started.

ETHAN: right

AOIFE: screenshotting this growth

ETHAN: don’t

SAMIR: too late

OLLIE: 20:35 possible if test prep closes first cycle

ETHAN: 20:35 then

MAYA: i said no twice

Ethan stared at the message.

He could see the solution.

That was the problem.

Move Maya’s social plan by thirty-five minutes. Ask Samir to leave practice immediately instead of staying for food. Have Aoife take the approval call in transit. Ollie could leave test prep at the first non-critical pause. Everybody would get most of what they wanted.

It was objectively efficient.

It also required four other people to treat their lives as adjustable around the fact that Ethan wanted all five in one place.

He closed the route map.

ETHAN: okay. who has ten minutes between 20:25 and 21:00 anywhere

AOIFE: this is a much less offensive question

SAMIR: 20:28 transfer C if practice ends normal

OLLIE: 20:35-20:47 maybe

MAYA: 20:31 at north food bridge then i leave

AOIFE: 20:30-20:50 north food bridge works if S1 doesn’t hate me

ETHAN: 20:36 north food bridge

SAMIR: why 36

ETHAN: because Ollie

OLLIE: unfair but accurate

MAYA: ten minutes means ten

ETHAN: yes

AOIFE: recording this too

Ethan locked the band.

He had done it.

Almost.

Not dinner.

Not an evening.

Ten minutes on a bridge between other things.

His band buzzed again.

Aoife, direct message.

AOIFE: are you trying to gather everyone because you’re leaving

Ethan read it once.

Then again.

He looked around the study volume.

A local student across from him was eating something from a sealed cup while arguing with a civic-simulation team over whether an emergency allocation counted as precedent. Two seats away, a teenager slept with their forehead on a closed tablet. Someone had clipped a wet jacket to the warm-air edge above the door despite a sign explicitly asking people not to do that.

Vela looked ordinary enough to make the question worse.

ETHAN: leaving where

AOIFE: excellent avoidance

ETHAN: i am literally here

AOIFE: also true

He started to type:

There’s an adult-track orientation.

He stopped.

Deleted it.

ETHAN: see you 20:36

Aoife did not answer for forty seconds.

AOIFE: emotionally suspicious time

Ethan smiled despite himself.

Then the private calendar reminded him about the orientation he had not told her about.

He dismissed the reminder.

* * *

The North Food Bridge was not a bridge in the Earth sense.

It was a broad inhabited connector between two ring volumes with food counters along one wall, seating pockets along the other and a transparent strip underfoot that made newcomers discover whether they had opinions about seeing structural depth beneath them.

Ethan had opinions.

He no longer looked down every time.

At 20:33 he had a table.

At 20:34 he lost half the table to three younger students who claimed the seating marker had reset between bookings.

At 20:35 Maya arrived with half-finished food in one hand and light paint still faintly visible near her hairline from Vela Night.

“You said ten minutes.”

“It has been zero.”

“I am establishing conditions.”

“You sound like Ollie.”

“That is cruel.”

She sat.

“Lea says you schedule friendship.”

Ethan looked at her.

“You discussed me with Lea?”

“We discuss many public problems.”

“I’m a public problem?”

“Growing.”

At 20:36 Aoife arrived exactly on time, which meant she had done it deliberately.

She put her recorder case on the seat beside Ethan without asking and stole one piece of Maya’s food.

Maya slapped her hand too late.

“That was mine.”

“It was socially available.”

“That is not a thing.”

“It is now.”

Ethan shifted the recorder case onto his lap so Samir would have somewhere to sit.

Aoife noticed.

She did not comment.

At 20:38 Samir appeared in training clothes with his hair still damp from the wash cycle and a strip of adhesive tape around two fingers.

“You’re injured,” Ethan said.

“No.”

“You have tape.”

“So does the equipment.”

“That isn’t reassuring.”

Samir sat.

Maya pointed at the tape.

“Did you win?”

“No.”

“Then the tape was wasted.”

“I’ll tell the finger.”

At 20:41 Ollie still had not arrived.

Ethan checked the route board.

Aoife said, “Don’t.”

“I’m checking transit.”

“You’re checking Ollie.”

“His transfer is late.”

“Then it is late whether you stare at it or not.”

Ethan closed the board.

Maya smiled.

“This is becoming educational.”

“I preferred all of you before personal growth became entertainment.”

Samir said, “No, you didn’t.”

Ethan looked at him.

Samir drank from his team bottle.

“Fair.”

For four minutes they were almost the five from home.

Maya complained that one of Vela’s clothing fabrication systems had interpreted “loose” as “structurally irresponsible.” Samir said his team had decided he needed a local nickname and rejected all of his suggestions because he had not made any. Aoife described an argument with Source S1 about whether a broken snack dispenser could be filmed from an angle that made it look “more broken.”

“You’re making documentary art about food machines now?” Maya asked.

“People care about the food machine.”

“People cared about the light routes.”

“Everyone filmed those.”

“You’re becoming difficult on purpose.”

“That is called authorship.”

Ethan laughed.

His band vibrated.

ADULT PATHWAY ORIENTATION — ATTENDANCE SLOT HELD UNTIL 23:00.

He turned his wrist inward.

Aoife was talking to Samir and did not see.

Or pretended not to.

At 20:45 Ollie arrived.

He looked tired and pleased in a way Ethan recognized from years of maker rooms: something had worked.

Ethan stood before he meant to.

“How did the test prep go?”

“Good.”

“Good meaning good or good meaning you found a new problem?”

“Both.”

“Of course.”

Ollie sat on the edge of the remaining seat.

Ethan grinned.

“Seriously. You’re doing actual commissioning work now.”

Ollie looked at him.

“Yes.”

“You wanted this.”

“I know.”

“You earned it.”

The words came out naturally.

For one second Ollie’s expression did something Ethan could not read.

Then it was gone.

“Thanks.”

Ethan heard himself a beat late.

It had sounded like approval.

Not congratulations.

Approval.

He almost corrected it.

Ollie had already opened his food pack.

Ethan let it go.

At 20:47 Maya stood.

“Time.”

“We just got Ollie.”

“And I told Lea I’d be there.”

“We have had six minutes.”

“You had me for twelve.”

“That is not how group time works.”

Maya laughed.

“It absolutely is now.”

She leaned over, stole the piece of food Aoife had stolen from her, and walked away eating it.

Aoife said, “Justice.”

Maya turned while still walking.

“Do not schedule the friendship.”

Ethan closed his mouth.

She disappeared into the crossing crowd.

At 20:49 Samir’s band pulsed.

He checked it.

“Team food.”

“You already ate,” Ethan said.

Samir looked at him.

“Different people.”

Then he seemed to realize how that sounded.

Ethan did too.

Neither repaired it.

Samir stood.

“I’ll see you tomorrow.”

“Probably,” Ollie said.

Samir pointed at him.

“Your disease is spreading.”

He left.

Aoife watched him merge into a group wearing the same practice markers.

“He looks happy.”

Ethan said, “He didn’t even ask whether we were doing anything after.”

Aoife turned toward him.

“He was doing something after.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

He looked at her.

She was not accusing him.

That made it harder to answer.

Ollie finished half his food in less than a minute.

His band flashed.

He checked it and stood.

“You too?” Ethan asked.

“Second cycle moved earlier.”

“You said 20:47.”

“I said maybe.”

Aoife said, “He did.”

Ethan looked between them.

“This is why civilization invented fixed times.”

“Civilization invented shifts,” Ollie said. “Different problem.”

He packed the rest of the food.

Ethan wanted to ask whether the second cycle could wait.

The question made it as far as his mouth.

He stopped it there.

“Good luck.”

Ollie nodded once.

Then he was gone.

At 20:52 Ethan and Aoife were sitting at a table meant for five.

Three younger students still occupied the other half.

One of them had built a card structure out of empty food sleeves and was trying to make it survive the vibration from passing transit.

Aoife pushed the recorder case toward Ethan.

“You can put that down.”

He had forgotten it was still on his lap.

He set it between them.

“That was successful,” he said.

“Extremely.”

“Everyone came.”

“For different quantities of came.”

“We had all five.”

“For six minutes.”

“Six is not zero.”

Aoife smiled.

“No. It isn’t.”

He expected the smile to make him feel better.

It did, slightly.

Around them, the bridge kept functioning without regard for whether five friends had managed to occupy the same table.

A food counter changed service mode. A school group left. Two workers in maintenance markings took their seats. Someone farther down the bridge shouted because a fabrication pickup had gone to the wrong side of the ring. The three younger students’ card structure survived one transit vibration and collapsed on the next.

Nobody was waiting for the group to become a group again.

Ethan’s band vibrated.

23:00 HOLD EXPIRY approaching.

Aoife’s eyes flicked toward his wrist.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That is statistically unlikely.”

He almost told her.

The sentence was ready.

There’s an adult-track orientation.

He could already predict the conversation after that.

She would ask whether he wanted it.

He did not know.

She would ask whether he was going.

He did not know.

She might say it was good.

He locked the screen before he could predict anything else.

Ethan stood.

“I have to confirm something.”

Aoife leaned back.

“Secret civic simulation?”

“No.”

“Secret second dinner?”

“No.”

“Secret adulthood?”

He looked at her.

Aoife’s expression changed.

Only slightly.

She had guessed too close by accident.

Ethan picked up the recorder case and handed it to her.

“I’ll see you tomorrow.”

“Ethan.”

He stopped.

She could have asked.

She did not.

“Thanks for carrying this.”

He nodded.

“Any time.”

He left before the phrase acquired more meaning than he could manage.

On the transfer down, he opened the adult-path notice again.

The orientation slot had seventeen minutes left on hold.

He pressed CONFIRM.

The system accepted him immediately.

No appeal.

No celebration.

No one else knew.

For the first time since arriving in Vela, Ethan had a future appointment that belonged only to him.

He should have felt more independent.

Instead he looked at the group chat.

MAYA: lea says your bridge food is terrible

SAMIR: correct

OLLIE: inaccurate. depends on counter

AOIFE: ethan has left the jurisdiction

MAYA: deported?

AOIFE: emotionally pending

Ethan typed:

I’m still here.

He read it.

Then sent it.
