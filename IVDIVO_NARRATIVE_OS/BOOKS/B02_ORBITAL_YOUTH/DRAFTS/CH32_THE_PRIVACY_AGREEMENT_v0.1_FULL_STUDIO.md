# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 32 — THE PRIVACY AGREEMENT

Lea was carrying three route markers, a coil of temporary floor tape and a grudge.

Maya recognized the first two immediately.

The third had lasted twelve days.

“Can I help?” Maya asked.

Lea shifted the markers higher against her shoulder.

“You are already standing in the doorway.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“It is preventing me from leaving.”

Maya moved.

Lea walked past her into the migration-support corridor.

Maya followed.

“Still helping.”

“You have not done anything.”

“I relocated myself.”

“Historic.”

That was better than being ignored.

Not much better.

The corridor outside the temporary route station had changed three times since morning. Two household moves had been pushed later. One service crossing was restricted until the next pressure-cycle check. Someone had put an orange arrow over a blue arrow and then written NOT THIS ONE under both.

Lea stopped beneath it.

“This is why people hate signs.”

Maya reached up and peeled the handwritten strip loose.

“People hate bad signs.”

“People also make bad signs.”

“Then we need better people.”

Lea looked at her.

“You are not allowed to solve civilization before lunch.”

“Is that a local rule?”

“No. Personal.”

They reached the route station.

Lea put the markers down.

Maya waited.

Lea began sorting them by destination.

Maya waited longer.

This part was terrible.

Usually if Maya wanted a conversation, she started one before the other person realized they were participating.

That had not been working particularly well lately.

“About Vela Night,” she said.

Lea kept sorting.

“Very old news.”

“Not apparently.”

“No.”

Maya looked at the markers.

One had a cracked clip.

She almost picked it up just to have something to do with her hands.

Did not.

“I made you the person who knew where I actually was while my host had the wrong version.”

Lea stopped sorting.

Maya continued before she could improve the sentence into something safer.

“You did not agree to that. I made your answer matter if anybody asked, and I did not ask whether you wanted that responsibility.”

Lea looked at her now.

Maya felt the old reflex arrive.

But I needed privacy.

But nothing happened.

But the host was overreacting.

She let all three die privately.

“I am sorry for that,” she said.

Lea leaned against the counter.

“Are you apologizing because you have the host meeting tonight?”

Maya hated that this was a reasonable question.

“Yes.”

Lea’s face closed.

Maya lifted a hand.

“No. Not only because of that.”

“That was fast.”

“Because it sounded worse out loud.”

“It sounded honest.”

“That is becoming inconvenient.”

Lea said nothing.

Maya exhaled.

“I want the host meeting to go well. I want late access back without being tracked. That is true.”

“Also true.”

“And I am still sorry I made you the backup story.”

Lea looked down at the route markers.

“If you lie again, do not make me the person who knows.”

“I won’t.”

“I mean it.”

“I heard you.”

“That is not the same as agreeing.”

Maya almost answered automatically.

Then stopped.

“Agreed.”

Lea nodded once.

No smile.

No hug.

No restoration ceremony.

She picked up the cracked marker.

“Can you take this to Zone Four support?”

Maya took it.

“That is trust.”

“That is transport.”

“Cruel.”

“Efficient.”

Maya left before she could ruin it.

* * *

The host household had moved dinner twenty minutes later because one member was still on migration support.

Maya considered this favorable.

Negotiations conducted around food were less likely to become historic documents.

Her host had placed a shared agreement screen at one end of the table.

Maya sat opposite it.

“No live location,” she said.

Her host put down a fork.

“We have not asked for live location.”

“I am establishing the boundary.”

“You established it last time.”

“Yes.”

“And then gave us false information.”

Maya looked at the food.

“Misleading information.”

Her host’s expression changed.

Maya closed her eyes briefly.

“False enough.”

“Better.”

“I dislike this process.”

“I am aware.”

Maya pulled the proposed agreement toward herself.

“Okay. Actual version.”

Her host waited.

“Truthful zone.”

“Meaning?”

“If I say Zone Three, I am in Zone Three. If I move to Four and Four is permitted, I update Four.”

“Before or after?”

“After I know the plan changed. Not after I am already home.”

Her host nodded.

“Contact?”

“Truthful local contact. Whoever is actually with me or responsible for the event, not whoever makes the agreement look easiest.”

“That sentence has experience in it.”

“Unfortunately.”

“Return?”

“Actual return condition. Last transfer I intend to use, or approved sleep backup if that becomes the plan.”

“And if the transfer changes?”

“I update it.”

“And if it changes twice?”

Maya frowned.

“Then it changed twice.”

Her host waited.

Maya pointed at the screen.

“I update twice.”

“Good.”

“That was not difficult.”

“You looked offended by the second update.”

“I was offended by reality becoming repetitive.”

A younger household member entered, took a drink and glanced at the screen.

“Privacy summit?”

“No,” Maya and the host said together.

The younger member left immediately.

Maya looked at her host.

“That was embarrassing.”

“For both of us.”

“Useful common ground.”

Her host scrolled.

“If Lea leaves?”

Maya’s hand stopped on the edge of the table.

“Lea is not automatically my cover anymore.”

“I did not say cover.”

“I know what you meant.”

“Do you?”

Maya corrected herself.

“If my registered contact leaves, I name the actual new contact or I activate the backup plan.”

“And if nobody agrees to be contact?”

“Then I do not invent one.”

“That answer I like.”

“Please do not sound pleased.”

“I will struggle.”

Maya read the next line.

NO CONTINUOUS LOCATION STREAM.

She looked up.

“That stays.”

“Yes.”

NO SCHEDULED HOURLY CHECK-IN IF AGREED CONDITIONS REMAIN TRUE.

“That also stays.”

“Yes.”

NO PHOTO PROOF / LIVE SOCIAL CONTACT LIST.

Maya looked at her host.

“You added that?”

“You were going to.”

“I was.”

“I saved time.”

Maya tried not to smile.

“Fine.”

Her host swiped to the trial period.

“Through the migration week.”

“Too short.”

“It is a trial.”

“Seven days is barely a personality.”

“Seven days is enough to see whether you send truthful updates when nothing dramatic forces you to.”

Maya opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Her host noticed.

“That was almost mature.”

“Do not ruin it.”

The agreement continued.

If Maya deliberately supplied false zone/contact/return information, late-mobility permission reverted to supervised review.

If the plan merely changed, she updated the changed field.

If an environmental or route warning made the current return condition invalid, she updated the return condition or activated backup.

If nothing changed, she did not have to announce that nothing changed.

Maya read that line twice.

“This is what I wanted.”

Her host said, “Mostly.”

“What part is not?”

“The part where you wanted privacy to include letting other people discover later that the plan had changed.”

Maya looked at the screen.

“Not anymore.”

Her host did not praise her.

That helped.

They both confirmed the trial.

The screen collapsed.

Dinner continued.

For almost four minutes nobody discussed Maya’s location.

Then her family thread buzzed.

FAMILY: are you still going out tonight

Maya stared at it.

Her host took another bite.

“Different jurisdiction.”

“Much worse jurisdiction.”

She answered her family.

* * *

At 22:41 Maya was in Zone Four, exactly where she had said she would be, helping Lea move late-route notices from one migration board to another because the official update had arrived faster than the floor crew.

Davi was there too.

This had not influenced Maya’s willingness to volunteer.

Probably.

“You put that one upside down,” Davi said.

“It is readable from the other direction.”

“There is no other direction.”

“There could be.”

Lea took the marker from Maya and turned it around.

“Civilization saved.”

Maya checked the time.

Her planned return was 23:26 through Transfer C.

At 22:47, the route board changed.

TRANSFER C — LATE SERVICE HOLD

REVISED CONNECTION: 23:38 VIA E / F

Maya looked at it.

Lea looked at Maya.

Davi looked between them.

“What?” he asked.

“Nothing,” Maya said.

Lea raised one eyebrow.

Maya took out her device.

The old impulse appeared with surprising speed.

It is only twelve minutes.

Same zone.

Same people.

Same destination.

No reason to create a conversation.

She opened the host thread.

MAYA: route changed. still Zone 4. Lea + Davi here. return now 23:38 via E/F. sleep backup unchanged.

She sent it.

Put the device away.

Davi said, “That looked painful.”

“Administrative injury.”

Lea went back to the board.

Maya’s device buzzed once.

HOST: received

That was it.

No question.

No permission.

No lecture.

Maya stared at the word longer than it deserved.

Lea saw.

“Did they send police?”

“No.”

“Tragic.”

“They said received.”

“Cruel.”

“I know.”

Lea looked at her.

Maya corrected herself.

“Efficient.”

“Better.”

Maya shoved her lightly with one shoulder.

Lea shoved back.

At 23:02 another route notice changed—not Maya’s return, but a migration support path between Three and the temporary storage junction.

Maya read it first.

“Wait.”

She pulled the route sheet closer.

“That closure kills the late cart path.”

Davi said, “Only direct.”

“F still runs.”

“F has the freight hold.”

“Until 23:20.”

Lea checked.

“Then it clears.”

Maya traced the alternate path with one finger.

“Storage to E, hold six minutes, F after freight release, then residential Three.”

A migration-support worker came over.

“You three know the late routes?”

Lea pointed at Maya.

“Planet has been obsessing over them.”

“I have been using them.”

“Same result.”

The worker handed Maya a route board.

“Can you mark the temporary cart path and bring it to the junction? Staff will confirm before it goes live.”

Maya took it.

“Do I get authority?”

“No.”

“Uniform?”

“No.”

“Food?”

“Possibly.”

“Accepted.”

She started toward the junction.

Davi called after her.

“Your return route?”

Maya checked the time.

Still valid.

She did not message anyone.

Nothing had changed.

At 23:38, she took E/F home.

The route worked.

The host household was mostly quiet when she arrived.

Nobody congratulated her for coming back where she said she would.

Nobody should have.

The next morning, Maya was assigned to late-route support for migration peak.

Move day began at 06:30.
