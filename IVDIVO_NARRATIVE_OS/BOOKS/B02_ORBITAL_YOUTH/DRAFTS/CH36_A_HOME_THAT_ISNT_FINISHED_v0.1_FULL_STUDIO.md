# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 36 — A HOME THAT ISN'T FINISHED

Four days after the safe-state event, Ollie's unrestricted Ring Six access was still suspended.

His band said so before it said anything else.

`REVIEW HOLD — UNRESTRICTED COMMISSIONING ACCESS NOT RESTORED.`

Under it was a new line.

`AUTHORIZED — R3 RECOVERY REDESIGN / REVIEW CONTRIBUTOR.`

`TEST-RIG ACCESS: READ ONLY.`

`ROUTE-SIDE REVIEW: SUPERVISED.`

Ollie read the new authorization twice.

Iri looked over his shoulder.

“Are you waiting for it to become larger?”

“No.”

“You have been reading it for twenty seconds.”

“I am checking the scope.”

“You checked it yesterday.”

“It could change.”

“Did it?”

“No.”

“Excellent work.”

They were standing beside a service junction outside Segment Three, where the redesigned operating profile had turned one ordinary maintenance crossing into a collection of temporary marks, test tags and removable sensors.

The ring was occupied again under a conservative inbound procedure. Normal transition commissioning remained suspended. R3-S had survived the first review but not unchanged: the manual contingency stayed available only during defined residual-recovery conditions, and the automatic state-aware controller remained a test-rig problem.

Ollie did not own any of that work.

There were three adult engineering teams, one safety review group, service staff, housing, resident representatives and a test schedule that already extended beyond the end of the Youth Cohort placement.

Iri handed him a route marker.

“Walk it.”

“What am I testing?”

“Whether the new sensor position is stupid.”

“That is not a test definition.”

“It is for me.”

Ollie followed the service path from the junction toward the recovery monitor. A trolley turned the corner behind him. He moved aside, watched its front wheel clear the wall and then watched the operator twist sideways to avoid the temporary sensor housing.

“Too far out,” Ollie said.

“Yes.”

“We can move it twelve centimeters inward.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Maintenance panel.”

Ollie crouched. The access seam was almost invisible behind the temporary mount.

“Then above.”

“Worker reach.”

“Below?”

“Cleaning machine.”

Ollie looked at Iri.

“You already knew all three.”

“Yes.”

“Then why did you ask me?”

“You notice different bad ideas.”

Ollie stood.

He looked at the route again instead of the sensor.

The trolley operator came back through from the other direction.

“Can you take that turn at normal load?” Ollie asked.

“With that thing there? Yes. I hate it, but yes.”

“What do you hate?”

The operator pointed with one hand.

“It makes me look at the wall instead of the people coming out of the side route.”

Ollie followed the sightline.

The sensor was not blocking the trolley.

It was blocking attention.

He opened the redesign note.

`POSITION B — ACCEPTABLE CLEARANCE / BAD SIDE-ROUTE VISIBILITY.`

Iri read it.

“Useful.”

Ollie added, “Try the housing above the route sign. Same physical read, no panel conflict, no operator sightline loss.”

“That is more work.”

“Yes.”

“I did not ask for more work.”

“You asked whether the position was stupid.”

Iri considered that.

“Fair.”

They walked toward the next junction.

On Ollie's band, the redesign task list continued for another nineteen items.

At the bottom was tomorrow's test-rig session.

Iri said, “I need you on the route-side comparison at twenty-one hundred tonight.”

Ollie checked his calendar.

“No.”

Iri stopped.

Ollie almost corrected himself into a longer answer.

“I can do tomorrow morning. Tonight I have dinner.”

“With the Earth group?”

“Yes.”

Iri looked at the task list.

“It is not urgent.”

“I know.”

“Then why did you look guilty?”

“I did not.”

Iri started walking again.

“Tomorrow at eight forty.”

“That works.”

“Do not arrive at eight.”

“I was not going to.”

“You were thinking about it.”

Ollie looked at the next sensor mount.

It was definitely too low.

* * *

Household 3-177 entered their room at 15:18 that afternoon.

The corrected inbound procedure was slower than the one they had been promised before the safe-state event. One household at a time through the Segment Three approach. Deep recovery hold after each defined service transition. No mixed resident/service release during the handoff window.

Housing hated the throughput.

Safety approved it.

The room credential worked on the first try.

The teenager went in first and immediately stopped.

“Where is the food box?”

His parent looked behind him.

“What food box?”

“The one we had at staging.”

“The one you put down?”

“I did not put it down.”

“You absolutely put it down.”

A housing runner checked the handoff inventory.

“Cold storage transferred it yesterday. Locker C-18.”

The teenager looked at the room.

“Where is C-18?”

“Outside the segment.”

He stared at her.

“So we finally moved into the room and dinner did not.”

“Yes.”

“That is impressive.”

The parent dropped a bag beside the sleeping module.

“School case?”

“Already here.”

“Laundry?”

The runner checked again.

“Your booking is still attached to the temporary address.”

The parent closed both eyes.

“Of course it is.”

“We can fix that.”

“Today?”

“Yes.”

“Then fix that before you find dinner.”

The teenager objected.

Nobody asked him.

Ollie saw them only because his route-side review took him past the open door thirty minutes later.

The teenager was on the floor trying to fit a school case into a storage slot that was three centimeters too narrow.

The parent was on a call with laundry services.

The second adult was moving a folding table for the third time.

They noticed Ollie in the corridor.

The parent paused.

Ollie stopped too.

Neither of them said anything for a second.

Then the parent gave him a small nod and returned to the laundry call.

The teenager looked up.

“Your ring has bad storage.”

Ollie looked at the school case.

“That part is not my ring.”

“Convenient.”

“Very.”

The teenager pushed the case under the sleeping platform instead.

It fit.

Ollie kept walking.

At the next junction, Maya was carrying a sealed school bag toward the transfer desk.

“Lea?” he asked.

“Yes.”

“Moving?”

“Not all of them. Fourth segment review is still next week.”

“That is bad.”

Maya shifted the bag to her other shoulder.

“She knows.”

“Are you helping?”

“I am carrying a bag.”

Ollie waited.

Maya pointed at him.

“Do not make that face.”

“What face?”

“The one where you expect me to explain personal growth.”

“I did not.”

“Good.”

Her band buzzed.

She checked it.

`LEA: desk not room. just the bag.`

Maya typed:

`MAYA: cruel reminder`

Then:

`MAYA: desk. bag. nothing else.`

She put the band away.

“Dinner tonight?” Ollie asked.

“If Ethan stops changing the time.”

“He has changed it twice.”

“Three times.”

“That was Samir.”

“Still counts.”

Maya left for the desk.

Across the corridor, another household was still on the waiting list.

Their room compatibility had not changed because one safe opening had gone well.

The housing board showed a date for their next review.

Not a promise.

A date.

* * *

The first dinner time was 19:30.

Samir rejected it because Crossband practice ended at 19:40 on the other side of Vela.

The second time was 20:10.

Maya rejected that because Lea's school bag had been sent to the wrong transfer desk and she had agreed to move exactly one bag and nothing else.

The third time was 20:30.

Aoife wrote:

`EXPORT RUNNING. 20:45 OR I ARRIVE WITH A COMPUTER AND HATE ALL OF YOU.`

Ethan opened a shared scheduling block.

Then closed it.

The group message appeared instead.

`ETHAN: when is everyone actually free?`

Samir answered first.

`SAMIR: 21:15 if transport behaves`

`MAYA: 21:10`

`AOIFE: 20:45 unless export decides violence`

Ollie checked the redesign message Iri had sent him.

`ROUTE-SIDE TEST CAN WAIT. TOMORROW 08:40.`

He replied:

`21:00 onward`

Ethan sent:

`21:20 food court south inner band`

Maya:

`that place is terrible`

Ethan:

`it is open`

Maya:

`different claim`

They arrived between 21:17 and 21:38.

Samir came last with a Crossband bag over one shoulder and two fresh strips of skin seal across his right hand.

Maya pointed at them.

“What happened?”

“Wall.”

“Did it win?”

“Yes.”

Samir sat down.

The food was, as Maya had predicted, terrible.

Ollie had something shaped like noodles that had been designed to survive three different gravity settings and had therefore lost any commitment to texture.

Aoife arrived with her recorder in her bag and her editing slate in her hand.

“Finished?” Ethan asked.

“Exported.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“It is tonight.”

“What is it called?” Maya asked.

Aoife hesitated.

“Waiting Routes.”

Samir took a drink.

“That sounds serious.”

“It is six minutes.”

“Very serious.”

Aoife put the slate on the table.

The final piece was smaller than the one she had imagined when Ring Six first began opening. No dramatic private accommodation details. No hidden identity reveal. No claim that one resident represented the whole housing problem.

The source line was there once, under the agreed context: waiting meant living on somebody else's timetable.

Aoife had put it between laundry access and a child trying to move a stuffed animal in an overfilled bag.

Maya watched the first thirty seconds.

“You kept the ugly route sign.”

“It was real.”

“You filmed my sign?”

“It was public infrastructure.”

“I designed the correction.”

“Nadia designed the correction.”

“I emotionally designed it.”

Aoife took the slate back.

“Absolutely not credited.”

Ethan laughed.

Aoife looked at him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You have an intake packet.”

Ethan had left it half visible under his tray.

Samir pulled it out before Ethan could stop him.

`ADULT TRACK — LATER INTAKE / SERVICE ACCESS DEPENDENCY REVIEW`

Samir looked at him.

“Not Ring Three.”

“No.”

“Not the placement you lost.”

“No.”

“Do you hate it?”

“I have not started it.”

“That was not the question.”

Ethan took the packet back.

“I hate the start time.”

Aoife looked.

“Six forty-five?”

“Six forty.”

“That is worse.”

“Thank you.”

“Is the work good?” Ollie asked.

Ethan shrugged.

“Service access dependencies on Ring Two. Less interesting than the first placement. More responsibility than observation. I meet the team tomorrow.”

Maya stole one of his fries.

“So complain tomorrow.”

“I am complaining now.”

“Then you are ready for adulthood.”

Ethan pointed at her.

“That sounded thematic.”

“It was theft.”

She took another fry.

Samir's band buzzed with the next Crossband practice assignment.

Ollie saw him read it and grimace.

“Early?”

“Saturday match moved.”

“To when?”

“Morning.”

Maya smiled.

“Excellent.”

“For you.”

“Can we come?”

Samir looked suspicious.

“To watch?”

“Yes.”

“No sensors.”

Aoife raised both hands.

“I did not say anything.”

“You own a recorder.”

“It is in the bag.”

“Keep it there.”

“Fine.”

Samir sent them the public match time.

The conversation moved to whether Maya had actually learned the South Span route or had simply become confident enough to be wrong faster.

Her host channel buzzed once.

She glanced at it and sent a two-line update.

`STILL SOUTH INNER BAND. LEAVING AFTER FOOD. RETURN CONDITION UNCHANGED.`

No one at the table discussed it.

Ollie's band buzzed next.

Iri:

`FOUND ANOTHER BAD SENSOR POSITION. NOT URGENT.`

There was a photo.

Ollie opened it.

It was terrible.

He started typing.

Then Samir pushed the noodle bowl toward him.

“You are not eating that?”

“I am trying.”

“That was not what I asked.”

Ollie looked at the photo again.

He locked his band and put it face down beside the bowl.

“I will answer tomorrow.”

Samir took the noodles.

“That was my plan.”

By 22:03, Aoife and Maya were arguing about whether the local film needed subtitles for one nonhuman speaker, Ethan was trying not to reorganize Saturday around Samir's match, and Samir had eaten half of Ollie's dinner.

Nobody had arrived at the original time.

Nobody had come from the same part of the station.

At 22:11, they ordered more food anyway.

* * *

The historical crosscheck returned at 02:14 the next morning.

It did not go to the Youth Cohort.

It entered the Ring Six post-incident archive queue as a low-confidence structural comparison.

`SOURCE: EARTH / PRE-CONFEDERATION`

`ARCHIVE CLASS: LEGACY SECURITY / PARTIAL ACCESS`

`SIMILARITY: RELATIONAL / NON-CAUSAL`

The attached scan was old enough that the line work had warped around the fold.

Three linked loops were drawn around a central return path. The labels used none of the Ring Six terminology.

In the margin, somebody had written by hand:

`CLEAR INDICATION PRECEDES RETURN CLEAR.`

Below it was a second note.

`DO NOT RELEASE NEXT PATH ON INDICATOR ALONE.`

The modern crosscheck had highlighted the relation, not the words.

No source explanation was attached.

No cause was proposed.

The ownership field read:

`OLD EARTH SECURITY ARCHIVE`

The incident-review system sent a routine context request.

`STATUS: PENDING OWNER RESPONSE`
