ORBITAL YOUTH
Chapter 16 — THE INFERENCE
Aoife published the smaller version at 18:12.

It felt wrong because nothing happened.

No surge.
No flood of messages.
No argument.

The platform registered the upload, verified the source-approval note attached to the excerpt and placed it into the same public feed where hundreds of other student, local and institutional pieces were appearing that evening.

For forty-three seconds, Aoife stared at the view count.

Then she became disgusted with herself and closed it.

She reopened it nine seconds later.

Twenty-three views.

One reaction to the broken dispenser.
Two people asking whether the protein squares were actually bad.
A local account correcting the service route name in a caption even though Aoife had used the public route label exactly as displayed.

Normal.

Good.

The excerpt opened on the food machine.

The hand-written sign.
The stack of unwanted squares.
The maintenance worker saying, “Oh, now you become interesting.”

Then the source.

No face.
No name.
No school.
Hands on the table edge.
A blurred service wall.
A voice complaining that a forty-minute trip for a two-hour food-service shift was absurd and that Ring Six would not magically repair every commute.

The line about the cupboard stayed.

“It has been fixed three times.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is if you know the cupboard.”

Aoife still liked that exchange best.

The source had approved it.

Everything explicit had been removed or retained exactly as agreed.

At 18:19 the source sent:

S1: machine still funny

AOIFE: high praise

S1: you cut the part where I sounded intelligent

AOIFE: at your request

S1: censorship

AOIFE: documented censorship

S1: worse

Aoife smiled.

Then sent the approval copy again with a marker showing the published segment.

AOIFE: last chance to claim I substituted a different person

S1: hands look familiar

AOIFE: dangerous evidence

S1: fine

Aoife put the band down.

She was in a public study pocket between two transit corridors, technically doing Earth coursework and actually watching the clip accumulate seventy-six views.

Ethan sat opposite her with his own tablet.

He had not mentioned the secret thing on his calendar.

Aoife had not asked.

This arrangement was going badly for both of them.

“What?” he said.

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You looked at me.”

“I have eyes.”

“You used them accusatorily.”

“That is not measurable.”

“Your face has a high-confidence mode.”

“Everyone in this city has become obsessed with faces.”

He returned to his work.

Aoife checked the clip again.

One hundred and twelve.

A specialist environmental-design account had reposted it.

Not a famous account.

Not even large.

The profile belonged to a postgraduate systems group based between two orbital habitats and one Earth university. Most of its recent posts were about humidity control in mixed-body public environments, sensor placement and why average-comfort settings produced terrible public design.

Their repost began:

Interesting background detail at 00:54.

Aoife opened it.

A still image appeared.

The source’s hands.
The table.
The structural rib.
The small angled corner of the humidity-buffer marker.

A red circle had been drawn around the corner.

Aoife’s stomach tightened before she knew why.

The caption continued:

Looks like service marking from a buffered accommodation corridor rather than generic food-service support. Anyone local confirm class?

Aoife read it twice.

Ethan looked up.

“What happened?”

“Nothing yet.”

“That is not your nothing face.”

“Stop.”

He stopped.

Immediately.

Aoife noticed because she had expected him not to.

She expanded the discussion.

Three replies.

One person said the marker could belong to several service environments.

Another said the angle and line width matched a narrow humidity-buffer class used near respiratory accommodation routes in mature Vela rings.

The third said:

Audio also has a support coupling tone at 00:58. Very faint. If same source, probably not random corridor marker.

Aoife’s fingers went cold.

She opened the excerpt timeline.

00:58.

The source said the word shift.

Under it:

ventilation.
Table contact.
A low single tone.

Not the two-note sound Aoife had removed.

Smaller.

Ordinary.

She had heard it a hundred times in the edit without hearing it.

Ethan said, “Aoife.”

She raised one finger.

He closed his mouth.

The specialist account added another frame.

This one was not from the source shot.

It was from the public transfer-board sequence eleven seconds earlier in the documentary.

The timestamp was visible.

The route delay was visible.

The caption read:

If sequence is continuous, source interview location must be within one transfer interval of this board after 16:xx. Narrows likely service alcoves significantly.

Somebody replied with a route map.

Not private data.

Public transit.
Public service locations.
Public environmental zoning.

Three candidate alcoves.

One of them matched.

Aoife opened the original clip and looked at the blurred wall.

The wall had no name.

No room number.

No route marker.

It did not need one anymore.

The discussion continued.

Someone wrote:

Young food-service worker + this accommodation corridor + support tone + planned Ring Six work move probably places source in one of two placement bands.

Another:

So much for “ordinary healthy orbital youth.” They edit the support infrastructure out and then tell Earth everything is normal.

Aoife stared at that one.

Wrong.

Not completely wrong about the support existing.

Wrong about why it had been removed.

Wrong about what it meant.

Wrong about the source.

Wrong in exactly the way the source had predicted people would be wrong.

Aoife opened the source agreement.

BODY / MEDICAL / SUPPORT DETAIL: NO.

She had complied.

The line looked absurdly small now.

Ethan said, very carefully, “Can I ask?”

Aoife turned the screen toward him.

He read.

His face changed.

Not into problem-solving yet.

Good.

He scrolled through the inference chain once.

“None of this is from something they said.”

“No.”

“The marker is public infrastructure.”

“Yes.”

“The tone is in the audio.”

“Yes.”

“The route time is public.”

“Yes.”

He looked at her.

Aoife said, “I removed the support sound.”

“You removed one.”

“Yes.”

“And they approved the frame.”

“Yes.”

Ethan did not say the obvious next sentence.

Aoife almost wanted him to.

It would have given her something to fight.

Instead he said, “Do they know?”

Aoife’s band vibrated.

S1: what is this

A link followed.

The specialist repost.

Then:

S1: take it down

Aoife deleted the public excerpt.

She did it before answering.

The platform confirmed removal.

The repost remained.

Aoife opened it.

The account had preserved two stills and a twelve-second quoted segment.

She requested removal through the platform’s source-privacy process.

The system asked whether the material had originally been published with authorization.

Aoife selected YES.

Then whether the complaint concerned subsequent inference or recontextualization.

YES.

Then whether the source faced immediate physical danger.

Aoife stopped.

She did not know.

She messaged:

AOIFE: original is down. repost still exists. are you physically unsafe / need immediate intervention?

The reply took twenty-two seconds.

S1: no

Then:

S1: I have people asking me whether I can go to Earth

Aoife closed her eyes.

Exactly the question.

Exactly the one the source had told her they hated.

AOIFE: I’m sorry

Three dots appeared.

Stopped.

Appeared again.

S1: you said you removed it

AOIFE: I removed the two-note support sound we marked. there is another low coupling tone under the word shift. neither of us caught it.

S1: and the wall

AOIFE: marker edge stayed in frame. mostly hidden.

S1: I told you not that side

Aoife looked at the frame.

The source had.

Then they had checked the moved frame and said okay.

Aoife typed:

You approved the frame.

She did not send it.

Deleted it.

Typed:

AOIFE: yes

S1: and the route

AOIFE: I removed your exact route. the preceding public transfer shot still had a timestamp. people linked the sequence.

No answer.

Aoife kept writing and erased each version.

I followed the agreement.

You approved the excerpt.

No explicit identifier remained.

Every sentence was true.

None answered the message she had received.

Ethan was still across the table.

He had stopped pretending to do coursework.

Aoife said, “Do not say anything useful.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

“You were.”

“I was going to ask what you need.”

“That is dangerously useful.”

“Sorry.”

She looked at the repost.

It had spread beyond the environmental-design account.

Not far.

Far enough.

An Earth politics account had taken the still and added:

ORBITAL YOUTH DOCUMENTARY ACCIDENTALLY REVEALS HIDDEN DEPENDENCE ON ENVIRONMENTAL SUPPORT.

A Vela account replied:

You have discovered infrastructure. Congratulations.

Another:

This is why Earth visitors should stop turning ordinary support systems into evidence about whether people belong in their own city.

Then somebody else wrote:

Maybe stop pretending all bodies can live everywhere without adaptation.

The argument multiplied.

Nobody knew the source.

Some people were already trying.

Aoife’s platform request returned:

REVIEWING DERIVATIVE PRIVACY CLAIM.

Estimated response: not immediate.

She hated the phrase not immediate.

The source finally replied.

S1: call

Aoife accepted before the second vibration.

No video.

For a moment neither spoke.

Then the source said, “How many copies?”

“I don’t know.”

“Good start.”

“The original is gone. I filed against the quoted clip and stills. The specialist account is the main source of the inference chain.”

“Main source.”

“Yes.”

“So there are others.”

“Some reposts.”

“How many?”

“I said I don’t know.”

The source breathed out hard enough for the microphone to catch it.

Aoife waited.

“What did you leave?” they asked.

“The corner of the humidity-buffer marker.”

“You knew what that was?”

“No.”

“You didn’t ask.”

“You told me not to ask.”

Silence.

Aoife heard herself immediately.

Defensive.

Technically correct.

Useless.

She said, “Sorry. That sounded like I was making it yours.”

The source did not answer.

Aoife continued.

“I didn’t know what the marker identified. I should have treated the fact that you wanted it out as enough reason to remove all of it, not just move the frame until you said okay.”

“You did move it.”

“Yes.”

“I checked it.”

“Yes.”

“Then how was I supposed to know?”

Aoife looked at the specialist thread.

“You weren’t.”

The source went quiet.

That answer did not help either.

They said, “And the sound?”

“One obvious coupling event was removed. There was a quieter single tone earlier. We both missed it.”

“Route?”

“The public board before your clip had a visible time.”

The source swore.

Aoife did not interrupt.

“You said exact route was gone.”

“It was.”

“Do not do that.”

Aoife stopped.

The source’s voice sharpened.

“Do not answer every sentence like you are proving the agreement was technically satisfied.”

Aoife looked down at the table.

“Okay.”

“That is why I trusted you.”

The sentence landed harder than anger.

The source continued.

“You wrote everything down. You turned things off. You asked before using things. You sent me the cut.”

“I know.”

“Then how am I supposed to do this better next time?”

Aoife had no answer.

She said, “I don’t know yet.”

The source laughed once without humor.

“Excellent.”

“I can stop using everything from you.”

“Yes.”

“I already took down the excerpt.”

“Yes.”

“I won’t use any remaining footage or audio without asking again.”

“Do not ask again today.”

“Okay.”

“And do not make a statement about me.”

“I won’t.”

“Do not explain the support system.”

“I won’t.”

“Do not tell people the political interpretation is wrong if correcting it gives them more clues.”

Aoife stopped.

She had been about to do exactly that.

“Okay.”

The source was breathing more evenly now.

Someone spoke near them off-call.

They answered away from the microphone.

Aoife heard food-service noise.

A drawer closing.
Someone asking for a replacement pack.

Ordinary shift life continuing around the worst thing in Aoife’s day.

The source came back.

“I have to work.”

“Right.”

“Do not contact my manager.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

“Good.”

The call ended.

Aoife looked at the dark screen.

Her project access list still showed Source S1 as ACTIVE.

She changed it herself.

SUSPENDED — SOURCE REQUEST.

Then she removed the excerpt from her working cut.

Ninety-two seconds disappeared.

The sequence around it broke.

She left the hole open.

* * *

By the time Aoife left the study pocket, the argument had reached the Youth Cohort channels.

Not the source’s identity.

The fact of the inference.

Someone had posted a comparison image showing how the background marker, coupling tone and public route timing combined.

A local student wrote:

THIS is what we mean when we say “anonymous” is sometimes a visitor word.

Another replied:

Locals infer people too. Stop pretending Earth invented gossip.

A third:

Difference is Earth accounts have millions of bored people.

Someone else:

This account had 600 followers.

Reply:

And now an Earth politics feed has it.

The argument became about scale.

Then intention.

Then whether the source should have expected public inference from public infrastructure.

Then whether that sentence was ridiculous.

Aoife stopped reading.

She entered a food volume and immediately heard her own accent from behind her.

Two Earth students from another temporary cohort were watching the repost on a shared screen.

One said, “I don’t understand what the secret is.”

The other said, “It’s not a secret. It’s like a medical accommodation.”

“Then why care?”

“Because people care.”

“That explains nothing.”

Aoife kept walking.

A local teenager at the next counter saw the documentary badge on her bag.

Their expression changed.

Not recognition.

Category.

“Are you with the Earth media group?”

Aoife stopped.

“No.”

The teenager looked at the recorder case.

Aoife corrected herself.

“I’m in the Youth Cohort. I’m making a documentary.”

“About us?”

The question had no warmth in it.

“About living here.”

The teenager laughed.

“Great.”

They turned back to the counter.

Aoife could have explained.

She did not.

At the far side of the room Maya was sitting with Lea and Davi.

Maya saw Aoife.

Raised one hand.

Then saw her face and lowered it.

Aoife walked over.

Lea’s screen was open to the same inference thread.

Nobody spoke for a second.

Maya said, “You took it down.”

“Yes.”

Lea asked, “After people figured it out.”

“Yes.”

“Did the source approve it?”

“Yes.”

Maya looked relieved.

Aoife hated that.

Lea did not.

“So?” Lea said.

Maya frowned.

“So she didn’t just post private stuff.”

“I didn’t say she did.”

“You’re making it sound like—”

Lea cut across her.

“I’m making it sound like the person still got exposed.”

Maya looked at Aoife.

Aoife said, “They did.”

Maya opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Davi, for once, stayed out of the conversation.

Lea pushed the screen toward Aoife.

The Earth politics caption was still there.

“Is that true?”

“No.”

“Which part?”

Aoife stopped.

If she explained too much, she could make the source easier to identify.

“The claim that the clip proves orbital youth are secretly dependent on hidden support is not supported by what I recorded.”

Lea looked at her.

“That sounded professionally useless.”

“It is as specific as I can be.”

“Because of the source?”

“Yes.”

Lea leaned back.

“Fine.”

Maya said, “So people are just making things up.”

“Some of them.”

“Then say that.”

“I can’t correct every wrong inference without confirming which clues are real.”

Maya looked frustrated.

Aoife understood the feeling.

Lea said, “Welcome to not being able to fix the internet.”

Davi finally spoke.

“I solved that by never being correct online.”

Maya turned on him.

“You are extremely committed to the method.”

“Thank you.”

Nobody laughed enough.

Aoife’s band buzzed.

Ethan.

ETHAN: I drafted three possible response structures and then remembered you would kill me

Aoife stared at the message.

Another arrived.

ETHAN: so I am not sending them

Then:

ETHAN: do you need anything practical

Aoife typed:

AOIFE: no response. no framing. no explanation.

ETHAN: understood

She hesitated.

AOIFE: copies may remain. source access suspended.

ETHAN: okay

AOIFE: that was not a request to solve it

ETHAN: I know

This time she believed him.

She put the band away.

Lea was still watching her.

“What?” Aoife asked.

“Nothing.”

“You have the face.”

Lea almost smiled.

“Everyone says that here now?”

“Unfortunately.”

Aoife sat down.

For the first time since arriving in Vela, she put the recorder case under the table instead of beside her hand.

Nobody asked her to.
