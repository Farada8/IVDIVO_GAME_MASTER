# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 25 — NO INTERVIEW

Samir lost his starting rotation because he tried to stop too quickly.

Kesh told him this before Samir had finished sliding into the outer-band wall.

“You braked like Earth.”

Samir pushed himself off the padded surface.

“I stopped.”

“You arrived at the wall.”

“Also stopped.”

Nera passed behind them on the lower-gravity line, caught the rebound ball one-handed and sent it through the blue gate before Samir had both feet under him.

“Point,” Nera said.

Kesh pointed at the bench rail.

“Two possessions out.”

Samir looked at the score display.

Their side was down three.

“You need me.”

“We need you to stop before the architecture.”

Samir went to the rail.

The Crossband volume was running on a shortened booking because one adjacent public space had been taken for temporary relocation staging. Half the youth teams in their schedule had been compressed into new slots, which meant everyone was playing too fast because nobody wanted to waste a possession discussing anything.

The game did not improve from Samir watching it.

Kesh moved into the middle band and got trapped by two defenders.

Nera cut down toward the inner line.

A younger player called Fen took the ball, panicked at the gravity change and passed too early.

Turnover.

Samir said, “That was not my fault.”

Kesh, still moving, said, “Remarkable contribution.”

The other team scored.

Five down.

Fen looked at the bench.

Samir lifted one hand.

“Next time hold until the second defender commits.”

Kesh heard him.

“Do not coach from punishment.”

“I am communicating.”

“You are suffering publicly. Do it quietly.”

Nera landed beside the inner gate, synthetic feet making almost no sound on the grip surface.

“Can we finish losing before the next booking arrives?”

“We are not losing,” Kesh said.

The score changed again.

Nera looked up.

“We are numerically experiencing something.”

Samir laughed before he meant to.

Kesh saw.

“One more possession.”

“That was already two.”

“It became three when you laughed.”

“That is not a rule.”

“Captain rule.”

“You are not captain.”

Kesh pointed at the captain band around their wrist.

Samir sat back.

“Temporary visual problem.”

The next possession went better.

Fen waited.

The second defender committed.

Fen sent the ball to Nera.

Nera crossed from inner to middle, took the three-count hold and threw high toward Kesh.

A defender overcommitted to Kesh.

The outer lane opened.

Samir saw it from the rail before anyone used it.

He also saw the booking clock.

Nine minutes.

Kesh scored.

Three down.

“Now?” Samir asked.

Kesh gave him a look.

Samir stood anyway.

“Now.”

He entered on the next dead ball.

This time he did not attack the outer band.

He let the first transition happen without him.

The other side expected him to cut high because he had done it three possessions in a row during warm-up.

He went middle instead.

Nera carried through the first gate.

Kesh crossed behind.

Fen reversed.

Two defenders followed the reversal.

Samir waited half a beat.

Then crossed into the outer band after the lane had already changed direction.

The pass reached him cleanly.

He did not brake.

He used the curve, let the higher gravity take speed from him and sent the ball back toward Nera before the defender could reset.

Second gate.

Nera held for three.

Kesh shouted, “Back!”

Samir was already moving.

The return pass came low.

He caught it, crossed the third band and did not touch the wall.

Their bench made more noise about that than the score.

Samir threw the ball through the last gate.

Tie.

Kesh called, “See? Architecture survives.”

Samir was breathing too hard to answer properly.

He raised one finger instead.

The next possession decided the game.

They lost by one.

Fen missed the final gate after doing everything else correctly.

The booking signal sounded before anyone could pretend there was time for another play.

For a second Fen stood still.

Then Nera took the ball from the gate and tossed it to them.

“You missed.”

Fen stared.

“Yes.”

“Good. That saves review time.”

Kesh said, “Nera.”

“What? They know.”

Fen laughed despite trying not to.

Samir walked over.

“You waited correctly on the second defender.”

“I missed.”

“Also true.”

“That does not help.”

“No.”

Fen looked at him.

Samir shrugged.

The next team was already entering the volume.

That ended the conversation more effectively than encouragement.

They collected their grips, wrist bands and one water pack that nobody admitted owning.

The score stayed on the wall for another twelve seconds before the next booking erased it.

They had lost.

Samir wanted the rematch more than he wanted to admit.

That was normal enough to feel good.

* * *

The first incident-review message arrived while Samir was trying to get food.

He ignored it because Kesh had taken the last hot item from the counter and this was a more immediate problem.

“You saw me behind you.”

“Yes.”

“And still took it.”

“Yes.”

“Hostile leadership.”

“Performance-based allocation.”

“You lost too.”

“I lost while wearing the captain band.”

“That improves nothing.”

“It improves documentation.”

Nera appeared with two cold packs and handed one to Samir.

He took it.

“What is this?”

“Food.”

“That answer is spreading.”

“It was cheap.”

“That is not reassuring.”

Nera had already opened theirs.

Samir opened his.

The food was fine.

He resented this slightly because it weakened the complaint.

They sat on the transfer steps outside the sport volume with the rest of the team.

Fen was replaying the last possession on a legal public-angle capture and stopping it exactly before the missed gate.

Kesh said, “If you watch only that frame forever, eventually it becomes a score.”

Fen did not look up.

“I am checking the pass.”

“The pass was good.”

“That is worse.”

Samir’s band vibrated again.

INCIDENT REVIEW — VOLUNTARY COMPARISON FOLLOW-UP.

He opened it this time.

The message referenced the public comparison request he had seen earlier that day.

It did not know about Service Transfer 4.

It asked whether Youth Cohort participants with relevant ordinary-environment observations wished to submit comparison material.

Optional fields included:

PERSONAL BAND TIMELINE.

MOVEMENT / ROUTE CAPTURE.

TEAM OR PEER CORROBORATION.

INTERVIEW AVAILABILITY.

BIOMETRIC / PERSONAL-SUPPORT DATA — OPTIONAL, SOURCE CONTROLLED.

CONTACT / PRIOR PROFILE CONTEXT — OPTIONAL.

Samir stopped reading at the last line.

Nera noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Kesh said, “That means something.”

Samir locked the screen.

“It is review intake.”

“Ring Six?” Fen asked.

“Yes.”

Kesh looked at him.

“You doing it?”

“I do not know.”

Nera ate another bite.

“Does it ask the profile question?”

Samir looked over.

Nera’s expression had not changed.

“Optional.”

“That means yes.”

“Basically.”

Kesh said, “Do you want us to leave?”

The question was so simple Samir almost answered no automatically.

He looked at the team.

Fen had gone back to the failed gate.

Two others were arguing about next week’s compressed booking slots.

Nobody was leaning toward him.

“No,” Samir said.

Kesh nodded.

The conversation returned to Crossband.

Samir unlocked the request.

He declined:

INTERVIEW.

CONTACT / PRIOR PROFILE CONTEXT.

BIOMETRIC / PERSONAL-SUPPORT DATA.

TEAM OR PEER CORROBORATION.

MOVEMENT / ROUTE CAPTURE.

The form left one box open.

RELEVANT OBSERVATION — TEXT ONLY.

Samir thought about Service Transfer 4.

21:38.

Crowd crossing.

Kesh ahead of him.

One person reversing.

Two moving the other way.

Partition held.

Airflow first.

System correction after.

At the time Ollie had asked him to ask the others what they had noticed.

Samir had said no.

He still meant it.

He entered:

SERVICE TRANSFER 4. APPROX 21:38:12. PERSONAL OBSERVATION ONLY. MIXED MOVEMENT / HELD TRANSITION. AIRFLOW CHANGE NOTICED BEFORE VISIBLE SYSTEM CORRECTION. I HAVE MOVEMENT ORDER FROM MEMORY / ORIGINAL NOTE. NO TEAM DATA AUTHORIZED.

The form offered:

REQUEST REVIEW FOLLOW-UP?

Samir selected yes.

A text field opened.

He typed:

WHAT EXACT QUESTION DO YOU NEED ANSWERED?

Then submitted it.

Kesh looked at him.

“Done?”

“No.”

“That sounds like review.”

“Yes.”

“Can we talk about your outer brake now?”

“No.”

“Good. Then review is improving you.”

Samir threw the empty food wrapper at Kesh.

Kesh caught it.

That was annoying.

* * *

The reply came during equipment return.

Samir was signing a grip strip back into the team locker when his band vibrated.

INCIDENT COMPARISON ANALYST — BOUNDED QUESTION.

He opened it.

The analyst had accepted his exclusions.

No interview required.

No teammate contact requested.

No profile context requested.

No raw movement history requested.

The exact question read:

**Can your original Service Transfer 4 observation establish relative order between local movement/door-hold state and the controller’s fixed correction event, using only your own timestamp/order statement plus the transfer controller log?**

Below it:

CONTROLLER LOG CAN BE QUERIED BY REVIEW TEAM. YOU DO NOT NEED ACCESS TO RAW SYSTEM HISTORY.

Samir read it twice.

That was better.

He selected:

YES — BOUNDED RESPONSE.

The analyst asked for the movement order as he remembered it.

Samir did not trust memory enough.

He searched his own old message thread with Ollie.

The messages were still there.

SAMIR: service transfer 4. 21:38. maybe nothing.

SAMIR: airflow changed before system correction.

OLLIE: how long

SAMIR: under ten seconds? not sure

OLLIE: what movement order

Samir had sent the sequence.

He copied only his own messages into the response field.

No Ollie commentary.

No other conversation.

Then he rewrote the order without names.

THREE PEOPLE CROSSED FORWARD.

I CROSSED AFTER THE THIRD.

ONE PERSON REVERSED DIRECTION NEAR THE TRANSITION.

TWO CROSSED OPPOSITE FLOW.

PARTITION / DOOR STATE HELD LONGER THAN NORMAL PASS-THROUGH.

I NOTICED AIRFLOW CHANGE BEFORE VISIBLE CONTROLLER CORRECTION.

PERSONAL TIMESTAMP APPROX 21:38:12.

The form asked:

CONFIDENCE IN ORDER?

Samir selected:

HIGH FOR MOVEMENT ORDER / MODERATE FOR SUBJECTIVE AIRFLOW ONSET / LOW FOR EXACT LEAD INTERVAL.

He liked that field.

The next question:

MAY REVIEW CONTACT IDENTIFIABLE PEERS PRESENT?

NO.

A warning appeared.

PEERS MAY OPT IN INDEPENDENTLY THROUGH PUBLIC INTAKE.

Samir selected:

UNDERSTOOD.

He submitted.

Nera was waiting beside the equipment locker.

“You kept us out?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Samir looked at them.

“That easy?”

Nera shrugged.

“You cannot consent for my morning.”

“No.”

“I cannot consent for yours.”

“No.”

“Excellent system.”

Kesh arrived behind them.

“What system?”

“Consent,” Nera said.

Kesh considered.

“Terrible for team scheduling.”

“Everything is terrible for team scheduling,” Samir said.

“That is true.”

They left the sport volume.

At the next junction the route planner sent Kesh and Nera one direction and Samir another.

Kesh pointed at him.

“Next practice. Outer brake.”

Samir kept walking.

“No interview.”

“That is not an interview.”

“No.”

“Coward.”

The route split them.

* * *

The review result arrived the next morning before Crossband.

Samir read it outside the volume while the previous team finished.

BOUNDED COMPARISON RESULT — SERVICE TRANSFER 4.

The analyst had matched Samir’s own timestamp and order statement against the controller event log.

The log showed:

DOOR / TRANSITION HOLD EXTENDED DURING MIXED FLOW.

LOCAL RETURN COMPENSATION BEGAN.

CONTROLLER DISPLAYED CORRECTION EVENT SEVERAL SECONDS AFTER THE MOVEMENT/HOLD STATE WAS ALREADY ACTIVE.

The exact interval could not be tied to Samir’s subjective airflow onset because his personal timestamp was approximate.

The result did not claim that he had sensed a hidden system variable.

It did not claim the event matched Ring Six.

It did not claim cause.

The useful line was smaller:

**INDEPENDENT MOVEMENT-ORDER CASE SUPPORTS TESTING WHETHER ORDINARY HELD / REVERSED TRANSITION CHOREOGRAPHY CAN ENTER A SHARED-LOOP STATE BEFORE THE FIXED CORRECTION IS DISPLAYED.**

Then:

NO TEAM DATA USED.

NO IDENTIFIABLE PEER DATA RETAINED.

NO PROFILE CONTEXT USED.

Samir read the last three lines first on the second pass.

Kesh came out of the Crossband volume carrying two balls.

“You are late.”

“I am four minutes early.”

“For being shouted at about your brake.”

“That is not scheduled.”

“It is now.”

Samir closed the review result.

Kesh tossed him a ball.

Samir caught it.

Nera followed them out.

“Did your review thing solve the habitat?”

“No.”

“Good,” Nera said. “We booked forty minutes.”

Samir looked at the court status.

Their slot had been shortened again by relocation scheduling.

Thirty-eight minutes.

He pointed.

“Thirty-eight.”

Kesh swore.

“That is the real emergency.”

Fen was already inside warming up.

Samir crossed the boundary.

Kesh called after him, “Outer band first.”

“No.”

“You need the reps.”

“I need the ball.”

Nera threw it past both of them.

Samir turned and ran.
