# ORBITAL YOUTH

## Chapter 25 — NO INTERVIEW

Samir lost his starting rotation because he tried to stop too quickly.

Kesh told him this before Samir had finished sliding into the outer-band wall.

“You braked like Earth.”

Samir pushed himself off the padding.

“I stopped.”

“You arrived at the wall.”

“Also stopped.”

Nera passed behind them on the lower-gravity line, caught the rebound ball one-handed and sent it through the blue gate before Samir had both feet under him.

“Point,” Nera said.

Kesh pointed at the bench rail.

“Two possessions out.”

Their side was down three.

“You need me.”

“We need you to stop before the architecture.”

Samir went to the rail.

The Crossband volume was running on a shortened booking because the adjacent public space had been taken for temporary relocation staging. Half the youth teams in their schedule had been compressed into new slots, which meant everyone played too fast because nobody wanted to waste a possession discussing anything.

The game did not improve from Samir watching it.

Kesh got trapped in the middle band.

Nera cut toward the inner line.

Fen took the ball, panicked at the gravity change and passed too early.

Turnover.

Samir said, “That was not my fault.”

Kesh, still moving, called back, “Remarkable contribution.”

The other team scored.

Five down.

Fen looked at the bench.

Samir lifted one hand.

“Next time hold until the second defender commits.”

“Do not coach from punishment,” Kesh said.

“I am communicating.”

“You are suffering publicly. Do it quietly.”

Nera landed beside the inner gate, synthetic feet making almost no sound on the grip surface.

“Can we finish losing before the next booking arrives?”

“We are not losing,” Kesh said.

The score changed again.

Nera looked up.

“We are numerically experiencing something.”

Samir laughed.

Kesh saw.

“One more possession.”

“That was already two.”

“It became three when you laughed.”

“That is not a rule.”

Kesh pointed at the captain band around their wrist.

Samir sat back.

“Temporary visual problem.”

The next possession went better.

Fen waited.

The second defender committed.

Fen sent the ball to Nera, who crossed from inner to middle, held for three and threw high toward Kesh.

A defender overcommitted.

The outer lane opened.

Samir saw it from the rail before anyone used it.

He also saw the booking clock.

Nine minutes.

Kesh scored.

Three down.

Samir stood before being invited.

“Now.”

He entered on the next dead ball.

This time he let the first transition happen without him.

The other side expected the high cut he had used three possessions in a row during warm-up.

He went middle.

Nera carried through the first gate. Kesh crossed behind. Fen reversed. Two defenders followed.

Samir waited half a beat and entered the outer band only after the lane changed direction.

The pass reached him cleanly.

He did not brake.

He let the curve take speed from him and sent the ball back toward Nera before the defender reset.

Second gate.

Nera held for three.

Kesh shouted, “Back!”

Samir was already moving.

The return pass came low. He caught it, crossed the third band and did not touch the wall.

Their bench made more noise about that than the score.

Samir threw through the last gate.

Tie.

Kesh called, “See? Architecture survives.”

Samir raised one finger because breathing was currently more useful than speaking.

They lost the next possession by one.

Fen missed the final gate after doing everything else correctly.

The booking signal sounded before anyone could invent another play.

For a second Fen stood still.

Nera pulled the ball from the gate and tossed it to them.

“You missed.”

Fen stared.

“Yes.”

“Good. Saves review time.”

“Nera,” Kesh said.

“What? They know.”

Fen laughed despite trying not to.

Samir walked over.

“You waited correctly on the second defender.”

“I missed.”

“Also true.”

“That does not help.”

Samir shrugged.

The next team was already entering the volume.

They collected their grips, wrist bands and one water pack nobody admitted owning.

The score stayed on the wall for twelve more seconds before the next booking erased it.

They had lost.

Samir wanted the rematch badly enough to avoid saying so.

* * *

The first incident-review message arrived while Samir was trying to get food.

He ignored it because Kesh had taken the last hot item from the counter.

“You saw me behind you.”

“Yes.”

“And still took it.”

“Yes.”

“Hostile leadership.”

“Performance-based allocation.”

“You lost too.”

“I lost while wearing the captain band.”

Nera appeared with two cold packs and handed one to Samir.

He opened it reluctantly.

The food was fine, which weakened his position.

They sat on the transfer steps with the rest of the team. Fen replayed the last possession on a legal public-angle capture and stopped it exactly before the missed gate.

Samir’s band vibrated again.

INCIDENT REVIEW — VOLUNTARY COMPARISON INTAKE.

This time he opened it.

The intake offered several ways to contribute: his own timeline, an interview, personal-band data, public or team capture, peer corroboration, optional prior Contact-cohort context.

Samir skipped most of them.

SERVICE TRANSFER 4. APPROX 21:38:12. PERSONAL OBSERVATION ONLY. MIXED MOVEMENT / HELD TRANSITION. AIRFLOW CHANGE NOTICED BEFORE VISIBLE SYSTEM CORRECTION. I HAVE MOVEMENT ORDER FROM MY OWN MEMORY / ORIGINAL NOTE.

Then:

NO TEAM DATA AUTHORIZED.

Nera noticed the screen.

“Ring Six?”

“Yes.”

“You doing it?” Kesh asked.

“One thing.”

“Need us gone?”

Samir looked at the team.

Nobody was leaning over his shoulder. Fen was still arguing with the replay. Two others were discussing the next booking slot.

“No.”

He submitted his own observation and put the band away.

Kesh pointed at him with the last piece of hot food.

“Then your outer brake.”

“No.”

“Review has improved nothing.”

Samir threw the wrapper from his cold pack.

Kesh caught it.

Annoying.

* * *

The analyst follow-up arrived during equipment return.

Samir was signing a grip strip back into the team locker when the validation request opened.

The default package wanted an interview, a short personal-band window, available transfer capture and an option to invite peers.

Samir declined all four.

He also declined the optional Contact-cohort context.

At the bottom he selected:

CONTINUE WITH LIMITED VALIDATION.

Then typed:

WHAT EXACT QUESTION DO YOU NEED ANSWERED?

Nera waited beside the locker while he sent it.

“You kept us out?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Samir looked at them.

“That easy?”

Nera shrugged.

“You cannot consent for my morning. I cannot consent for yours.”

Kesh arrived behind them.

“What are we consenting to?”

“Nothing,” Samir said.

“Excellent. Fast meeting.”

They left the volume.

At the junction, Kesh pointed at him.

“Next practice. Outer brake.”

Samir kept walking.

“No interview.”

“That is not an interview.”

“Still no.”

The routes carried them apart.

The analyst answered three minutes later.

BOUND QUESTION:

Can the old Service Transfer 4 event establish relative order between mixed movement / held-transition state and the controller’s fixed correction event using only your own timestamp/order statement and the transfer controller log held by review?

No interview, peer identity, team capture, body data or Contact context required.

Samir stopped beside the corridor wall.

That was narrow enough.

He selected:

YES — BOUNDED RESPONSE.

The request asked for movement order as he remembered it.

Samir did not trust memory enough for that.

He searched his old message thread with Ollie.

SAMIR: service transfer 4. 21:38. maybe nothing.

SAMIR: airflow changed before system correction.

OLLIE: how long

SAMIR: under ten seconds? not sure

OLLIE: what movement order

Then Samir’s own sequence message.

He copied only his messages and rewrote the order without names.

THREE PEOPLE CROSSED FORWARD.

I CROSSED AFTER THE THIRD.

ONE PERSON REVERSED DIRECTION NEAR THE TRANSITION.

TWO CROSSED OPPOSITE FLOW.

PARTITION / DOOR STATE HELD LONGER THAN NORMAL PASS-THROUGH.

I NOTICED AIRFLOW CHANGE BEFORE VISIBLE CONTROLLER CORRECTION.

PERSONAL TIMESTAMP APPROX 21:38:12.

The form asked for confidence.

Samir entered:

HIGH — MOVEMENT ORDER.

MODERATE — SUBJECTIVE AIRFLOW ONSET.

LOW — EXACT LEAD INTERVAL.

Then one final permission request:

MAY REVIEW CONTACT IDENTIFIABLE PEERS PRESENT?

NO.

PEERS MAY OPT IN INDEPENDENTLY THROUGH PUBLIC INTAKE.

He submitted.

The analyst did not ask for anything else.

Samir liked that more than he expected.

* * *

The comparison result arrived the next morning before Crossband.

Samir read it outside the volume while the previous team finished.

BOUNDED COMPARISON RESULT — SERVICE TRANSFER 4.

Review had matched his own order statement and approximate timestamp against the controller event log.

The controller showed:

MIXED FLOW / TRANSITION HOLD EXTENDED.

LOCAL RETURN COMPENSATION BEGAN.

FIXED CORRECTION DISPLAYED SEVERAL SECONDS AFTER THE HELD / REVERSED MOVEMENT STATE WAS ALREADY ACTIVE.

His approximate timestamp could not establish the exact relation to his subjective airflow onset.

The result did not claim he had sensed a hidden variable.

It did not claim Service Transfer Four shared Ring Six’s cause.

The useful line was narrower:

INDEPENDENT MOVEMENT-ORDER CASE SUPPORTS TESTING WHETHER ORDINARY HELD / REVERSED TRANSITION CHOREOGRAPHY CAN ENTER A SHARED-LOOP STATE BEFORE FIXED CORRECTION IS DISPLAYED.

NO TEAM DATA USED.

NO IDENTIFIABLE PEER DATA RETAINED.

NO CONTACT-PROFILE CONTEXT USED.

Kesh came out of the Crossband volume carrying two balls.

“You are late.”

“I am four minutes early.”

“For being shouted at about your brake.”

“That is not scheduled.”

“It is now.”

Samir locked the result.

Kesh tossed him a ball.

Nera followed them out.

“Did your review thing solve the habitat?”

“No.”

“Good. We booked forty minutes.”

Samir checked the court status.

“Thirty-eight.”

Kesh swore.

“That is the real emergency.”

Fen was already inside warming up.

Samir crossed the boundary.

Kesh called after him, “Outer band first.”

“No.”

“You need the reps.”

“I need the ball.”

Nera threw it past both of them.

Samir turned and ran.
