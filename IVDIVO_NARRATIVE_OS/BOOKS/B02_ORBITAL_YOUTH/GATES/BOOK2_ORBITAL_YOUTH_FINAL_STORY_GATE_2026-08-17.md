# 1. STORY CORE — WHOLE BOOK

## HERO
**Ollie** — primary hero inside a genuine youth ensemble.

He enters Book 2 competent, useful and ambitious, but with a dangerous pressure pattern:
he wants real attributable work and professional legitimacy strongly enough that uncertainty can become a problem he tries to own.

## WANT
To do real consequential orbital work, be recognized as genuinely useful, and earn a legitimate place inside the civilization rather than remain merely a talented youth observer.

Private pressure underneath:
replacement/status anxiety — if other people or systems can do the work without him, what makes his place real?

## WHY NOW
Ch1 moves him out of the Earth maker-team environment and into supervised orbital maintenance/commissioning work.

Ring Six gives the want a concrete object:
- a real inhabited system;
- a real housing need;
- real commissioning decisions;
- people waiting for rooms;
- a system where technical judgment changes lives.

## OPPOSITION
Not one villain.

The book's effective opposition is the interaction of:
- a complex inhabited orbital system;
- incomplete/competing causal models;
- real institutional safety and authority boundaries;
- human movement that refuses to behave like a clean test;
- civic allocation and scheduling trade-offs;
- Ollie's own reflex to turn uncertainty into model ownership, access pressure and technical control.

Adults are not automatically obstruction.
Residents are not noise.
Iri is not oracle.
The institution can be right, wrong, limited and adaptive without becoming stupid.

## WRONG STRATEGY
`GET CLEANER DATA -> PROVE THE MODEL -> GAIN ACCESS/RECOGNITION -> FIX THE SYSTEM`.

Failure modes inside that strategy:
- preferring reproducible machine conditions over lived conditions;
- treating person/sequence/context as contamination until forced to reconsider;
- wanting a cause strongly enough to prefer confirmation;
- choosing datasets too early;
- confusing useful insight with entitlement to authority;
- assuming technical optimization can stand in for social/civic choice.

## PRICE
The price accumulates rather than arriving only at climax:

1. Ch1 — Ollie leaves the maker-team life; belonging becomes less automatic.
2. Early orbit — the five separate into real lives; competence does not preserve proximity.
3. Ch15 false victory — recognition increases his attachment to the model and raises the eventual fall.
4. Ch20 — wrong causal model has public consequences: injury/service damage/displacement and loss of trust.
5. Review Hold — access/authority is restricted through legitimate procedure.
6. Iri relationship — useful collaboration becomes harder because trust and permission were mishandled.
7. Housing/migration — every safety/commissioning choice carries costs for people who need rooms, schools, jobs and routes.
8. Ch31 — Ethan independently pays a professional price, preventing the book from making only Ollie sacrifice.
9. Ch35 — the correct bounded safety action still prevents a real family from entering its room that night.

Correct action does not erase price.

## FALSE VICTORY
**Ch15 — LIMITED OPEN.**

The model appears to work under bounded occupancy.
Ollie receives exactly the kind of recognition he wants.

This is not filler success; it increases his confidence and the cost of Ch20.

## MIDPOINT / MAJOR REVERSAL
**Ch20 — THE WRONG CAUSE.**

The system fails under a missing causal class built from ordinary individually legal actions.
Adults isolate/evacuate.
People are injured/displaced.
Ollie's analysis had materially supported the public YES.

The story question changes from:
`Can Ollie prove/fix the model?`

to:
`What causal class did the model exclude, what evidence is legitimate, and what action may Ollie take without exceeding authority?`

## METHOD CHANGE
Ch21–27 constitute the real learning arc, not an apology montage.

Ollie learns through resistance:
- useful != permitted;
- raw access can be denied legitimately;
- minimum necessary data can be enough;
- negative cases are not inconvenient noise;
- a model needs failure conditions;
- Iri's local knowledge is evidence input, not prophecy;
- Aoife's source/consent boundaries matter;
- Mara/adult safety review owns deployment decisions;
- an apparent successful reproduction must be rejected if a negative/control case breaks the claim.

The clearest reversal arrives in Ch26:

Old method:
`What variable proves my model?`

New method:
`What would make this fail before we build a test around it?`

Ch27 operationalizes the change under adult-designed controlled reproduction.

## LATE ACT ESCALATION
Ch28–34 translate technical uncertainty into civic/operational choices:

- Ch28: Ethan removes a private weighted score because it encodes value judgments that belong to civic authority.
- Ch29: partial segmented opening replaces all-or-nothing success; Ollie removes his own hidden allocation/sort rule.
- Ch30–32: relocation consequences become lived household, route, school and trust problems.
- Ch33: migration actually works, proving partial success is real rather than fake optimism.
- Ch34: reduced redundancy makes the system less forgiving under real clustered load; R3-S becomes a pre-authorized one-way local fail-safe rather than hidden commissioning power.

## CLIMAX CHOICE
**Ch35 — SAFE STATE.**

Ollie activates R3-S before legacy automatic certainty arrives.

The choice is earned because:
- the trigger envelope was pre-registered;
- reports/Iri are corroboration only, not trigger;
- adult primary has equivalent local authority;
- R3-S is narrow, local and one-way;
- Ollie cannot reopen, tune thresholds or seize wider control;
- Household 3-177 is visibly approaching the gate, making early action socially costly.

The climax is not:
`Does Ollie believe in himself?`

It is:
`Will he execute the exact costly action society has authorized now that the evidence is sufficient for that action, even though the full ontology remains incomplete?`

He acts.
The family loses access that night.
The system continues moving with lag.
A later fixed-system response rises in the predicted direction and legacy isolation criteria are eventually met after the local path has already safe-stated.

Thus the early action is technically justified **for this event**, without proving a universal root cause.

No full coupled oscillation/injury is required to make the climax bigger.

## RESOLUTION
**Ch36 — A HOME THAT ISN'T FINISHED — accepted clean v0.3.**

- Ethan's adult path advances without refunding the exact lost Ch31 opportunity;
- Aoife makes a smaller truthful work product;
- Maya's autonomy protocol has become boring ordinary responsibility;
- Samir's Crossband/social life survives the technical plot;
- the five fail to synchronize cleanly, then choose an imperfect shared dinner;
- Ollie receives a non-urgent work request and leaves it for tomorrow.

The final accepted emotional beat belongs to the five young people, not to franchise machinery.

# 2. WHOLE-BOOK CAUSAL SPINE

## ACT I — LEAVE / ARRIVE / WANT THE FUTURE

Ch1 establishes price before spectacle:
Ollie chooses orbital work over automatic maker-team belonging.

Ch2–3 establish the future through ordinary friction:
- Dublin orbital delay/freight/public transport;
- restricted recording and Aoife's refusal to fake an unrecorded experience;
- Maya's body learning orbital traffic and gravity;
- host/privacy/route rules shaping actual youth independence.

Ch4–7 spread the five into orbital life and introduce Ring Six as a **wanted home**, not an abstract engineering object.

Crucial early causal setup:
- residents need shorter commutes, storage, privacy and school access;
- clean/static tests pass;
- lived movement produces timing/sequence relations the static model does not capture;
- Ollie's testing reflex is visible before the major failure.

## ACT II-A — SUCCESS MAKES THE WRONG MODEL STRONGER

Ch8–14 grow work, youth life, status, friendship, sport, attraction and ordinary orbital belonging.

Ch13 Vela Night is structurally important:
orbit is desirable enough that later loss/restriction matters; the characters are not merely technicians waiting for an incident.

Ch15 gives the model a bounded real success and gives Ollie recognition.

The story therefore earns the later reversal instead of manufacturing failure immediately.

## ACT II-B — WRONG CAUSE / REAL CONSEQUENCES

Ch16–19 intensify confidence/operation while preserving competing evidence.

Ch20 breaks the model under lived coupled behavior.
The failure costs real people and institutions.

Ch21 turns the technical failure into social/professional consequence:
review hold, access loss, trust damage, competing responsibilities.

Ch22–27 rebuild the investigative method through constraints, bounded data, negative cases, falsifiability and adult-controlled testing.

This section changes the protagonist's method before the climax; the climax does not suddenly teach him a lesson he has never practiced.

## ACT III-A — ENGINEERING BECOMES CIVIC LIFE

Ch28 removes private optimization from civic priority.
Ch29 replaces full-opening fantasy with segmented partial operation.
Ch30–32 pay waiting/relocation/relationship/autonomy consequences.
Ch33 proves the partial system can become ordinary life.

The book's stakes therefore remain double:
`KEEP PEOPLE SAFE` and `LET PEOPLE ACTUALLY LIVE THERE`.

## ACT III-B — BOUNDED RISK / BOUNDED AUTHORITY

Ch34 begins reduced redundancy under rational adult conditions and real operational pressure.
R3-S becomes available only inside a pre-authorized narrow envelope.

Ch35 forces the one-way choice before legacy certainty.
Correct action carries immediate human cost, later fixed confirmation and institutional review.

## ACT IV — LIFE AFTER THE SOLVED PROBLEM

Ch36 shows:
work, home, laundry, school, later intake, truthful media work, Crossband, host updates and friendship continuing after the climax.

The future remains unfinished enough to be lived in.

## CHANGE THROUGH ACTION
He gradually learns to:
- accept denied access;
- ask for minimum evidence;
- preserve negative cases;
- distinguish report from proof;
- let other people own their data/experience;
- state what would falsify his model;
- remove value judgments he has no authority to make;
- accept partial/segmented success;
- act decisively only inside an explicit legitimate scope.

## CLIMAX CHANGE
He does not wait for metaphysical certainty and does not seize wider authority.
He performs the exact bounded action the evidence and society permit.

## END STATE
He is trusted enough to work and bounded enough that the lesson remains real.
He can leave a nonurgent test until tomorrow and stay with friends.
