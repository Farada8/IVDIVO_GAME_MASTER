## TWO BAD OPTIONS — GREEN STRONG

### A — FULL-RECOVERY INTERLOCK
Real advantages:
- simpler evidence basis;
- less dependence on incomplete estimator;
- potentially faster route to a restricted occupied profile.

Real costs:
- lower throughput/flexibility;
- service queues/manual staging;
- deferred mature-ring maintenance;
- reduced first-wave household capacity;
- unequal return timing.

### B — STATE-AWARE CONTROL
Real advantages:
- potentially preserves intended capacity and route flexibility;
- more selective control.

Real costs:
- longer validation;
- greater model dependence;
- false-negative performance against dangerous escalation remains undefined;
- escalation condition and embodied/support role remain unresolved.

Neither option is morally or technically caricatured.

## HYBRID — GREEN

The natural `A now / B later` proposal is not prohibited. It is shown to have costs:
- A requires its own validated operating profile;
- B consumes overlapping test/engineering capacity;
- households may move twice;
- people excluded under A may still wait through B;
- service schedules must be requalified.

Hybrid remains possible long-term, but it does not erase present distributional choice.
