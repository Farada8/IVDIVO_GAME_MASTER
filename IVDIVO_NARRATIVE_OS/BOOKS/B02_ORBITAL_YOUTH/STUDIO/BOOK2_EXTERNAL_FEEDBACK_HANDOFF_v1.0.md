## 2. BLIND FIRST-PASS FORM

Ask the reader to answer immediately after finishing, without reopening the manuscript if possible.

### A. Completion / propulsion
1. Where did you most want to keep reading?
2. Where, if anywhere, did you consider putting the book down for reasons other than ordinary fatigue?
3. Name up to three places where you felt you were doing “homework” rather than reading a story.
4. Were there any stretches you skimmed? If yes, identify the chapter or event as precisely as you can.

### B. Hero / emotional wait
5. Who did you experience as the main character?
6. What did that character want most by roughly one-third of the book?
7. What were you emotionally waiting to see happen to or inside that character?
8. Did the protagonist change? Describe the change in your own words, without using the book’s technical language.

### C. Midpoint / causality
9. Did the Chapter 20 failure feel earned by what came before, surprising-but-fair, arbitrary, or confusing? Why?
10. After the failure, did you understand why the characters could not simply return to the first solution?
11. Was there any point where the technical problem seemed to change into a different problem without enough connective tissue?

### D. Ensemble / youth life
12. Which two secondary characters felt most distinct from each other, and why?
13. Which character, if any, felt more like a function than a person?
14. Did the five friends feel like teenagers with different lives, or mainly like a problem-solving team?
15. Which ordinary orbital-life detail or scene stayed with you most strongly?

### E. Climax / ending
16. Before Ollie acts in the climax, did you understand what he might lose by acting too early and what might happen if he waits?
17. Did the climax feel like a real decision under uncertainty, or like the story had already told you the correct answer?
18. Did the later confirmation feel satisfying, too convenient, too technical, or appropriately limited?
19. Did Chapter 36 feel complete, merely quiet, or unfinished? Why?
20. What is the final image/action you remember from the book?

### F. Open recall
21. What do you think the book was “about,” underneath the Ring Six plot?
22. What unresolved question did you expect the novel itself to answer but it did not answer?
23. What would you tell a friend is the strongest reason to read this book?
24. What is the single biggest reason you would hesitate to recommend it?

## 3. SECOND-PASS DIAGNOSTIC FORM

Only after the Blind First-Pass Form is frozen, reveal these targeted questions.

### Watch 1 — Ch05–07 technical-onboarding density
- Did Chapters 5–7 contain a continuous stretch where new terminology or system explanation displaced character desire/conflict for too long?
- If yes, mark the **smallest exact interval** that produced the effect.
- What story function were you waiting to return to?
- Did an ordinary-life, status, apprenticeship, friendship or wonder beat reset your attention before frustration became cumulative?

### Watch 2 — Ch26–29 second-model / civic-decision density
- Did Chapters 26–29 feel like escalation, repetition, or explanation?
- Which chapter changed the dramatic question most clearly?
- If you wanted compression, identify one scene/paragraph function that felt duplicated by another; do not answer merely “less technical detail.”
- Did the move from model evidence to real household/segment consequences feel causally continuous?

### Watch 3 — `THE WRONG CAUSE` expectation
- When you encountered the title `THE WRONG CAUSE`, did you expect the novel later to reveal one singular “right cause”?
- At the end, did you feel that promise was broken, or had the prose recalibrated the mystery toward an operational/state problem?
- If broken, identify the earliest sentence/scene that created the stronger promise.

### Watch 4 — body / technology-access clarity
- Did any background reference make complete artificial/synthetic human bodies feel like routine, locally available ordinary-human technology?
- If yes, quote or locate the exact passage.
- Did you distinguish embodied/interface differences from artificial-body technology, or did the text blur them?

These questions are diagnostic. They do not assume a defect exists.

## 4. EVIDENCE CAPTURE STANDARD

Every external note must be normalized into one record:

`Reader ID | reader type | chapter/location | spontaneous vs prompted | symptom | severity | repetition | proposed cause | confidence`

### Reader types
Use at least these distinctions when available:
- genre SF reader;
- YA/coming-of-age reader;
- general commercial-fiction reader;
- technically literate reader;
- editor/publishing professional.

Do not average all readers into one score. A specialist objection and a broad-reader propulsion failure are different evidence classes.

### Spontaneous evidence outranks prompted evidence
A reader independently saying “26–29 felt repetitive” is stronger than a yes/no answer after being told to inspect 26–29.

### Location beats adjective
“Too technical” is not yet actionable.  
“Ch27 control-room exchange repeated the same uncertainty I had already understood in Ch26, so I skimmed until the household consequence appeared” is actionable evidence.

## 5. SEVERITY / REOPENING RULE

### FATAL
Reopen immediately if external evidence reveals:
- main causality cannot be followed;
- climax action is not understandable/credible;
- ending does not close the book’s main story;
- major canon contradiction;
- a factual/technical impossibility that collapses the premise.

### MAJOR
Reopen a bounded corridor if one of these is demonstrated by strong evidence:
- protagonist not experienced as causal center;
- Ch20 failure reads as arbitrary punishment rather than earned incomplete-model failure;
- long technical corridor causes repeatable disengagement/skimming;
- Ch35 choice lacks real uncertainty/cost;
- Ch36 repeatedly reads as unfinished rather than quiet completion;
- relationship/character payoff is consistently invisible to readers rather than merely subtle.

### MEDIUM
Local repair may be justified for:
- one repeated explanation;
- one unclear transition;
- one misleading line/title implication;
- one local voice convergence;
- one body/technology-access phrase that violates current canon.

### LOW / taste only
Do not reopen prose solely because one reader:
- wants a romance lock;
- wants more lore;
- wants a villain;
- wants a universal Ring Six root cause;
- wants the Old Earth archive tail restored;
- prefers a different POV distribution;
- dislikes an intentionally bounded ending.

---

## 6. MINIMUM EVIDENCE BEFORE REWRITE

A manuscript patch should normally require at least one of:
1. the same reader-visible failure appears independently in **2+ readers**;
2. one professional/editorial reader demonstrates a concrete structural or continuity failure with exact evidence;
3. one factual/canon error is objectively verifiable;
4. Founder explicitly requests a change.

Exception: a true FATAL does not require multiple votes.

For pacing, require **location agreement**, not just adjective agreement. Three readers saying “a bit technical” across different places is weaker than two independent readers both skimming the same corridor for the same dramatic reason.

---

## 7. REPAIR LAW AFTER EXTERNAL FEEDBACK

If evidence crosses threshold:
1. state the reader-visible symptom;
2. locate the smallest causal corridor;
3. protect all already-paid story functions;
4. design the smallest repair card;
5. patch prose only inside authorized scope;
6. run local Reader Advocate + Dialogue/Voice + Continuity + Red Team;
7. compare against currently accepted version;
8. accept only if the reader-visible defect improves without new regression;
9. persist GitHub + Drive;
10. read back before changing source-of-truth.

No global rewrite may be inferred from one local complaint.
