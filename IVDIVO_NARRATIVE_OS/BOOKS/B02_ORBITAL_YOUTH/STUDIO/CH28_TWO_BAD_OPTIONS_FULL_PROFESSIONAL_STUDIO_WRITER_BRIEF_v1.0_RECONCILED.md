Two operational directions are real but not yet chosen:

A. **CONSERVATIVE / FULL-RECOVERY INTERLOCK**
Require near/full physical return recovery before certain service-transition combinations.
- simpler evidence basis;
- lower model dependence;
- can be validated sooner;
- reduces throughput/flexibility;
- increases queues / door holds / manual staging;
- lowers near-term usable Ring Six capacity and may shift burden into mature-ring routes/services.

B. **STATE-AWARE CONTROL**
Estimate residual/shared-loop state in real time and inhibit only dangerous candidate combinations.
- potentially preserves more capacity/flexibility;
- more complex and model-dependent;
- requires further validation / shadow-mode or Phase 2 work before operational use;
- dangerous escalation condition still unknown;
- false positive/false negative behavior not yet established.

Neither option is “safe vs reckless.”

## ETHAN'S ERROR

Ethan converts the consequence map into an optimization problem.

He may create a weighted score using categories like:
- safety margin;
- housing-days displaced;
- service disruption;
- access/equality;
- validation uncertainty.

The math itself can be correct.

The error is choosing/normalizing the **weights** as though they were technical facts.

Someone should expose this concretely, not philosophically.

Examples:
- Lea: `Why is another month in temporary housing worth 0.7 and not 1.4?`
- Mara: `Where did you get the cost of an unknown false negative?`
- Nadia: `Your capacity score counts households. It does not show which households.`

Do not use all three as clever lines. Pick one or two and let resistance change the scene.

## NO PERFECT THIRD OPTION

Characters may naturally suggest `do A now, build B later`.

Do NOT ban this thought. Instead show why it is not free:
- A requires a revalidated operating profile and altered schedules;
- staffing/testing capacity is limited;
- moving households into a reduced A profile may create another disruptive move/recommissioning if B replaces it soon;
- B validation continues while Ring Six remains partly/fully constrained;
- an interim path still decides who gets housing first and who waits.

The hybrid may become a long-term strategy, but it does not erase the current distribution of cost.
