**VERDICT: PROTECT.**

Samir does **not** have a classic `bad belief → mistake → punishment → corrected belief` arc. The accepted prose does not support inventing one.

He has a coherent **boundary-development arc**:

`ordinary desire for local belonging`
→ `R-14 identity follows him into new life`
→ `he states a specific boundary without surrendering participation`
→ `he keeps personal sport from becoming evidence/content`
→ `he later volunteers bounded operational contribution under consent`
→ `by the ending he allows friends into his public sport life while preserving No sensors / no extraction`.

The movement is from **defensive compartmentalization toward chosen permeability**, not from healthy privacy to less privacy.

This is sufficient for a secondary ensemble arc because Samir changes **how participation and boundary coexist**, while the novel correctly refuses to manufacture a moral defect out of consent.

# 2. SUBPLOT INCITING PRESSURE — Ch08

Accepted source:
`DRAFTS/CH08_NOT_A_VISITOR_SPORT_v0.2_FULL_STUDIO.md`

Samir wants a real local sport experience, not an identity hearing.

He asks to join as a learner:
`Explain it like I'm not stupid.`

He is genuinely bad at first because planet-trained body assumptions do not map cleanly onto local play. He is embarrassed, learns, improves, and earns interest through play.

Then R-14 recognition arrives:
`He's the R-fourteen one.`

The social question shifts from `can he play?` toward `does he need to prove/report why he is allowed here?`

Samir's answer is exact:
- `I don't want to be the story people tell about why I get to be here.`
- `You can ask me. You cannot ask me to prove I am allowed to play.`
- `I am asking whether you want me on the rail.`

The team chooses on the actual local question: whether they want him to play.

**PRICE:** Samir risks social rejection at the moment when he wants local belonging. He does not purchase acceptance by performing R-14 identity or proving entitlement.

This is a real paid choice, even though the boundary itself is not a mistake.

`my sport / my body / my social life`
≠
`your evidence / your story / your model`.

The partition is healthy but deliberately broad.

# 4. DEVELOPMENT TURN — Ch25

Accepted source:
`DRAFTS/CH25_NO_INTERVIEW_v0.3_PASS_B_CANDIDATE.md`

Ch25 is the crucial change because Samir does **not** simply repeat Ch13's blanket refusal.

He agrees to participate in a controlled movement/service-transition recreation, but sets terms:
- `No interview.`
- `No recorder.`
- approved operational timing only;
- no conversion of participation into R-14 framing;
- no personal/public extraction;
- no magical-sensor status;
- movement sequence must be represented honestly.

He allows approved timing/position evidence because the context is different from private sport footage.

### Changed choice
Earlier:
`personal activity requested as analysis material` → NO.

Now:
`bounded operational contribution with explicit consent / purpose / data limits` → YES.

The change is not `Samir learns to share himself`.

It is:
**Samir learns that refusing extraction does not require refusing contribution.**

This is the center of his secondary arc.

# 6. ARC MAP

## WANT
Have a local youth life—especially sport and friendship—where he is wanted for who he is doing now, not used as the explanation of R-14.

## PRIVATE DESIRE
Belong without being required to perform the exceptional history that made others notice him.

## FEAR
Attention becomes extraction; contribution becomes identity ownership; every new relationship turns into another request to prove or explain himself.

## PRESSURE / OPPOSITION
- public R-14 recognition;
- local uncertainty about his status;
- Ollie's legitimate desire for useful timing evidence;
- later operational need where Samir genuinely can help;
- Aoife's recorder/content world;
- old friends wanting access to a new social world.

## INITIAL STRATEGY
Strong compartmentalization:
`personal sport/social life stays outside evidence/content systems.`

This is not morally wrong. Its limitation is that if generalized absolutely, it would also forbid Samir from making a contribution he actually wants to make.

## PRICE
1. Ch08: he risks rejection from the local team rather than prove entitlement through R-14.
2. The new local life remains partly separated from the old group; Ch13 marks this explicitly through `different people` and refusal to release match material.
3. Ch25 participation carries the exact risk he fears—being instrumentalized again—so he must build and enforce terms rather than simply disappear.

The price is primarily **social exposure and the risk of identity capture**, not punishment for a bad moral choice.

## MID-ARC CHOICE
Ch25: contribute operationally under explicit data/consent boundaries.

## CHANGED BEHAVIOR
He distinguishes:
- private sport ≠ evidence;
- public match attendance ≠ sensor access;
- voluntary operational timing ≠ interview;
- contribution ≠ surrender of identity.

## RESOLUTION
Ch36: friends are invited into the public sport space under `No sensors`; Samir gives them the public match time and returns to ordinary friendship.

# 7. RED TEAM — IS THIS TOO EASY?

## Objection A — `Samir has no mistake.`
**TRUE in the classic correction-arc sense.**

The manuscript does not show a destructive false belief that must be punished. His privacy/identity boundary is largely correct.

Inventing a mistake now would likely produce the wrong thematic implication: that a young person damaged by unwanted legibility must become more available to be mature.

**Do not do this.**

## Objection B — `Then there is no change.`
**FALSE.**

There is behavioral development:
- Ch13 protects personal sport through a categorical no;
- Ch25 accepts bounded operational contribution;
- Ch36 allows friends into public sport while keeping extraction out.

The object of consent changes, and Samir becomes more precise rather than simply more closed.

## Objection C — `The price is weak.`
**PARTLY TRUE / NONBLOCKING.**

His price is less spectacular than Ollie's or Ethan's. It is social risk and repeated boundary labor rather than lost job/status/housing.

For a secondary ensemble arc, this is proportionate. The book should not manufacture a larger wound merely for symmetry.

## Objection D — `He functions as a moral model because he is usually right.`
**WATCH, but not confirmed MAJOR.**

Protection comes from ordinary-life evidence:
- he is bad at the sport before he improves;
- he gets hurt by a wall in Ch36;
- he steals fries/noodles;
- he has practice conflicts and local teammates;
- he can be suspicious and curt;
- he is not asked to solve Ring Six.

Final Story/Reader pass should preserve these ordinary imperfections.
