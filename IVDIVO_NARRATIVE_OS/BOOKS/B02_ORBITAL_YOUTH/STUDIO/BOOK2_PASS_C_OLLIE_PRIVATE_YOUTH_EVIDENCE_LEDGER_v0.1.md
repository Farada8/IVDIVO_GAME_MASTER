## QUESTION

Does Book Two give its primary hero a private teenage life and emotional payoff beyond engineering competence, or has Ollie become only the A-line systems function?

## VERDICT

FATAL: 0
MAJOR: 0
blocking MEDIUM: 0

The feared Book-Two character failure is **not confirmed**.

Ollie's private youth life is not a separate romance/hobby subplot. It is embedded in the central contradiction of the book:

`I want real work badly enough to sacrifice ordinary life`
→ `work gives me the recognition/access I wanted`
→ `the cost becomes visible in missed friends, missed Earth-team ownership and late arrival to Vela life`
→ `after failure I must learn to contribute without owning the system`
→ `at resolution I can leave a nonurgent problem until tomorrow and keep dinner with friends`.

This is a full private/emotional movement, not merely technical growth.

Healthy technical ambition is NOT treated as Ollie's flaw. The flaw is the conversion of usefulness into indispensability/status/ownership.

# ON-PAGE EVIDENCE

## Ch01 — THE LAST GROUND

### PRIVATE OBJECTS / SENTIMENT
Ollie's packing choices are explicitly nonfunctional and personal:
- signed maker-team hoodie;
- old repair manual with years of marginal notes and a wheel-bearing drawing;
- warped useless first-prototype resin wheel with his baked-in fingerprint.

He leaves the team-owned calibration jig even though it matters to him.

This establishes a useful distinction early:
**things can matter to Ollie without being technically necessary or owned by him.**

### FRIENDSHIP / EARTH HOBBY LIFE
Priya, Lidia and Jamal are not service staff or exposition sources. The rescue-rover chat contains teasing, shared history and changed ownership of a project Ollie cares about.

They fix the cable routing without him. His instinct is to check what may still be wrong; they have already checked it.

Price begins before orbit:
**the team can continue without him.**

### WORK VERSUS SOCIAL TIME
The public-education placement would give Ollie better hours and more free residency time. He chooses maintenance because he wants real work.

That choice is not punished immediately; it is the correct external choice and the beginning of the private cost.

### STATUS WOUND
Ethan's sincere approval irritates Ollie because it lands like certification from the person who already tends to receive visible responsibility.

This makes Ollie's status hunger relational rather than an abstract ambition label.

## Ch05 / EARLY VELA — FIRST SHIFT

Ollie's first placement reward is narrow access, not glamour.

Ordinary/private surfaces exist around the work:
- bad/cheap food;
- missed group contact;
- scarce workshop time;
- first discretionary materials allowance;
- personal fabrication choice;
- local peer irritation;
- coursework competing with shifts.

Most revealingly, he spends discretionary access on fabrication rather than fun. The prose does not present this as virtue. It is character evidence that work identity is already consuming leisure.

## Ch12 — THE CLEAN MODEL

The Earth maker team advances without him again while his Ring Six work becomes more prestigious.

The model receives an `ORIGINATING ANALYST — OLLIE [YOUTH COHORT / SUPERVISED]` attribution. Ollie minimizes the header even though recognition is exactly what part of him wanted.

The chapter therefore contains both sides of his contradiction:
- wants consequential recognition;
- feels exposed by receiving it;
- keeps choosing the work anyway.

Vela Night is beginning outside the service bay while he remains inside refining the test request.

The city/social event is not absent from his life; he is choosing to arrive late to it.

## Ch13 — VELA NIGHT

This is the major private-youth price beat.

Before Ollie arrives:
- Samir plays with his local Crossband team;
- Maya/Lea/Davi build local jokes and route history;
- Aoife/Ethan share food, jealousy and accidental time alone;
- Iri performs with the low-g percussion group;
- the city produces wonder, food, booking conflicts and ordinary festival life.

At 19:12, Ollie is still at work.

Maya sends him proof of what he missed: Samir being `almost competent`.

The group leaves the sport volume without him.

Ollie arrives later into social history already created by other people.

This is not generic `hero works too much` exposition. The book has shown the specific life that happened while he was absent.

The positive Vela experience is therefore also private-character pressure on the hero.

## Ch15 / Ch19 — RECOGNITION BECOMES REAL

Ollie receives the professional thing he wanted:
- his analysis changes real review work;
- his name appears in the decision record;
- his bounded recommendation influences a consequential opening decision.

But the adult authority structure remains explicit.

Mara's correction in Ch19 is crucial:
`Your analysis changed the decision.`
`It did not make the decision.`

This is not merely technical scope. It directly attacks Ollie's private desire to become indispensable.

Then people actually move into the conditions he helped define.

Recognition now carries a human price.

## Ch20–23 — FAILURE / LOSS OF STATUS

The midpoint failure removes the identity reinforcement Ollie had begun to receive:
- Ring Six closes;
- Tomas is injured;
- Ollie loses access/credibility;
- Iri trust ruptures;
- he is forced into ordinary maintenance/light-duty comparison work rather than the high-status commissioning role.

The low point therefore attacks both external want and private status desire.

The book does not merely tell him to value friends instead. It takes away the environment that allowed usefulness to become social rank.

## Ch26–29 — CONTRIBUTION WITHOUT OWNERSHIP

Ollie's changed strategy appears before the climax:
- asks `what are we missing?` before locking the second model;
- preserves negative cases;
- does not mine Samir/Aoife/Maya raw private life;
- writes explicit falsifiers;
- accepts adults may run his test without him;
- remains under REVIEW HOLD;
- helps define segment/state questions without ranking which people deserve housing.

This is character change because the same technical strength is now used without the old ownership strategy.

## Ch33–35 — HERO REMAINS ACTIVE WITHOUT BECOMING COMMANDER

Ollie returns to foreground pressure through migration and reduced redundancy, but the later arc protects the learned boundary:
- real residents/households drive the stakes;
- he remains bounded by authorization;
- in Ch35 he uses only pre-authorized local R3-S;
- adult primary/Mara retain wider command;
- the decision costs Household 3-177 materially;
- delayed evidence validates only the bounded event decision, not Ollie as universal oracle.

His climax therefore gives him consequential usefulness **without restoring indispensability or ownership of the system**.

## Ch36 — A HOME THAT ISN'T FINISHED

This is the private-youth payoff and the strongest reason no compensatory hobby scene should be invented.

Ollie's unrestricted commissioning access remains suspended.

Iri offers a nonurgent route-comparison problem for that evening.

Old strategy:
`take the problem because I am useful and it matters.`

Changed choice:
**Ollie says no for tonight and offers tomorrow.**

At the five-person dinner:
- schedules have to be negotiated because everyone now has separate work/sport/life;
- Samir arrives from Crossband;
- friendship is chosen rather than automatic proximity;
- another nonurgent Ring Six message reaches Ollie;
- he starts to answer;
- Samir pushes the noodle bowl toward him;
- Ollie locks the band face-down;
- the answer can wait until tomorrow.

This is behavioral closure of the hero's core private conflict.

He has not stopped caring about work.
He has stopped requiring every solvable problem to become his immediate problem.
