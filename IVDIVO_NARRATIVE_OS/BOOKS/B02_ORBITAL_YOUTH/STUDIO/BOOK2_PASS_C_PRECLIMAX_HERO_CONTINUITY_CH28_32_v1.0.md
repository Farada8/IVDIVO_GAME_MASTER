# 6. DOES THREE NON-OLLIE POVs IN A ROW CREATE A PACING SAG?

## Risk
After Ch29, reader waits three chapters before returning to Ollie.
For a conventional technical thriller, this could weaken pre-climax acceleration.

## Evidence against MAJOR
The three chapters are not lateral:

### Ch30
**social consequence of partial opening** — movers/waiters become real.

### Ch31
**personal cost of staying through migration** — Ethan closes adult-track decision.

### Ch32
**actual route preparation for move day** — clock advances directly to 06:30.

Each chapter reduces unresolved secondary load before the physical climax. The sequence gets closer to move day, not farther away.

The book's genre promise is ORBITAL YOUTH, not pure engineering thriller. Removing these chapters or inserting token Ollie scenes would make the novel more A-line efficient but less truthful to its ensemble/youth promise.

## Reader-pressure progression
Ch29: **who gets to move?**  
Ch30: **what does moving/waiting cost in ordinary life?**  
Ch31: **will Ethan leave before the commitment is finished?**  
Ch32: **can Maya be useful without taking over, and are routes actually ready?**  
Ch33: **now do it with real households.**

The questions change, but all point toward migration.
