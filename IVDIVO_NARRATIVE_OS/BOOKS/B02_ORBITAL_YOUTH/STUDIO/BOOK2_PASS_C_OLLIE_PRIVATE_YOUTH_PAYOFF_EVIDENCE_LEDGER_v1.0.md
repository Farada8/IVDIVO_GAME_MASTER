**VERDICT: GREEN STRONG / PROTECT.**

Ollie's primary arc is behaviorally paid across the book. He does not simply become a better engineer. He changes what he treats as evidence of his own worth and what he does when useful work competes with ordinary life.

Core movement:

`If they need me, I matter`
→ `real work deserves the rest of my life`
→ `my useful model can still be wrong and hurt people`
→ `scope / negatives / other people's authority matter more than being central`
→ `I can be useful inside a narrow role without owning the system`
→ `nonurgent work can wait; friendship is also something I choose to maintain`.

No compensatory hobby scene is needed. Adding one would weaken the existing causal arc.

This establishes the private equation:

**usefulness / access / difficult work = personal value.**

He has friends and humor from page one, but work already has priority status over them.

The private strategy is weakening before the main failure, which prevents the arc from feeling like an instant lesson learned after punishment.

# 4. PRICE / MIDPOINT CONSEQUENCE — Ch19 / Ch20

## Ch19 — GO / NO-GO

Ollie is no longer merely analyst. He is directly asked whether he supports opening under the restricted profile.

He says **YES**, but only after stating limits.

Important:
- the board/adult authority remains the decision-maker;
- Ollie's analysis materially influences the decision;
- his name is now attached to a real inhabited consequence.

Status hunger and responsibility become inseparable.

## Ch20 — THE WRONG CAUSE

The restricted opening fails because individually allowed states combine into a missing distributed causal class.

The consequences are not symbolic:
- residents displaced again;
- service disruption;
- Tomas is injured;
- Ring Six shuts;
- Ollie loses active commissioning access under REVIEW HOLD.

This is the paid midpoint/character break.

His model was:
- careful;
- useful;
- honest about known limits;
- still incomplete.

Therefore the lesson is not `do not be ambitious` or `young people should not work`.

It is:
**being useful does not make you central, complete, or entitled to broader authority.**

The climax therefore pays both halves of his arc:
- **do not act merely because you want to be useful**;

This is mature responsibility, not reduced ambition.

# 9. ARC MAP

## WANT
Get real work and prove that he is genuinely useful inside orbital civilization.

## PRIVATE DESIRE
Be needed enough that other people cannot easily continue without him.

## FEAR
Replaceability / irrelevance / being only a supervised visitor-youth rather than someone whose competence matters.

## WRONG STRATEGY
Treat difficult work, broader access and continued necessity as evidence of personal worth; allow work to pre-empt ordinary youth life because usefulness feels safer than belonging.

## PRICE
- misses/shortens social life and Vela Night material;
- experiences Earth rover team moving beyond his solutions;
- local/access friction;
- publicly backs limited opening;
- Ch20 failure contributes to household displacement, Tomas injury and service harm;
- loses Ring Six access and credibility under review hold;
- has to rebuild from ordinary maintenance and bounded questions rather than a hero role.

## MIDPOINT / BREAK
Ch20 proves a careful useful model can still be incomplete and materially costly.

## NEW STRATEGY
- narrow questions;
- visible negative cases;
- accept other people's authority;
- distinguish interesting from actionable;
- act only inside real assigned scope;
- let nonurgent work belong to someone else or tomorrow;
- choose friends deliberately rather than assuming friendship survives whatever work takes.

## CLIMAX CHOICE
Use the narrow authority he truly owns (R3-S) before certainty, accepting material/social price, without turning the act into a claim of universal knowledge or station command.

## RESOLUTION
Continue meaningful technical work under restriction **and** refuse a nonurgent evening task for dinner; later postpone another nonurgent sensor issue until tomorrow.

# 10. RED TEAM

## Objection A — `He still works constantly; therefore no balance arc.`
**REJECTED.**

The arc never promises that Ollie stops loving work. That would be a false resolution. His change is priority/authority discipline, not reduced technical identity.

## Objection B — `Ch35 rewards him for being the special young genius.`
**REJECTED.**

R3-S is pre-authorized, narrow and local. Adults retain wider command. Ollie's action creates immediate social/material cost. Validation is event-specific and delayed. His unrestricted access is still suspended in Ch36.

## Objection C — `Final dinner payoff is too small.`
**REJECTED.**

Its smallness is exactly why it works. The book's character problem is whether every useful thing must become an emergency claim on Ollie's attention. A genuinely nonurgent bad sensor arriving during dinner is the cleanest possible final test.

## Objection D — `Need a hobby/romance for Ollie.`
**REJECTED.**

No evidence supports inserting compensatory hobby or romance. Ollie's ordinary-life counterweight is friendship, Earth rover relationships, local apprentice relationships, food, city events and the ability to stop working. Adding a new late hobby would look engineered.

## Objection E — `The arc is merely professional ethics.`
**REJECTED.**

The private wound is present before Vela: he is hurt when the rover team no longer needs him. Ch12 explicitly reveals the desire for their improved solution to fail enough to require him. Ch36 tests the same wound through nonurgent technical work and friendship. The professional choices are the behavioral expression of a personal need.
