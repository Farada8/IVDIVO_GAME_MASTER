The MAJOR WATCH is not confirmed.

Ollie's private-life material is intentionally quieter than Ethan/Aoife, Maya/Lea or Samir/Crossband because his central Book Two temptation is precisely that **work is the place where he feels most legible, useful and locally legitimate**. The novel makes this visible as a youth/status problem rather than treating engineering as neutral plot function.

The full-book movement is already on page:

`want real orbital usefulness / local legitimacy`
→ `boring first work tests whether he can earn trust`
→ `access/status and adult recognition become emotionally rewarding`
→ `work repeatedly displaces ordinary friendship/social time`

No hobby/romance/subplot insertion is justified.

# 1. WANT / STATUS / APPROVAL

## Ch05 — FIRST SHIFT

Ollie's first job is deliberately boring inventory and calibration support.

On-page desire:
- he wants work one permission layer closer to the real system;
- he watches the crawler feed while doing inventory;
- when Mara gives him one extra access tab, he tries and fails to hide pleasure;
- he asks more questions, wants more work and voluntarily stays around handover pressure;
- local apprentice Iri's judgment matters because Iri represents actual local competence, not abstract institutional praise.

The key youth/status beat is Ethan's private message after the traceability catch. Ollie smiles, then becomes irritated that he smiled because Ethan is not his supervisor and does not get to certify that maintenance was the correct choice. The kindness still matters.

This is not only competence. It is **approval hunger + independence from old group status**.

# 2. EMBARRASSMENT / PEER STATUS / SELF-CONSCIOUSNESS

## Ch12 — THE CLEAN MODEL

Ollie's status desire is explicit and socially visible:
- Ren identifies the face Ollie makes when he thinks he found something and wants others to ask;
- apprentices joke that he has multiple recognizable model-faces;
- when Mara says the model is `good enough to justify a bounded test`, the wording lands harder than praise;
- later `ORIGINATING ANALYST — OLLIE [YOUTH COHORT / SUPERVISED]` appears in the review record;
- Ollie minimizes the header when he notices it; Iri sees him do it and says nothing.

He wants legitimacy and is embarrassed by wanting it. That is a private/status pressure, not a technical requirement.

# 3. WORK COSTS FRIENDSHIP / ORDINARY YOUTH TIME

## Ch13 — VELA NIGHT

At 19:12, during Samir's exhibition and Vela Night, **Ollie is still at work**.

The group sends him the event/social thread; Maya says he missed Samir being almost competent; Ollie answers with the test request. The others leave the sport volume without him.

The point is not that Ollie has no friends. The point is that his desired local legitimacy is already consuming the exact youth life the book promises.

## Ch14 — ALMOST LOCAL

Ethan tries to gather all five.

Ollie can offer only a narrow uncertain window because of test prep. He finally reaches the bridge late, `tired and pleased` because something worked. Ethan tells him he earned the commissioning work; the approval lands awkwardly.

Within minutes Ollie's band calls him back because the second cycle moved earlier. He leaves the group again.

The five only share roughly six minutes together.

This is a clear behavioral price: **work does not merely occupy chapters; it displaces friendship time on page**.

# 4. ORDINARY PEER LIFE INSIDE WORK

Ollie's work chapters are not sterile capability demonstrations.

Across Ch05/12/15:
- Ren and Iri tease him about faces, access, breaks and being an Earth kid;
- people eat bad/unfinished food and bring performance leftovers to work;
- Iri has a private workshop block and performance-group life Ollie is curious about but not entitled to;
- Ollie experiences jealousy/status comparison around local permissions;
- he is embarrassed when his name appears in briefing material;
- he likes being credited and dislikes that he likes it;
- he learns that local belonging includes boring maintenance, visitor status, break timing and colleagues who refuse to answer every question.

His dominant intimacy line with Iri is work-rooted, but it is relational rather than mentor-oracle exposition.

# 5. PRICE AFTER FAILURE

## Ch20 → Ch21 — WRONG CAUSE / SHUT

The Ring Six failure turns status desire into consequence.

Ch21 gives Ollie a concrete personal price:
- incident data/access is placed on hold;
- he repeatedly tries to reopen a file he already knows he cannot access;
- he re-reads the decision chain looking for an impossible percentage of personal responsibility;
- his own support for LIMITED GO remains in the record beside board/safety/housing inputs;
- Mara refuses privileged raw access while his work is under review;
- Ollie explicitly says he wants to be useful **to Ring Six**, not merely useful somewhere;
- Mara sends him to an unrelated mature-ring calibration job instead;
- that ordinary job does not change his access state.

This is crucial. His central reward—being useful inside the important system—is removed.

Iri then names a deeper relationship failure: Ollie logged the messy resident/interface reports but did not fully hear them because they were not clean enough to enter the model as primary variables.

Ollie must practice stopping before defending himself and accept useful work that cannot restore his status.

# 6. CHANGED RELATION TO USEFULNESS

The later reconstruction does not restore Ollie's original fantasy.

He returns through:
- bounded evidence;
- explicit uncertainty;
- supervised scope;
- adult-owned authorization;
- narrower claims;
- willingness to leave unresolved evidence visible;
- action under pre-authorized local conditions rather than personal command.

His development is therefore not `become better engineer -> regain bigger authority`.

It is `learn that usefulness is real even when it does not give ownership, status or control`.

# 7. ORDINARY PLEASURE / HOME PAYOFF

## Ch33 — MIGRATION

This chapter closes the strongest remaining CHARACTER BEFORE ABILITY concern.

Ollie receives a narrow migration-watch role and likes it because the boundary is explicit.

Then ordinary life begins:
- people complain about storage;
- a bedroom light is slow;
- laundry, school bags, bread, food deliveries and badly routed carts become the problem;
- a maintenance ticket is completely unrelated to the Ring Six control mystery.

Iri catches Ollie smiling at the unrelated slow-light complaint.

Ollie's answer is effectively: **they are complaining about the room**.

The room is finally ordinary enough to disappoint its residents.

The prose then gives the decisive payoff:
for the next hour, nothing important happens; Ollie watches people miss turns, carry too much, ask about laundry and discover poor storage, and it is **one of the best hours he has spent on Vela**.

This is direct on-page ordinary pleasure. He is no longer happiest because the model is elegant. He is happy because the system has become someone's imperfect life.

# 8. FRIENDSHIP CHOICE / RESOLUTION

## Ch36 — A HOME THAT ISN'T FINISHED

Iri asks Ollie for a 21:00 route-side comparison.

Ollie checks his calendar and says:
`No.`

He nearly converts the answer into a longer justification, then gives the actual reason:
`I can do tomorrow morning. Tonight I have dinner.`

The work is nonurgent. Iri accepts tomorrow 08:40.

Ollie still feels guilty. The reflex is not magically erased.

Later the five negotiate dinner around Crossband, Maya's one-bag commitment, Aoife's export and Ollie's redesign work. The original fixed time fails; they arrive from five different lives.

During dinner Iri sends a photo of another bad sensor position marked `NOT URGENT`.

Ollie opens it, starts typing, then locks the band and says he will answer tomorrow.

This is the behavioral climax of his private-life line:
**he does not renounce work; he distinguishes urgent from merely interesting and chooses friends when the work can wait.**
