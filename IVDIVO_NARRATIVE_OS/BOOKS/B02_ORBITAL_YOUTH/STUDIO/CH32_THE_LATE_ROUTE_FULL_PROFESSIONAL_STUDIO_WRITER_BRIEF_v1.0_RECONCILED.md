## STORY FUNCTION

Convert Maya's already-earned autonomy into ordinary responsibility and a relationship payoff with Lea without replaying Ch24's privacy/honesty test.

Maya wants to repair things by being useful. Her wrong move is not lying again; it is turning usefulness into control — solving a household problem Lea did not ask her to solve. The chapter must end with a smaller but real form of trust: Lea asks Maya for one practical thing because Maya has learned to stay inside the requested boundary.

## HERO ENGINE

**HERO:** Maya.  
**WANT:** make things less bad with Lea and prove she can be useful during relocation pressure.  
**WHY NOW:** migration preparation is accelerating and Lea's household is still living with partial-opening consequences.  
**OPPOSITION:** damaged trust, household logistics that resist clever shortcuts, Maya's reflex to turn route competence into action before asking what other people want.  
**WRONG STRATEGY:** over-help; treat Lea's household like a solvable route problem; use competence as an apology.  
**PRICE:** Lea rejects the workaround and refuses to let Maya convert practical help into restored trust.  
**MIDPOINT:** Maya realizes the route solution is technically viable but socially wrong because it requires a household trade Lea did not choose.  
**CLIMAX CHOICE:** Maya asks what help is actually wanted and follows the narrower instruction even though it feels less impressive.  
**RESOLUTION:** Maya's existing autonomy protocol operates quietly in background; relocation staff use her in a bounded route-accompaniment role; Lea later initiates one practical request. Functional trust, not forgiveness.  
**HANDOFF:** Ch33 migration begins with Maya's route knowledge legitimately useful but adults retaining safety/clearance authority.

## SCENE 98 — JUST CARRY IT

Maya approaches Lea during practical move/waiting prep. Do not open with a speech.

Lea has concrete work: school case, storage bag, temporary-route handoff, laundry/household item or similar.

Maya starts to apologize / explain. Lea cuts the function down:
`I need hands, not another apology.`

Lea gives one specific task.

Maya's discomfort: the task is too small to feel like repair.

Turn: she accepts it anyway.

## SCENE 99 — THE WRONG HELP

A route or handoff complication appears.

Maya spots a clever solution using local late-route knowledge. The workaround is technically plausible but would require Lea's household to accept an unchosen trade — e.g. split handoff, extra move, changed school route, separate child/bag timing, or loss of predictability.

Maya starts acting as if the workaround is obviously better.

Lea stops her:
- do not turn Lea into a policy voice;
- keep the grievance concrete;
- the line underneath is `I asked you to help with this thing, not decide what my family should tolerate.`

Maya must not win the argument by proving the route is efficient.

Midpoint turn: Maya asks, `What do you actually want me to do?`

Lea gives a narrower task.

Maya follows it.

## SCENE 100 — THE LATE ROUTE

Maya's social plan changes while she is still in relocation work.

Use Ch24 autonomy protocol once, briefly and boringly:
- truthful zone/contact;
- material-plan-change update;
- no live tracking;
- host acknowledges.

No negotiation. No lesson. No danger.

Because Maya has legitimate late mobility and knows actual youth/pedestrian routes, an adult relocation coordinator gives her a bounded support task:
- accompany younger/peer residents through an approved late route;
- check whether signs make sense from the approach angle;
- carry/handoff one item;
- stop and call if route status changes.

Maya has **no clearance authority, no safety override, no command role**.

She can still make her social plan afterward. Freedom and responsibility coexist; this is not another sacrifice chapter.

Relationship payoff: Lea later messages/asks Maya first for one specific practical route help. Maya does exactly that, no extra fixing.

Exit into Ch33 migration.
