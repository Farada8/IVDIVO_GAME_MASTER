## STORY JOB
Move the book from technical diagnosis into civic/distribution conflict.

Ch27 gives two technically live operating paths:

A. **CONSERVATIVE RECOVERY INTERLOCK**
Wait for near/full physical return recovery before allowing relevant service combinations.
Pros: simpler safety argument, less dependence on live state estimation.
Costs: lower throughput, longer queues/holds, tighter maintenance windows, slower housing capacity.

B. **STATE-AWARE CONTROL**
Estimate residual/shared-loop state in real time and inhibit only risky combinations.
Pros: preserves more route/service capacity.
Costs: higher model dependence/complexity; dangerous escalation remains incompletely explained; embodied/support timing role unresolved.

Neither option is villainous. Neither is acceptable to everyone.
