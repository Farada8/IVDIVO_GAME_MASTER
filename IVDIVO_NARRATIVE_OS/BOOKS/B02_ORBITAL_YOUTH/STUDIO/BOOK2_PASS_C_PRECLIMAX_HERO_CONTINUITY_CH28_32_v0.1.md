## 1. Ch28 — HERO ABSENCE HAS FUNCTION

Ch28 belongs to Ethan because the question is not `what should the control system do?` but `who pays for each technically plausible direction?`.

Ethan is explicitly told to map consequences, not find the answer. He nevertheless builds a private scoring layer, exposes his own hidden weights and then removes them when he realizes engineering cannot supply the exchange rate between safety uncertainty, displaced households, access, maintenance and waiting.

This chapter therefore prepares a decision Ollie must not own. Giving it to Ollie would weaken the book's authority/epistemic law.

The Ring Six A-line remains central throughout.

## 2. Ch29 — OLLIE RE-ENTERS BEFORE THE ENSEMBLE RUN

Ch29 is a full Ollie POV, not a token cameo.

Protected protagonist work:
- Ollie remains under review hold / no unsupervised commissioning access;
- helps convert two bad global options into segment-level operating profiles;
- recognizes room compatibility is insufficient without route/service compatibility;
- privately creates a household sorter, then deletes the ranking rule when he sees that the order only appears after he chooses what matters first;
- faces Lea's household directly: two members fit, two do not;
- wants the work to return something to someone he knows but does not use the model to favor Lea;
- does not explain away her anger;
- accepts partial success: some households move, some wait, no clean alignment between technical categories and human fairness.

This is a major protagonist turn immediately before Ch30–32.

## 3. Ch30 — CONSEQUENCE, NOT SIDE-PLOT DETOUR

Aoife's WHO WAITS keeps the main story physically present:
- partial opening has already changed routes and household possibilities;
- families carry boxes and split school equipment from rooms;
- laundry, temporary addresses, guest access and route detours make `home` material;
- Source S1 remains waiting even though others move;
- Tomas's Ch20 injury/light-duty and changed commute remain active;
- a resident can support partial opening and still be left behind by it.

The chapter does not ask a new unrelated question. It pays the human consequence of the segmented solution Ollie helped build.

Ollie's physical absence is therefore not story absence.

## 4. Ch31 — ETHAN PAYOFF REMAINS INSIDE MIGRATION PRESSURE

THE ADULT DOOR pays the Book-One/Book-Two adulthood line but does not replace Ring Six:
- the preferred adult placement begins during active migration commitment;
- Ethan can leave Ring Six support and is replaceable;
- later intake is real but the same assignment is not guaranteed;
- he tells Aoife before deciding instead of managing her reaction after the fact;
- he chooses later intake and loses the immediate preferred slot;
- migration remains the current commitment and A-line.

This chapter gives another young character a real independent cost created by the same moment in the book rather than pausing the novel for unrelated closure.

## 5. Ch32 — MOVE-DAY HANDOFF, NOT EXTRA SETTLEMENT

THE LATE ROUTE is the strongest anti-drift evidence.

Maya/Lea's relationship repair is embedded in relocation work:
- Maya carries school-transfer equipment;
- she tries to optimize Lea's split household and is told the family already chose not to split;
- she learns to do the requested task rather than improve the household;
- route staging moves and Maya gives only the practical update;
- her standing host protocol is used rather than renegotiated;
- a bad temporary school-route marker becomes a concrete relocation problem;
- Maya earns only route/signage walk-through work; staff confirm changes and retain authority.

The chapter ends with Maya at a relocation cart at **06:30**.

Ch33 begins with Ollie at migration watch at **06:31**.

That one-minute continuity makes Ch32 an on-ramp to the climax rather than a delay after the story.

## 6. Ch33 — OLLIE DOES NOT NEED A PROTAGONIST REINTRODUCTION

Ch33 opens directly on Ollie's constrained migration assignment:
- review hold still exists;
- temporary role is read/mark/request-stop, no configuration authority;
- Mara makes him state the boundary;
- first households enter immediately;
- ordinary household life and operational pressure merge around the same Ring Six problem.

There is no recap explaining where Ollie has been, no new motivation, no reactivation scene and no new authority grant. The prose assumes continuous reader ownership of his arc.

That is evidence the Ch30–32 ensemble run has not broken protagonist continuity.

## 7. READER-PRESSURE TEST

Potential concern from the earlier Reader Advocate audit was valid: three consecutive non-Ollie POV chapters after Ch29 could feel like `settling accounts` before migration.

Current accepted prose resolves the risk through escalation rather than cameo insertion:
- Ch30 = what partial opening does to residents now;
- Ch31 = what migration timing costs Ethan now;
- Ch32 = move-day route reality now;
- Ch33 = actual migration one minute later.

Every exit narrows distance to move day.

Adding Ollie appearances to Ch30–32 would likely reduce rather than improve the design by making the ensemble orbit the protagonist and by stealing independent consequences from Aoife/Ethan/Maya.
