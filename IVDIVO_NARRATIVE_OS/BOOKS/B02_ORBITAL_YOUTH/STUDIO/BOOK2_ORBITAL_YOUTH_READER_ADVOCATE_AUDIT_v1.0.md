## EXECUTIVE VERDICT

**Why turn the page?** The novel has a credible answer in every act, but not always at equal intensity.

**Strongest reader engine:** `I want to live here -> can they make Ring Six genuinely livable -> Ollie's clean solution fails -> can they reopen anything without lying about safety -> will real people get home -> will Ollie act before certainty and accept the price?`

**Main reader risk:** the middle can temporarily shift from **story pressure** to **process pressure**. When that happens, the question changes from `what will happen to these people?` to `what will the next review discover?` The latter can sustain a chapter or two, but not long runs without housing, relationship, social or ordinary-life counterpressure.

**Second reader risk:** Ch28–32 contains excellent human material but may feel like the novel is settling accounts before allowing the promised migration/climax to happen.

**FATAL reader problems:** 0  
**MAJOR reader watches:** 2  
**MEDIUM watches:** 5  
**Immediate rewrite authorization:** NO

# 1. READER EMOTIONAL PROMISE BY ACT

## ACT I / Ch1–6

**Emotional question:** `Can Ollie and the others stop being visitors and begin having lives here?`

**What reader waits for:**
- first real address;
- first real independent competence;
- first work that matters;
- first place they want, not merely a place they are shown;
- first concrete proof orbital civilization changes ordinary youth life.

**Turn-page engine:** novelty + independence + social status + Ring Six desire.

**Reader Advocate verdict:** STRONG if Ollie's personal want is unmistakable by Ch3–6. Ch6 `THE RING PEOPLE WANT` is structurally crucial because it converts engineering real estate into emotional real estate.

**Watch:** if Ch1–2 read as transition spectacle rather than personal pressure, the novel may feel like it begins in Ch3. Continuous read must verify opening urgency.

## ACT I-B / Ch7–12

**Emotional question:** `Can Ollie become useful here without pretending he understands a civilization/system he just entered?`

**What reader waits for:**
- competence proof;
- local humiliation/correction;
- friendship/social life outside work;
- the first model that could actually change whether Ring Six opens.

**Energy sources:** Ch8 sport, Ch10 independence, Ch11 Aoife/source boundaries.

**Risk:** Ch7/9/12 can pull toward professional-process narrative. The youth/social chapters prevent that from becoming monotone.

**Reader Advocate verdict:** GOOD ARCHITECTURE / PROSE DENSITY WATCH.

## ACT II-A / Ch13–20

**Emotional question:** `The model is starting to work — is Ollie actually right, and what happens if everyone starts trusting it?`

**Turn-page engine:** false confidence + belonging + authorization + looming real-world test.

Ch13–14 are valuable because the reader gets ordinary life/belonging before the model gains institutional weight.

Ch15 LIMITED OPEN and Ch19 GO / NO-GO both promise consequence. Ch20 THE WRONG CAUSE pays that promise by breaking the model in the real world.

**Reader Advocate verdict:** STRONG POTENTIAL, but this is the first MAJOR WATCH.

### MAJOR WATCH A — FALSE-SUCCESS SATURATION
The reader must feel three distinct emotional escalations, not three technical approvals:
- Ch12: `Ollie may understand the problem.`
- Ch15: `This understanding may safely affect real operation.`
- Ch19: `People/institution are now prepared to act on it.`

If continuous prose makes those distinctions primarily procedural, Ch20 arrives after too much confirmation rather than after rising risk.

**Reader repair if confirmed:** strengthen the different **human thing now at stake** at each approval rather than adding more technical explanation.

## ACT II-B / Ch21–27

**Emotional question:** `After being wrong in a way that hurt someone, can Ollie earn a legitimate path back to useful action without grabbing control or treating people as data?`

This is the book's most intellectually coherent run and the highest **homework risk**.

The sequence contains:
- shutdown/responsibility;
- media/privacy aftermath;
- restricted evidence;
- Maya autonomy consequence;
- Samir sport/data boundary;
- second model;
- controlled reproduction.

The human chapters are not filler. They are what keeps the book's argument from becoming `better engineering fixes engineering`.

### MEDIUM WATCH B1 — PROCESS CLUSTER Ch21–23
Reader may experience `shutdown -> review -> evidence restriction` as one long institutional consequence unless Ch22 feels emotionally different enough.

### MEDIUM WATCH B2 — RECONSTRUCTION DELAY Ch23–26
After Ch23 establishes a lead, Ch24–25 defer Ollie's explicit second-model chapter. This can feel like healthy life continuing or like the mystery waiting for side plots.

The decision depends on whether Ch25's movement-order case delivers a clear new causal input into Ch26.

### MEDIUM WATCH B3 — Ch26–27 TECHNICAL CONCENTRATION
Second model + controlled reproduction are necessary, but the reader question must stay:
`Can this produce a safe way for people to live there?`
not merely:
`Will the test reproduce the state relationship?`

**Reader Advocate verdict:** STRUCTURALLY GOOD / HIGHEST CONTINUOUS-READ PRIORITY.

## ACT III-A / Ch28–32

**Emotional question:** `Now that the technical problem has narrowed, who gets what, who waits, and what does this cost the young characters' actual lives?`

This section is emotionally rich:
- Ch28 distributes technical consequences across housing/service/access;
- Ch29 creates partial solution;
- Ch30 makes waiting material;
- Ch31 pays Ethan's adult choice;
- Ch32 pays Maya/Lea/autonomy.

### MAJOR WATCH B — PRE-CLIMAX DEFERMENT
By Ch27, the reader has likely earned the desire to see the solution tested on actual life. Ch28–30 directly serves that desire. Ch31–32 may be experienced as two additional settlements before Ch33 finally starts migration.

This is not a reason to cut Ethan/Maya payoffs. It is a pressure/timing test:
- Are Ch31 and Ch32 brisk?
- Is move day imminent in their texture?
- Does every chapter exit push toward migration?
- Does Ollie's absence become felt as protagonist absence rather than ensemble breadth?

If yes, repair with tighter transitions/compression, not by removing character consequence.

**Reader Advocate verdict:** HIGH HUMAN VALUE / CLIMAX-DELAY WATCH.

## ACT III-B / Ch33–35

**Emotional question:** `Will the partial solution survive real people, and will Ollie act before certainty when doing so hurts people?`

This is the strongest sustained thriller/story run in the late book.

Ch33 earns hope first. Real households move in. Ordinary complaints prove habitation.

Ch34 makes the danger rational: planned reduced redundancy, competent adults, real load clustering, bounded new contingency, household approaching its room.

Ch35 makes the climax a choice rather than a surprise failure. Ollie acts under earned authority; the old system confirms later; residents still pay.

**Reader Advocate verdict:** STRONG / PROTECT.

Do not dilute this run in global edits with extra lore, additional reports or broader emergency scope.

## RESOLUTION / Ch36

**Emotional question:** `What did all of this actually change in how these people live?`

Required payoffs exist:
- work without coronation;
- home without perfection;
- different youth lives;
- friendship chosen despite schedules;
- no automatic external lore stinger under current ending authority.

**Reader Advocate verdict:** STRONG CURRENT CLOSURE. No epilogue needed unless continuous read finds an unpaid major emotional promise.

# 3. WHERE WONDER LIVES

Wonder in this book should not depend on giant Confederation lore reveals.

Current wonder sources:
- vertical/orbital transport and arrival;
- multiple addresses / habitat living;
- work in a system young characters genuinely participate in;
- Crossband / gravity-dependent sport;
- route/environment/body differences;
- first households entering a new ring;
- the absurdity of ordinary domestic life inside advanced orbital architecture.

**MEDIUM WATCH B4:** technical middle can become so diagnostic that wonder disappears. Continuous read should note the last moment in each 4-chapter block where the reader sees something they could only experience in this orbital civilization.

If a block has none, repair with existing world action, not lore exposition.

# 4. WHERE HUMOR LIVES

Humor functions best when produced by character/work reality:
- Ollie's literal category corrections;
- Maya physical/social confidence;
- Samir deadpan sport boundaries;
- Aoife framing irony;
- Ethan organization colliding with humans;
- Iri understatement;
- moving-house mistakes / bad food / bad storage / route signage.

**MEDIUM WATCH B5:** review/model chapters can strip humor out completely and make voices converge into exact institutional speech. Global line pass must preserve some human friction without inserting jokes into danger scenes.

# 5. WHAT IS LOST IF THEY FAIL?

The book's stakes are strongest when failure means:
- people remain in temporary housing;
- families stay split or routes remain damaging;
- young characters lose work/access/trust/opportunities;
- a new home exists physically but cannot yet become a life;
- competence without humility injures people;
- friendships survive only if characters stop managing one another.

The weakest version of the book would frame failure as `the engineering model is wrong`.

The strongest accepted version frames it as `people cannot safely live the future they were promised, and the young people may lose the legitimacy to help build it`.

Continuous read should flag any technical passage that loses the latter frame for too long.

# 6. SPECIFIC CONTINUOUS-READ QUESTIONS

Before authorizing repairs, answer these from prose, not architecture:

1. By the end of Ch3, do I care specifically what Ollie wants?
2. By Ch6, do I personally want Ring Six to work?
3. Does Ch12 feel exciting rather than merely competent?
4. Are Ch15 and Ch19 emotionally distinct approvals?
5. Does Ch20 hurt because I believed the model, or only because the plot says it failed?
6. Do Ch21–23 feel like consequence or paperwork?
7. Does Ch25 feel like Samir's sport/social life first and evidence second?
8. Does Ch26 restore Ollie's protagonist momentum strongly enough?
9. Does Ch27 close enough technical mystery that Ch28's civic question feels like escalation, not delay?
10. Do Ch31–32 make me impatient for Ch33 in a good way or a bad way?
11. Does Ch33 make me happy people are moving in before Ch34 threatens that success?
12. At the Ch34 cut, do I understand what Ollie may lose by pressing the control?
13. In Ch35, do I believe both `he was right to act` and `the household is right to be angry`?
14. Does Ch36 make me want to remain in this civilization even after seeing its costs?

# 7. REPAIR AUTHORIZATION STANDARD

A watch becomes a repair only if the continuous read produces a concrete symptom, e.g.:
- `I forgot why Ring Six mattered from ChX to ChY`;
- `Ch15 and Ch19 felt like the same chapter function`;
- `I stopped feeling Ollie was protagonist for N consecutive chapters`;
- `I understood the technical distinction but stopped caring about the next result`;
- `Ch31/32 made the climax feel delayed rather than intensified`.

Then create one targeted repair card with minimum intervention.

Do not repair a chapter merely because the architecture says a risk exists.
