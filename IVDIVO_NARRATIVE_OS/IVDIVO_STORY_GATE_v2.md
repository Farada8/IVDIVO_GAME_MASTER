# IVDIVO STORY GATE v2

**Status:** ACTIVE PRODUCTION GATE / WORKING REFINEMENT  
**Authority:** subordinate to Founder instruction and approved IVDIVO canon.  
**Reference support:** `REFERENCE_MCKEE_STORY_MECHANICS.md` plus existing IVDIVO Writers’ Room law.

---

# A. BOOK / MAJOR STORY CORE — MUST EXIST BEFORE PROSE

1. HERO
2. WANT
3. WHY NOW
4. INCITING IMBALANCE
5. OPPOSITION
6. WRONG STRATEGY
7. PRICE
8. MIDPOINT / POINT OF NO RETURN
9. CRISIS CHOICE
10. CLIMACTIC ACTION
11. RESOLUTION
12. PERMANENT CHANGE
13. SERIES HOOK — only after main conflict closes

If any of 1–12 is missing, the story is not ready for prose.

---

# B. CAUSALITY GATE

For every major sequence verify:

`HERO ACTION -> EXPECTED RESULT -> GAP -> ACTUAL RESULT -> CONSEQUENCE -> NEXT NECESSITY`

FAIL if major episodes can be freely reordered without damaging causality.

FAIL if coincidence solves the central problem.

FAIL if exposition rather than action supplies the decisive turn.

---

# C. PROGRESSIVE PRESSURE GATE

Each major sequence must increase at least one dimension:

- risk;
- cost;
- opposition;
- social consequence;
- emotional consequence;
- moral difficulty;
- irreversibility;
- loss of options.

After a point of no return, do not return to lesser stakes as though nothing changed.

---

# D. CHARACTER TRUTH GATE

For each major character ask:

- What do they claim to value?
- What do they actually want?
- What do they fear losing?
- What choice will expose the contradiction?
- What do they pay?

FAIL if character is proven only through biography, exposition or other characters praising them.

Character must be demonstrated by behavior and choice under pressure.

---

# E. SCENE GATE

Every scene must answer:

1. WHO WANTS WHAT?
2. WHY NOW?
3. WHAT STOPS THEM?
4. OPENING VALUE.
5. ACTION / TACTIC.
6. EXPECTED RESULT.
7. GAP / UNEXPECTED RESPONSE.
8. TURN.
9. CLOSING VALUE.
10. WHAT CHANGES / WHAT CAUSE IS CREATED NEXT?

CUT / REBUILD if the scene merely delivers lore, mood or information and nothing changes.

---

# F. DIALOGUE GATE

Important dialogue requires:

`OBJECTIVE -> RESISTANCE -> TACTIC CHANGE -> COUNTERACTION -> STATUS / KNOWLEDGE / RELATIONSHIP CHANGE`

FAIL if:

- characters take equal turns explaining;
- everyone gives the smartest possible answer;
- nobody has a private objective;
- conversation ends exactly where it began;
- dialogue explains the theme after the action already showed it.

---

# G. MIDPOINT GATE

Midpoint must do more than reveal information.

It should create at least two of the following:

- irreversible commitment;
- reinterpretation of the goal;
- new level of opposition;
- loss of an easier path;
- personal betrayal / relationship turn;
- price becoming real;
- protagonist becoming causally responsible for what follows.

---

# H. CRISIS / CLIMAX GATE

CRISIS = unavoidable choice between genuinely costly alternatives.

CLIMAX = protagonist acts on that choice.

FAIL if climax is:

- a speech;
- a realization with no decisive action;
- rescue by a superior authority;
- coincidence;
- antagonist suddenly becoming stupid;
- technology introduced at the last moment;
- Confederation solving a human story merely because it can.

---

# I. RESOLUTION GATE

Before a series hook opens, confirm:

- central external conflict is resolved;
- hero’s decisive choice has consequences;
- price is visible;
- key relationship state has changed;
- world has permanently changed where relevant;
- reader understands what this story was about through events, not a final lecture.

---

# J. WORLD-THROUGH-STORY GATE

For every major worldbuilding element ask:

- Which character needs it?
- What does it let them do?
- What problem does it create?
- What social behavior has grown around it?
- What happens if it fails?

FAIL if a technology/institution can be removed without changing character decisions or plot.

---

# K. FINAL STORY FORMULA

`DESIRE -> ACTION -> GAP -> CONSEQUENCE -> HARDER ACTION -> PRICE -> POINT OF NO RETURN -> CRISIS CHOICE -> CLIMACTIC ACTION -> COMPLETED RESOLUTION -> PERMANENT CHANGE`

**STORY FIRST. CHARACTER SECOND. WORLD THROUGH STORY. SAGA THROUGH COMPLETED BOOKS.**
