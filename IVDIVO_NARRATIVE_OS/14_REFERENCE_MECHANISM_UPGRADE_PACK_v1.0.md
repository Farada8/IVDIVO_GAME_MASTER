---

# 0. SOURCE DISCIPLINE

Reference evidence is weighted by source quality, not file count.

Required distinctions:
- `LIKELY_FULL` — may support macro-architecture analysis after completeness verification where ending matters;
- `FRAGMENT_EXPLICIT` — opening/demo only; never infer whole-book arc or ending;
- `COLLECTION_UNVERIFIED` — split/identify works before structural conclusions;
- `SCANNED_TEXT_UNAVAILABLE` — visual/source exists but text evidence is insufficient;
- `DUPLICATE` — mirror/copy; adds zero independent weight.

Every reusable mechanism should record:
`FUNCTION / HUMAN EFFECT / ABSTRACT FORMULA / CONDITIONS / FAILURE MODE / COPY BOUNDARY / POSSIBLE IVDIVO USE`.

---

# 1. CONTROLLED REVELATION LOOP

Preferred general loop:

`CONCRETE HUMAN TASK OR WANT`
→ `COLLIDES WITH A RULE / UNKNOWN / INSTITUTION`
→ `OBSERVABLE CONSEQUENCE`
→ `CHARACTER UPDATES THEIR MODEL`
→ `NEW KNOWLEDGE CREATES A HARDER DECISION`.

This is stronger than exposition because information changes action.

Failure modes:
- explanation arrives before lived consequence;
- new fact does not change any decision;
- reveal exists only to feed lore/franchise setup;
- character accepts explanation without testing it against desire/fear.

---

# 2. EXPERIENCE BEFORE EXPLANATION

Use:
`SYMPTOM / RESULT / BEHAVIOR / SOCIAL CONSEQUENCE -> INTERPRETATION -> TERMINOLOGY -> DEEPER ONTOLOGY`.

World rules become memorable when the reader first experiences what they cost somebody.

Do not turn this into permanent vagueness; explanation should arrive when it enables a more consequential action.

---

# 3. COMPETENCE BEFORE CONFESSION

A character becomes credible through specific work, judgment, restraint, error-correction and responsibility before explanatory backstory or emotional confession.

Useful sequence:
`TASK -> METHOD -> TRADEOFF -> CONSEQUENCE -> HUMAN DISCLOSURE`.

Failure: competence pornography with no price, or confession used to substitute for demonstrated character.

---

# 4. ATTACHMENT THROUGH BEHAVIOR

Attachment is stronger when readers infer it from repeated choices:
- noticing;
- remembering;
- making room;
- protecting another’s agency;
- accepting cost;
- changing routine;
- respecting a boundary;
- returning when there is no applause.

Do not require explicit romantic declaration for every relational movement.

---

# 5. ONTOLOGY SERVES A HUMAN WANT

A metaphysical/scientific mystery should attach to a concrete want, fear, obligation or relationship.

Test:
`If the ontology vanished, what immediate human problem would remain?`

If the answer is “none,” the story may be lore-led rather than character-led.

---

# 6. ANSWER -> ACTION, NOT ANSWER -> LECTURE

A reveal is valuable when it alters:
- next objective;
- suspect/hypothesis;
- relationship trust;
- authority;
- risk;
- location;
- resource;
- moral choice.

Each important answer may open a larger question, but the larger question must change conduct rather than merely enlarge mythology.

---

# 7. FINITE NEAR-TERM PRESSURE WINDOW

Large worlds become dramatic when a huge problem compresses into a concrete deadline, access window, custody period, election, shift, launch, medical threshold, evacuation, contract, hearing, journey or other finite near-term constraint.

`LARGE SYSTEMIC PROBLEM + FINITE LOCAL WINDOW = PLAYABLE PRESSURE`.

Avoid arbitrary ticking clocks with no causal basis.

---

# 8. HIERARCHY THROUGH BODY / LABOR / ACCESS

Before naming classes, ranks or institutions, show them through:
- who waits;
- who carries;
- who repairs;
- who gets a private room;
- who has keys/permissions;
- who can interrupt whom;
- who has transport;
- who can refuse;
- who gets believed;
- who absorbs risk.

This reveals social systems through lived behavior instead of policy explanation.

---

# 9. CONTROL IS NOT COMPETENCE IS NOT STATUS IS NOT OFFICE

Track separately:
- formal office;
- practical expertise;
- physical/technical control;
- social prestige;
- legal authority;
- information access;
- emotional leverage.

Scenes become more specific when these do not collapse into one generic “power.”

---

# 10. ROLE / PERSON CONTRADICTION

Strong characters often contain a conflict between institutional/social role and private person.

Examples as abstract forms only:
`PROTECTOR who must respect refusal`;
`EXPERT who cannot control outcome`;
`PUBLIC SYMBOL who wants private autonomy`;
`INVESTIGATOR who becomes emotionally non-neutral`;
`CARETAKER whose help can become control`.

The contradiction should generate choices, not just biography.

---

# 11. SELF-PROTECTIVE INTERPRETATION

Characters do not merely receive facts. They interpret them in ways that preserve identity, status, hope, innocence, love or prior commitment.

Use:
`FACT -> SELF-PROTECTIVE MODEL -> ACTION -> COST -> MODEL PRESSURE`.

This creates believable delay without making the character stupid.

---

# 12. HELP WITHOUT CONSENT CAN REPEAT CONTROL

A benevolent act may reproduce the same domination the story criticizes if it removes another person’s choice.

Check:
- Was help requested?
- Could the person refuse?
- Is important information withheld “for their own good”?
- Is rescue being used to purchase intimacy/obedience?
- Does competence erase consent?

Do not apply mechanically; the point is to distinguish care from control under the actual story conditions.

---

# 13. MEANINGFUL CHOICE BEFORE CULPABILITY

When possible, let a suspect/character make an autonomous consequential choice before the narrative fully decides how guilty/innocent the audience should believe they are.

This protects personhood from becoming merely a mystery function.

For mysteries, preserve the strongest reasonable innocent interpretation until evidence actually defeats it.

---

# 14. CHOICE PROVES PERSONHOOD

In AI/synthetic-life/nonhuman/consciousness stories, personhood is more dramatically persuasive through:
- independent preference;
- refusal;
- responsibility;
- sacrifice with alternatives;
- self-authored goal;
- conflict between programmed/assigned role and chosen action.

Avoid solving personhood only with speeches about consciousness.

---

# 15. CHAPTER / EPISODE EXIT LAW

Prefer exits that change:
`INFORMATION / DECISION / RELATIONSHIP / STATUS / LOCATION / RISK / COMMITMENT / QUESTION QUALITY`.

Do not rely on generic danger, arbitrary mid-action cuts or empty “something moved in the dark” hooks.

A good exit makes the next unit more necessary or more desired.

---

# 16. MECHANISM COMBINATION LAW

Originality improves when independent mechanisms are combined through the active story rather than when one admired reference is imitated deeply.

Recommended process:
1. identify current story problem;
2. retrieve 2–3 mechanisms by function;
3. remove source-specific content;
4. combine only compatible causal functions;
5. transform through active hero/world/relationship/institution;
6. run similarity/source-distance audit;
7. reject anything still recognizably dependent on one source’s distinctive execution.

---

# 17. MECHANISM ACCEPTANCE TEST

A mechanism enters current work only if it improves at least one real decision:
- stronger causality;
- more specific character behavior;
- better reader/listener comprehension;
- stronger pressure;
- cleaner evidence logic;
- more truthful relationship movement;
- more original world expression;
- more effective scene/episode propulsion.

Interesting terminology alone is not enough.
