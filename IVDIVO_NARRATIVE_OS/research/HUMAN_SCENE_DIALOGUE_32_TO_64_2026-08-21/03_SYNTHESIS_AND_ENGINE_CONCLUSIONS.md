# 03 — SYNTHESIS + ENGINE CONCLUSIONS

**Status:** RESEARCH SYNTHESIS COMPLETE  
**Source cycle:** 32/32 prompts executed  
**Authority boundary:** conclusions improve writing execution; they do not alter locked story facts.

## A. Central diagnosis

The current IVDIVO stack already has strong macro-story, causality, continuity, mystery, character-objective and scene-gate control. The remaining credibility risk is often **micro-causal**:

`correct story beat -> polished generated line -> insufficiently specific human cause`.

The system therefore must validate not only WHAT a scene needs communicated, but WHY THIS PERSON notices THIS stimulus, interprets it THIS way, chooses THIS tactic and reaches for THESE exact words NOW.

## B. Three levels of dialogue truth

### Level 1 — Structural truth
Does the scene have a real conflict/turn, or is dialogue doing structural work that the scene failed to create?

### Level 2 — Behavioral truth
Are lines caused by desire, risk, status, perception, listening, relationship history and tactical change?

### Level 3 — Linguistic/performance truth
Do vocabulary, syntax, cadence, register, humor, silence, translation and performance belong to this character and medium?

**Repair order is 1 -> 2 -> 3.** Never polish Level 3 to hide a Level 1 failure.

## C. Promoted/recommended mechanisms

### M01 — CAUSE-TO-LINE TRACE
For every suspect major line:
`stimulus/perception -> interpretation -> immediate want -> risk -> tactic -> wording -> intended effect`.
If one link is missing, classify the line rather than cosmetically rewriting it.

### M02 — EVIDENCE / PERCEPTION LADDER
Separate:
`OBSERVED -> HEARD/TOLD -> REMEMBERED -> INFERRED -> SUSPECTED -> BELIEVED -> KNOWN -> UNKNOWN -> WRONG`.
Then add confidence. This prevents overly knowing/perceptive characters and protects mystery causality.

### M03 — ATTENTION SPLIT
A character can possess knowledge without actively attending to it. Track:
`AVAILABLE KNOWLEDGE / ACTIVE ATTENTION / INTERPRETATION`.
This is critical under stress and in group scenes.

### M04 — EMOTIONAL LATENCY REGISTER
Classify response to important emotional stimuli:
`IMMEDIATE / DELAYED / DISPLACED / SUPPRESSED / PRIVATE_PROCESSING / ACTION_BEFORE_WORDS`.
Perfect instant self-understanding becomes a review trigger rather than default.

### M05 — RELATIONSHIP SHORTHAND + MISREAD LEDGER
For recurring pairs/groups preserve:
- mutually known facts;
- private jokes/gestures;
- repeated arguments;
- avoided subjects;
- what each routinely misreads in the other;
- phrases/actions that have acquired relationship-specific meaning.
This permits compression without explanatory dialogue.

### M06 — SPEECH PATTERN MATRIX
Character voice is not only vocabulary. Track by speaker:
- sentence frames;
- correction/reframe habits;
- question forms;
- certainty/hedging;
- metaphor domains;
- humor geometry;
- argument choreography;
- response length under pressure;
- apology/refusal style;
- phrases/devices that are specifically NOT theirs.

### M07 — DIALOGUE BURDEN CHECK
Ask whether dialogue is being forced to generate missing structure, theme or causality. If removing a speech collapses basic scene logic, inspect upstream architecture before line-editing.

### M08 — BEAT DEPENDENCY TEST
For important dialogue sequences, beat N+1 must be causally altered by beat N. If lines can be freely reordered, the scene is likely activity/exposition rather than dramatic action.

### M09 — GROUP ATTENTION GRAPH
For 3+ character scenes map who is actually watching/listening to whom, what else competes for attention, where alliances/status redirect attention, and which signals are missed by some speakers. Avoid round-robin “everyone heard everything” staging.

### M10 — HUMOR OWNERSHIP
Define what kind of humor each recurring character uses, under what pressure, and what they would not naturally joke about. Shared dry semantic wit across the whole cast becomes cross-speaker leakage unless earned by culture/relationship.

### M11 — INSTITUTIONAL REGISTER QUARANTINE
In technical/procedural stories map who naturally owns formal vocabulary, who paraphrases it, who resents it, who misuses it, who hides behind it and who avoids it. Prevent the world bible from speaking through every character.

### M12 — COMPETENCE / PERSONHOOD SPLIT
Technical competence must not become the entire voice. In competence-heavy scenes preserve a personhood signal that affects behavior: shame, impatience, class/status sensitivity, humor, memory, family pressure, bodily strain, attraction, resentment, fear of error, etc.

### M13 — NARRATOR STYLE VS SPEAKER LEAKAGE
Author/narrator house style can exist. The defect is when unrelated speakers inherit the same rhetorical structures. Audit narrative voice separately from character voice.

### M14 — THREE-LAYER MEDIA TRIAGE
For performed/localized fiction classify a break as:
`SOURCE_TEXT / LOCALIZATION / PERFORMANCE`.
Do not rewrite English source text because Russian localization or TTS timing failed.

## D. New mandatory diagnostic chain for high-risk scenes

`SCENE STRUCTURE PASS -> HUMAN STATE -> EVIDENCE/PERCEPTION -> ATTENTION -> RELATIONSHIP SHORTHAND/MISREAD -> IMMEDIATE WANT/RISK -> TACTIC -> CAUSE-TO-LINE -> SPEECH PATTERN -> BEAT DEPENDENCY -> HOUSE-STYLE/GROUP ATTENTION -> TEXT READ-ALOUD -> LOCALIZATION/PERFORMANCE TRIAGE -> HUMAN BELIEF GATE`.

## E. Severity additions

### FATAL
- line transfers impossible knowledge that breaks causality/mystery;
- wording falsifies consent/authority/identity material to the story.

### MAJOR
- scene depends on dialogue doing missing structural work;
- multiple major speakers converge on one rhetorical engine;
- emotional insight arrives implausibly without latency where stakes demand it;
- group scene assumes universal attention and comprehension when status/task conditions contradict it;
- institutional/documentation language overwhelms personhood.

### MEDIUM
- one local correction cluster;
- weak trigger-to-response link;
- repeated syntax across speakers;
- overly neat subtext resolution;
- humor owned by the author rather than the character.

## F. Decisions by project

### LESSON ZERO
- no bulk rewrite;
- current forensic signals remain hypotheses;
- select representative 45–60 minute sample;
- preserve exact source text on first premium performance diagnostic;
- add speech-pattern matrix, relationship shorthand and source/localization/performance triage;
- patch only moments confirmed as source-text defects.

### BLOODBOUND
- continue live use immediately because the new layer already caught one real correction-cluster defect;
- apply M01–M13 selectively to E08–E24 and block gates;
- keep custody/consent power geometry explicit.

### SMITH BOOK 3
- use full high-risk scene chain before substantive prose for first appearances, institutional confrontations, intimate conflict, major mystery interviews, midpoint relationship turns, climax and emotional resolution;
- build speech matrices before large drafting rather than attempting a whole-book cleanup later.

### Doramas
- prioritize relationship shorthand, status/permission, emotional latency, humor ownership, subtext and beat dependency;
- do not confuse serial cliffhanger efficiency with people speaking in trailer lines.

### Audio
- use performance as diagnostic evidence, not automatic proof of prose weakness;
- preserve listener markers and map each break to source/localization/performance layer.

## G. Final synthesis

The next engine objective is:

**NOT:** “make dialogue sound more like real life.”

**YES:** “make every important line feel like the unavoidable but still surprising consequence of a specific human being perceiving, wanting, risking and acting in a specific relationship at a specific moment.”

The 64-prompt next cycle is designed to test, operationalize and stress this conclusion rather than repeat the 32 basic questions.
