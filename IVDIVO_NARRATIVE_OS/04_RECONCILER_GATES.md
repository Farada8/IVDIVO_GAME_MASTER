# IVDIVO NARRATIVE OS — RECONCILER & GATES

**Status:** CANONICAL OPERATING STANDARD  
**Version:** 1.0

---

# 1. WHY RECONCILIATION EXISTS

A multi-agent studio fails if every specialist’s advice is applied simultaneously.

Typical conflict:
- Character Agent wants another intimacy scene.
- Reader Reception says the middle drags.
- Market Agent wants a faster pace.
- World Agent wants more institutional context.
- Mystery Agent wants to delay an answer.
- Continuity says the proposed scene is impossible on the current date.

The Reconciler does not average these positions.

It decides which problem is real, which recommendation actually solves it and what collateral damage each change creates.

---

# 2. RECONCILER DECISION ORDER

When reports conflict, apply this order:

1. Founder’s direct instruction.
2. Governing canon.
3. Locked consequences from earlier books.
4. Completion of the current story.
5. Causality.
6. Character truth.
7. Emotional impact.
8. Reader engagement.
9. Continuity.
10. World logic.
11. Genre promise.
12. Market fit.
13. Franchise opportunity.
14. Prose polish.

A lower level cannot overrule a higher level without explicit Founder decision.

---

# 3. CONFLICT RECORD

For every material disagreement A19 records:

`ISSUE`  
`POSITION A`  
`POSITION B`  
`EVIDENCE`  
`COST IF A`  
`COST IF B`  
`DECISION`  
`WHY`  
`REGRESSION RISK`  
`VALIDATION TEST`

Do not record minor stylistic disagreements unless they affect the result.

---

# 4. STORY CORE GATE

A novel/major story cannot advance to architecture until all are present:

- hero;
- concrete want;
- why now;
- opposition;
- wrong strategy;
- stakes/price;
- midpoint function;
- climax choice;
- resolution;
- permanent change;
- series hook after closure.

## RED automatically if
- protagonist has no actionable want;
- stakes are only lore importance;
- opposition does not resist;
- climax is only revelation or spectacle;
- main story has no resolution;
- book exists mainly to set up Book N+1.

---

# 5. ARCHITECTURE GATE

Requires:
- causal movement from opening to ending;
- escalation;
- meaningful midpoint;
- protagonist action causing consequences;
- major relationships changing;
- world responding to events;
- setup/payoff plan;
- no major canon contradiction.

## Tests
### Because Test
Can major movements be phrased:
`because X, therefore Y` rather than `and then`?

### Removal Test
If a movement is removed, does later causality change?

### Protagonist Agency Test
Does the story still happen if the protagonist stays home? If yes, redesign protagonist function.

### Ending Test
Does the climax choice arise from the book’s central pressure rather than a late new problem?

---

# 6. CHAPTER MAP GATE

Every chapter needs:
- immediate function;
- POV;
- entering state;
- want;
- resistance;
- turn;
- price/change;
- exit state;
- causal relation to adjacent chapters;
- reader reason to continue.

## RED if
- chapter exists only for exposition;
- function repeats prior chapter with no escalation;
- entry state equals exit state;
- cliffhanger is the only reason chapter exists;
- chapter can move anywhere in book with no effect.

---

# 7. SCENE GATE

Every scene must answer:
- who wants what;
- why now;
- what stops them;
- what tactic they try;
- how resistance changes;
- what choice/action occurs;
- what changes at exit.

## Scene decision
`KEEP / REBUILD / MERGE / CUT`.

Do not keep a scene only because the dialogue is attractive.

---

# 8. PRE-DRAFT GREENLIGHT

Before A15 writes prose, A19 verifies:

- Story Core GREEN;
- architecture GREEN;
- relevant chapter/scene cards GREEN;
- continuity checked;
- POV assigned;
- character wants defined;
- forbidden inventions listed;
- system rules known where relevant;
- setup/payoff obligations known.

Output: `GREENLIGHT_DECISION`.

No GREEN = no full prose, unless Founder explicitly orders exploratory drafting.

Exploratory prose must be labelled WORKING and cannot silently become canon.

---

# 9. POST-DRAFT GATE

After drafting, evaluate the text that actually exists.

Check:
- did prose preserve architecture?
- did characters make the intended choices for believable reasons?
- did dialogue perform action?
- did scene geography remain clear?
- did worldbuilding enter through story?
- were unintended new canon facts introduced?
- did pacing differ from outline expectations?
- did prose create new contradictions?

A good outline does not automatically make a good chapter.

---

# 10. RED TEAM GATE

A16 returns severity.

## FATAL
Must repair before continuation.

## MAJOR
Normally repair before book lock; may proceed temporarily only if dependency requires larger context.

## MEDIUM
Prioritize by reader effect and recurrence.

## POLISH
Do not block structural work.

A19 may reject Red Team recommendations if evidence is weak or repair cost damages higher priorities.

---

# 11. COMPLETE STORY UNIT GATE

For case/episode/multi-chapter unit verify:

`INITIAL STATE -> DISRUPTION -> GOAL -> RESISTANCE -> ATTEMPTS -> ESCALATION -> ERROR/PRICE -> NEW UNDERSTANDING -> DECISIVE CHOICE -> CLIMAX -> RESULT -> CONSEQUENCE -> NEW STATE`.

Local story closes before series hook.

If the unit ends only because pages stop, RED.

---

# 12. READER RECEPTION GATE

Use simulated reception for diagnosis, but use real readers when available for lock-changing decisions.

Track:
- first curiosity;
- first boredom;
- stop/skim point;
- favorite character without prompting;
- confusion;
- emotional expectation;
- payoff satisfaction;
- desire for next chapter/book.

Classify:
`CONSENSUS FAILURE / SEGMENT ISSUE / PERSONAL TASTE / INTENTIONAL FRICTION`.

Do not rewrite from one reader’s prescribed solution.

Look for convergence on the symptom.

---

# 13. FINAL STORY GATE

A book passes only if:

- main conflict resolved;
- climax is caused by earlier story;
- protagonist makes meaningful choice;
- price is real;
- major character arc reaches a new state;
- world retains consequences;
- ordinary life has not vanished into franchise machinery;
- setup/payoff obligations are satisfied or intentionally carried forward;
- series hook does not invalidate closure;
- no FATAL continuity contradiction remains.

Verdict:
`PASS STRONG / PASS / PASS WITH SPECIFIC PATCH / FAIL`.

---

# 14. DEVELOPMENT LOCK GATE

Development may lock after:
1. Story Core.
2. Architecture.
3. Causal Chapter Map.
4. Draft.
5. Development Edit.
6. Red Team.
7. Character/Relationship Pass.
8. Reader Advocate/Reception.
9. Line Pass.
10. Final Story Gate.

Lock means:
- no new structural pass without new evidence;
- market/package work does not reopen story automatically;
- copyedit does not expand into developmental rewriting;
- future books inherit consequences.

---

# 15. UNLOCK GATE

A locked book may reopen only on:

- Founder instruction;
- convergent external reader feedback;
- acquiring editor/publisher note identifying concrete problem;
- continuity/factual error;
- demonstrated comprehension failure;
- demonstrated pacing failure;
- new canon decision that necessarily changes the locked text.

Before reopening A19 records:
`NEW EVIDENCE -> AFFECTED AREA -> WHY LOCK NO LONGER HOLDS -> MINIMUM REQUIRED SCOPE`.

Do not reopen whole book if one chapter needs correction.

---

# 16. REGRESSION GATE

After every significant patch ask:

1. Did the targeted problem improve?
2. Did causality change?
3. Did motivation weaken?
4. Did voice change?
5. Did pacing shift?
6. Did continuity break?
7. Did setup/payoff move?
8. Did mystery evidence change?
9. Did world rules change?
10. Did new exposition appear?
11. Did local story still close?
12. Did series hook remain secondary?

Output:
`PASS / PARTIAL / REGRESSION`.

Never call a patch successful only because the requested sentence was changed.

---

# 17. SAGA CONTINUITY GATE

Before Book N lock:

Check previous books for:
- ages;
- dates;
- injuries;
- relationships;
- deaths/loss;
- knowledge;
- secrets;
- abilities;
- technology;
- institutions;
- geography;
- political state;
- unresolved setups;
- promises to reader.

New book must inherit consequences, not reset the world.

---

# 18. MARKET CONFLICT EXAMPLES

## Market says “add romance.”
If relationship engine does not support it -> reject.

## Market says “cut 15k.”
First identify reader/production friction. Cut explanation/repetition before story-bearing life.

## Market says “add villain.”
If system conflict is stronger and causally active -> reject cartoon villain.

## Market says “make climax bigger.”
Bigger spectacle does not outrank more meaningful choice.

---

# 19. CHARACTER VS PACING EXAMPLES

A character scene may remain even if low-action when it:
- changes relationship;
- creates later choice;
- pays emotional investment;
- establishes ordinary-life cost;
- changes reader attachment.

A fast action scene may be cut if it changes nothing.

Pacing is not synonymous with speed.

---

# 20. FINAL RECONCILER LAW

The Reconciler’s product is not consensus.

It is a defensible production decision with known tradeoffs.
