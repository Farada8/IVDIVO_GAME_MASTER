## Global execution verdict

The current Naturalism Engine is directionally strong. The most important remaining weaknesses are not `more dialogue rules`; they are:
1. character-specific **perception/listening models** before words;
2. **relationship-specific conversational history** and learned shortcuts;
3. explicit distinction between **competence voice** and **private voice**;
4. **emotional latency** and delayed cognition;
5. group-conversation **attention topology** rather than turn-taking symmetry;
6. **speech entropy / convergence** checks across chapters and speakers;
7. better separation of **page-text quality vs actor/performance quality vs localization**;
8. a production loop that turns real listener failures into targeted Writing Engine improvements rather than global rewrites.

# CLUSTER A — HUMAN PSYCHOLOGY / PERCEPTION / LISTENING

### P01 — What did the character actually perceive before answering?
**Execution:** Current engine asks what was heard, but production artifacts should store `PERCEPTION_TARGET`: words / tone / silence / gesture / status move / environmental event. Many writerly replies are caused by answering the author's intended meaning rather than what the person plausibly perceived.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT -> add perception target to scene card/QC.

### P02 — What did the character fail to notice?
**Execution:** Natural speech depends as much on missed information as processed information. Perfect observation produces overly perceptive dialogue.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT -> add `MISSED_SIGNAL` field for high-risk scenes.

### P03 — How does stress narrow cognition?
**Execution:** Under fear, fatigue or overload, characters should often compress vocabulary, repeat known frames, answer concretely, or fixate. Polished synthesis under acute stress requires character-specific justification.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P04 — Does emotion arrive before understanding?
**Execution:** Current prose sometimes allows characters to understand the emotional meaning immediately. Realistic scenes often need `AFFECT_FIRST -> INTERPRET_LATER`.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT -> emotional latency mechanism.

### P05 — Is the response driven by self-protection or information processing?
**Execution:** A line can be logically accurate but psychologically false when a threatened character would first defend status/identity.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P06 — Does the speaker know what they feel?
**Execution:** Characters should not automatically possess therapist-level emotional labels. Emotional vocabulary must be fingerprinted.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT -> `SELF_AWARENESS_BAND`.

### P07 — What memory is activated by this line?
**Execution:** Relationship dialogue is often filtered through earlier conflicts. Store only canon-supported `ACTIVE_MEMORY_TRIGGER`; do not invent flashback psychology.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT WITH LIMIT.

### P08 — What is the character trying not to think about?
**Execution:** Avoidance can cause topic shifts, overprecision, jokes and task-focus. This is stronger than adding generic pauses.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

# CLUSTER B — RELATIONSHIP REALITY / HISTORY

### P09 — What conversational shortcuts has this relationship earned?
**Execution:** Close relationships use abbreviated references, unfinished sentences, known jokes and shared silences. Generic complete sentences underuse relationship history.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT -> `RELATIONSHIP_SHORTHAND`.

### P10 — What topics have already been argued before?
**Execution:** Repeating a familiar conflict should not sound like first-time exposition. Characters may skip premises and jump to old fault lines.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P11 — What phrase would uniquely irritate this partner?
**Execution:** Conflict becomes specific when wounds are relationship-specific rather than universally eloquent accusations.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P12 — What kindness would this person distrust?
**Execution:** History changes how neutral lines are received; this improves subtext without adding dialogue.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P13 — What apology form is credible for this speaker?
**Execution:** Some characters repair through action, logistics, humor or partial admission rather than explicit emotional language.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P14 — What does each person routinely misread in the other?
**Execution:** Stable relationship misreads create recurring believable friction and later growth.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P15 — What is safe to joke about, and what is not?
**Execution:** Humor needs relationship permission. A clever line can fail if intimacy has not earned the category of joke.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT -> applies directly to `failing investment bank` test.

### P16 — Who gets to end the conversation?
**Execution:** Exit power is a major status signal often missing from line-focused dialogue design.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P24 — Can speaker identity survive paraphrase?

# CLUSTER D — SCENE BEHAVIOR / CONVERSATION TOPOLOGY

### P25 — Who is actually attending to whom in a group scene?
**Execution:** Group dialogue cannot be treated as round-robin. Build `ATTENTION_GRAPH`: speaker -> intended target -> overhearers -> ignored participant.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT -> major ensemble upgrade.

### P26 — Who is doing another task while talking?
**Execution:** Task competition changes timing and can naturally fragment conversation. Use only story-real tasks.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P27 — What environmental event can legitimately steal attention?
**Execution:** World sound/action should affect cognition, not merely decorate audio.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT WITH LIMIT.

### P28 — Does physical distance change what can be said?
**Execution:** Proximity, privacy and eavesdropping risk should constrain content/volume.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT; integrates writing with microphone choreography later.

### P29 — Are all characters waiting politely for turns?
**Execution:** Perfect turn allocation creates staged feel. Interruptions/overlap must derive from power, urgency and relationship.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P30 — Is a silence actually an action?
**Execution:** Silence should force a partner choice, change status or reveal refusal; otherwise it is decorative.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P31 — Does the conversation have local irreversibility?
**Execution:** Important scenes need a beat after which participants cannot return to entry-state behavior.  
**Disposition:** ACCEPT.

### P32 — Could the same scene happen by text message without loss?
**Execution:** If yes, embodied scene may not use presence/status/body enough. Not an automatic failure for digital-story scenes.  
**Disposition:** HOLD AS DIAGNOSTIC.

# EXECUTED SYNTHESIS — NEW MECHANISMS TO PROMOTE

## M1 — PERCEPTION / MISSED-SIGNAL MODEL
Add per major beat:
`PERCEIVED_SIGNAL / MISSED_SIGNAL / INTERPRETATION / RESPONSE_IMPULSE`.

## M2 — EMOTIONAL LATENCY
Allow:
`EVENT -> BODY/AFFECT -> DEFENSE -> LATER COGNITIVE UNDERSTANDING`.
Do not require immediate articulate insight.

## M3 — MULTI-REGISTER SPEECH FINGERPRINT
Recurring principals get:
`PUBLIC / PROFESSIONAL-AUTHORITY / FRIEND / INTIMATE / CRISIS` registers where relevant.

## M4 — RELATIONSHIP SHORTHAND + MISREAD MAP
Store earned abbreviations, recurring argument history, unique wound triggers, apology forms and stable misreads.

## M5 — GROUP ATTENTION GRAPH
For ensemble scenes:
`SPEAKER -> INTENDED TARGET -> ACTUAL LISTENER(S) -> IGNORED/EXCLUDED -> ATTENTION SHIFT`.
This replaces artificial round-robin conversation.

## M6 — SPEECH PATTERN MATRIX
Measure suspect frames by:
`pattern x speaker x relationship x register x chapter`.
Global frequency alone cannot classify defect.

## M7 — COMPETENCE/PERSONHOOD SPLIT
Expert/institutional dialogue must preserve professional accuracy while showing character-specific patience, certainty, defense, status and emotional cost.

## M8 — TEXT / PERFORMANCE / LOCALIZATION TRIAGE
Every listener complaint routes first to:
`WRITING / PERFORMANCE / ADAPTATION / TRANSLATION-LOCALIZATION / CONTEXT / NO DEFECT`.
No prose rewrite until earliest failing layer is identified.
