# CHAPTER 1 — THE LAST GROUND

## SCENE 1 — THE BOX THAT DOESN'T GO
**POV:** Ollie  
**Time:** early January 2033, departure morning  
**Location:** Ollie's home / maker storage area, Northern Virginia

**Immediate want:** finish packing without treating departure like a sentimental event.  
**Obstacle:** one rescue-rover component / fabrication jig belongs to the local maker team and cannot simply follow him to orbit.  
**Action:** Ollie checks work gear, mass allowance, tool permissions and leaves the component behind for the team.  
**Hidden pressure:** he wants the orbital placement to prove that the things he missed for Contact were worth missing.  
**Ordinary life:** group chat from his maker team about a build problem they will solve without him.  
**World reveal:** orbital residency packing is driven by mass, compatibility, work rules, body adaptation and host storage—not “take a suitcase to space.”  
**Turn:** the team does not ask him how to solve the rover problem; they have already assigned it to someone else.  
**Relationship delta:** Ollie is proud of them and quietly displaced by them.  
**Price:** he leaves knowing home systems continue without him.  
**Setup:** Book One maker-team sacrifice; Ollie's status hunger.  
**Exit pull:** he chooses the maintenance placement that will give him the least free time.  
**Forbidden:** melodramatic farewell; “I am going to space” exposition; treating his Earth friends as disposable old life.

## SCENE 2 — THE PLACEMENT
**POV:** Ollie  
**Location:** transport staging / family drop-off

**Immediate want:** lock his preferred orbital work assignment.  
**Obstacle:** a safer public-education/media placement remains available and would leave more time with his friends.  
**Action:** Ollie selects supervised habitat maintenance / commissioning.  
**Hidden pressure:** he is tired of being the friend with useful machines while Ethan gets visible responsibility.  
**Resistance:** parent/guardian or coordinator asks whether he understands shift burden and tool/liability restrictions.  
**Turn:** Ollie says yes before checking how the schedule conflicts with his maker-team remote milestone.  
**Relationship delta:** Ethan approves immediately, which annoys Ollie because it sounds like validation from the group's unofficial leader.  
**Price:** chooses work over synchronized friendship before orbit even begins.  
**Exit pull:** the five meet at departure and discover their travel prep requirements are already different.  
**Forbidden:** Ollie stating his envy directly.

## SCENE 3 — LAST ORDINARY FOOD
**POV:** Ollie  
**Location:** airport/transfer-side food area before international leg

**Immediate want:** have one easy meal with the five.  
**Obstacle:** health-prep timing, Aoife recording, Maya messaging orbital contacts, Samir's sport equipment rules, Ethan checking transfer windows.  
**Action:** they eat badly, trade things they cannot take, argue about who has overpacked.  
**World reveal:** the future begins inside ordinary inconvenience.  
**Turn:** their boarding/transfer groups are called separately because their preparation categories differ.  
**Relationship delta:** first small goodbye occurs inside the larger departure.  
**Emotional beat:** Ollie sees the maker-team message: rover problem solved without him. He sends a thumbs-up and does not correct their solution.  
**Chapter change:** leaving Earth means accepting that other people's lives proceed when you leave.  
**Exit pull:** Dublin is not a stopover; it is part of the orbital transport system.

# CHAPTER 2 — DUBLIN VERTICAL

## SCENE 4 — NOT A SPACEPORT POSTCARD
**POV:** Aoife  
**Location:** Dublin arrival / ordinary city-to-transfer corridor

**Immediate want:** record a usable opening for her orbital-youth project without making Dublin look like a promotional film.  
**Obstacle:** old streets, buses, workers, tourists and impossible-looking transfer infrastructure create easy spectacle.  
**Action:** Aoife records sounds first: traffic, announcements, construction, Irish/English speech, orbital freight cues, ordinary complaints.  
**Hidden pressure:** she wants her new work to prove she learned from Book One and is not merely the group's recorder.  
**World reveal:** Ireland's exceptional orbital-tech access is physically normal here: vertical/deep transfer systems, freight priority, specialized maintenance, orbital commuters.  
**No lore dump:** nobody explains why Ireland has this access.  
**Turn:** an exhausted commuter complains about a delayed orbital connection exactly like somebody complaining about a train.  
**Relationship delta:** Aoife realizes wonder is stronger when locals are bored by it.  
**Exit pull:** Ethan suggests an opening narration structure.

## SCENE 5 — NOT YOUR DOCUMENTARY
**POV:** Aoife  
**Location:** transfer queue / vertical staging

**Immediate want:** keep control of her project.  
**Obstacle:** Ethan offers a genuinely good structural suggestion.  
**Action:** she refuses to use it simply because he is folding her work into his mental plan again.  
**Subtext:** attraction makes the boundary more irritating, not less.  
**Resistance:** Ethan says he was only helping; Aoife says helping can still be annoying.  
**World reveal:** travelers are sorted by body adaptation, cargo, destination ring and transfer timing rather than class-coded “space tourist” categories.  
**Turn:** Ethan's route assignment separates him from Aoife sooner than either expected.  
**Relationship delta:** they wanted to keep arguing; instead the door closes.  
**Price:** a small unfinished intimacy.  
**Setup:** later distance/jealousy.  
**Exit pull:** the five discover they will not even experience arrival together.

## SCENE 6 — VERTICAL
**POV:** Aoife  
**Location:** deep/vertical orbital transfer sequence

**Immediate want:** capture the transition without narration telling the audience how to feel.  
**Obstacle:** she cannot record parts of the safety sequence and must put the device away.  
**Action:** experience through body: acceleration, pressure, sound, waiting, cramped practical procedures, workers doing routine jobs.  
**World reveal:** technology is advanced but still has queues, inspections, timing windows and people whose job is cleaning/maintaining it.  
**Turn:** during a prohibited-recording interval, Aoife experiences the strongest moment of the trip and cannot capture it.  
**Character change:** she chooses not to recreate it afterward with fake sound.  
**Price:** the “best” media moment exists only in memory.  
**Exit pull:** next permitted recording begins after Earth has visually ceased to function as ground.

# CHAPTER 3 — ARRIVAL WITHOUT A FLOOR

## SCENE 7 — WRONG FEET
**POV:** Maya  
**Location:** [VELA — WORKING NAME], arrival transfer / spin-transition zone

**Immediate want:** step into orbital life looking as if she belongs.  
**Obstacle:** local movement norms assume people understand rotational transitions she has only trained for.  
**Action:** Maya moves too early/stands in the wrong lane and forces commuters to route around her.  
**World reveal:** “up” changes operationally; floor markings encode momentum and body-adaptation rules.  
**Turn:** a local teenager, not staff, tells her exactly where she is being a problem.  
**Social price:** embarrassment in front of peers.  
**Maya response:** first defensive, then notices the local is right.  
**Forbidden:** slapstick injury; adult lecture; instant mastery.

## SCENE 8 — THE ROUTE ISN'T THE MAP
**POV:** Maya  
**Location:** central transit/service spine

**Immediate want:** reach host housing faster than the route AI recommends.  
**Obstacle:** shortest geometric path is not fastest because transfers, spin synchronization and environmental queues matter.  
**Action:** Maya tries to outsmart routing with a visually shorter path.  
**Result:** arrives later, after an awkward body-adaptation wait.  
**World reveal:** transit is part physics, part scheduling, part social convention.  
**Ordinary life:** locals complain about transfer queues, blocked view spaces and late friends.  
**Turn:** she runs into the same local teenager, who does not remember correcting her because planet-born mistakes are routine.  
**Relationship seed:** Maya is more bothered by being unmemorable than by being wrong.  
**Exit pull:** host rules are stranger than transit.

## SCENE 9 — A ROOM THAT ISN'T PRIVATE THE EARTH WAY
**POV:** Maya  
**Location:** host residence

**Immediate want:** establish independence immediately.  
**Obstacle:** privacy rules are different because atmosphere zones, shared life-support spaces, host accountability and night transit create shared-risk obligations.  
**Action:** Maya negotiates what she must report and what is none of the host's business.  
**World reveal:** housing privacy is real but technologically interdependent.  
**Turn:** host grants more freedom than Maya expected—on condition that location-zone and return-condition information remain truthful.  
**Character pressure:** she now has actual autonomy to misuse later; adults did not force the conflict.  
**Setup:** Ch10/Ch24 privacy mistake.  
**Chapter change:** orbital independence is not less freedom; it is freedom inside maintained shared systems.

# CHAPTER 4 — FIVE ADDRESSES

## SCENE 10 — DINNER AT 19:00
**POV:** Ethan  
**Location:** his host residence / group scheduling

**Immediate want:** get all five together for dinner.  
**Obstacle:** incompatible shifts, sport orientation, documentary access, hosts and transfer windows.  
**Action:** Ethan builds the optimal schedule.  
**Hidden pressure:** if he can preserve the group timetable, orbit has not changed them yet.  
**Resistance:** nobody is refusing him personally; the city itself makes the plan unrealistic.  
**Turn:** Ollie cannot make the “best” time because accepting it would cost his first maintenance handover.  
**Relationship delta:** Ethan nearly tells him what matters more, catches himself.  
**Price:** dinner loses Ollie.  
**Exit pull:** Aoife says she cannot make the replacement time either.

## SCENE 11 — FIVE HOMES
**POV:** Ethan  
**Location:** moving between two housing/environment zones

**Immediate want:** understand why housing assignments are so inconveniently dispersed.  
**Obstacle:** assignments are not social preference alone; gravity, atmosphere, accessibility, host capacity, commute and compatibility constrain them.  
**Action:** he visits or helps carry something between two friends' residences.  
**World reveal:** housing scarcity exists even in an advanced civilization. Private space, compatible location and commute time have value.  
**Ordinary tension:** someone has a better view but worse commute; someone has less privacy but easier sport access.  
**Turn:** Ethan realizes there is no single “better” assignment he could have optimized for everyone.  
**Character delta:** the city resists his ranking instinct.  
**Exit pull:** Aoife teases him for mentally redesigning housing allocation.

## SCENE 12 — GROUP CHAT IS NOT A ROOM
**POV:** Ethan  
**Location:** distributed evening / transit

**Immediate want:** recover group intimacy through constant messaging.  
**Obstacle:** everyone is actually doing different things.  
**Action:** chat fragments across shifts, transit, sport, host dinner, audio editing.  
**Relationship:** Aoife sends him one private message, then disappears into work before he answers.  
**Turn:** the five miss each other without any fight.  
**New state:** friendship now requires deliberate effort, not physical co-location.  
**Setup:** later choice to keep commitments despite divergent futures.  
**Chapter change:** group becomes a network.  
**Forbidden:** sentimental speech about growing apart.

**World reveal:** tool custody, maintenance traceability, liability boundaries, shift handovers.  
**Relationship:** Iri (working name) answers one question and ignores three.  
**Turn:** Ollie notices a tag mismatch / wear pattern / connector seating problem nobody expected him to catch.  
**Payoff:** not genius—attention.  
**Exit pull:** Mara checks whether his observation survives verification.

## SCENE 14 — VERIFIED
**POV:** Ollie  
**Location:** service bay

**Immediate want:** have his finding be real.  
**Obstacle:** experienced technician initially thinks it is harmless install variance.  
**Action:** crew tests it; issue is minor but genuine.  
**Mara:** credits Ollie without overpraising him.  
**Iri:** respects that he did not dramatize the finding.  
**Hidden pressure:** this small success feels better to Ollie than the public praise he expected.  
**Turn:** Mara assigns him a narrow diagnostic comparison tied to occupancy commissioning.  
**Price:** shift runs long; he misses a planned group call.  
**Relationship delta:** local work trust +1, friend synchrony -1.  
**Exit pull:** data from the ring's realistic occupancy run arrives next morning.

## SCENE 15 — ALLOWANCE
**POV:** Ollie  
**Location:** post-shift service/food area

**Immediate want:** buy something small / celebrate first shift.  
**World reveal:** apprentice work gives limited discretionary allowance/workshop privilege; scarcity still exists in imported food, fabrication time, private space, event bookings.  
**Obstacle:** he can spend the allowance on a planetary import he wants or reserve fabrication time for a local maker task.  
**Action:** chooses fabrication access almost automatically.  
**Iri reaction:** “You came to orbit to spend your first pay on a machine.”

**Setup:** later cost when being useful becomes self-worth.

# CHAPTER 6 — THE RING PEOPLE WANT

## SCENE 16 — NOT A PROJECT
**POV:** Ollie  
**Location:** finished residential zone, Ring Six

**Immediate want:** finally see the system as a completed engineering object.  
**Obstacle:** future residents are already treating it as home.  
**Action:** tour moves through laundry, kitchens, school routes, shared repair points, compatible atmosphere modules, youth hangout, ordinary storage.  
**World reveal:** the ring is socially designed, not a dormitory or lab.  
**Resident anchor:** Lea (working name) argues over a bedroom/view/commute issue with family.  
**Turn:** Ollie realizes a module he sees as a subsystem is someone's answer to months of bad temporary housing.  
**Emotional change:** opening deadline becomes personal.  
**Forbidden:** perfect-utopia tour.

## SCENE 17 — WHO PAYS FOR A MONTH
**POV:** Ollie  
**Location:** transit link / resident staging

**Immediate want:** understand why adults resist “just delay it.”  
**Resident anchors:** Lea's split household; Tomas (working name) with multi-transfer commute / shift instability.  
**Action:** he hears conflicting resident positions: one wants delay until certainty; one wants move now under restrictions; one cannot sustain temporary commute another month.  
**World reveal:** housing is life-support capacity + school/work geography, not ordinary real-estate preference.  
**Turn:** Iri challenges Ollie: safety decisions create costs even when nobody is injured.  
**Character pressure:** Ollie wants a technical answer to a social trade-off and does not get one.  
**Exit pull:** occupancy run begins.

## SCENE 18 — SETTLING
**POV:** Ollie  
**Location:** Ring Six commissioning control / occupied test sector

**Immediate want:** watch a boring successful run.  
**Action:** residents/volunteers move through realistic routines; sensors remain within tolerance.  
**Observable phenomenon:** local environmental settling takes longer than predicted after a particular movement/partition sequence.  
**Obstacle:** delay is small, intermittent and disappears when occupancy changes.  
**Mara:** correctly pauses the affected sector and asks for logs; no panic.  
**Iri/body report:** someone describes a physical/environmental sensation that precedes fixed-sensor deviation.  
**Ollie response:** notes it as anecdotal clue, not evidence.  
**Turn:** the ring passes static safety while failing one dynamic timing expectation.  
**Mystery starts:** not “what machine broke?” but not yet understood.  
**Chapter change:** the desirable home has a real problem.  
**Exit pull:** Ollie volunteers to stay on diagnostics instead of taking an easier prestige rotation.
