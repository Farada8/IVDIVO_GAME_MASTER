# IVDIVO — P53 EXECUTABLE SCENE & DIALOGUE PROMPTS v1.0

## PROMPT 1 — FULL DIALOGUE P53

P53 THIS DIALOGUE.

Restore canon, exact relationship state, P51 voices and P52 temperature. Do not change causality, clue timing, professional truth or power boundaries.

Diagnose each character's objective, private desire, fear, status, information asymmetry, push, pull, private cost, hidden emotional cost, female-gaze noticing, safety/consent, subtext, micro-reward, transformative choice and relationship delta.

Add maximum 1–2 earned chemistry micro-beats. Use the after-action emotional peak if danger/action occurred. Do not use generic romantic body language. Do not make competent people stupid. Do not add romance if scene is not romance-bearing.

Return: diagnosis → highest-leverage repairs → one integrated revised dialogue → P51/P52/P53 verification → PASS/REVISE.

## PROMPT 2 — P53 LITE / PROFESSIONAL SCENE

P53-LITE THIS SCENE.

This is not automatically a romance scene. Preserve professional objective first.

Check only: status, asymmetry, private cost, what each person notices, dignity protection, changed trust, one possible micro-reward if earned, and voice integrity.

Do not add flirtation. Return only changes that make the scene more human and relationally consequential.

## PROMPT 3 — AFTER-ACTION PASS

P53 AFTER-ACTION PASS.

Keep action/rescue/confrontation causally correct and safe. Do not slow danger for chemistry.

After danger resolves, inspect the next 0.5–3 seconds / one short prose beat. Can the stronger emotional peak occur through ending necessary contact, breath returning, public mask crack, almost-eye-contact, checking the other, or a professional question replacing unsayable intimacy?

Add only one strongest beat.

---

## PROMPT 4 — MALE VULNERABILITY

P53 MALE VULNERABILITY PASS.

Preserve competence and agency. Find whether this arc contains a real private price beneath public control.

If missing, add the smallest earned evidence: disrupted speech rhythm, uncertainty admitted, asking instead of commanding, fatigue/fear/shame visible, help accepted, costly choice or private mask drop.

Do not infantilize. Do not require heroine mothering. Do not excuse bad behavior.

---

## PROMPT 5 — HEROINE HIDDEN COST

P53 HEROINE HIDDEN-COST PASS.

Preserve heroine competence, agency and independent objective. Identify whether competence has visible emotional price.

If missing, add one character-specific cost: delayed answer, controlled reaction, work chosen before emotion, softer line withheld, private fear, or correct professional choice at personal cost.

No repetitive stock gestures.

---

## PROMPT 6 — PUSH–PULL

P53 PUSH-PULL PASS.

Identify what pushes them apart, what pulls them together, why both are legitimate, and what changes at scene end.

Remove fake misunderstanding and repetitive hot/cold behavior. Push/pull must arise from values, goals, truth, status, duty or fear.

---

## PROMPT 7 — MICRO-REWARD

P53 MICRO-REWARD PASS.

Find prior setup. Add or strengthen one earned reward: remembered detail, honored no, reliable competence, dignity protection, private phrase, disclosure, changed behavior or voluntary staying.

No random sweetness.

---

## PROMPT 8 — TRANSFORMATIVE CHOICE

P53 CHOICE PASS.

Do not improve the scene with a better speech first. Find the harder choice that demonstrates actual change.

Rewrite so relationship movement is proved by behavior: truth, permission, surrender of control, consequence, staying, leaving, refusing a lie or choosing another's method.

Choice must cost something.

---

## PROMPT 9 — SUBTEXT

P53 SUBTEXT.

For every major line classify silently: SAID / UNSAID / UNSAYABLE. Remove lines that state all three.

Use interruption, silence, object, professional language with private meaning, wrong answer revealing right fear, tactical change or incomplete reply. Preserve P51 voice.

---

## PROMPT 10 — ANTI-CLICHÉ

P53 ANTI-CLICHÉ.

Find generic romance language and repetitive body behavior: electric, magnetic, spark, eyes darkened, heart skipped, time stopped, breath hitched, jaw tightened, looked at, lips parted.

Replace only where necessary with character-specific attention + story consequence. Do not replace one cliché with another.
