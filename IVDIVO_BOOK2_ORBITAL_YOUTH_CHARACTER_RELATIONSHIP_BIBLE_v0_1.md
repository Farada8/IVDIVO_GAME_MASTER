# 0. CHARACTER ROOM LAW

**OLLIE is the Book-Two protagonist.**

Book One ends with five friends functioning as a recognizable group. Book Two must end with five young people who now have different jobs, friends, routes, ambitions and intimacies and therefore must choose friendship rather than simply inhabit it.

The four other Book-One leads remain important but may not steal the A-story merely because the reader already knows them.

---

# 1. OLLIE — PRIMARY HERO

**Surname / exact age:** preserve locked Book One; do not invent if unknown.

**Public want:** do real orbital maintenance work and help commission a home people will actually inhabit.  
**Private want:** become the person people call when something real breaks.  
**Shame:** envy of Ethan’s visible leadership/status.  
**Fear:** adults value his competence only while consequences are simulated.  
**Strength:** practical systems thinking, verification, boring work, feasibility.  
**Flaw:** turns messy human disagreement into system variables because machines feel more honest.  
**False belief:** if the model is correct enough, the human problem will resolve around it.  
**Wrong strategy:** become indispensable by producing the cleanest model.  
**Need:** understand residents, habits, routes, body/environment states and embodied feedback as causal parts of habitat behavior.

**Midpoint responsibility:** his valid analysis materially removes one legitimate conservative objection; competent adults update reasonably and later the model fails exactly where its evidence hierarchy was incomplete.

**Climax:** use narrow pre-authorized `SAFE-STATE SEAL` before automatic fixed-sensor threshold because corrected lag model + early embodied signature satisfy the defined condition.

**Price:** access, credibility, local trust, social time, clean launch, recognition, comfort in technical neutrality.

**End-state:** trusted with uncertainty rather than admired for certainty.

**Voice:** concrete, specific, feasibility-first, dry. Emotional statements arrive late/sideways. No ethics speeches.

**Ordinary-life requirements:** rescue-rover maker team, first-job hierarchy, allowance/workshop access, bad food, missed events, replacement anxiety, late-book choice not to abandon friends for a non-urgent alert.
