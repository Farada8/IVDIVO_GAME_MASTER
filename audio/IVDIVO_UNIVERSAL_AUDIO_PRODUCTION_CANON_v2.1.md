# IVDIVO — UNIVERSAL AUDIO PRODUCTION CANON v2.1

**Status:** CANON / UNIVERSAL PRODUCTION STANDARD  
**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Scope:** ALL IVDIVO audio production, regardless of genre, book or platform.

## 0. Authority

Founder newest direct instruction > project locked canon/production master > this universal audio canon > specialized overlays > working documents > references/archive.

A project overlay may specialize density, intimacy, mystery, youth, comedy, horror, documentary or children treatment, but may not break locked story causality or universal QC.

## 1. Primary law

`STORY → LISTENER ATTENTION → CHARACTER ACTION → PERFORMANCE → SPACE → PHYSICAL SOUND → EMOTIONAL SOUND → MUSIC → MIX → QC`.

Never begin with “what effect can be added?”. Begin with:
- what must the listener understand;
- what must the listener feel;
- what is physically happening;
- what changed;
- what should be heard consciously;
- what may be only subconsciously perceived;
- what should be removed.

Silence, distance, reduced ambience and withheld music are production events.

## 2. Delivery modes

A. NARRATED AUDIOBOOK — narrator-led, restrained support sound.  
B. MULTI-VOICE AUDIOBOOK — narrator + recurring character voices.  
C. DRAMATIZED AUDIOBOOK — multi-voice + selective ambience/Foley/SFX/music/spatial staging.  
D. FULL AUDIO DRAMA — scene-native multi-cast production with complete sound dramaturgy.

Text protection is mode-dependent. Locked exact-text projects cannot be naturalized without explicit permission.

## 3. Scene audio dramaturgy

Every scene has:
`START_STATE / WHO_WANTS_WHAT / WHY_NOW / RESISTANCE / AUDIO_ARC / FOCUS_1 / FOCUS_2 / BACKGROUND / SUPPRESS / TENSION_CURVE / DENSITY_CURVE / REVERSAL / AFTERMATH / END_STATE / NEXT_LISTEN_QUESTION`.

A scene without an audible dramatic change is not ready for production.

## 4. Character and relationship context continuity

Every render block carries inherited context:
- `STATE_IN`;
- recent event memory;
- immediate want;
- tactic;
- subtext;
- relationship state;
- status before/after;
- fatigue/physical condition;
- secret/knowledge asymmetry relevant to delivery;
- `STATE_OUT`;
- next-entry impulse;
- forbidden performance signals.

Provider calls must not reset a character to generic neutrality between blocks.

## 5. Actor direction and expressiveness

Performance is directed through playable behavior, not literary adjectives.

Required where relevant:
`OBJECTIVE / TACTIC / RESISTANCE / STATUS / ENERGY / TEMPO / SENTENCE RHYTHM / LISTENING STATE / BREATH / REPLY LATENCY / INTERRUPTION / OVERLAP / PROXIMITY / UNSAID / UNSAYABLE / FORBIDDEN PERFORMANCE`.

Expressiveness is contrast, not constant intensity. Ordinary behavior is the baseline that makes emotional change audible.

## 6. Pause Director

Pauses are semantic events. Define by function, not random milliseconds:
- THOUGHT;
- HESITATION;
- RECOGNITION;
- STATUS;
- REFUSAL;
- ATTRACTION;
- SHOCK;
- INTERRUPTION WINDOW;
- LISTENING;
- OBJECT ACTION;
- BREATH RECOVERY;
- AFTERMATH;
- COMIC TIMING;
- NO_REPLY.

Before accepted dialogue timing, use semantic anchors and ranges. Exact timing is resolved after render alignment/editing.

Protected pauses may forbid music, ambience, Foley or all nonessential sound.

## 7. Listening acting and breath

Characters continue acting while silent. Reactions may be `NONE / BREATH HOLD / INHALE / RESTRAINED EXHALE / SWALLOW / FAILED LAUGH / MICRO-LAUGH / VOICE PREPARATION / MOVEMENT / WITHDRAWAL / INTERRUPTION ATTEMPT`.

Breath must have a physical/psychological cause. Do not add generic sighs, gasps, sobs or moans simply to “humanize” synthetic speech.

## 8. Human microtexture law

Human presence may be conveyed by micro-sounds when story- and action-earned:
- lips separating before speech;
- small mouth movement;
- saliva click only when natural and very low-level;
- dry-mouth swallow;
- throat clear;
- nasal inhale;
- clothing against skin;
- hair/hood/scarf movement;
- hand on fabric;
- chair compression;
- bed/sofa movement;
- hand contact on table/object;
- body weight shift;
- footsteps and stopping;
- eating/drinking textures;
- utensil/cup/glass contact;
- breathing under physical effort or intimacy.

Microtexture is not an ASMR default. It must never become gross, fetishized, repetitive or louder than the dramatic action unless the project specifically requires that aesthetic.

## 9. Food and drink Foley

Eating/drinking scenes may use a causal chain:
`OBJECT PICKUP → CONTAINER/UTENSIL → BITE/SIP → MOUTH TEXTURE → CHEW/LIQUID MOVEMENT → SWALLOW → BREATH/RESPONSE → OBJECT RETURN`.

Possible events:
- bite/crunch/soft bite;
- restrained chewing;
- lip closure/opening;
- sip/slurp only if character/action warrants it;
- liquid pour;
- glass/cup resonance;
- straw/ice;
- swallow/gulp;
- cutlery/plate;
- packaging/wrapper;
- napkin;
- throat reaction to heat/spice/alcohol when story-earned.

Do not sonify every chew. Choose only events that establish reality, character, comedy, intimacy, discomfort, class/status or causality.

## 10. Blocking, proximity and body geography

Every scene may define:
`CHARACTER_POSITION / ORIENTATION / DISTANCE_TO_PARTNER / DISTANCE_TO_MIC / MOVEMENT_PATH / HEAD_TURN / OCCLUSION / CONTACT_EVENT / OBJECT_POSITION`.

Distance is dramatic information. Movement must affect level, direct/reverb ratio, HF loss, stereo position and occlusion rather than pan alone.

## 11. Spatial / binaural law

Spatial staging may use:
- left/right bias;
- near/far depth;
- front/behind;
- crossing motion;
- off-axis speech;
- through-door/wall/vehicle perspective;
- close whisper near one ear;
- two characters occupying different ear-biased positions;
- binaural/HRTF rendering when target delivery justifies it.

Critical story information must never depend solely on left/right placement. Mono fold-down must remain intelligible.

Hard ear-specific proximity is a high-impact device: use selectively for intimacy, danger, disorientation or subjective POV, not as constant gimmick.

## 12. Acoustic Passport

Each location defines:
`ROOM TONE / SIZE / MATERIALS / EARLY REFLECTIONS / REVERB TAIL / NOISE FLOOR / EXTERNAL BLEED / STEREO WIDTH / DISTANCE LAW / OCCLUSION / DIALOGUE TREATMENT / AMBIENCE LAYERS / SPECIAL EXCEPTIONS`.

A character physically moving between locations must audibly move between acoustic systems.

## 13. Ambience architecture

Ambience is layered, not one generic bed:
1. BASE ROOM TONE;
2. ENVIRONMENTAL BED;
3. DISTANT ACTIVITY;
4. WEATHER/MECHANICAL LAYER;
5. OCCASIONAL WORLD EVENTS;
6. STORY-SPECIFIC EXCEPTIONS.

Ambience must establish place, time, scale, material and life without inventing plot clues.

## 14. Foley causality

Every significant Foley event has:
`CHARACTER / ACTION / OBJECT / MATERIAL / CAUSE / RESULT / STORY_FUNCTION / ATTENTION_PRIORITY / ANCHOR`.

Do not add Foley just because an action can make a sound. Add it when it clarifies embodiment, orientation, causality, character behavior or emotional physicality.

## 15. Sound texture and object continuity

Recurring objects receive `OBJECT_AUDIO_ID` and stable identity: telephone, key ring, door, vehicle, weapon, bag, recorder, cup, bed, medical device, etc.

Character physical signatures may exist through walking rhythm, object handling, clothing, speed of movement and habitual restraint/impulsiveness, but must not become caricature.

## 16. Emotional sound design

Emotion is not represented by arbitrary “sad”, “romantic” or “scary” SFX.

Preferred emotional tools, in order:
1. performance;
2. reply latency/pause;
3. breath/listening behavior;
4. proximity/distance;
5. Foley/body action;
6. ambience subtraction/addition;
7. spectral/dynamic contrast;
8. motif/music only after the scene earns it.

Non-diegetic emotional effects may be used only when the project style authorizes them and they do not falsely state guilt, romance, danger or ontology.

## 17. Sound density and contrast

Each beat may carry `DENSITY 0–10` and `FOCUS COUNT`.

Do not keep every scene equally full. Strong events often require fewer competing elements. Before a reveal, attraction beat or traumatic recognition, subtracting sound may be more powerful than adding it.

## 18. Music dramaturgy

Music cues define:
`CUE_ID / STORY_FUNCTION / VALUE_CHANGE / ENTRY_ANCHOR / EXIT_ANCHOR / THEME_ID / INTENSITY / INSTRUMENT_FAMILY / RHYTHMIC_DENSITY / HARMONIC_ROLE / DIALOGUE_DUCKING / FORBIDDEN_IMPLICATIONS / NO_MUSIC_WINDOWS`.

Functions may include `IDENTITY / DESIRE / ATTRACTION / MEMORY / LOSS / THREAT / CHOICE / AFTERMATH / TRANSITION / END_BUTTON`.

Leitmotifs may develop across a book/season. Music must never provide a clue, confession, romantic certainty or villain signal before story evidence earns it.

## 19. Transitions and montage

Transition types:
`HARD CUT / SOUND BRIDGE / PRE-LAP / POST-LAP / AMBIENCE MORPH / OBJECT BRIDGE / MUSIC BRIDGE / MATCH CUT / TEMPORAL ELLIPSIS`.

Montage may combine selected voice fragments, recurring objects, environmental shifts and music to compress time without losing causality.

## 20. Provider compilation / ElevenLabs adapter law

Director psychology is never dumped raw into a provider prompt.

Compile:
`DRAMATIC INTENT → PLAYABLE BEHAVIOR → PROVIDER-SAFE INSTRUCTION`.

Dialogue should normally be generated clean. Keep ambience, Foley, evidence SFX, heavy device processing and music as separate assets/stems when practical.

Use provider performance tags sparingly. Tag density is budgeted. Additional takes must test a specific hypothesis, not random rerolls.

Spatial/binaural positioning is primarily a post/render-mix responsibility unless the provider returns a controlled multichannel/spatial asset.

## 21. Edit Director

Before regenerating a good performance, test whether the defect can be fixed by:
- trim;
- pause extension/reduction;
- breath preservation/removal;
- room-tone bridge;
- overlap timing;
- crossfade;
- reaction placement;
- consonant protection;
- clip gain;
- perspective automation.

`EDIT BEFORE REGENERATE` when acting intention and words are already correct.

## 22. Mix and masking

Priority:
1. spoken clue/human action;
2. causal evidence sound;
3. dialogue intelligibility;
4. orientation/proximity;
5. emotional silence;
6. Foley/ambience texture;
7. music.

Check speech masking, low-mid buildup, sibilance, reverb masking, phone/device intelligibility and transient competition.

## 23. Mastering

Loudness compliance must not destroy micro-dynamics, breath, distance, silence or scene contrast. Preserve authored dynamic relationships while meeting target delivery requirements.

## 24. QC

Mandatory relevant tests:
- exact text/source protection;
- speaker identity;
- pronunciation;
- character/relationship truth;
- performance continuity;
- pause function;
- human microtexture naturalism;
- Foley causality;
- spatial continuity;
- mono fold-down;
- headphones/earbuds;
- phone speaker;
- low-volume playback;
- music contamination;
- clue intelligibility;
- AI artifacts;
- dialogue/SFX masking;
- loudness/peak;
- clicks/dropouts;
- listener comprehension and emotional response.

## 25. Final law

The target is not “more sound”. The target is a believable audible world in which voice, breath, silence, body, objects, room, distance, atmosphere and music all behave as one dramatic system.
