# IVDIVO — AUDIO PRODUCTION CANON ADDENDUM v2.0

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Status:** CANON / UNIVERSAL AUDIO PRODUCTION STANDARD — ADDITIVE AUTHORITY  
**Scope:** all IVDIVO narrated fiction, multi-voice fiction, dramatized audiobooks, audio drama, dorama, pilots, trailers, and audio-first production.

This addendum extends `IVDIVO_AUDIO_PRODUCTION_CANON_AND_BOOT_OS_v1.0.md`. Where this addendum is more specific, v2.0 controls. Project-specific locked canon and Founder instructions remain higher authority.

## Primary production law

`STORY → LISTENER ATTENTION → CHARACTER ACTION → PERFORMANCE → SPACE → SOUND → MUSIC → MIX → QC`.

Never begin with “what sound can we add?” Begin with:
1. What must the listener understand now?
2. What must the listener feel now?
3. What must the listener wait for next?
4. Which audible element is necessary to create that result?

Sound may be added, reduced, delayed, spatially moved, or deliberately removed. Silence is an authored event.

## 1. Audio dramaturgy law

Every scene and episode receives an `AUDIO ARC` before provider requests are compiled:
- START STATE;
- LISTENER ATTENTION TARGET;
- TENSION LEVEL;
- EMOTIONAL TEMPERATURE;
- INFORMATION PRIORITY;
- ESCALATION POINTS;
- REVERSAL / RECOGNITION;
- AFTERMATH;
- END STATE;
- NEXT-LISTEN QUESTION.

A scene without an audible dramatic change is not ready for production.

## 2. Attention law

At each meaningful beat define:
- `FOCUS_1`: must be consciously understood;
- `FOCUS_2`: may be subconsciously felt;
- `BACKGROUND`: world continuity;
- `SUPPRESS`: material that must get out of the way.

Dialogue, evidence, relationship action, and causal sound outrank decoration. When an impossible or emotionally decisive event occurs, subtraction is preferred before additional scoring.

## 3. Performance continuity law

Every character block carries:
- `STATE_IN`;
- `EVENT / PRESSURE`;
- `STATE_OUT`;
- `LISTENING_STATE`;
- `NEXT_ENTRY_IMPULSE`.

The next block inherits the previous `STATE_OUT`. Provider calls must never reset characters to generic neutral unless the story itself resets them.

## 4. Listening acting law

A character exists while silent. Audible reactions may include breath hold, restrained exhale, swallow, failed laugh, movement, interruption attempt, withdrawal, or no reaction.

Reactions must have a cause and story function. No random sighs, gasps, laughs, sobs, or breath decoration.

## 5. Breath law

Breath is directed by cause, not mood labels. Define when relevant:
- pre-speech inhale;
- held breath;
- restrained exhale;
- recovery breath;
- laugh-breath;
- fear-shortening;
- intimacy-softening;
- post-cry instability;
- exertion.

Breath must not be inserted merely to make TTS sound “human”.

## 6. Blocking and body geography law

Every scene establishes where characters physically are and how they move:
- position;
- body orientation;
- distance to partner;
- distance to microphone/listener perspective;
- movement path;
- face turn / off-axis state;
- occlusion;
- contact event.

Spatial audio follows action. A character who walks away cannot remain acoustically close without a story reason.

## 7. Proximity and relationship law

Distance is a dramatic variable. Use `SOCIAL / WORK / PERSONAL / INTIMATE / WITHDRAWAL` distance states where relevant. A change in proximity may itself be a relationship beat.

For romance-oriented production: `ANTICIPATION > DECLARATION`, `PROXIMITY > GENERIC ROMANTIC MUSIC`, `LISTENING > SPEECH`, `PHYSICAL DETAIL > GENERIC PASSION`.

## 8. Touch / anticipation law

When touch matters, direct the whole chain:
`AWARENESS → APPROACH → HESITATION → NEAR-CONTACT → CONTACT → REACTION → WITHDRAWAL/CONTINUATION`.

Do not reduce intimacy to a generic kiss/touch SFX.

## 9. Audio composition law

Every scene receives an `AUDIO COMPOSITION SCORE` containing:
- dialogue/narration;
- silence;
- ambience;
- Foley;
- action SFX;
- clue/evidence SFX;
- media/device processing;
- music;
- spatial movement;
- automation;
- transitions.

The score uses semantic anchors before render timing: `SCENE_START / BEFORE_TURN / AFTER_TURN / BEFORE_EVENT / AFTER_EVENT / DURING_ACTION`. Absolute timestamps are resolved only after accepted performance timing exists.

## 10. Density and dynamic contrast law

Track `SOUND_DENSITY`, `VOICE_ENERGY`, `STEREO_ACTIVITY`, `SPECTRAL_DENSITY`, `MOVEMENT`, and `MUSIC_INTENSITY` across beats. Do not keep an episode at one constant density.

Before major reveals, reduction of competing sound is often stronger than escalation.

## 11. Rhythm law

Scene rhythm is not WPM alone. Direct:
- speech tempo;
- response latency;
- pause density;
- overlap density;
- Foley density;
- music pulse;
- scene acceleration/deceleration.

Reply modes: `IMMEDIATE / QUICK / NORMAL / HESITANT / HELD / INTERRUPT / OVERLAP / NO_REPLY`.

## 12. Interruption / overlap law

For any interruption define:
- who interrupts;
- why;
- triggering word/action;
- who yields;
- who continues;
- resulting status change.

Do not create overlap as generic realism.

## 13. Transition and montage law

Supported transition functions include:
`HARD_CUT / SOUND_BRIDGE / MUSIC_BRIDGE / MATCH_CUT / PRE_LAP / POST_LAP / AMBIENCE_MORPH / OBJECT_BRIDGE / TEMPORAL_ELLIPSIS`.

Montage is a first-class production structure and may combine selected voice fragments, recurring objects, environmental shifts, and music to compress time without replacing causality.

## 14. Narrator law

Where narration exists, define:
- POV distance;
- narrator knowledge;
- emotional involvement;
- pace;
- proximity;
- handoff into character dialogue;
- return from dialogue.

Narrator and character voices must feel part of one work, not alternating unrelated recordings.

## 15. Sound-world continuity law

Maintain:
- Acoustic Passports by location;
- Foley causality graph;
- object audio IDs;
- recurring clue asset registry;
- media/device authenticity profiles;
- character physical sound signatures when story-earned.

The same recurring object or clue sound must retain identity across chapters/episodes once locked.

## 16. Media authenticity law

Phone, radio, recorder, intercom, archive tape, speaker and other media are physical transmission paths, not generic EQ presets. Define bandwidth, noise, distortion, dropout behavior, dynamics, distance and playback source.

Generate/record the human performance first; degrade it through the medium second unless the provider specifically requires another safe workflow.

## 17. Music dramaturgy law

Every music cue has a function:
`IDENTITY / DESIRE / ATTRACTION / MEMORY / LOSS / THREAT / CHOICE / AFTERMATH / TRANSITION / END_BUTTON`.

Each cue defines `ENTRY_CAUSE`, `EXIT_CAUSE`, `INTENSITY`, `INSTRUMENTATION`, `DIALOGUE_POLICY`, and `NEGATIVE_IMPLICATIONS`.

Music must never reveal guilt, romance, danger, clue importance, or ontology before the story earns it. No continuous underscore by default.

Recurring themes use a `LEITMOTIF REGISTER` with association, first use, variants, instrument family, and forbidden-use rules.

## 18. Provider translation law

Director psychology must never be sent blindly to a TTS provider. Compile in three stages:

`PSYCHOLOGY → PLAYABLE BEHAVIOUR → PROVIDER-SAFE INSTRUCTION`.

Example: `afraid but hiding it` may compile to `restrained, slightly slower, small hesitation, do not emphasize fear`, not simply `[frightened]`.

Provider tags are implementation details, not canon.

## 19. Tag budget law

Use the minimum tag density that produces the intended performance. Ordinary dialogue should carry few or no tags. Emotional turns may use limited state-change tags. Reject a workflow that needs a tag on every line.

## 20. Take-purpose law

Additional takes require a hypothesis:
- A = baseline;
- B = one controlled performance change;
- C = only for critical clue/emotional beats.

Never regenerate merely to “try again”.

## 21. Edit-before-regenerate law

If the words, voice identity and dramatic intention are correct, attempt edit repair before provider regeneration when appropriate:
- trim;
- pause extension/reduction;
- crossfade;
- reaction placement;
- room-tone bridge;
- overlap timing;
- consonant/tail protection.

Regenerate only when the performance or source audio is the earliest failing layer.

## 22. Mix-as-story law

Mix may express distance, intimacy, pressure and orientation while remaining subtle. Use gain, early-reflection ratio, direct/reverb balance, HF rolloff, occlusion, width and pan as consequences of physical staging.

Do not flatten all scenes to identical perceived distance or density.

## 23. Masking law

Before release, verify speech/clue masking by music, ambience, reverb and transients. Repair with ducking, EQ space, reduced reverb, narrowed ambience, transient control or stem level before rewriting story.

## 24. Mastering protection law

Delivery loudness targets must not destroy authored micro-dynamics, breath, proximity, silence or distance. Mastering serves the mix, not the meter.

## 25. AI artifact gate

Detect and review:
`VOICE_DRIFT / UNNATURAL_CADENCE / SYLLABLE_SMEAR / WRONG_STRESS / BREATH_GLITCH / DUPLICATED_WORD / TRUNCATED_WORD / FAKE_LAUGH / TIMBRE_JUMP / UNNATURAL_SILENCE / OVER_EMPHASIS / SPOKEN_TAG / HARD_SPLICE`.

## 26. Human listening gates

Numerical QC cannot issue final creative PASS alone. Before `MASTER_LOCK`, use the relevant human gates:
- CONTENT LISTEN: story/comprehension;
- PERFORMANCE LISTEN: believable people and intentions;
- SOUND LISTEN: believable space and causality;
- EMOTIONAL LISTEN: intended feeling appears without explanation;
- BLIND LISTEN: AI artifacts or synthetic behavior are not materially distracting.

Do not fabricate human test results.

## 27. Canonical production dependency order v2.0

`AUTHORITY RESTORE → DELIVERY CONTRACT → PROJECT AUDIO OVERLAY → STORY AUDIO ANALYSIS → SCENE AUDIO DRAMATURGY → ATTENTION MAP → CAST/VOICE LOCK → PRONUNCIATION LOCK → CHARACTER STATE/PERFORMANCE SCORE → BLOCKING/PROXIMITY → AUDIO COMPOSITION SCORE → ACOUSTIC PASSPORT → SOUND/OBJECT/CLUE PLAN → MUSIC DRAMATURGY → PROVIDER PERFORMANCE COMPILATION → CLEAN DIALOGUE RENDER → TAKE SELECTION → EDITING DIRECTOR → TIMELINE RESOLUTION → FOLEY/SFX/AMBIENCE/PROCESSING → MUSIC → SPATIAL/EMOTIONAL AUTOMIX → TEXT/CONTENT/TECHNICAL QC → HUMAN LISTEN GATES → SELECTIVE REPAIR → FINAL QC → MASTER LOCK → RELEASE`.

## 28. Core v2.0 subsystems

1. Dramatic Audio Director
2. Performance Director
3. Blocking & Proximity Director
4. Audio Composition Director
5. Sound World Director
6. Music Dramaturgy Director
7. Provider Performance Compiler
8. Edit / Mix / QC Director

These are logical production subsystems. Do not create needless microservices merely because a field exists.

## 29. Security / provider isolation

External providers receive only the minimum material required to render the assigned task. Project canon, internal mechanism banks, decision corpora and production reasoning remain internal unless technically required.

## 30. Final law

A high-quality audio work is not `TTS + background music + effects`.

It is a causally directed performance in which voice, silence, body, room, object, sound, music, edit and mix all answer the same question:

**WHAT MUST CHANGE FOR THE LISTENER IN THIS MOMENT?**
