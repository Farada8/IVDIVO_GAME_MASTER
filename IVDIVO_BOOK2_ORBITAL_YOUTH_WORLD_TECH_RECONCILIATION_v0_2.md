# 1. VELA — WORKING CITY IDENTITY

**Name:** VELA — WORKING / OPTION.  
**Political status:** human orbital polity / city-habitat with its own civic and habitat authorities. Exact constitutional sovereignty remains UNKNOWN.

Vela is **not**:
- politically Irish by default;
- controlled by the Galactic Confederation;
- the capital of all orbit;
- a station-school built for the five protagonists.

## Founding history
Vela began as a logistics / construction / transfer complex and accreted residential rings, service zones and civic functions over generations.

This history explains local culture:
- maintenance competence has status;
- people notice who does operational work;
- infrastructure is civic identity;
- “prestige visitor” is a recognizable local suspicion.

## Architectural signature
A mostly non-rotating / low-gravity central service-and-transit spine connects multiple rotating residential/work rings through controlled transfer structures.

Exact radii / RPM remain **TO ENGINEERING LOCK** before manuscript copyedit, not before character prose.

Different rings may maintain different effective gravity/environment profiles.

The city therefore feels **braided**, not like one skyscraper or one wheel.

# 2. WHY THE SHORTEST ROUTE CAN BE SLOWER

Orbital transit combines:
- geometric distance;
- rotational phasing;
- transfer synchronization;
- queue load;
- environmental compatibility;
- maintenance closures;
- cargo priority;
- body adaptation.

Therefore:
**shortest map distance ≠ shortest travel time.**

This is a permanent Vela everyday-life rule and supports:
- Maya’s early embarrassment;
- friendship scheduling;
- dating/late-night logistics;
- work lateness;
- relocation pressure;
- occupancy-model error.

No exotic explanation is needed in dialogue. Characters learn it by being late.

Retain the Technical Passport v0.1 core.

AHE coordinates:

- gas composition by zone;
- thermal/humidity control;
- airflow/filtering;
- dynamic environmental boundaries;
- active damping linked to partition/load changes;
- emergency isolation;
- occupancy forecasting;

## Three input layers
A. fixed certified building sensors;  
B. infrastructure/device telemetry;

# 9. REVISED FAILURE CONDITION

Working technical term:
**COUPLED OCCUPANCY OSCILLATION**.

The failure emerges from a phase-lag interaction between:
- dense mixed occupancy;
- rapid transition between environmental zones;
- dynamic partition changes;
- different local thermal/atmospheric loads;
- personal/species/body environmental regulators;
- rerouted real-world movement;
- privacy-filtered or lower-priority Layer-C timing;
- building control responding to slower averaged fixed-sensor state.

No single factor is unsafe.

## Causal loop
1. local micro-environment begins drifting;
2. some residents/personal systems react first;
3. those reactions change local thermal/airflow/resource demand;
4. fixed sensors report the averaged change later;
5. building controls adjust partitions/airflow based on a state already changing;
6. movement/rerouting changes occupancy again;
7. another delayed correction arrives out of phase;
8. oscillation grows.

This makes the **time difference** between embodied/local response and fixed building response a measurable system property.

# 10. WHY TESTS MISSED IT

Previous testing reasonably covered:
- empty systems;
- fixed loads;
- homogeneous occupancy;
- low-density mixed occupancy;
- component faults.

It under-sampled:
- dense residential behavior;
- spontaneous route substitution;
- adolescent/social crowd movement;
- repeated transitions over hours;
- multiple personal environmental regulators reacting faster than building averages.

This is commissioning blind spot, not cartoon negligence.

- one medically real injury/safety event.

- “aliens suffocate, humans fine” simplification;
- sudden station destruction.

Adults evacuate competently.

# 12. CORRECTED MODEL

After midpoint, the model adds:
- explicit lag;
- real route substitution;
- occupancy transition order;
- confidence-banded embodied reports;
- privacy-preserving timing abstractions;
- slower partition transitions where necessary;
- segment-specific limits;
- safe-state isolation.

Resident reports do not replace instruments.

The book’s technical conclusion is:

> the causal model must include information about how people and their support systems respond over time.

Not:
> feelings are better than sensors.

# 13. SEGMENTS, NOT PEOPLE

This is the correct Act-III engineering/social consequence.

Risk certification attaches primarily to:
- zone;
- density;
- transition rate;
- environmental profile;
- route state;
- known body-support interactions.

It should **not** default to:
“this category of person may not enter.”

Some people will still be unable to use some zones temporarily because compatibility is real.

But the system tries to certify **situations**, not stigmatize identities.

# 14. SAFE-STATE SEAL

Ollie’s climax authority is narrow.

He may:
- stop inbound local transit;
- isolate one defined residential segment;
- place that segment into pre-engineered safe-state.

He may not:
- command Vela;
- override citywide safety;
- rewrite AHE;
- open closed sectors;
- order adults.

Conditions for use are established before climax:
- known transition/load state;
- early embodied signature;
- corrected lag-model match;
- local authority explicitly delegated after retraining.

He acts **before automatic fixed-sensor threshold** because waiting for that threshold is the discovered failure mode.
