**GREEN/YELLOW.**

The chapter is a real story unit: Ollie wants to retain control of the rover, loses that control, leaves home, rejoins friends, then is physically separated from them as orbital residency begins. It has a beginning-state and a changed end-state.

No fatal structural problem.

However, v0.1 should **not** be promoted as accepted prose yet.

---

# WHAT WORKS

## 1. OPENING OBJECTIVE IS IMMEDIATE
Ollie wants to touch/fix the rover despite being told not to. The conflict exists on line one.

## 2. CHARACTER THROUGH OBJECTS
His attention goes to load curves, branch permissions, tool custody, the mechanical pencil and access panels. This is better than explaining “Ollie is an engineer.”

## 3. PRIVATE CONTRADICTION APPEARS
He wants the rover team to succeed but also wants his absence to matter. That is emotionally useful and not flattering.

## 4. ORDINARY LIFE SURVIVES THE SF
Parents pack sandwiches, maker teammates joke, Maya has too much luggage, group scheduling is annoying. Orbit has not erased teenage life.

## 5. CHAPTER TURN IS CLEAN
The chapter begins with Ollie unable to leave a system he knows and ends with him entering a system where nothing he built is waiting for him.

That is a genuine state change.

# MAJOR 1 — TOO MUCH BOOK-ONE RECAP IN THE TRANSFER HUB

Current passage summarizes that the five:
- were tested;
- argued with adults;
- broke/repaired trust;
- found old records;
- finished with an official title.

## Problem
It pauses a live departure scene to brief a new reader.

## Repair
Keep only what Ollie would actually feel now:
- the absurdly formal Youth Cohort title;
- memory that they had survived enough together for the group to feel ordinary again.

Move any necessary Book-One recap later, only when a current consequence activates it.

---

# MAJOR 2 — OLLIE AND ETHAN SHARE TOO MUCH VERBAL RHYTHM

Both use dry corrective logic and quick literal counterarguments.

## Desired separation

### Ethan
Fast social-management wit. Notices schedules, people, hierarchy, embarrassment, group control.

### Ollie
Object/system wit. Notices physical tolerances, tool logic, definitions, mechanisms. More likely to answer after inspecting the thing than after reading the room.

## Repair
Reduce Ollie’s number of perfectly timed social comebacks.
Let him occasionally miss the obvious interpersonal implication until a beat later.

---

# MAJOR 3 — GROUP DIALOGUE BECOMES TOO SYMMETRICAL

Moment where all four answer “No” to Ethan’s shared schedule is funny but close to the project’s banned “five smart answers / ensemble chorus” pattern.

## Repair
Make reactions asymmetrical:
- Maya rejects immediately;
- Aoife asks what he scheduled without permission;
- Samir silently checks his own calendar;
- Ollie genuinely wants to inspect the schedule as a system, then notices why everyone is angry.

This differentiates voices and status.

---

# MAJOR 4 — FAMILY SCENE IS EFFECTIVE BUT GENERIC

Parents are warm and practical, but they currently function mostly as “supportive parents before journey.”

## Repair
Give one family-specific pressure with future consequence.

Recommended:
Ollie’s father has already agreed to receive maker-team emergency calls while Ollie is away, which Ollie dislikes because it formalizes the fact that the team can solve problems without him.

Do not add family melodrama.

# MEDIUM 3 — FINAL LINE IS GOOD BUT SLIGHTLY ABSTRACT

“He found that he liked the feeling. He found that he hated it.” is clean, but somewhat writerly after a chapter otherwise grounded in objects/actions.

## Repair OPTION
End on the equipment case / door / assignment line rather than naming both emotions.

Example function:
Ollie enters after reading `HABITAT SYSTEMS / MAINTENANCE` and instinctively checks the latch on the equipment case—then realizes there is nothing else he can check yet.

Object -> action -> implication.
