# ORBITAL YOUTH
## BOOK TWO
### CHAPTER THREE — ARRIVAL WITHOUT A FLOOR

**Status:** WORKING DRAFT v0.3 / FOUNDER-DIRECTIVE RECONCILED / NOT CANON

Maya's first mistake in orbit lasted less than three seconds.

Unfortunately, twelve people saw it.

The transfer corridor from Dublin opened into an arrival chamber that seemed designed by somebody who had never agreed that floors should remain beneath people.

There was a floor. Mostly.

A pale traffic band curved up the far side of the chamber until Maya's body insisted it had become a wall, even though commuters kept walking across it. Families, freight staff, school-age kids and people in body frames Maya did not recognize moved through the curve without looking down.

One of them had four narrow load-bearing limbs and carried a sleeping child against their chest. The child had lost one shoe. This appeared to concern nobody except Maya.

She stopped just beyond the gate.

Her stomach arrived a fraction later.

The sign over the lane shifted into English.

ROTATIONAL ENTRY — BLUE BAND

MATCH WALKING PACE

DO NOT STOP IN TRANSITION

The last instruction appeared while Maya was already stopped.

Someone behind her said, "Seriously?"

She moved.

Too fast.

Her left foot came down where she expected. Her right corrected for a pull that was not exactly sideways. She crossed the edge of the blue band and made an older man with a shopping carrier twist around her.

His carrier corrected better than she did.

A girl coming the other way caught Maya by the forearm.

"Blue keeps moving," she said. "White is where you stop."

Maya straightened.

"I know."

The girl glanced at Maya's feet.

"Then do that."

She let go.

Heat climbed into Maya's face. The older man had already continued. So had everyone behind him. Nobody had fallen. No alarm sounded. No adult arrived to explain safety, authority or the historic responsibility of the Experimental Human Youth Cohort.

Maya had simply stood in the wrong place during a commute.

That should have made it smaller.

It did not.

The girl was about Maya's age, maybe a little older. Dark hair cut at the jaw. Gray trousers. A jacket with three narrow reflective strips at one shoulder and a transit patch Maya did not recognize.

"First day?" she asked.

Maya looked back at the transfer gate.

"What gave it away?"

The girl pointed at the blue lane.

Then she stepped into the curve and kept walking. Four paces later she was moving at an angle Maya's inner ear still rejected.

Maya waited for the white band before stopping again.

Her wrist buzzed.

ETHAN: Transfer complete?

MAYA: obviously

AOIFE: define complete

OLLIE: equipment cleared

SAMIR: finding regroup

Maya looked around.

Seven exits. Three visible levels. Two moving lanes that became walls. A central shaft where passenger cabins crossed in directions her brain had not yet assigned names to. Route signs changed language, color and information density as different travelers approached them.

MAYA: somewhere extremely intuitive

ETHAN: Send route tag.

Maya did not.

She had been on Vela for maybe four minutes. The thought of Ethan locating her through a route tag already felt unbearable.

The city system opened a route instead.

COHORT REGROUP — ARRIVAL COMMON

EARLIEST ARRIVAL: 08 MIN

FOLLOW WHITE / BLUE / SPINE C

The route began by sending her away from a door through which she could see Ethan.

He stood on the far side of a transparent partition, arguing with a staff member and pointing at what was almost certainly a schedule.

Maya considered ignoring the route.

Then she saw the blue band curl upward between them.

The local girl was gone.

Maya followed the system.

This time, when the markings changed, she watched three commuters before she moved.

Nobody congratulated her.

Maya missed the applause more than she wanted to admit.

---

Samir's shortest route was four hundred and twelve meters.

The city estimated it would take twenty-six minutes.

The route that covered nearly twice the distance would take fourteen.

Samir stood outside his arrival gate with the sports bag cutting into his shoulder and assumed he had selected the wrong display setting.

He checked.

EARLIEST ARRIVAL

MINIMUM DISTANCE

MINIMUM TRANSITIONS

LOW-ADAPTATION LOAD

He selected MINIMUM DISTANCE again.

The same route appeared: one short corridor, one direct connector, four hundred and twelve meters.

Twenty-six minutes.

Samir looked through the transparent side of the arrival level. The regroup common was visible across the central gap. He could see part of its white sign. He could almost see people moving inside it.

He switched back to EARLIEST ARRIVAL.

The route sent him in the opposite direction, down the length of the spine, through two transfer points and back along a different arc.

Fourteen minutes.

His wrist offered a quiet explanation.

DIRECT CONNECTOR PHASE CONTACT: 17 MIN

Samir read it.

He understood every word separately.

The sentence still felt unreasonable.

He chose the direct connector.

The first corridor took three minutes. At the end of it, the floor lost most of its weight and the sports bag tried to continue past him. Samir caught the strap before it hit a passenger waiting in a clear environmental hood filled with slow-moving mist.

"Sorry."

The passenger adjusted the case in their lap and changed the route display beside them from text to three bands of pulsing color. They did not appear offended.

The connector platform was close enough to the regroup common that Samir could now see Ethan through two layers of glass.

Between them, nothing connected.

A transit bridge sat ninety degrees away, rotating toward the platform with the ring. Its arrival clock read:

NEXT PHASE CONTACT — 13:42

Samir looked back at the route he had rejected.

EARLIEST ARRIVAL: 11 MIN

MINIMUM DISTANCE: 23 MIN

The city had deducted the three minutes he had already walked. It had not changed its opinion.

Two teenagers in dark practice jackets waited beside him. One carried a curved frame with grip tape along both ends. The other had a ball clipped into a mesh restraint at her hip.

She looked at Samir's sports bag, then at the Earth transfer mark on his sleeve.

"You picked closest."

It was not a question.

Samir shifted the bag higher.

"I was carrying this."

She nodded toward the empty gap.

"Now you're carrying it while standing still."

Her friend laughed. She did not. She had already gone back to checking a team message.

Samir looked through the glass again.

On the far side, Aoife entered the regroup common. Maya arrived a minute later. Ollie appeared with his equipment case on an automated cart.

The long route had delivered all of them before the short route delivered him.

His wrist buzzed.

ETHAN: We can see you.

Samir looked across the gap.

Ethan raised one hand.

Samir raised his.

Maya sent:

MAYA: very efficient

SAMIR: I chose the shortest route.

AOIFE: We know.

SAMIR: That is the problem.

The bridge continued its patient rotation.

Nobody on the platform complained about the wait itself. They complained about what it was making them late for: practice, a shift change, food pickup, a younger sibling, a view-space booking that apparently carried a cancellation penalty.

Samir had measured the walk. Everyone else on the platform had measured the missed practice, shift, pickup, sibling. By the time the bridge arrived, four hundred and twelve meters no longer seemed like a useful answer.

When the bridge finally met the platform, the doors opened for forty-two seconds. Everyone moved before Samir did. He followed, sports bag tight against his side, and did not stop in the blue band.

---

For seven minutes, the five of them were in one place.

It was not an impressive place.

Arrival Common C had six benches, a water point, a wall of host notices and an exterior view mostly blocked by service structure. Two cleaning units argued silently over the same patch of floor until a child moved a dropped scarf out of their path.

Aoife recorded that.

"Not us?" Ethan asked.

"The cleaners have conflict," she said.

"We have conflict."

"You have a calendar."

Ollie's equipment cart began moving toward a service gate.

He caught it by the handle.

"No."

The cart displayed:

VERIFIED EQUIPMENT TRANSFER

SERVICE RESIDENTIAL / RING SIX APPROACH

HANDOVER WINDOW OPEN

"It may be right," Ethan said.

"That is not the issue."

Ollie pulled up the custody record, read it twice and let go only after confirming the receiving technician's name.

Maya looked at Samir.

"How was the short route?"

"Long."

"I heard."

"Everyone heard."

"Good."

Samir set down the sports bag.

"How was walking?"

Maya's face changed by less than most people would have noticed.

Samir noticed.

"Fine," she said.

Aoife looked up from the cleaning units.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

"Who saw?" Samir asked.

Maya stared at him.

He sat on the end of a bench.

"Twelve," she said.

"That's not many."

"It was enough."

Ethan stepped closer to the host wall as it activated.

"Addresses are releasing."

The five provisional host codes they had carried from Earth expanded into actual destinations.

ETHAN COLE

RESIDENTIAL RING TWO / DAVIN HOUSEHOLD

ESTIMATED TRANSIT: 21 MIN

AOIFE BYRNE

RESIDENTIAL ARC THREE / SOUTH HOST CLUSTER / A-19

ESTIMATED TRANSIT: 18 MIN

MAYA TORRES

EAST RESIDENTIAL FOUR / NARA HOUSEHOLD

ESTIMATED TRANSIT: 27 MIN

SAMIR RAHMAN

OUTER RING FOUR / GARDEN CLUSTER / HANA HOUSEHOLD

ESTIMATED TRANSIT: 33 MIN

OLIVER MERCER

SERVICE RESIDENTIAL / RING SIX APPROACH / S-08

ESTIMATED TRANSIT: 39 MIN

For a moment nobody spoke.

The addresses spread across Ethan's map until Vela had to zoom out to keep all five visible.

No two shared a host.

No two shared a full transfer chain.

The city did not look enormous. Several of their destinations were visible from Arrival Common as arcs, lights or pieces of structure across the central dark.

They were close enough to point at and too far apart to live together by accident.

Ethan recovered first.

"Dinner at nineteen hundred. Central food level, Spine C. Tomorrow, not tonight."

Aoife looked at him.

"We've been here seven minutes."

"That's why I said tomorrow."

"I have a source orientation block."

"At nineteen hundred?"

"It does not yet exist for your scheduling purposes."

Ollie's wrist chimed.

SERVICE HANDOVER — DEPART WITHIN 01:30

He picked up his personal bag.

"I have to go."

"Your shift is tomorrow," Ethan said.

"The handover isn't."

"How long?"

"Unknown."

Aoife pointed at Ollie without looking away from Ethan.

"See? A beautiful word."

Samir's host message appeared next.

HANA HOUSEHOLD: EQUIPMENT CHECK MOVED TO 18:10. OUTER FOUR TRANSFER QUEUE EXTENDED.

Samir looked at the route estimate.

"Sport orientation may run into dinner."

"Tomorrow?"

"Tomorrow."

Ethan opened his mouth.

Samir waited.

Ethan closed it again.

Maya's host arrival window flashed amber.

NARA HOUSEHOLD: RECOMMENDED ROUTE UPDATED. EAST FOUR LOCAL CLOSURE IN 29 MIN.

"I have to leave too," Maya said.

"Take the recommended route," Samir told her.

"I was planning to."

"Good."

"I know how routes work."

Samir looked through the glass at the connector he had just waited for.

"I don't."

That ended the argument.

The service gate called Ollie's name.

He left first, following his equipment cart at a pace that made it clear he was trying not to look eager.

Aoife's recorder authorization restored. A local contact request arrived almost immediately. She put on one headphone, listened for three seconds and pointed toward a different exit.

"My host intake moved."

"Message when you get there," Ethan said.

Aoife looked at him.

He added, "If you want."

"Dangerous progress."

She went.

Samir shouldered his sports bag and studied the route options before selecting EARLIEST ARRIVAL.

"Learned something?" Maya asked.

"Temporarily."

He took the outer-ring lane.

Then Maya's departure band turned white.

She stepped onto it before it changed blue.

Ethan remained beside the host wall with the five addresses open on his wrist.

"Tomorrow," he called.

"Maybe," Maya said.

"What does maybe mean?"

The band started moving.

"It means we have five addresses."

She expected him to answer.

The curve carried her out of sight first.

---

The recommended route to East Residential Four went away from East Residential Four before returning toward it.

Maya disliked this on principle.

She took it anyway.

The route crossed the non-rotating spine, waited forty seconds for a residential transfer and sent her through a pressure-neutral passage where a delivery worker complained that the best communal view space had been booked for three consecutive nights by the same music group.

The complaint did more to make Vela feel inhabited than the exterior view did.

By the time Maya reached East Four, the city had stopped feeling like a structure.

It felt like timing.

Doors became useful only when the correct ring reached the correct phase. A place could be visible and unavailable. People said "two transfers" the way people on Earth said "five minutes," then argued about which two.

At the residence intake desk, a small boy stared at the Earth mark on Maya's luggage.

"Does the ocean really move all the time?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Gravity."

He looked disappointed and went back to his game.

Nobody else asked.

In Northern Virginia, Contact had made the five visible without asking them. Here, Maya was another arrival trying not to stand in the wrong place.

She had not decided whether that was insulting or wonderful when her host opened the door.

"Maya?"

"Yes."

"Nara. Come in."

No comment about the cohort. No welcome speech about humanity's future.

Nara took one end of Maya's large case, tested the weight and gave it back.

"You packed for a larger room."

"I packed for possible events."

"The room is still smaller."

She stepped aside.

The residence was narrower than the pictures had suggested, but every surface seemed to have more than one job. The entry wall held shoes, outer layers, emergency masks and two folding seats. A table extended from a partition only when Nara touched it.

Plants grew along one wall.

"Food garden?" Maya asked.

"Humidity buffer. Basil too."

A boy appeared from deeper inside with a sports bag over one shoulder. He was close to Maya's age and looked at the Earth mark on her case before looking at her.

"Did the floor win?"

"No."

He nodded.

"Good."

Then he went back into the kitchen.

Maya stared after him.

"My son," Nara said. "Kellan. He has training in twenty minutes and has already used most of today's words."

"I like him."

"Give it time."

Nara pointed down the corridor.

"Last room. Bathroom is shared with Kellan. Kitchen is shared with everyone. Quiet cycle starts at twenty-three thirty unless we agree otherwise. If your sleep schedule changes, tell the house system."

Maya stopped at the bedroom door.

"What do I have to report?"

"To me?"

"Start there."

Nara leaned one shoulder against the wall.

"What do you want to do?"

"Go out."

"Yes."

"Late."

"Probably."

"Without asking permission every time I change locations."

"Fine."

Maya waited.

"That's it?"

"No continuous tracking," Nara said. "If your return changes by more than an hour, send a message. If you sleep somewhere else, tell me the host. If transit closes or a zone changes state, give me the real route condition."

"My parents agreed to that?"

"Eventually."

"How eventually?"

"Forty-eight messages."

Maya stared at her.

Nara looked toward the kitchen.

"Your father wanted a twenty-two thirty return. I sent him the night-transit schedule."

"You didn't."

"I did."

Maya laughed despite herself.

The bedroom door opened to her host profile. The room was narrow but private: a bed partly folded into the wall, storage inside the curve, one section of the ceiling showing a live exterior view.

Stars.

No Earth.

Maya put her bag down and looked longer than she meant to.

Nara did not comment. That made Maya like her more.

"Anything else?" Maya asked.

"If you say you're coming back, come back or change the plan. Dinner in fifteen."

Then Nara left her alone.

No lecture about being sixteen. No demand to know who she talked to. No live dot on somebody else's screen.

For months, every new freedom at home had turned into a negotiation about the last one. Maya had expected to arrive in orbit carrying a permanent warning label.

Instead Nara had given her more freedom in five minutes than Maya knew what to do with. Maya found herself wishing for one unreasonable rule to push against.

---

Dinner was noodles.

This disappointed every documentary Maya had ever seen about life in orbit.

Nara cooked. Kellan burned part of the sauce while arguing through an audio channel about a court reservation. Maya cut vegetables. The basil from the humidity wall went into the food.

The kitchen system warned that one pan was heating unevenly.

Nara moved it three centimeters and kept cooking.

Maya's wrist buzzed against her leg.

The group map was still open.

Five address pins glowed across Vela.

Ollie's pin had stopped near a service gate. Samir's moved steadily toward Outer Four. Aoife's disappeared when she closed location sharing. Ethan's remained at Arrival Common for almost a minute after everyone else had left, then began moving toward Ring Two.

Maya closed the map before she could wonder why he had stayed.

Kellan glanced over.

"Important?"

"My friends."

He nodded as though that explained the problem and returned to the sauce.

Another message arrived.

ETHAN: Dinner tomorrow 19:00. Spine C common food level. Hold if possible.

Aoife renamed the calendar event within forty seconds.

ETHAN ATTEMPTS SOCIAL CONTROL THROUGH FOOD

Samir accepted, then added:

SAMIR: practice orientation may change this

Ollie marked TENTATIVE.

Maya hovered over ACCEPT.

Kellan set the sauce down.

"Some people are going to the inner social level after training. My mother says I should tell you."

"Did she tell you to invite me?"

He considered this.

"More or less."

"Terrible invitation."

"You can say no."

Maya immediately wanted to go.

"What's there?"

"Food. Music. Courts. Festival bookings."

"What kind of courts?"

"Whichever ones aren't booked."

He picked up his bowl and went back to eating. He was leaving whether she went or not.

Maya selected MAYBE on Ethan's dinner.

"When do you leave?" she asked.

"Twelve minutes."

"I'll be ready."

Kellan nodded and kept eating.

---

Before they left, the host agreement appeared on Maya's wrist.

FIRST-WEEK RETURN CONDITION:

— no continuous location feed required

— zone update if return changes beyond agreed window

— sleeping host must remain truthful

— route condition must remain truthful during service interruption

Maya read it twice.

Then approved it.

At the end of the corridor, Nara called from the kitchen.

"Zone?"

"Inner social level. Back before one unless I message. Sleeping here."

"Good."

Nara went back to the kitchen.

Maya stepped into the residential corridor with Kellan. The station curved away in both directions, full of doors that were homes rather than program rooms.

The floor markings changed ahead.

Kellan entered the blue band without slowing.

Maya watched the people already moving in it, matched their pace and stepped on beside him.

On her wrist, five address pins began moving through five different parts of Vela.

She did not zoom out to keep them all on the screen.
