# YOUTH FRANCHISE REFERENCE MAP v0.1 DRAFT

Date: 2026-08-13
Status: DRAFT / MARKET-REFERENCE / NOT CANON

Purpose: extract reusable franchise architecture for IVDIVO youth development without copying protected story expression.

## Reference patterns

### Harry Potter
Use for: age continuity, yearly progression, stable institution as hub, mysteries that deepen with age, tone maturation, one world supporting books/films/stage/parks/collectibles.
Key lesson: let the audience grow with the same characters and let the institution/world reveal deeper layers each year.

### Percy Jackson
Use for: younger entry point, humorous contemporary voice, quests as world-learning, mythology as a research gateway, same protagonist later reaching high-school senior/college transition, expansion into adjacent character and myth lines.
Key lesson: a 12-ish entry can feed later teen/young-adult stories without abandoning the core world.

### Stranger Things
Use for: mixed-age ensemble, simultaneous child/teen/adult points of identification, friendship group as retention engine, one mystery serving different age bands.
Key lesson: a 14-17 ensemble can broaden the audience if each age has genuine agency rather than being a sidekick.

### The Hunger Games
Use for: immediate youth stakes, selection/system pressure, clear public rules hiding deeper institutional logic, fast escalation from personal survival to societal conflict.
Key lesson: begin with one understandable youth problem; reveal the larger system only after emotional investment.

### My Hero Academia
Use for: academy/program structure, entrance/qualification, training, teams, internships, licenses, distinction between ability and legitimate professional authorization.
Key lesson: an institution can generate endless story modules while characters progress from aspirants to practitioners.

### Naruto
Use for: young aspirant + academy + team + missions + ranks + mentors, long progression, large world expansion, later generational continuity and multi-format exploitation.
Key lesson: progression works when rank, ability, team role, mission responsibility and personal identity are not one number.

### Star Wars / Young Jedi Adventures
Use for: age-segmented entry products inside one large IP. Lucasfilm explicitly designed Young Jedi Adventures as a young audience's first step into the larger Star Wars world.
Key lesson: do not make every product for every age; preserve one universe and create age-appropriate doors into it.

## IVDIVO synthesis

Recommended franchise architecture:

1. FLAGSHIP YOUTH 14-18
   - Core hero: 16-17.
   - Close friends: 14-17.
   - Central hook: wants access to an 18+ IVDIVO-linked program before age eligibility.
   - Story engine: selection, tests, friendship, rivalry, first love, rule-breaking, missions, discovery that ability/competence/status/authority are not identical.

2. NEXT 18/19-22
   - Same characters move from applicants/trainees into real service and responsibility.
   - Work, permissions, consequences, independent life, missions, hierarchy, larger-world expansion.

3. JUNIOR 11-13
   - Separate but connected entry line after flagship traction exists.
   - Mystery, friendship, first exploration, camps/labs/expeditions, older Youth characters may appear as aspirational figures.

## Franchise continuity rule

Do not launch three full lines at once.
Build one flagship first, but plant bridges:
- younger recurring characters who can later lead Junior stories;
- older trainees/mentors who can lead Next stories;
- recurring institutions, places, symbols, artifacts and unresolved mysteries;
- events seen differently at different ages;
- characters who age into new responsibilities.

## Creative borrowing boundary

Borrow only abstract mechanics: age progression, institution hub, team structure, qualification gates, mission cadence, world expansion, multi-age entry points, cross-product continuity.
Do not copy protected characters, settings, scenes, dialogue, names, visual identity or distinctive plot sequences.
