Status: SOURCE-BASED ANALYSIS
Date: 2026-08-13
Scope: approximately first 20% of each work. The Hunger Games source currently supports the opening through early Capitol material; Fourth Wing source is likely full.

## The Hunger Games — opening sequence functions
1. Katniss wakes on reaping day: family attachment + immediate threat + poverty.
2. Illegal hunt: competence is shown before explanation; world rules arrive through action.
3. Gale scene: trusted peer + possible future + social injustice through tesserae and class difference.
4. Reaping ritual: institutional mechanism becomes visible and personal.
5. Prim selected: abstract system becomes direct family catastrophe.
6. Katniss volunteers: protagonist identity proven by irreversible costly choice.
7. Peeta selected: survival objective acquires relational complication and old debt.
8. Goodbyes: external stakes translated into concrete people who will suffer if Katniss dies.
9. Train / Haymitch: mentor initially fails; protagonist and Peeta force him to engage.
10. Capitol arrival: social contrast + audience/sponsor system introduced as a second battlefield.
11. Cinna / presentation: image management becomes a survival resource.
12. Chariot entrance: first public payoff; protagonist gains hope through changed social position.

Opening engine: HUMAN OBLIGATION → SYSTEMIC THREAT → IRREVERSIBLE CHOICE → RELATIONAL COMPLICATION → PROFESSIONALIZED SURVIVAL → PUBLIC IMAGE AS RESOURCE.

## Fourth Wing — opening sequence functions
1. Conscription Day: survival threat stated immediately; protagonist is physically mismatched to the assigned role.
2. Mother/Mira conflict: family authority creates the forced objective; competence gap is explicit.
3. Parapet entry: first measurable survival test; Rhiannon relationship begins; Jack becomes direct threat.
4. Dain reunion: old emotional safety appears, but also a competing plan — escape from the Riders Quadrant.
5. Squad assignment: institutional structure is taught through live sorting; Xaden deliberately moves Violet into his chain of command.
6. Xaden / dragon demonstration: status fantasy is punctured; riders are not elite-safe but prey.
7. Death roll: institution normalizes mortality; loss becomes routine procedure.
8. Sparring / challenges: Violet converts intelligence, preparation and rule knowledge into practical survival advantages.
9. Jack / rival pressure: recurring personal antagonist keeps abstract danger personalized.
10. Gauntlet: progression is measurable; small successes alternate with visible deaths, including Aurelie.
11. Peer network expands: Rhiannon, Ridoc, Sawyer and others create belonging inside the deadly system.
12. Dain relationship begins to split: protection becomes control; rule-obedience conflicts with Violet's adaptive survival.
13. Xaden remains status threat + mystery + attraction vector, so one relationship carries several narrative functions.
14. By roughly chapter 8, the reader understands the repeating loop: survive test → adapt → gain/lose relationship → learn rule → face harder test.

Opening engine: FORCED ROLE → VISIBLE COMPETENCE DEFICIT → TRIAL → GROUP BELONGING → STATUS PRESSURE → ADAPTATION → NEW TRIAL.

## Comparative cadence
### Shared mechanisms
- Human vulnerability is established before deep lore.
- Main rule is embodied in a procedure, not explained in a detached lecture.
- The protagonist proves character through action within the first major turn.
- Relationship network is introduced early and each relationship has a different function.
- Competence is visible and task-based.
- Institution is a source of both opportunity and danger.
- Every few sequences there is a hard state change: selected, reassigned, tested, publicly reframed, allied, threatened, promoted, exposed.

### Important differences
- The Hunger Games is obligation-first: family duty drives the entire opening.
- Fourth Wing is progression-first: repeated tests and status movement drive the opening.
- The Hunger Games uses one enormous irreversible turn early (volunteering), then prepares for the arena.
- Fourth Wing uses a ladder of smaller survival gates that repeatedly refresh danger and social position.
- The Hunger Games introduces public image as a survival economy very early.
- Fourth Wing introduces rank, squad, training and peer belonging as the main social economy.

## Youth IVDIVO implications — hypotheses only
1. Do not open with IVDIVO cosmology. Open with a young person who can lose a person, position, future, body, access or belonging today.
2. By the first 10% there should be one irreversible or near-irreversible commitment.
3. By the first 20% the protagonist should have a stable social network of at least 5 functional relationships.
4. The audience should be able to describe the local repeatable engine in one sentence by 20%.
5. Every new ontology term must change a current choice, danger, status or relationship.
6. Status must be observable in access, duties, restrictions, privileges and consequences — not merely titles.
7. Young-protagonist progression should not mean only 'more power'; it can mean new access, responsibility, jurisdiction, trust, visibility or cost.
8. The institution must contain both real competence and real structural error; otherwise rebellion becomes trivial.
9. Relationship conflict should intersect with the rule system rather than run as a detachable romance subplot.
10. Every 8–15 audio minutes should produce a real state delta: new task, changed relationship, status shift, actionable information, loss, payoff or reclassification.

## Candidate original youth engine for testing
PERSONAL PROBLEM → FORCED/CHOOSEN ENTRY → FIRST TEST → NEW GROUP → STATUS LABEL → CONTRADICTION BETWEEN LABEL AND REAL CAPABILITY → RELATIONSHIP SPLIT → SECOND TEST → ACTIONABLE IVDIVO RULE → COSTLY CHOICE → LARGER SYSTEM QUESTION.

This is not final franchise canon. It is a testable opening architecture derived from two distinct commercial mechanisms and must later be cross-checked against Night Watch, DCC/Cradle, Red Rising, Ninth House and the IVDIVO canon map.
