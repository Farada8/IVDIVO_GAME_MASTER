## Core mechanisms extracted

1. **Dialogue is verbal action.** Every line should arise from a need/desire and perform a tactic rather than merely transmit information.
2. **Action / reaction progression.** Exchanges should change the scene beat by beat; talk that causes no reaction is a candidate for compression or removal.
3. **Subtext is the pressure source.** Spoken behavior is the visible tactic; desire, fear, intention and conflict underneath generate the line.
4. **Said / unsaid / unsayable layers.** Character speech can operate across explicit expression, private thought and motives the character cannot yet articulate.
5. **Character-specific vocabulary.** Distinct voice begins not with decorative quirks but with syntax, rhythm, tonal habits and especially word choice linked to identity and experience.
6. **Conflict gives language meaning.** Ordinary chatter is condensed into story dialogue by pressure, competing desires and consequential choices.
7. **Inner speech is still action.** Internal dialogue should have a target, purpose, resistance or self-conflict; thought is not an exemption from drama.
8. **Dialogue across media differs in form but not purpose.** Prose, stage, screen and direct address use different containers while retaining desire/tactic/action logic.
9. **Avoid on-the-nose explanation.** Strong dialogue allows readers to infer thought and feeling from tactics, contradiction, omission and pressure.
10. **Scene design precedes wording.** Better lines cannot rescue a scene whose desires, conflict and turn are weak.

## IVDIVO application

Before writing an important exchange, record for each participant:
`DESIRE -> OBSTACLE -> TACTIC -> LINE/ACTION -> REACTION -> TACTIC CHANGE`.

For youth dialogue, voice differentiation must come from age, peer status, family role, insecurity, group context and desired self-image—not from slang pasted onto adult thinking.

For metaphysical/technical exposition, a character should speak because they are trying to accomplish something with another person. Information is allowed, but it should be weapon, reassurance, concealment, test, bargain, seduction, challenge, instruction under pressure, or another concrete tactic.

Do not reproduce McKee's examples, quoted scenes or distinctive wording. Use abstract mechanisms and diagnostic logic only.
