## Extracted mechanisms

### 1. POV = information architecture

`EVENT → AVAILABLE PERCEPTION → CHARACTER ATTENTION → INTERPRETATION → NARRATED INFORMATION → READER MODEL`

POV determines not just who narrates, but what information can fairly enter the reader's model and how it is filtered.

### 2. Genre-reader sophistication

Experienced readers recognize recurring story grammar and clue patterns quickly. Freshness therefore often comes from:

- unusual but credible attention filters;
- distinctive interpretation;
- information timing;
- voice;
- perspective-specific stakes;

rather than from simply hiding more information.

### 3. Mystery information parity

When a mystery invites the reader to solve along with the investigator, reader and sleuth should have access to the same **essential** evidence. Minor POV scenes may add texture or nonessential information, but should not accidentally reveal or conceal the decisive solution mechanism.

Operational test:

`IS THIS FACT SOLVE-CRITICAL?`
- YES → who knows it, when, and can the reader access it?
- NO → does the alternate POV still improve tension/character enough to justify its cost?

### 4. Reader-distance control

Readers can simultaneously identify with a character and evaluate them from outside. POV should therefore create both:

- experiential intimacy;
- enough interpretive room for reader judgment.

This is especially useful for IVDIVO characters whose conclusions may be emotionally understandable but epistemically incomplete.

## Failure modes

- multi-POV leaking the answer too early;
- tight POV hiding a necessary premise until after the reveal;
- every character noticing the same things despite different backgrounds;
- using POV shifts only to deliver exposition;
- confusing author knowledge with character knowledge;
- treating genre convention as compulsory formula rather than reader contract.
