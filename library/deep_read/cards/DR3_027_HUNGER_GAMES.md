## Unique strength
The book is unusually strong at making youth agency inseparable from political economy, family responsibility, food/work, public spectacle and institutional control. The protagonist's skills, fears and choices exist before the arena because survival has already structured daily life.

## Transferable mechanisms
1. **Worldbuilding through subsistence.** Food, electricity, work, fencing, market, medicine and illegal trade reveal the political system before abstract explanation.
2. **Youth can carry adult responsibility without becoming adult psychologically.** Katniss feeds family and makes hard decisions, yet sibling attachment, jealousy, uncertainty and public identity remain developmentally charged.
3. **Skill grows from ordinary necessity.** Hunting competence comes from family survival/history, not protagonist entitlement.
4. **Institutional injustice alters small daily behavior.** Surveillance changes speech, facial expression, school conversation and risk tolerance.
5. **Family obligation blocks easy escape.** Independence choices are constrained by who depends on the young protagonist.
6. **Class changes formal risk.** The reaping/tessera system makes institutional danger materially unequal rather than merely rhetorically unfair.
7. **Public persona becomes a survival tool.** Institution pressures hero to perform an identity for spectators; authenticity and strategy become entangled.
8. **Relationship can be both real and instrumental.** Affection, alliance, audience interpretation and survival tactic coexist without clean separation.
9. **Humor is pressure release.** Jokes about oppressive ritual make fear bearable and show relationship intimacy.
10. **Knowledge learned in ordinary life becomes climactic leverage.** The nightlock payoff transforms prior survival knowledge into a political choice.
11. **Climax can be refusal of the institution's binary.** Hero creates a third option at personal risk rather than merely winning by superior force.
12. **Victory generates new political danger.** Local conflict resolves, but action permanently changes how institution sees hero; consequence feeds series without erasing Book 1 closure.

## Failure modes
- Treating oppression as lore while daily economy feels comfortable/unchanged.
- Making youth hero competent in everything with no origin/cost.
- Using rebellion language before the protagonist has personal stakes.
- Converting public-performance conflict into speeches rather than choices.
