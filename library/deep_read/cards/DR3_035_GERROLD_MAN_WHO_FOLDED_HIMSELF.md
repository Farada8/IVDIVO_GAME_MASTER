## Unique strength
The novel is useful for treating alternate versions of one person as increasingly independent subjects. Shared origin does not preserve a single identity once experience diverges. The high concept externalizes narcissism, loneliness, self-love/self-disgust and responsibility to one's possible selves.

## Transferable mechanisms
1. **A copy/branch becomes a different person through experience.** Common memory up to split is not enough to keep identity singular afterward.
2. **Meeting oneself removes easy self-deception.** Traits normally projected onto others become physically undeniable.
3. **Self-recognition can produce attraction, rivalry, disgust and care simultaneously.** Identity conflict need not be only legal or violent.
4. **Alternate selves create symmetry problems.** Each may believe they are the natural reference point; story must not automatically privilege POV version metaphysically.
5. **Repeated branching multiplies responsibility.** A choice that creates another viable self is not consequence-free because 'another timeline' exists.
6. **Loneliness can increase despite unlimited access to versions of self.** Similarity is not relational otherness.
7. **Self-love can become narcissistic closure.** Relationship only with oneself may prevent encounter with genuine difference.
8. **Sex/gender/relationship variants can test what is stable vs contingent in identity.** Use only abstractly; do not reproduce source scenarios.
9. **Family/parental paradox makes continuity relational.** Identity questions become sharper when roles across generations/timelines overlap.
10. **A record/manuscript can become inheritance to another version.** Information continuity is not same as experiential continuity.
11. **The final meaningful question is prospective.** Given knowledge of possible selves, what future does this subject choose now?
12. **Metaphysical certainty is less dramatic than responsibility.** Even if mechanism is explained, character still has to decide what obligations exist between versions.
13. **Variant conflict should arise from diverged values, not arbitrary evil copies.** Same starting person can become incompatible through accumulated choices.
14. **Recognition by others cannot be reduced to private certainty.** Multiple valid continuations force loved ones/institutions to make their own choices.

## Failure modes
- Evil-copy cliché.
- Treating one version as 'real' only because it owns POV.
- Timeline mechanics becoming puzzle with no relationship/self consequence.
- Duplicates used to avoid death/consequence cheaply.
- Copy retains identical personality forever despite divergent experience.
