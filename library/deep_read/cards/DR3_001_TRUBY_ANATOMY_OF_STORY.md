## Unique strength

Truby is strongest in treating story as an organic system rather than an external act template. Character, plot, revelations, world, moral argument, symbol system, scenes and dialogue are interdependent. His most useful operational idea for IVDIVO is that story movement alternates between ACTION and LEARNING: a character pursues a desire, acts, encounters resistance, learns, decides and changes course.

## Do NOT take

- Do not import Truby's example stories, scene sequences or terminology as IVDIVO canon.
- Do not turn the 22 steps into a mandatory checklist.
- Do not let moral argument become author speeches.
- Do not confuse an abstract character web with actual dramatic scenes.

## Structural map

The book develops: story as organism → premise/design principle → character web → weakness/need/desire → opponent → plan/battle/self-revelation → moral argument → story world/symbols → plot/revelations → scene weave/dialogue → construction process.

## Transferable mechanisms

### T01 — Desire drives the causal track
Trigger: protagonist is interesting but passive.
Sequence: weakness/problem → desire → action → resistance → result.
Effect: reader can track what the hero is trying to change.
Failure: vague desire such as 'understand myself' with no operational target.

### T02 — Story walks on ACTION + LEARNING
Trigger: chapters contain events but no model change, or information but no action.
Sequence: attempt → new information → decision → revised attempt.
Effect: plot becomes causal and intelligent.
Failure: revelation used only as lore.

### T03 — Weakness/need must precede lesson
Trigger: character arc feels attached after plot.
Sequence: initial psychological/moral weakness → external manifestations → pressure → costly self-revelation.
Effect: change is earned.
Failure: hero states the theme after climax.

### T04 — Psychological vs moral weakness
Psychological weakness harms or limits self; moral weakness harms others.
Use: give major IVDIVO characters consequences beyond private insecurity.
Failure: flawless hero with only trauma.

### T05 — Surface problem exposes deeper weakness
Trigger: opening needs immediate trouble without biography dump.
Sequence: simple specific crisis → behavior → audience infers weakness/need.
Effect: character and plot start together.

### T06 — Opponent is structural, not cosmetic evil
Trigger: antagonist exists only to be bad.
Sequence: hero wants X → opponent has incompatible route/goal → each forces the other to reveal capacities and flaws.
Effect: intelligent opposition.
Use: foundational for OLD EARTH SECURITY ↔ CONFEDERATION SECURITY.

### T07 — Inciting event connects NEED and DESIRE
Trigger: protagonist remains in stasis.
Sequence: external event → problem becomes actionable → hero forms goal.
Effect: WHY NOW becomes causal.
Failure: arbitrary mission assignment.

### T08 — Revelations are the learning engine of plot
Trigger: mystery or institutional story is flat.
Sequence: current model → hidden fact → reinterpretation → changed strategy → deeper hidden layer.
Effect: complexity grows under the surface rather than by adding random enemies.

### T09 — Fewer opponents can generate deeper reveal plots
Trigger: mystery keeps adding disposable threats.
Mechanism: keep core opponents present; hide motives, alliances, methods, past actions and true stakes.
Effect: revelations magnify existing relationships.

### T10 — Design backward from meaningful self-revelation
Trigger: ending feels unearned.
Sequence: define what the climactic choice reveals → identify beginning weakness/need → build intervening failures and discoveries.
Effect: plot points toward a specific transformation.
Failure: predetermining a slogan rather than a choice.

### T11 — Double reversal
Hero and opponent can both learn/change; their revelations can illuminate each other.
Use: conflicts where neither side is wholly correct.
Failure: fake equivalence when evidence clearly makes one side wrong.

### T12 — Television as mini-society
Multiple characters pursue different desires in one social system and change simultaneously.
Use: ORBITAL YOUTH ensemble ecology.
Failure: equal-airtime subplots with no causal collisions.

## Character mechanisms

- Define hero through weakness/need/desire and through contrast with a character web.
- Make strength capable of becoming weakness when overextended.
- Let change occur through costly action, not explanation.
- Opponent should reveal the hero's hidden weakness.

## Scene/dialogue implications

- Every important scene should either advance action or learning, preferably both.
- Dialogue is downstream of character desire/opposition; do not solve weak scene design with better phrasing.
- A revelation that does not change behavior is usually inert exposition.

## Escalation/payoff

Escalation should increase the difficulty of the hero's desire while deepening what the hero learns about the opponent, world and self. Payoff is strongest when final choice resolves external desire while exposing or changing the weakness/need established at the beginning.

## Cross-source check

AGREES WITH McKee: desire, opposition, causality, escalating antagonism, climax through choice/action.
DIFFERS IN EMPHASIS: Truby foregrounds story as an organic web and revelation/learning; McKee gives finer local engineering of expectation-result gaps and value turns.
AGREES WITH Swain/Bickham: action must produce consequence that motivates the next action.

## IVDIVO transformation

Permanent use: `WANT → ACTION → RESISTANCE → LEARNING → DECISION → CHANGED ACTION → PRICE`.
For ORBITAL YOUTH, pair developmental need with concrete orbital desire/status/job/family pressure.
For SMITH, use reveal plots built from a small number of institutions/actors with hidden histories rather than endless anomaly-of-the-week additions.
