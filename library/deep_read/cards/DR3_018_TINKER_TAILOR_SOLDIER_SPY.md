## Unique strength
Le Carré is strongest at showing a security institution threatened by its own memory, loyalties and classification system. The mystery is solved not by a magic clue but by reconstructing who knew what, when, through which channel, and whose biography/status made certain interpretations invisible.

## Transferable mechanisms
1. **Trusted system can be the threat surface.** Security failure can originate inside the organization that defines what counts as trusted evidence.
2. **Biography is operational data.** Old friendships, recruitment history, promotions, humiliations and ideological wounds affect present access and blind spots.
3. **Archive + testimony create competing models.** File record, memory and personal account rarely align perfectly; discrepancy is itself evidence.
4. **Information asymmetry is role-based.** Different departments/grades know different slices; nobody possesses the whole truth legitimately.
5. **Institutional fashion can suppress contrary evidence.** A successful doctrine/network makes dissent look obsolete or disloyal.
6. **Old failure leaves procedural scars.** Disgraced operation changes careers, trust and what later investigators are allowed to ask.
7. **Investigation can require working outside official chain.** Not because hero is a rebel archetype, but because chain may be compromised.
8. **Quiet interviews can be action scenes.** Objective is to elicit, test, conceal or reframe information; status and history are weapons.
9. **The mole is embedded in relationships, not only access logs.** Betrayal hurts because system treated belonging and friendship as evidence of safety.
10. **Negative space matters.** Missing report, unusual routing, inconsistent procedure or absence of expected communication can reveal structure.
11. **Professional competence includes patience and revising assumptions.** Investigator accumulates pattern rather than forcing first theory.
12. **Resolution changes institution, not only culprit status.** Exposure forces reassessment of careers, doctrine and past operations.
