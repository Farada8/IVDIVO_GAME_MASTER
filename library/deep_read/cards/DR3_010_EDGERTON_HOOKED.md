## Unique strength

Edgerton is narrowly strong at deciding WHERE the story begins and distinguishing the immediate problem that drives action from the deeper problem the story gradually exposes. This is useful for preventing IVDIVO openings from starting with lore, biography or atmospheric setup before the actual story exists.

## Do NOT take

- Do not treat publishing-market fashions from 2007 as timeless law.
- Do not force literal page-one danger in every genre.
- Do not discard necessary setup; compress and delay it until the current scene needs it.
- Do not turn 'story-worthy problem' into a hidden therapy diagnosis the protagonist must verbalize.

## Transferable mechanisms

### E01 — Begin when the story's trouble begins
Material before the durable problem is usually setup/backstory unless it is itself active story.
Use: Opening Chapter Lab.

### E02 — Inciting Incident creates the initial surface problem
The disturbance should alter the protagonist's world enough to require action.
Failure: interesting anomaly with no actionable consequence.

### E03 — Surface problem drives immediate behavior
The hero can understand and act on the surface problem even while deeper meaning remains hidden.

### E04 — Story-worthy problem is deeper and gradually revealed
It is tied to the protagonist's inner/relational condition and remains more stable while surface problems mutate.
Failure: reveal the entire psychological answer in opening exposition.

### E05 — Surface and deep problem must be organically linked
External trouble should expose/pressure the deeper problem rather than coexist as an unrelated 'character arc'.

### E06 — New surface problems grow from preceding ones
Decision/problem A → consequence → problem B; avoid episodic replacement problems.
Use: chapter causality.

### E07 — Inciting Incident should be dramatized
If it fundamentally changes the protagonist and launches the problem, reader should live through it rather than receive a later summary.

### E08 — Setup is a snapshot, not a history lecture
Give only enough present-context information for the active scene to be understood.

### E09 — Backstory arrives after present-story interest exists
Release past information when a current action/question makes it valuable.

### E10 — Opening character is established by response to trouble
Behavior under disturbance is more efficient than descriptive dossier.

### E11 — Opening creates a promise about the kind of story ahead
Tone, conflict, world and questions should not bait-and-switch the reader.

### E12 — Foreshadow the ending through the opening problem/pattern
The best opening contains structural DNA of what the story will ultimately require the protagonist to face.

### E13 — Small decisions can form the causal fuse
A seemingly minor choice can create the next choice and ultimately produce major trouble, provided links are legible.

### E14 — Important moments earn scene treatment
When emotion/change matters, dramatize enough for reader participation instead of explaining the result afterward.

## Character mechanisms

- Opening problem should expose existing vulnerability, contradiction or habitual strategy.
- Protagonist need not consciously know the deeper problem.
- Early behavior establishes character more efficiently than biography.

## Scene/dialogue implications

Opening audit:
`DISTURBANCE → SURFACE PROBLEM → FIRST RESPONSE/CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE → FIRST GLIMPSE OF DEEPER PROBLEM → FORWARD QUESTION`.
Setup enters only where this chain would otherwise be confusing.

## Escalation/payoff

Surface problems may change, but they should remain tethered to the deeper story-worthy problem. Ending payoff resolves or transforms the deeper issue through what the hero ultimately chooses/does, not merely by solving the first practical problem.

## Cross-source check

AGREES WITH Truby: external problem can manifest weakness/need; inciting event activates desire.
AGREES WITH McKee: opening disturbance creates the central question and predicts the climactic confrontation.
AGREES WITH Swain/Bickham: later problems must arise causally from prior results/decisions.
DIFFERS IN EMPHASIS: Edgerton is specialized for opening placement and information economy.

## IVDIVO transformation

Before approving Chapter 1 ask:
1. What is the first problem that forces action?
2. Could the preceding material be moved later without breaking understanding?
3. What deeper contradiction does the immediate problem begin to expose?
4. Does the hero make a choice whose consequence produces the next problem?
5. Does this opening honestly promise the novel we intend to complete?
