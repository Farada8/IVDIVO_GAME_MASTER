## Transferable mechanisms

### S01 — Motivation precedes reaction
External stimulus → focal-character reaction.
Effect: reader understands cause, orientation and emotional logic.
Failure: reaction appears before reader knows what caused it, or character reacts to omitted information.

### S02 — Motivation-Reaction Units generate micro-causality
Each selected stimulus matters to POV character; reaction changes state of mind/affairs and becomes part of the next stimulus chain.
Use: action, horror, attraction, social embarrassment, body/interface scenes.

### S03 — POV selection determines significance
The same event means different things to different characters; choose details according to what the focal character values/fears/notices.
Failure: neutral camera inventory in close POV.

### S04 — Scene = Goal → Conflict → Disaster
Goal must be specific enough to act toward now.
Conflict comes from opposition to that immediate goal.
Disaster means the outcome changes the situation enough that the next move cannot be simple continuation.

### S05 — Goal must be concrete and immediate
A goal should be photographable/operational rather than abstract aspiration.
Use: scene-level want vs book-level private desire.

### S06 — Conflict must develop, not rehash
Introduce new related difficulties, leverage or tactics rather than repeating one argument.

### S07 — Disaster should be motivated by established story forces
Random unrelated trouble gives less satisfaction than consequence produced by opponent, prior choice or known environment.

### S08 — Sequel translates disaster into new goal
`REACTION → DILEMMA → DECISION` creates the bridge from a changed situation to the next purposeful scene.
Effect: consequences are lived, not skipped.

### S09 — Reaction allows consequence to register
After a major setback, character needs enough emotional/cognitive processing for the next choice to be credible.
Failure: instant new mission with no human aftermath.

### S10 — Dilemma narrows options
Character considers alternatives that carry different costs; this creates decision rather than author fiat.

### S11 — Decision creates the next causal scene
Decision establishes a new immediate goal; next conflict exists because of what character decided after prior disaster.

### S12 — Scene/sequel ratio controls tempo
More continuous conflict speeds experience; more processing slows and deepens it. Neither mode should be default for all material.

### S13 — Story is desire versus danger
Long-form movement comes from a focal character trying to attain/retain something while trouble makes the attempt costly.

### S14 — Important emotional events deserve dramatized cause/effect order
Chronological clarity helps readers feel emotion rather than merely be told its label.

## Character mechanisms

- Character becomes legible through what stimuli matter and how they react.
- Decision after setback exposes priorities.
- Different characters can receive the same event as radically different motivation because their value systems differ.

## Scene/dialogue implications

Nested IVDIVO causal chain:
`STIMULUS → REACTION` inside
`GOAL → CONFLICT → CHANGED RESULT` followed by
`REACTION → DILEMMA → DECISION → NEW GOAL`.

## Escalation/payoff

Escalation works when each scene outcome removes the possibility of simply returning to the prior state. The sequel/decision then commits the protagonist to a more consequential route.

## IVDIVO transformation

Use after every major scene in architecture:
1. What did the protagonist try?
2. What changed against expectation?
3. What do they feel/understand because of that result?
4. What alternatives now exist?
5. What decision creates the next scene?

For ORBITAL YOUTH, do not skip from emergency to next adventure: show social/family/work/status aftermath when it materially changes the next decision.
