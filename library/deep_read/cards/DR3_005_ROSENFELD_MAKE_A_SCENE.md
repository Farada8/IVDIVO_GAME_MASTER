## Unique strength

Rosenfeld is strongest at translating high-level story causality into prose-scale scene construction. Her scene model integrates real-time momentum, POV, goal, new information, tension, sensory world, emotional movement and selective summary rather than reducing a scene to dialogue or plot function alone.

## Do NOT take

- Do not require every ingredient in equal amount in every scene.
- Do not mistake sensory density for immersion.
- Do not turn scene-type labels into a rigid sequence.
- Do not use 'attention' as justification for artificial constant urgency.

## Transferable mechanisms

### R01 — Momentum distinguishes scene from static material
A scene is strongest when events and behaviors unfold in simulated real time.
Failure: description/analysis with no active present.

### R02 — Scene intention gives local purpose
Character enters pursuing a concrete job/goal connected to the larger plot.
Failure: character wanders so writer can reveal worldbuilding.

### R03 — New information should be consequential
Discovery/discussion/event deepens plot or character and normally grows from prior scenes.
Use: causal chapter linking.

### R04 — Tension grows from stakes, not melodrama
Ask what can be lost socially, emotionally, materially, professionally or physically in this scene.

### R05 — POV is part of scene mechanics
The chosen lens determines what is perceived, misread, ignored and emotionally weighted.
Use: youth social danger and mystery.

### R06 — Setting should participate
Physical environment can obstruct, expose, reveal status, trigger memory, shape body behavior or create opportunity.
Failure: decorative backdrop.

### R07 — Sensory detail can carry theme/subtext
Select sensations that express emotional situation rather than inventory all five senses.

### R08 — Scene launch orients and activates quickly
Bring character/action/problem into the scene before passive explanation makes the reader wait.
Use: enter late.

### R09 — Scene middle must complicate intention
Middle is not maintenance; pressure, discovery, resistance or emotional turn must alter the attempt.

### R10 — Scene ending changes the next available move
Exit after a decision, discovery, reversal, complication, emotional shift or new goal.
Failure: end because conversation has run out.

### R11 — Summary is allowed when drama is not needed
Use compact telling to cross low-value time/information so important moments can be dramatized fully.

### R12 — Scene types create rhythm
Suspense, contemplation, dialogue, climax, flashback, epiphany and ordinary-life scenes can vary intensity while serving one causal narrative.

## Character mechanisms

- Motivation must be visible through intention and behavior.
- Character development occurs inside actions, sensory attention and resistance, not solely interior explanation.
- Secondary characters should alter the focal character's progress or understanding.

## Scene/dialogue implications

Minimum IVDIVO scene diagnostic:
`WHO WANTS WHAT? → WHY NOW? → WHAT RESISTS? → WHAT HAPPENS IN REAL TIME? → WHAT NEW INFORMATION/FEELING ENTERS? → WHAT CHANGES?`

## Escalation/payoff

Scenes accumulate when each altered state creates the next intention. Pacing comes from varying scene type, length and intensity without breaking causality.
