## Unique strength
The corpus integrates a serious mystery/procedural engine with school hierarchy, money, family collapse, sexual/social reputation and adolescent status. Youth life is not downtime between cases; it produces cases, clues, clients, loyalties and costs.

## Transferable mechanisms
1. **Professional competence + student life in same frame.** Surveillance equipment can sit next to a math book; ordinary obligations do not disappear because hero is capable.
2. **Institutional corruption mirrors youth hierarchy.** Sheriff office and school administration can belong to the same social power ecosystem without being identical institutions.
3. **Status fall creates durable daily consequences.** Family disgrace changes housing, money, friendships, romance and treatment at school.
4. **Case engine emerges from youth life.** Rumors, cheating, adoption, revenge images, drugs, pregnancy, identity, teachers and dating can generate genuine mysteries.
5. **Main mystery and episodic case cross-feed.** Local investigation can expose information relevant to the long mystery while still resolving its own client problem.
6. **Youth competence has economic origin.** Veronica gains PI skills partly because family business needs help; skill is embedded in work/money, not bestowed as specialness.
7. **Public cynicism protects private injury.** Sarcastic/noir voice masks longing, humiliation, loyalty and unresolved attachment.
8. **Voice can carry social analysis while action proves it.** Commentary is strongest when immediately paired with observable privilege/exclusion.
9. **Friendship begins through costly intervention.** Helping someone publicly can create a relationship because it changes social risk, not because plot announces 'best friend'.
10. **Adult/youth alliances are unstable.** Parent can be mentor, employer, authority and emotionally compromised person at once.
11. **Information changes family trust.** Investigation can threaten the hero's safest relationship, making clue discovery emotionally expensive.
12. **Social wrongdoing can be as plot-worthy as violent crime.** Reputation, coercion and institutional framing have real stakes.
