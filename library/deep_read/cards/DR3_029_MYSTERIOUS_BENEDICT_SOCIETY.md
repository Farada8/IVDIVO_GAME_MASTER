## Unique strength
The novel is strong at building a youth team from complementary forms of competence, loneliness and moral choice. Tests reveal HOW children solve problems, not merely who has the highest intelligence score. Adult mentorship respects agency while acknowledging that dangerous work asked of children is genuinely frightening.

## Transferable mechanisms
1. **Test character, not only skill.** Instructions, cheating temptation, route-finding and ambiguity reveal judgment, ethics and improvisation.
2. **Complementary competence builds ensemble.** Memory, reasoning, physical resourcefulness and rule-defiance solve different classes of problems.
3. **Shared loneliness can become belonging without instant friendship.** Team forms through need, trust and work.
4. **Youth agency requires meaningful consent.** Mentor distinguishes voluntary agreement from obedience even when mission is urgent.
5. **Adults can admit the moral cost of asking youth to take risk.** Competent mentor does not pretend danger is harmless.
6. **Children's testimony has low institutional status.** Being correct is not enough; they need evidence because adult systems discount them.
7. **Secret institution tests social as well as intellectual vulnerabilities.** Pressure exploits fear, need for approval and belonging.
8. **Family can be found through interdependence.** Connection becomes visible when loss of one member would damage the whole.
9. **Antagonist underestimates youth as category.** Structural adult bias becomes tactical weakness.
10. **Climax uses previously observed human weakness, not only superior force.** Careful attention and reasoning become action.
11. **Team problems should require different members at different moments.** Avoid a single genius solving everything while others watch.
12. **Humor and eccentricity can coexist with real stakes.** Tone need not become grim to validate danger.
13. **Institutional mystery should demand proof, communication and infiltration.** Discovery must change what team tries next.
14. **Success does not erase need for care/home.** Adventure and belonging are linked rather than treating domestic life as irrelevant.

## Failure modes
- 'Gifted children' reduced to superpowers in separate specialties.
- Mentor uses children as tools without ethical tension.
- Team bonds instantly because plot says so.
- Adult institutions are stupid merely so children can win.

## IVDIVO transformation
Orbital Youth teams should be assembled around different lived competencies—maintenance intuition, social translation, AI negotiation, habitat navigation, finance/work knowledge, nonhuman cultural fluency—not five versions of the same brilliant cadet. Adult mentors must face the ethics of what risk they ask young people to carry.
