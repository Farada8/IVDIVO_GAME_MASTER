# 3. Normalized claim set

C14-C01. Character is most convincingly inferred from behavior under conditions, not descriptive labels.

C14-C02. Motive changes the meaning and moral interpretation of the same action.

C14-C03. Past, reputation, habits, talents, tastes, body and relationships are useful only insofar as they alter present expectation/action/interpretation.

C14-C04. A person behaves differently across relationship networks; voice and conduct should therefore vary by relational context.

C14-C05. Reader acceptance repeatedly depends on three functions: reason to care, reason to believe, and sufficient clarity to understand the intended uncertainty.

C14-C06. The first available explanation for a character choice is often generic; causal interrogation should continue until the answer changes story possibilities.

C14-C07. Story factor dominance controls what kind of closure the book owes. A story cannot end merely because enough milieu or lore has been displayed if its controlling problem remains unresolved.

C14-C08. Character hierarchy is an attention contract: major/minor/walk-on status should control page budget, POV access, arc depth and payoff promise.

C14-C09. Emotional stakes are not proportional to pain quantity. Chosen sacrifice, anticipation and consequence may outperform repeated injury.

C14-C10. Sympathy can coexist with disagreement; opposition need not become villainy.

C14-C11. Comic distance and serious belief use different calibration; humor can coexist with danger but must not erase the moral residence of the scene.

C14-C12. Large character change requires proportionally strong causal preparation or an explicitly signaled mystery about the cause.

C14-C13. POV is not pronoun choice. It regulates knowledge, distance, attitude, selection, honesty and reader intimacy.

C14-C14. First-person withholding must arise from the narrator's situation/limits, not merely the author's need to hide an answer.

C14-C15. Third-person depth is a controllable continuum; permanent maximal depth and permanent camera distance are both limiting defaults.

# 4. Mechanism library

## C14-M01 — ACTION → MOTIVE → REINTERPRETATION
`OBSERVABLE ACTION → INITIAL READER MODEL → MOTIVE EVIDENCE → REVISED MODEL → NEW EXPECTATION`.
Use when a reveal should deepen character rather than add biography.

## C14-M02 — CHARACTER NETWORK TEST
For each major recurring person:
`FRIEND SELF / FAMILY SELF / AUTHORITY SELF / RIVAL SELF / INTIMATE SELF / STRANGER SELF`.
At least two contexts should produce meaningfully different register or behavior without turning the person into a contradiction.

## C14-M03 — PRESENT-EFFECT BACKSTORY
`PAST EVENT → CURRENT HABIT / FEAR / EXPECTATION / RELATIONSHIP → PRESENT DECISION`.
If the past does not alter the present, compress it.

## C14-M04 — CLICHÉ-SHELF INTERROGATION
For any first explanation:
`WHY? → WHAT CAUSED THAT? → WHAT PURPOSE? → WHAT RESULT? → WHAT ALTERNATIVE?`
Require multiple candidate motives before locking unusual behavior.

## C14-M05 — STORY DOMINANCE / END CONDITION
Before outline:
`THE NOVEL IS OVER WHEN ______.`
If answer describes lore coverage rather than problem resolution / decisive human change, ONE MAIN STORY is not yet secured.

## C14-M06 — CHARACTER ATTENTION BUDGET
Assign explicit tier:
`PRIMARY MAJOR / SECONDARY MAJOR / SUPPORTING MAJOR / MINOR / WALK-ON`.
Allocate POV, biography, jokes, wounds, secrets and climactic agency accordingly.

## C14-M07 — CHOSEN COST
`VALUE A vs VALUE B → VOLUNTARY COST → CONSEQUENCE → RELATIONSHIP/SELF MODEL CHANGE`.
Prefer this over random damage when the scene's job is character revelation.

## C14-M08 — PLAUSIBILITY BUDGET
`UNUSUALNESS × CAUSAL IMPORTANCE = REQUIRED PREPARATION`.
Major improbable climax behavior needs early support; trivial oddities do not need elaborate foreshadowing.

## C14-M09 — SYMPATHETIC OPPOSITION
Give both sides:
`LEGITIMATE WANT + COMPETENCE + BLIND SPOT + NONNEGOTIABLE VALUE + COST OF LOSING`.
Then force an actual choice.

## C14-M10 — TRANSFORMATION PROOF
`OLD PATTERN → PRESSURE → FAILED/INSUFFICIENT STRATEGY → COST → NEW CHOICE → NEW PATTERN`.
No narrator-announced arc without changed behavior.

## C14-M11 — NARRATOR PURPOSE
For first person:
`WHY TELL? → TO WHOM? → WHY NOW? → WHAT DOES THE TELLING SEEK TO ACHIEVE?`
This governs selection, omission, tone and reliability.

## C14-M12 — FAIR WITHHOLDING
Track separately:
`CHARACTER KNOWS / NARRATOR WILL SAY / READER KNOWS / READER IS MEANT TO QUESTION`.
Do not hide known decisive facts solely to preserve a twist.

## C14-M13 — LIMITED THIRD SWITCH COST
Switch POV only when new viewpoint materially changes at least one of:
`KNOWLEDGE / IRONY / EMOTIONAL PRICE / DECISION ACCESS / RELATIONSHIP INTERPRETATION`.
Re-establish person/place/time immediately.

## C14-M14 — POV HEAT CONTROL
`CINEMATIC ↔ LIGHT ↔ DEEP`.
Choose by scene function: action clarity, social observation, interior pressure, mystery fairness, recovery.

## C14-M15 — BODY AS CAUSAL CHARACTER DATA
Body matters when it changes:
`SENSATION / CAPABILITY / RISK / SOCIAL RESPONSE / SELF-IMAGE / DECISION`.
Physical inventory without causal effect is low-value description.

# 5. Failure modes

- trait-label narration after action has already shown the trait;
- biography dump with no present effect;
- every named character treated as major;
- memorable eccentric minor accidentally promising a major payoff;
- 'it really happened' used as substitute for fictional plausibility;
- technology/ideology mouthpiece without independent wants and relationships;
- universal witty voice distributed across ensemble;
- suffering inflation after saturation;
- villainization of competent opposition merely to simplify climax;
- arbitrary transformation for climax convenience;
- first-person cheating by hiding a fact the narrator plainly knows;
- third-person head hopping without orientation or payoff;
- all scenes written at one fixed POV temperature;
- visual body description mistaken for characterization;
- period stereotypes treated as current demographic truth;
- MICE used as rigid ontology rather than dominance diagnostic.

# 6. Cross-source synthesis

**Card + Nancy Kress:** Card's action/motive/network framework and Kress's motivation/frustration response converge on character causality. Kress is stronger on frustration fingerprints; Card is stronger on hierarchy and reader contract.

**Card + Jordan Rosenfeld:** both reject POV as pronoun choice. Rosenfeld is stronger on embodied sensory intimacy; Card on narrator purpose, knowledge architecture and depth modulation.

**Card + James Scott Bell (Voice):** both place attitude and selection upstream of style. Bell is stronger on prose voice; Card integrates attitude with believability and network.

**Card + Hodgins:** both treat character, scene, POV and setting as interacting systems. Hodgins is stronger on scene/setting integrity; Card on attention hierarchy.

**Card + Zunshine:** fair withholding aligns with source-monitoring: a belief/claim must remain attached to who knows or asserts it.

**Card + Oatley:** Card's reader care/belief/clarity and Oatley's goal-plan simulation complement each other. Card explains delivery; Oatley better explains reader identification through adopted goals.

**Card + Vorhaus:** controlled comic distance plus character-owned humor yields a strong anti-universal-wit gate.

**Tensions retained:** Card's older social stereotypes and broad claims about heroism/gender/readers are not operationalized as laws. Current psychology, canon and beta-reader evidence outrank them.

# 9. Final production gates from Card

- **C14-G01 ACTION BEFORE LABEL:** show behavior before naming trait.
- **C14-G02 MULTI-MOTIVE:** generate at least three plausible motives for a major surprising act before locking one.
- **C14-G03 PRESENT-EFFECT BACKSTORY:** no history without current behavioral/relationship effect.
- **C14-G04 NETWORK:** recurring major character has meaningful life outside protagonist-facing function.
- **C14-G05 RELATIONSHIP REGISTER:** same person must not sound identical to friend, parent, mentor, rival, lover, robot peer and review board.
- **C14-G06 END CONDITION:** one sentence states when the novel is over.
- **C14-G07 HIERARCHY:** page/POV/arc budget follows character tier.
- **C14-G08 NO PAIN INFLATION:** escalate choice/meaning/consequence before gore or repeated suffering.
- **C14-G09 CHOSEN COST:** climax character revelation should preferably include voluntarily accepted cost.
- **C14-G10 PLAUSIBILITY PROPORTIONALITY:** unlikely important behavior receives proportional preparation.
- **C14-G11 NO IDEA SERVANT:** ideological/technical character has independent human/robotic causality.
- **C14-G12 TRANSFORMATION EVIDENCE:** changed pattern, not changed label.
- **C14-G13 NARRATOR PURPOSE:** first-person telling situation defined.
- **C14-G14 FAIR WITHHOLDING:** do not hide what POV genuinely knows merely for suspense.
- **C14-G15 POV SWITCH PRICE:** switch earns new knowledge/irony/emotion/decision access.
- **C14-G16 POV HEAT:** depth selected deliberately, not by habit.
- **C14-G17 BODY CAUSALITY:** physical detail changes perception/action/risk/social meaning or is compressed.
- **C14-G18 CURRENT-EVIDENCE OVERRIDE:** beta readers, current youth psychology, current canon and Founder corrections outrank dated universal claims.
