## Unique strength
Brown is useful primarily for clue-chain propulsion: one answer simultaneously resolves a local question, changes interpretation and produces a new concrete question/location/task. Domain expertise becomes action because knowledge changes what characters can do next.

## Transferable mechanisms
1. **Opening leaves an actionable encoded residue.** Victim's final act creates a problem that surviving characters must interpret.
2. **Clue has more than one function.** It can identify intended recipient, indicate decoding method and redirect investigation.
3. **Answer → new question.** Solving a clue should reduce one uncertainty while opening a narrower next task.
4. **Knowledge must change movement.** Symbol/history/math matters when it points to a person, place, object, method or danger.
5. **Complementary experts interpret different layers.** Cryptography and symbolic/history expertise see different patterns in same evidence.
6. **Personal relationship can be encoded into clue design.** Shared childhood/family meanings make evidence legible to one person without arbitrary author withholding.
7. **False official interpretation creates pressure.** Investigator and suspect can read same evidence through different causal models.
8. **Coherent clue family increases fairness.** Repeated motifs should belong to one underlying logic rather than arbitrary riddles.
9. **Pursuit can force imperfect interpretation.** Time pressure makes characters act on provisional answers, allowing correction later.
10. **Clue chain needs escalating consequence, not only harder puzzles.** Each step should change danger, trust or scope.
11. **Expert explanation is safest when immediately required for action.** Long knowledge material should earn its place by altering the next move.
12. **Final reveal should reinterpret earlier clues rather than negate them.** Reader reward comes from pattern consolidation.
