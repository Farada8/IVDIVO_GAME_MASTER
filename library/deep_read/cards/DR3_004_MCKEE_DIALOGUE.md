## Unique strength

McKee's strongest contribution is to define dialogue as ACTION rather than information or realistic chatter. A line is a tactic used by a character to satisfy a desire. Character voice then emerges from culture, experience, psychology, syntax, rhythm, tonality and vocabulary under pressure.

## Do NOT take

- Do not polish lines before scene objectives/opposition work.
- Do not make every line maximally clever or metaphorical.
- Do not force heavy subtext where genre/style calls for direct speech.
- Do not confuse realism with transcription of everyday filler.

## Transferable mechanisms

### D01 — Every line performs a task
Trigger: dialogue exists to explain.
Test: What is the speaker trying to make the listener do, believe, reveal, forgive, fear, permit or stop?
Failure: line has content but no tactic.

### D02 — Dialogue creates action/reaction beats
Line/tactic → listener reaction/countertactic → changed pressure.
Use: conversations should progress, not alternate opinions.

### D03 — Said / unsaid / unsayable layers
Text carries chosen expression; subtext contains consciously withheld material; deeper drives may not be consciously formulable.
Effect: dimensional character.

### D04 — Character-specific vocabulary
Voice differentiation begins in what a person notices and what words are available to them, not cosmetic slang.
Use: especially youth voices, professions, species/cultures, Smith vs Confederation personnel.

### D05 — Syntax/rhythm/tonality are behavioral signatures
Different characters should build sentences, interrupt, evade, joke and qualify differently.
Failure: every intelligent character shares author cadence.

### D06 — On-the-nose is usually a design failure
When people state their fullest thoughts and emotions directly, subtext dies.
Rewrite by changing objectives, resistance or situation before paraphrasing words.

### D07 — The third thing / trialogue
Two people can conduct their real conflict through a shared object, job, meal, repair, game, document, child, body, mission or unrelated-seeming topic.
Use: relationships and institutional conflict without speechifying.

### D08 — Repetition trivializes beats
If several exchanges perform the same action, keep the strongest or escalate/change tactic.
Use: Red Team dialogue compression.

### D09 — Inner speech is still action
Internal dialogue should pursue a purpose: justify, rehearse, deny, fantasize, accuse, solve, soothe.
Failure: interior monologue as static essay.

### D10 — Narration can be biased verbal action
First-person narration may seek sympathy, forgiveness, authority or control of reader interpretation.
Use: unreliable or self-protective POV.

### D11 — Dialogue problems are story problems
Weak talk often originates in vague desire, absent conflict, low stakes, undefined relationship or no scene turn.
Use: diagnose upstream before line edit.

### D12 — Fictional talk is compressed life
Eliminate greetings/filler unless they perform status, avoidance, threat, intimacy or another scene action.

## Character mechanisms

- Build a 'walking dictionary' for major characters: concrete nouns, verbs, taboo words, favorite comparisons, professional vocabulary, evasions.
- Voice must reflect culture + family + education + work + wounds + status + immediate objective.
- The more psychologically complex a character, the less interchangeable the voice should be.

## Scene/dialogue implications

Conversation architecture:
`OBJECTIVE A → TACTIC → RESPONSE/RESISTANCE → TACTIC SHIFT → LEVERAGE CHANGE → WITHHELD/REVEALED MATERIAL → SCENE CHANGE`.

## Escalation/payoff

Escalate conversations by changing tactics and leverage, not volume. Payoff occurs when withheld material, status or relationship changes because someone is forced to choose what to say/not say/do.

- Why can't they say the whole truth directly?
- What tactic is each line performing?
- What third thing can carry the conflict physically/socially?
- Where does leverage change?
- Do voices remain identifiable without dialogue tags?
