## Transferable mechanisms

### MCK01 — Principle, not formula
Strong form is generated by material and causality; conventions may bend when something more important must be preserved.
Use: prevents beat-sheet imitation.

### MCK02 — Archetypal conflict inside specific world
Universal conflict gains power through rare, concrete cultural/social detail.
Use: orbital civilization should feel specific while emotional conflicts remain legible.

### MCK03 — Inciting Incident creates the central dramatic question
Sequence: status quo upset → desire activated → audience asks how this will end.
Failure: disturbance with no durable question.

### MCK04 — Inciting Incident projects the Obligatory Scene
The opening disturbance implicitly promises a later confrontation with the strongest forces it awakens.
Use: Story Gate check that climax answers the book that actually began.

### MCK05 — Object of Desire clarifies the quest
Ask what the protagonist wants strongly enough to act for.
Failure: theme or condition masquerading as goal.

### MCK06 — Gap between expectation and result
Character acts expecting one response; reality answers differently.
Sequence: action → unexpected reaction/result → new effort.
Effect: story energy and revelation.

### MCK07 — Progressive Complications eliminate weaker options
Each failed/partial action means the hero cannot simply repeat a cheaper tactic; subsequent action must spend more will, risk or resources.
Use: escalation audit.

### MCK08 — Scene is story in miniature
Scene objective → action → conflict → unexpected reaction → meaningful state/value change.
Failure: dialogue/information that leaves the situation functionally identical.

### MCK09 — Antagonism is total pressure, not just villain
Inner conflict + relationships + institutions + environment can combine against desire.
Use: SMITH jurisdiction conflicts and ORBITAL YOUTH social/institutional pressure.

### MCK10 — Composition needs causal unity plus experiential variety
Causal spine must lock opening disturbance to climax, while scenes vary in tone, conflict level, location, rhythm and emotional charge.
Failure: either episodic fragments or monotonous repetition.

### MCK11 — Crisis is ultimate decision under maximum pressure
A true dilemma involves irreconcilable goods, lesser evils or equivalent costly alternatives.
Effect: character revealed through choice.
Failure: climax solved by information, rescue or power without protagonist decision.

### MCK12 — Climax is meaning, not noise
Climax's major reversal must complete the story's central meaning through action/consequence.
Use: a quiet refusal can out-climax an explosion if it resolves the core conflict.

### MCK13 — Resolution lets consequence register
After climax, give enough aftermath for the reader to understand changed relationships/world/state.
Use: important IVDIVO law—finish current book before series hook.

### MCK14 — Coincidence may start trouble but should not solve late story
Early randomness can gain meaning; late outcomes should increasingly be produced by character choices and established forces.

## Character mechanisms

- Pressure reveals character more reliably than biography.
- Stronger antagonism forces more complete expression of capacity and weakness.
- Crisis choice is the sharpest character definition.

## Scene/dialogue implications

- Every scene needs an objective and a turn/change.
- Exposition works best when embedded in conflict or action.
- A scene's words are secondary to the pressure pattern beneath them.

## Escalation/payoff

Escalation: repeated expectation/result gaps force stronger actions and points of no return.
Payoff: Inciting Incident → accumulated antagonism → Crisis decision → meaningful Climax → consequence-bearing Resolution.

AGREES WITH Bell on meaningful trouble as reader propulsion.

## IVDIVO transformation

Permanent Story Gate questions:
1. What durable dramatic question does WHY NOW create?
2. What later confrontation does the opening promise?
3. What does each major failed action make impossible to repeat cheaply?
4. What is the protagonist's true Crisis choice?
5. Does Climax answer the main conflict through the protagonist's action?
6. Is there enough Resolution before the series hook?
