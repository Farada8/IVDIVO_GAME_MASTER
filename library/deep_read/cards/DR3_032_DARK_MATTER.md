## Unique strength
Dark Matter is strongest when high-concept identity questions are anchored to an ordinary chosen life: spouse, teenage child, home, cooking, career compromise and memory. Alternate selves become dramatic because each version carries plausible consequences of different choices and incompatible claims to the same relationships.

## Transferable mechanisms
1. **High concept must threaten something concrete already loved.** Family night gives multiverse loss emotional scale.
2. **Abandoned possibilities are character pressure before they become plot mechanism.** Career regret and partner's sacrificed art establish the road-not-taken theme in ordinary dialogue.
3. **Identity is relational, not only autobiographical.** Knowing memories/facts is insufficient when multiple versions share them; behavior and present choice matter.
4. **Same origin can produce morally divergent subjects.** Shared past does not guarantee shared entitlement, restraint or strategy after branching experience.
5. **Duplicate personhood creates rights conflict.** More than one version can sincerely experience the same spouse/home/child as 'mine'.
6. **The body is not a unique identity key.** Near-identical bodies intensify the need for relational, experiential and ethical criteria.
7. **Ordinary family knowledge can become recognition evidence.** Intimacy is made of accumulated small patterns rather than dramatic biographical facts.
8. **Alternate worlds should reflect consequences of choices, not merely spectacle.** Each variation earns value by testing desire/regret/fear.
9. **Self-competition externalizes inner regret.** Rival versions dramatize what the protagonist could become if one desire dominates others.
10. **Return is not resolution if category problem persists.** Reaching 'home' can create a new conflict over who has legitimate claim.
11. **Love does not automatically settle ownership.** Relationship partner/child should retain their own agency when variants compete.
12. **Climax should choose a future, not prove metaphysical uniqueness.** The meaningful act is what the family/subject decides to do now.

## Failure modes
- Alternate bodies/worlds as touristic spectacle.
- 'Original' automatically has greater moral rights without story examination.
- Family reduced to prize for protagonist.
- Identity settled by exposition about consciousness.

## IVDIVO transformation
For consciousness transfer or copied states, never ask only `Is this the same consciousness?` Also dramatize: Who remembers what? Who has continuous relationships? Who bears prior obligations? Can two valid continuations coexist? Who gets to choose the next body/life? What rights do loved ones have in recognition/refusal?
