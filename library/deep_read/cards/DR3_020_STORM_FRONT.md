## Unique strength
Storm Front is useful for a first-person professional hidden-world case engine where rent, client work, police jurisdiction, supernatural law, personal history and immediate murder investigation all press the protagonist at once. It also demonstrates how one case can make the hero suspect under a second legal system.

## Transferable mechanisms
1. **Profession has economics.** Expert needs clients, rent and reputation; honesty can cost money.
2. **Ordinary institution hires hidden-world expertise without fully understanding it.** Police need consultant but cannot access all rules/sources.
3. **Two legal systems can judge same act differently.** Mortal investigation and supernatural council produce conflicting authority.
4. **Past offense turns current expertise into suspicion.** Hero's relevant competence is also evidence against him.
5. **Case becomes personal through procedural logic, not coincidence alone.** Solving crime is necessary to avoid institutional punishment.
6. **Withheld professional rules create relationship strain.** Consultant cannot tell police everything, so trust degrades even when both want solution.
7. **Parallel client case can converge with central mystery.** Separate paid work should eventually share causal structure rather than remain filler.
8. **Expert voice includes uncertainty and mundane frustration.** Humor/rent/technology problems keep hidden-world professional human.
9. **Research itself can be dangerous/illegal.** To reproduce or understand hostile method, investigator may need to approach forbidden knowledge.
10. **Authority figure can be legitimately vigilant and personally biased.** Warden suspicion arises from real history yet becomes distorted by prejudice.
11. **Hidden-world law should constrain power.** Rules matter because violation carries consequence, not because lore says so.
12. **Case closes while professional/relational consequences continue.** Serial engine inherits changed trust and unresolved institutional position.

## Failure modes
- Copying wizard-detective voice or urban-fantasy trappings.
- Withholding rules only to surprise reader.
- Police/Confederation made stupid so expert shines.
- Personal history coincidentally connected to every case.
