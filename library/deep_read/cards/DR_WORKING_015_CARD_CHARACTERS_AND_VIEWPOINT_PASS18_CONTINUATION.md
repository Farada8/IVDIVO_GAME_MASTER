## New mechanisms extracted in Pass 18

### C15-M15 — REAL LIFE IS RAW MATERIAL, NOT PLAUSIBILITY PROOF
`OBSERVATION → MULTIPLE MOTIVE HYPOTHESES → TRANSFORMATION → STORY FUNCTION`.
A fact that happened in reality can still read as false in fiction if causal preparation is missing.

### C15-M16 — ORDINARY-TO-AUTHOR CAN BE EXOTIC-TO-READER
Writers often undervalue their everyday environment because it is familiar. For Orbital Youth, post-money routines, robot-human coexistence, gravity norms and ordinary civic habits should be mined before inventing another spectacular technology.

### C15-M17 — IDEA-SERVANT CHARACTER FIREWALL
If a character exists to carry a thesis, technology explanation or ideology, ask:
`WHY DOES THIS PERSON CARE? → WHAT DO THEY WANT APART FROM THE ARGUMENT? → WHO DO THEY LOVE/RESENT/OWE? → WHAT WOULD THEY DO IF THE debate vanished?`
If there is no answer, the character remains an exposition device.

### C15-M18 — COMEDY DISTANCE / SERIOUSNESS BALANCE
Comic signaling gives permission to laugh at pain or incongruity. In a serious YA/SF story, humor must be modulated so that real injury, consent violation, betrayal or moral cost does not become weightless.

### C15-M19 — PLAUSIBILITY BUDGET IS PROPORTIONAL
The more unusual the behavior and the more causally important it is, the earlier and more deeply it must be justified. Do not overprepare trivial details and underprepare climax behavior.

### C15-M20 — MOTIVE REVELATION REWRITES PRIOR EVENTS
New motive information should change the reader's interpretation of previous action, not merely add biography. This is especially useful for mystery, relationship conflict and institutional misunderstanding.

### C15-M21 — ATTITUDE IS WORLD REVEAL
Description is not neutral inventory in close POV. What the character notices and judges simultaneously reveals world + person. Exposition that has no attitude is a candidate for compression or rerouting.

### C15-M22 — IMPLIED PAST OVER EXPLANATORY FLASHBACK
Habits, expectations, social recognition, reflexes and shared-history dialogue can imply a long past without stopping the current action for biography.

### C15-M23 — CHARACTER NETWORK PROVES OFFSTAGE LIFE
A recurring character should have relationships and obligations outside the protagonist's current scene. A named conscious robot should especially pass this test; otherwise it risks reading as equipment with dialogue.

### C15-M24 — NARRATOR PURPOSE GATE FOR FIRST PERSON
Before first-person prose, answer:
`WHY IS THIS PERSON TELLING THIS STORY? TO WHOM? WHAT DO THEY WANT THE TELLING TO DO?`
The answer governs selection, omission, tone and honesty.

### C15-M25 — FAIR WITHHOLDING FIREWALL
A narrator/viewpoint may withhold what the character genuinely does not know. It should not routinely hide information the viewpoint character already possesses merely to preserve suspense, unless the narrating situation itself justifies that artifice.

### C15-M26 — UNRELIABLE ≠ AUTHOR CHEATING
Unreliability needs discoverable motive, pattern and/or corroborating reality. The reader may be uncertain about truth but should be able to understand why the narrator distorts it.

### C15-M27 — LIMITED THIRD SWITCH COST
A limited-third POV switch spends reader orientation. Switch only when the new viewpoint changes knowledge, stakes, irony or emotional access enough to pay that cost; establish person/time/place immediately after the break.

### C15-M28 — POV HEAT MODULATION
Deep penetration = high subjectivity/intensity; cinematic distance = observational clarity; light penetration bridges them. Select depth by scene function rather than maintaining a fixed stylistic temperature.

### C15-M29 — FIRST PERSON MUST LIVE FROM INSIDE
A first-person narrator who merely camera-describes their own body/actions while withholding motive, attitude and meaning wastes the form's main advantage.

### C15-M30 — PAIN HAS DIMINISHING RETURNS
Escalating suffering is not equivalent to escalating reader investment. Repetition can produce numbness, irritation or comedy. Increase meaning, choice and consequence before increasing gore/intensity.

### C15-M31 — CHOSEN COST > RANDOM DAMAGE
For many scenes, a character voluntarily accepting a meaningful cost for another value is more revealing than simply being harmed by events.

## Failure modes strengthened
- `IT REALLY HAPPENED` defense used instead of causal plausibility.
- thesis-mouthpiece / technology-tour character.
- comic relief placed immediately after a beat that needs moral/emotional residence.
- over-justification that falsely promises a later payoff.
- first-person narrator who knows the answer but hides it only because the author needs a mystery.
- limited-third head-hopping without transition.
- permanent deep POV that becomes exhausting; permanent cinematic POV that strips prose of interior advantage.
- increasing suffering after reader saturation instead of changing kind of cost.

## Cross-source comparison — Pass 18
- **Card + Kress:** motive and frustration are character causality; Card adds plausibility/attitude/network mechanisms, Kress more explicitly stresses character-specific response.
- **Card + Rosenfeld:** both reject POV as pronoun choice; Rosenfeld is stronger on embodied/sensory intimacy, Card is stronger on narrator architecture and penetration temperature.
- **Card + Bell Voice:** both make attitude central. Bell is more style/voice focused; Card ties attitude to believability and viewpoint access.
- **Card + Zunshine:** Card's fair withholding supports Zunshine's source-monitoring principle: information must retain who knows/claims it and when.
- **Card + Vorhaus:** Card's controlled disbelief complements Vorhaus's character-owned humor; together they prohibit universal witty voice and humor that collapses stakes.
- **TENSION:** Card's period-specific universal claims about heroism, gender and reader response remain heuristics, not laws. Current beta evidence outranks them.
