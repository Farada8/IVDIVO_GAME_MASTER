# IVDIVO DEEP READ WAVE 02 — ORBITAL YOUTH SPINE v0.1

Status: WORKING PRODUCTION STANDARD / SOURCE-SYNTHESIS
Date: 2026-08-16
DR3 sources: Jessica Brody; Veronica Mars; Friday Night Lights; My So-Called Life; Teen Wolf; The Hunger Games; Skyward; The Mysterious Benedict Society.

This is a production synthesis, not canon. No source world, character, scene or signature invention is imported.

# 1. PRIMARY LAW

ORBITAL YOUTH is not `teenagers + space setting`.

A youth story passes only if age/development and orbital civilization materially change causality:
- dependence on family/adults vs first autonomy;
- first work/career and money;
- housing and transport permissions;
- peer status and public shame;
- attraction/romance/sexual reputation;
- training and institutional gatekeeping;
- body changes and synthetic embodiment;
- friendship/team belonging;
- future horizon;
- mistakes made for developmentally credible reasons.

If the same scene works almost unchanged for a 40-year-old on Earth, it needs redesign.

# 2. CROSS-SOURCE MECHANISMS

## OY01 — Ordinary life precedes extraordinary plot
Hunger Games begins with food/work/family; Teen Wolf with sport/body/status; Skyward with jobs/training/stigma; Veronica Mars with school + paid work.

IVDIVO RULE: before a major ability, anomaly or political revelation matters, establish what the young person is trying to do in ordinary orbital life.

## OY02 — Future civilization must be visible as labor and access
World is experienced through who gets which job, training slot, housing, transport, food/resource privileges, medical/body access and reputation.

IVDIVO RULE: explain orbital society through transactions and constraints, not civic lectures.

## OY03 — Youth competence does not equal adult maturity
A young person may pilot, investigate, repair, negotiate with AI or survive danger while remaining inexperienced in love, status, family conflict, self-presentation and long-term judgment.

## OY04 — Peer status is real leverage
Rumor, exclusion, attraction, team role, embarrassment and public labeling can alter decisions before formal institutions act.

## OY05 — Family is part of the causal engine
Family can supply responsibility, money pressure, inherited stigma, work, care, permission, emotional safety and conflict. Parents need not be villains to oppose a youth choice.

## OY06 — Inherited status should have material consequences
Family reputation/class/history changes opportunities only if it affects actual access, work, housing, training, trust or public treatment.

## OY07 — Ability/body change must rewrite daily life
New perception, synthetic body, AI interface or environmental adaptation changes sport, work eligibility, attraction, privacy, family fear, insurance/status, transport and social identity.

## OY08 — Friendship is earned through costly action
Shared risk, cover, care, problem-solving and keeping confidence create friendship more credibly than declared closeness.

## OY09 — Ensemble = complementary competence + incompatible wants
A team should not be five smart people with different exposition specialties. Each member needs ordinary life, desire, fear, status and a kind of competence the others genuinely cannot replace.

## OY10 — Adult mentor ethics remain visible
When adults ask young people to take serious risk, the story must acknowledge power asymmetry, consent, alternatives, responsibility and aftercare.

## OY11 — Inner youth voice and spoken youth voice differ
Young characters may analyze privately with sophistication but speak in evasions, jokes, fragments, bravado or bad timing because status and shame interfere.

## OY12 — Public persona vs private self generates story
Athlete/cadet/influencer/gifted trainee/nonhuman exchange student can perform one identity publicly while carrying a contradictory fear, debt or desire.

## OY13 — Group membership is both safety and identity pressure
School cohort, work crew, habitat guild, cadet wing, species community or online/public group provides resources/status while demanding conformity.

## OY14 — Bad decisions need good subjective reasons
Curiosity, attraction, fear of exclusion, loyalty, shame, desire for independence, family protection or proving worth can rationally outweigh distant risk from the youth point of view.

## OY15 — Mystery/case engines should grow from youth civilization
Cases can arise from access fraud, work placement, reputation attacks, synthetic-body coercion, mixed-species relationship disputes, housing, AI privacy, transport permissions, exam cheating, maintenance shortcuts or family contracts.

## OY16 — Public institutions can exploit and celebrate the same youth
A habitat may honor cadets/athletes/operators while using their bodies, labor or image. Avoid simple good/bad institution binaries.

## OY17 — Youth politics should emerge from lived contradiction
Rebellion becomes convincing after protagonist has paid for inequality through family, work, body, status or relationships—not because someone gives a correct ideological speech.

## OY18 — First-book closure before saga expansion
A youth series installment must resolve its own main conflict and permanently alter status/relationships/world. Larger institution or civilization problem may expand afterward.

## OY19 — Long-series youth cannot reset
Age, work level, housing, family trust, romance, injuries, legal status, body choice, money and public reputation accumulate from book to book.

## OY20 — Humor and boredom are civilization proof
Young people joke, flirt, procrastinate, get embarrassed, waste time, compete and get bored even in advanced societies. Removing this makes them plot operators, not youth.

# 3. ORBITALIZATION TEST

For every youth chapter ask:

1. What in this scene could happen only, or differently, because this is an orbital civilization?
2. What does the young character want that is specific to their current age/dependence/status?
3. What ordinary system is present: money, work, school/training, housing, food, transport, family, sport, entertainment, healthcare, AI, body access?
4. How does orbital infrastructure change the social stakes?
5. Could this scene be moved to an ordinary Earth school with only nouns changed? If yes: REDESIGN.

# 4. YOUTH CHARACTER GATE

Each major youth character needs:
- concrete current goal;
- private desire;
- family situation;
- money/work relation;
- peer status;
- friendship relation;
- attraction/romance or meaningful absence of it;
- embarrassment/shame trigger;
- future aspiration;
- contradiction;
- bad-decision pattern;
- ordinary hobby/pleasure;
- body relation;
- one competence;
- one area of obvious inexperience;
- price and change.

# 5. YOUTH DIALOGUE GATE

Fail dialogue if:
- everyone speaks in polished policy language;
- each teen accurately diagnoses their own psychology;
- status/attraction/embarrassment never affect what is said;
- friends never interrupt, tease, misread or withhold;
- all voices share the same vocabulary and sentence length.

Prefer:
`PRIVATE THOUGHT ≠ SAFE SPEECH → TACTIC/JOKE/EVASION → PEER REACTION → STATUS/RELATIONSHIP CHANGE`.

# 6. ORBITAL YOUTH TEAM ENGINE

`DIFFERENT ORDINARY LIVES`
→ `SHARED INSTITUTION/EVENT`
→ `INCOMPATIBLE PERSONAL WANTS`
→ `PROBLEM REQUIRES COMPLEMENTARY SKILLS`
→ `FIRST COOPERATION WITH FRICTION`
→ `PUBLIC/PRIVATE STATUS COST`
→ `TRUST EARNED BY ACTION`
→ `BAD DECISION OR SECRET`
→ `GROUP FRACTURE`
→ `CHOICE TO REJOIN/LEAVE/REDEFINE GROUP`
→ `LOCAL CONFLICT RESOLVED`
→ `NEW RELATIONSHIP/STATUS STATE`.

# 7. BODY / SYNTHETIC YOUTH ENGINE

`ORDINARY BODY/IDENTITY PROBLEM`
→ `NEW BODY/INTERFACE/ABILITY`
→ `IMMEDIATE PRACTICAL BENEFIT`
→ `SOCIAL BENEFIT OR ATTENTION`
→ `CONTROL/PRIVACY/DEPENDENCE PROBLEM`
→ `FAMILY/ROMANTIC/WORK CONSEQUENCE`
→ `PUBLIC CATEGORY CHANGES`
→ `CHOICE ABOUT USE/OWNERSHIP/IDENTITY`
→ `PRICE`
→ `NEW NORMAL`.

Do not treat embodiment as a power-up montage.

# 8. RED TEAM — ORBITAL YOUTH FLAGS

FATAL:
- youth age is cosmetic;
- orbital setting is cosmetic;
- book's main conflict does not close;
- protagonist has no decisive climactic choice.

MAJOR:
- youth characters speak like adult committees;
- competence erases developmental vulnerability;
- parents/adults vanish when inconvenient;
- money/work/housing never matter;
- power/technology has no ordinary-life consequence;
- team members are interchangeable;
- romance/friendship is detached from main causality;
- social status resets after crisis;
- institution is purely evil or purely wise;
- every young character wants independence in the same way.

# 9. ROUTING

Use Brody for developmental-vs-genre check and series/local-closure check.
Use Veronica Mars for youth case engine + status/family/work integration.
Use FNL for social ecology, ensemble, family, jobs, body and longitudinal consequence.
Use MSCL for authentic youth voice, embarrassment, attraction, family and inner/outer mismatch.
Use Teen Wolf for ability/body change intersecting ordinary youth life.
Use Hunger Games for political economy, family responsibility, public persona and lived institutional pressure.
Use Skyward for future-career gatekeeping, inherited stigma, labor society and training/team identity.
Use Benedict Society for complementary youth teams, tests, belonging and mentor ethics.
