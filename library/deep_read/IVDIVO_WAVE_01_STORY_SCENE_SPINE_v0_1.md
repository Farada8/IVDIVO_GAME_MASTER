# IVDIVO DEEP READ WAVE 01 — STORY / SCENE SPINE v0.1

Status: WORKING PRODUCTION STANDARD / SOURCE-SYNTHESIS
Date: 2026-08-16
Sources promoted to DR3: Truby / Anatomy of Story; McKee / Story; McKee / Dialogue; Rosenfeld / Make a Scene; Bell / Conflict & Suspense; Swain / Techniques of the Selling Writer; Bickham / Scene & Structure; Edgerton / Hooked.

This file is not new canon. It is a production synthesis of cross-source mechanisms that survived comparison.

# 1. SHARED CORE — WHAT MULTIPLE SOURCES AGREE ON

## 1.1 Story begins when a person is forced into meaningful change

`EXISTING LIFE/WEAKNESS → DISTURBANCE → ACTIONABLE PROBLEM → WANT/GOAL`

Edgerton emphasizes beginning at durable trouble; Truby connects inciting event to need/desire; McKee frames Inciting Incident as the primary cause of the central dramatic question.

IVDIVO RULE: do not open a novel with lore that can be postponed. Open with a person, a concrete life, and the event/problem that starts the book's causal chain.

## 1.2 Story advances by action meeting reality

Unified mechanism:
`GOAL/WANT → ACTION → RESISTANCE → RESULT DIFFERENT FROM EXPECTATION → NEW INFORMATION/EMOTION → DECISION → NEW GOAL/ACTION`

- Truby: acting + learning.
- McKee: expectation/result gap + progressive complications.
- Swain/Bickham: goal/conflict/result and sequel/decision.
- Rosenfeld: scene intention + momentum + new information.
- Bell: meaningful trouble and local suspense.

IVDIVO RULE: every major scene/chapter must alter the next available move.

## 1.3 Consequence must be lived, not skipped

A major outcome should produce enough reaction, dilemma or relationship fallout to make the next choice credible.

IVDIVO RULE: after crisis, humiliation, injury, discovery, betrayal, supernatural event or public failure, do not jump automatically to the next mission. Ask what changed in body, money, family, work, status, trust, fear and available options.

## 1.4 Opposition is an intelligent force, not delay

Unified mechanism:
`DESIRE A ↔ INCOMPATIBLE DESIRE/SYSTEM/FORCE B → TACTIC ↔ COUNTERTACTIC → LEVERAGE CHANGE`

McKee broadens antagonism to inner/personal/institution/environmental pressure; Truby and Bell insist on structural opposition; Swain/Bickham require motivated conflict.

IVDIVO RULE: opposition must have a reason to resist and enough agency/logic to change tactics.

## 1.5 Escalation eliminates cheap repetition

Progressive complication means the hero cannot keep trying the same low-cost solution. Prior failures/results must narrow options, raise price, reveal stronger resistance or force strategy change.

IVDIVO RULE: if chapter 8 could be solved by the same tactic tried in chapter 2, escalation is probably fake.

## 1.6 Scene is a local causal machine

Unified scene spine:
`WHO WANTS WHAT NOW? → WHAT RESISTS? → WHAT TACTIC? → WHAT COUNTERPRESSURE? → WHAT NEW FACT/FEELING? → WHAT CHANGES?`

Scene may be action, conversation, meal, date, repair, class, commute, hearing, training or contemplation. Physical violence is not required.

## 1.7 Dialogue is scene action in words

`OBJECTIVE → VERBAL TACTIC → RESPONSE → TACTIC SHIFT → LEVERAGE/RELATIONSHIP CHANGE`

McKee Dialogue supplies subtext/voice; Rosenfeld supplies intention; Bell supplies conflict; Swain/Bickham supply stimulus-response continuity.

IVDIVO RULE: if dialogue could be replaced by an email containing the same information with no loss of dramatic function, redesign the conversation.

## 1.8 Climax is protagonist choice under maximal relevant pressure

McKee: Crisis dilemma → meaningful Climax. Truby: battle/self-revelation/moral choice. Swain/Bickham: causal sequence must culminate rather than reset.

IVDIVO RULE: main conflict closes because protagonist chooses/acts/pays, not because a stronger ally arrives, lore is explained or power activates automatically.

## 1.9 Resolution is part of the book

Climax is not permission to cut directly to franchise setup.

IVDIVO RULE: show enough aftermath to establish what is now true: relationship, status, institution, body, world, knowledge, price and changed self. Only then open series hook.

# 2. DIFFERENCES — WHAT NOT TO COLLAPSE

## Truby vs McKee

Truby is preferred for organic whole-story architecture, character web, revelations, learning and moral change.
McKee is preferred for Inciting Incident promise, scene turning mechanics, progressive complications, total antagonism, Crisis/Climax/Resolution.
Do not choose one as universal master system.

## Rosenfeld vs Swain/Bickham

Rosenfeld is preferred for prose-scene completeness: POV, senses, setting, momentum, emotional journey, varied scene types.
Swain/Bickham are preferred for explicit causal joints between scene result and next decision/goal.

## Bell vs McKee

Bell is preferred as reader-propulsion and suspense diagnostic.
McKee is preferred when evaluating structural pressure and meaning of the final reversal.

## Edgerton

Edgerton is specialized: use primarily to locate the opening, separate surface/deeper problem and police setup/backstory. Do not make his opening conventions the structure of the whole novel.

# 3. IVDIVO MASTER CAUSAL LOOP

For chapters and major scene chains:

`ORDINARY/EXISTING CONDITION`
→ `DISTURBANCE / WHY NOW`
→ `CONCRETE WANT`
→ `ACTION`
→ `MOTIVATED RESISTANCE`
→ `RESULT ≠ EXPECTATION`
→ `NEW INFORMATION + HUMAN CONSEQUENCE`
→ `REACTION`
→ `DILEMMA / OPTION NARROWING`
→ `CHOICE`
→ `NEW ACTION AT HIGHER PRICE`

Repeat with genuine change until:

`CRISIS CHOICE`
→ `CLIMACTIC ACTION`
→ `MAIN CONFLICT RESOLVED`
→ `PRICE / AFTERMATH`
→ `NEW EQUILIBRIUM`
→ only then `SERIES HOOK`.

# 4. IVDIVO SCENE GATE v0.1

A scene fails if several of these cannot be answered:

1. WHO owns the scene?
2. WHAT do they want now, concretely?
3. WHY NOW?
4. WHAT resists them and why?
5. WHAT tactic do they try?
6. WHAT does reality/opponent do that they did not fully expect?
7. WHAT new information, emotion, leverage or cost enters?
8. WHAT changes before exit?
9. HOW does that changed state motivate the next scene?
10. IS the scene dramatizing an important moment or merely explaining one that should have been dramatized elsewhere?

# 5. IVDIVO DIALOGUE GATE v0.1

1. What does each speaker want from the other?
2. What can each not safely say?
3. What tactic does each line perform?
4. Are they repeating the same beat?
5. Can a third thing — task, object, body, document, meal, game, repair, child, mission — carry the conflict?
6. Does vocabulary/syntax/rhythm belong to this character?
7. Does status or leverage change?
8. Does the conversation end in a different relationship/decision/knowledge state?

# 6. OPENING GATE v0.1

1. What is the first durable trouble?
2. What immediate surface problem does it create?
3. What deeper weakness/relationship problem does it begin to expose?
4. What first choice does the protagonist make?
5. What consequence does that choice create?
6. What setup is truly needed now?
7. What backstory can wait?
8. What question/promise carries the reader forward?
9. Does the opening contain structural DNA of the climax the book will owe?

# 7. RED TEAM FATAL/MAJOR FLAGS ADDED FROM WAVE 01

FATAL:
- Opening starts one book but climax resolves another.
- Protagonist never makes the decisive climactic choice.
- Major causal links are missing; events can be reordered without consequence.
- Main conflict ends by rescue/deus ex/lore explanation rather than established action.

MAJOR:
- Surface plot and character problem are unrelated.
- Same tactic repeated after it already failed.
- Promised confrontation occurs off-page or is summarized afterward.
- Scene has no meaningful changed state.
- Dialogue exchanges information but performs no action.
- Consequence is skipped, making next decision emotionally implausible.
- Antagonist/institution obstructs only because plot needs delay.
- Resolution is replaced by sequel advertising.

# 8. ROUTING

Use Truby when: whole architecture, character/opponent web, revelations, learning, moral change.
Use McKee Story when: inciting promise, escalation, turning point, Crisis/Climax, antagonism.
Use McKee Dialogue when: conversation objectives, subtext, voice, repetitive/on-the-nose dialogue.
Use Rosenfeld when: prose scene feels thin, static, ungrounded or monotonous.
Use Bell when: reader propulsion, suspense, middle sag, local dramatic questions.
Use Swain when: beat-to-beat cause/effect or scene→aftermath→decision chain is broken.
Use Bickham when: revising scene goals/results, skipped confrontations, circular scenes, subplot suspense.
Use Edgerton when: Chapter 1 begins too early, setup/backstory overwhelms action, external and deeper problems are disconnected.
