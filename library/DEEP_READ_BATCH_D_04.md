## 31. Jessica Townsend — *Nevermoor: The Trials of Morrigan Crow*

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full EPUB available; 26-chapter structure inspected.

### What the source is doing
The opening makes the young protagonist socially toxic before it makes her special: she is blamed for unrelated misfortunes and treated as a burden. The arrival of a desirable alternative place changes the dramatic question from mere survival to belonging. The middle does not consist only of tests: hotel life, holidays, friendship, embarrassment, status, patron relationships, and institutional rules continually redefine what belonging would cost. The ending resolves the immediate admission/belonging conflict before opening the larger series danger.

### Transferable mechanisms
- **Stigma → desired belonging:** begin with an identity imposed by society, then create a place the protagonist actively wants to earn.
- **Belonging has procedures:** membership is not granted because the hero is narratively important; it is tested through action, relationships, reputation, and institutional judgment.
- **Wonder through ordinary life:** food, rooms, celebrations, chores, etiquette, friendship and embarrassment make the extraordinary place desirable.
- **Hidden trait complicates belonging:** the thing that may make the hero valuable may also make admission dangerous.
- **Patron is not belonging:** one adult can open a door, but peers/institution/community must still be negotiated.
- **Local closure first:** the immediate status question can resolve while a larger threat remains.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
ORBITAL YOUTH: housing, apprenticeship, crew membership, civic licence, expedition slot, school/work cohort, station citizenship and peer status can carry the same *function* without importing a magical academy surface.

### FAILURE MODES / DO NOT COPY
Do not copy the cursed-child premise, magical hotel, patron rescue pattern, trials as spectacle, Wundrous Society, signature powers, terminology, or scene sequence. The IVDIVO version must arise from orbital society and its actual institutions.

## 32. Philip Pullman — *Northern Lights / The Golden Compass*

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full Book 1 FB2 archive available; three-part / 23-chapter structure inspected.

### What the source is doing
The child enters the adult conflict by hiding, listening, misreading and acting, rather than by receiving a lore lecture. A metaphysical fact of the world is present as ordinary embodied life before its ontology is fully explained. The protagonist's concrete personal objective—finding/rescuing someone she cares about—pulls her through widening institutional and metaphysical conflict. Later, violation of the embodied rule turns metaphysics into horror and moral stakes. Betrayal converts cosmological revelation into personal cost.

### Transferable mechanisms
- **Phenomenon before ontology:** show an impossible/advanced reality functioning in daily life before naming the full system behind it.
- **Embodied rule before doctrine:** a metaphysical or technological law becomes memorable when breaking it hurts a person the reader cares about.
- **Personal want pulls the world open:** rescue, loyalty, family or friendship should lead into cosmology—not the other way around.
- **Child agency through information asymmetry:** the young protagonist sees/hears something adults do not intend them to know and acts on an incomplete model.
- **Travel as model revision:** each new place/faction should alter the hero's explanation of the same underlying phenomenon.
- **Trusted authority can become price:** revelation matters when it forces a relationship choice, not merely a new fact.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
Observable-first reveal of consciousness embodiment, synthetic bodies, nonhuman neighbours, Old Earth anomalies and Confederation phenomena. Especially useful for keeping metaphysics inside action.

### FAILURE MODES / DO NOT COPY
Do not import dæmons, Dust, alethiometers, Magisterium analogues, polar expedition beats, child-abduction machinery, or the novel's sequence of revelations. Those are signature surfaces.

## 33. Orson Scott Card — *Ender's Game*

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full novel archive available; 15-chapter structure inspected.

### What the source is doing
The institution does not merely train the protagonist; it engineers his social environment, difficulty curve, isolation, information and moral choices. Competence creates dependence: the better the child performs, the more the system is willing to use him. The central manipulation converts apparent training/game success into a moral catastrophe and permanently changes the protagonist's self-understanding.

### Transferable mechanisms
- **Institution as active antagonist:** change rules, access, peers and time pressure—not just issue orders.
- **Competence creates new exploitation risk:** success can reduce a young person's freedom instead of increasing it.
- **Pressure reveals contradiction:** tactical brilliance can coexist with fear of becoming cruel.
- **Withheld context can revalue prior action:** a later truth should change the moral meaning of choices already made.
- **Victory must have aftermath:** institutional success can still be a personal failure or wound.
- **Empathy can be weaponized:** understanding an opponent does not automatically make a system ethical.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
Institutional ethics, training/selection systems, manipulation by security organisations, and the price of youth competence.

### RED-TEAM WARNING FOR ORBITAL YOUTH
This source is as useful as a warning as it is as a model. Do **not** turn all young characters into military prodigies, strategic adults or policy committees. Preserve friends, attraction, family, money, embarrassment, boredom, jobs and ordinary bad decisions.

### FAILURE MODES / DO NOT COPY
Do not copy battle-school structure, zero-gravity games, child commander premise, alien-war revelation, sibling geopolitical plot, or specific training escalations.

## 34. Adrian Tchaikovsky — *Children of Time*

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full FB2 available; eight large movements and nested chapter structure inspected.

### What the source is doing
The novel alternates human survival with the independent development of a nonhuman civilisation. Technology, biology, communication, religion, politics, war and scientific method emerge as interacting systems. First contact repeatedly fails because each side categorises the other through an inadequate model. The decisive movement is not simply military victory but a change in what counts as intelligence, civilisation and possible kinship.

### Transferable mechanisms
- **Nonhuman civilisation gets independent causality:** do not define another species only by its relation to humans.
- **Technology generates institutions:** a new capability changes labour, family, hierarchy, religion, warfare, law or status.
- **Category error is conflict:** the threat may persist because one side is using the wrong ontology for the other.
- **Signal → interpretation → test → revised model:** first contact should have epistemic work, not instant translation.
- **Resource need can corrupt perception:** existential scarcity encourages extermination logic even in otherwise rational actors.
- **Shared category must be earned:** cooperation becomes credible after evidence forces both sides to update models.
- **Scale still needs human anchors:** long civilisational movement must periodically return to individual desire, fear, memory and relationship.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
Confederation/nonhuman contact, alien sociology, AI/synthetic civilisation, long technological consequences, and conflicts where neither side initially has an adequate category for the other.

### FAILURE MODES / DO NOT COPY
Do not copy uplifted spiders, nanovirus mechanisms, ark architecture, Kern, the alternating historical sequence, or biological solutions. Large-scale sociology must not become an excuse for encyclopaedic exposition.

## 35. Matt Dinniman — *Dungeon Crawler Carl* #1

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full Book 1 archive available; prologue, two-part chapter structure and epilogue inspected.

### What the source is doing
A catastrophic premise is followed immediately by survival action; rules arrive after the characters have something concrete to lose. The system converts survival into an economy of metrics, rewards, spectacle and audience attention. Short danger loops provide retention, but the Carl/Donut relationship, humour and anger prevent progression mechanics from becoming the whole story. A local level/problem closes before the next escalation is opened.

### Transferable mechanisms
- **Action before rulebook:** let characters hit the system before explaining the system.
- **Micro-retention loop:** danger → discover constraint → attempt → result → payoff → new constraint.
- **Systemic exploitation behind rewards:** incentives can be antagonistic even when they help the protagonist survive.
- **Audience/status pressure:** public metrics change behaviour and relationships.
- **Humour protects humanity under horror:** comedy can come from character/relationship without cancelling stakes.
- **Local closure → larger hook:** finish the immediate dramatic unit before advertising the next one.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
Serial/audio retention, escalating station emergencies, competitive systems, reputation economies and bureaucratic/game-like structures that exploit participants.

### FAILURE MODES / DO NOT COPY
Do not import dungeon floors, RPG UI, achievements, loot, televised alien game, character voice or tonal surface. Gamification is a pressure mechanism, not IVDIVO's world premise.

## 36. Mark J. P. Wolf — *Building Imaginary Worlds*

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full DOCX text available; conceptual structure inspected.

### Primary thesis/use
Worlds can extend beyond any single narrative, but they become convincing through interacting invention, completeness and consistency. As worlds become larger and more inventive, consistency becomes harder rather than easier. World depth can be implied through partial evidence, infrastructures, history, objects, maps, institutions and narrative threads.

### Transferable mechanisms
- **World ≠ plot:** the world may be larger than the current book; the current book still owes the reader a complete story.
- **Invention / completeness / consistency trade-off:** every added rule creates downstream obligations.
- **Implied depth:** wear, bureaucracy, habits, slang, architecture, prices, transport and background consequences can suggest history without a lecture.
- **Infrastructure links:** economy, ecology, law, transport, language and institutions should affect one another.
- **Canon grows risk:** scale requires explicit continuity governance.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
World Bible, Continuity Editor, saga-scale infrastructure, transmedia planning **after** current-book causality is secure.

### FAILURE MODE
Worldbuilding can become autonomous collection instead of story. Wolf is a world-system source, never permission for lore dumps.

## 37. Ignasi Ribó — *Prose Fiction: An Introduction to the Semiotics of Narrative*

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full open-access PDF text available; chapters on plot, setting, characterisation, narration, language and theme inspected.

### Primary use
A neutral diagnostic vocabulary for separating narrative problems that are often blurred together. Especially useful is the distinction between narrator and focalisation: who speaks is not the same question as whose perspective filters the information.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Diagnose **plot / setting / characterisation / narration / language / theme** separately before rewriting.
- Separate **who speaks?** from **who perceives/filters?**
- Track inward, outward, fixed, variable or multiple focalisation consciously.
- Distinguish information access from narrative voice.
- Use story/discourse distinction to test whether a problem is in events or in their presentation.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
Development edit, POV control, ensemble scenes, mystery information flow and line-to-architecture diagnosis.

### FAILURE MODE
This is a diagnostic skeleton, not a story generator. Classification must never replace desire, opposition, choice and price.

## 38. Lisa Zunshine — *Why We Read Fiction: Theory of Mind and the Novel*

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full PDF text available; Attributing Minds / Tracking Minds / Concealing Minds structure inspected.

### Primary use
Fiction continually recruits the reader to infer mental states and to track the source of beliefs, claims, memories and lies. Mystery intensifies this by making the reader simultaneously want to solve the problem and want the uncertainty to continue.

### Transferable mechanisms
- **Mind-state graph:** for important scenes track who knows, believes, suspects, wants and falsely assumes what.
- **Source tracking:** a fact and a character's belief about that fact are different story objects.
- **Layered minds create tension:** A thinks B believes C intends X.
- **Mystery is controlled mistrust:** clues matter partly because readers infer motives around them.
- **Relationship conflict can be epistemic:** attraction, jealousy and betrayal often depend on wrong mind-reading, not only external events.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
Mystery Architect, Dialogue Editor, Relationship/Romance, ensemble politics, Smith investigations and youth social scenes.

### FAILURE MODE
Treat the cognitive account as a useful interpretive model, not settled universal law. Do not reduce character emotion to a neuroscience diagram.

## 39. *The Cambridge Companion to Science Fiction*

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full PDF text available; history, critical approaches, life sciences, hard SF, space opera, alternate history, utopia, politics, gender, race and religion sections inspected.

### Primary use
A genre-context map. It prevents the Studio from treating SF as a synonym for spacecraft or futuristic hardware. SF assumptions about biology, politics, class, gender, religion and institutions are story choices that carry social consequences.

### Transferable mechanisms
- **Technology implies social assumptions:** biological or technical change should alter status, work, law, family or politics.
- **Subgenre is a pressure system:** hard SF, space opera, utopia/dystopia and alternate history foreground different reader expectations.
- **Space opera after conquest:** once travel is normal, stories can come from governance, livelihood, diplomacy, work and ordinary civilisation.
- **Biology is never neutral:** a fictional life-science assumption brings ethical and social consequences.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
Genre sanity check; Orbital Youth civilisation; Confederation politics; synthetic-life thematic breadth.

### FAILURE MODE
The volume is a 2003 historical/critical reference, **not a current science manual**. Technical canon must use contemporary science/engineering sources when accuracy matters.

## 40. David Lodge — *The Art of Fiction*

**Status:** DEEP_READ_COMPLETE_v0.1  
**Source integrity:** full PDF text available; 50 technique-centred essays inspected.

### Primary use
A prose/craft counterweight to architecture manuals. The book shows how beginnings, voice, POV, suspense, implication, unreliable narration, place, motivation, time, the uncanny, ideas and endings operate through actual prose choices.

### Transferable mechanisms
- **Crafted youth voice ≠ transcript:** colloquial authenticity is designed, compressed and selected; literal speech transcription is usually unreadable.
- **Implication uses reader labour:** gaps and silences can make the reader infer instead of receiving explanation.
- **Technique is local:** different narrative effects require different tools; no single structural system governs every page.
- **Voice carries worldview:** vocabulary, syntax and selection reveal character/social position without explanatory commentary.
- **Ending is form, not stoppage:** the final movement should complete the work's governing expectation/change.

### BEST FOR IVDIVO
Line Editor, youth voice, implication/subtext, POV, prose variation and anti-generated-text pass.

### FAILURE MODE
Examples demonstrate effects; they are not scene templates to imitate.

# BATCH D SYNTHESIS — 10 CALLABLE ENGINES

## D1. BELONGING ENGINE
`imposed stigma/status → glimpse of desired social place → active attempt to belong → ordinary relationships/rituals → institutional resistance → hidden contradiction → costly claim of identity → changed status`

Primary source function: Nevermoor.  
IVDIVO transformation: orbital housing/crew/job/citizenship/apprenticeship rather than magical-school surface.

## D2. EMBODIED PHENOMENON ENGINE
`ordinary observable phenomenon → intimate relationship to it → rule violation → embodied consequence → institutional exploitation → partial explanation → harder personal choice`

Primary source function: Pullman.  
IVDIVO transformation: synthetic bodies, consciousness carriers, multidimensional phenomena, nonhuman life.

## D3. INSTITUTIONAL PRESSURE ENGINE
`selection → engineered social environment → rising constraints → competence → deeper dependence → withheld truth → success → moral revaluation → aftermath`

Primary source function: Ender.  
Use with Red Team warning against adultifying all young characters.

## D4. CIVILISATION MODEL-UPDATE ENGINE
`unfamiliar behaviour → category error → independent development → signal → failed interpretation → test → resource conflict → evidence → model revision → new relation`

Primary source function: Children of Time.

## D5. OPPRESSIVE RETENTION ENGINE
`immediate danger → discover rule → act → result → payoff → new constraint`, nested inside a larger conflict that still receives local closure.

Primary source function: Dungeon Crawler Carl.

## D6. WORLD DEPTH ENGINE
`visible consequence/detail → implied infrastructure/history → only story-relevant explanation → continuity check`

Primary source function: Wolf.

## D7. NARRATIVE DIAGNOSIS ENGINE
Before rewriting, identify whether the break is primarily: `plot / setting / characterisation / narration-focalisation / language / theme`.

Primary source function: Ribó.

## D8. MIND-TRACKING ENGINE
For every important scene: `who knows? who believes? who suspects? who wants? who is wrong about whom? what changes?`

Primary source function: Zunshine.

## D9. SF CONSEQUENCE ENGINE
`speculative change → physical affordance/constraint → work/economy → law/status → relationship/family → politics/ethics → story pressure`

Primary source function: Cambridge Companion + task-specific current science sources.

## D10. PROSE IMPLICATION ENGINE
`concrete object/action/voice choice → reader inference → emotional/social meaning`; cut the explanatory moral if the implication already lands.

Primary source function: Lodge.

# CORE-40 DEEP-READ GATE

A Core source may be invoked in production only under these rules:

1. State the current story problem first.
2. Call no more than 2–4 complementary Core sources for one problem unless Red Team identifies a genuine gap.
3. For a fiction source, remove all proper nouns, signature inventions and scene-specific surface. If the extracted mechanism stops making sense, it is not abstract enough.
4. Current-book causality and character truth outrank source pattern.
5. A diagnostic framework never becomes a compulsory beat sheet.
6. Historical/critical SF material never authorises current technical canon by itself.
7. When two independent sources converge on the same diagnosis and the scene/story solution is clear, **STOP RESEARCH AND WRITE**.
8. Worldbuilding is called only when a story decision requires it.
9. Series hooks open only after the current book/episode conflict closes.
10. Every applied mechanism must be transformed through IVDIVO's own characters, institutions, technology, society and canon.
