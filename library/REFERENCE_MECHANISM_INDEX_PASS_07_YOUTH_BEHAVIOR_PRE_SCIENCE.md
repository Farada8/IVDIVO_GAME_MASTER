# M117 — INTERNAL SOPHISTICATION / SPOKEN CLUMSINESS GAP

REFERENCE PATTERN:
Young characters may internally analyze a situation in detail yet speak in fragments, jokes, evasions, overstatements, silence or badly timed comments.

STORY FUNCTION:
Creates age-specific voice, embarrassment and subtext.

IVDIVO RULE:
Do not let teenagers consistently say their best internal formulation aloud.

RED FLAG:
Teen dialogue sounds like a writers' room summarizing the theme.

---

# M118 — THE PEER AUDIENCE CHANGES THE SCENE

REFERENCE PATTERN:
The same choice feels different when friends, rivals, teammates, romantic interests or a whole school can see it.

STORY FUNCTION:
Adds social stakes without physical danger.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
In orbital civilization, visibility can include cohort feeds, work ratings, habitat rumor networks, team rosters and mixed-species peer groups.

TEST:
Would the character make the same choice if nobody their age would ever know?

---

# M119 — PUBLIC EMBARRASSMENT CAN OUTWEIGH PRIVATE LOGIC

REFERENCE PATTERN:
Characters sometimes protect image, dignity or belonging even when a quieter option would be strategically smarter.

STORY FUNCTION:
Produces believable bad choices.

IVDIVO RULE:
A young character may knowingly choose the socially safer immediate move over the institutionally safer long-term move — but the script must show why that social cost feels real to them.

---

# M120 — IDENTITY IS PERFORMED BEFORE IT IS STABLE

REFERENCE PATTERN:
Clothes, friends, music, humor, dating, sport, academic identity, rebellion and chosen communities can function as experiments in self-definition.

STORY FUNCTION:
Lets identity develop through behavior rather than biography.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Orbital identity experiments may include habitat affiliation, species-mixed subculture, body presentation, interface use, work track, Earth nostalgia/rejection, synthetic embodiment stance and transport freedom.

FAILURE MODE:
Every young character already knows exactly who they are and what they believe.

---

# M121 — FAMILY DEPENDENCE AND AUTONOMY COEXIST

REFERENCE PATTERN:
A young person can reject parental control while still depending on family resources, emotional protection, transport, housing, money or social legitimacy.

STORY FUNCTION:
Creates contradiction rather than simple rebellion.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Orbital youth autonomy should be materially defined: who pays habitat fees, who controls travel authorization, medical consent, education track, body access, work contracts or emergency permissions?

---

# M122 — ATTRACTION DISTORTS ATTENTION

REFERENCE PATTERN:
A crush can change what a character notices, misreads, remembers, risks or ignores.

STORY FUNCTION:
Makes romance affect cognition and plot.

IVDIVO RULE:
Attraction is not a separate subplot if it changes tactical attention, loyalty, secrecy or status behavior.

---

# M123 — STATUS INFORMATION TRAVELS FASTER THAN TRUTH

REFERENCE PATTERN:
Rumor, visible association, reputation and labels can alter social treatment before anyone verifies events.

STORY FUNCTION:
Social consequences can precede factual understanding.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
A false or partial interpretation on a habitat feed can change roommate access, team selection, job trust, parent reaction or relationship status before the underlying anomaly is understood.

---

# M124 — BAD DECISION MUST HAVE A GOOD SUBJECTIVE REASON

REFERENCE PATTERN:
Young characters make mistakes from fear, pride, attraction, loyalty, boredom, status pressure, resentment, curiosity or overconfidence.

STORY FUNCTION:
Generates plot from character.

IVDIVO RULE:
Never write “teenagers are irrational” as explanation. Write the local reward the bad decision promises.

TEST:
What does the character think they gain in the next ten minutes, hour or day?

---

# M125 — ADULTS MISREAD YOUTH STRATEGIES

REFERENCE PATTERN:
Adults can interpret withdrawal as laziness, sarcasm as disrespect, secrecy as guilt, romance as triviality or risk-taking as stupidity while missing the actual social/emotional problem.

STORY FUNCTION:
Creates intergenerational conflict without requiring villain adults.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Confederation professionals, parents and instructors may understand system risk better than youth while understanding the youth's social world worse.

---

# M126 — YOUTH MAY KNOW THE RULE AND STILL TEST THE BOUNDARY

REFERENCE PATTERN:
Knowing a prohibition does not guarantee compliance when curiosity, status, identity or autonomy is at stake.

STORY FUNCTION:
Allows rules to become scenes rather than lore.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Do not explain a dangerous orbital rule in a lecture if a character can test, bend, exploit or violate it for a personally credible reason.

---

# M127 — HUMOR IS OFTEN A SOCIAL TOOL

REFERENCE PATTERN:
Jokes can protect status, test attraction, conceal fear, attack hierarchy, create belonging or prevent sincerity.

STORY FUNCTION:
Dialogue performs action.

IVDIVO RULE:
Give different young characters different humor functions. Not everyone should produce the same clever banter.

---

# M128 — FRIEND GROUPS HAVE UNEQUAL INFORMATION

REFERENCE PATTERN:
One friend knows the secret, another knows the social consequences, another misreads the romance, another sees family trouble.

STORY FUNCTION:
Prevents committee dialogue and creates asymmetry.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
A youth group should not share a single mind. Track who knows what, who suspects what, who cares about what and who is hiding what.

---

# M129 — SOCIAL EXCLUSION IS AN ACTIVE FORCE

REFERENCE PATTERN:
Being left out of a table, event, team, relationship network or information loop changes behavior.

STORY FUNCTION:
Exclusion creates goals.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Orbital exclusion can involve restricted recreation zones, expedition slots, workshop access, invite-only feeds, species-specific circles, expensive transport, body clubs or housing groups.

---

# M130 — COMPETENCE AND MATURITY ARE NOT THE SAME AXIS

REFERENCE PATTERN:
A young person can be academically, technically or tactically excellent while remaining insecure, jealous, avoidant, impulsive or dependent in relationships.

STORY FUNCTION:
Prevents gifted-youth characters from sounding 45 years old.

IVDIVO RULE:
Technical brilliance does not grant emotional completion.

---

# M131 — FIRST REAL RESPONSIBILITY CHANGES IDENTITY

REFERENCE PATTERN:
A job, team role, caregiving burden, public failure or serious secret can force a young person to discover whether they can be relied upon.

STORY FUNCTION:
Coming-of-age emerges from consequence.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
First maintenance shift, emergency duty, paid contract, body-license responsibility, younger sibling care or nonhuman exchange assignment can function as an adulthood threshold.

---

# M132 — YOUTH CONSEQUENCE MUST PROPAGATE INTO ORDINARY LIFE

REFERENCE PATTERN:
A dramatic event matters because the character must still go back to school, home, practice, work, friends and family afterward.

STORY FUNCTION:
Prevents action from floating outside life.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
After anomaly/adventure scenes, show schedule disruption, missed work, money loss, rumors, sleep, damaged equipment, family questioning, attraction shift or academic consequence.

# CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

## S22 — YOUTH DECISION LOOP

`DESIRE/STATUS NEED → PEER/FAMILY PRESSURE → FAST SUBJECTIVE REWARD → BAD OR PARTIAL STRATEGY → ACTION → PUBLIC/PRIVATE CONSEQUENCE → REVISED SELF-MODEL`

A mistake is strongest when the reader can understand why it was attractive before seeing why it was wrong.

---

## S23 — YOUTH VOICE LOOP

`INTERNAL THOUGHT → SOCIAL FILTER → SPOKEN TACTIC → OTHER PERSON MISREADS/RESPONDS → EMBARRASSMENT OR STATUS SHIFT → NEW TACTIC`

Use to prevent on-the-nose dialogue.

---

## S24 — AUTONOMY LOOP

`DEPENDENCE → REQUEST FOR FREEDOM → ADULT LIMIT → SECRET WORKAROUND / NEGOTIATION → CONSEQUENCE → NEW TRUST LEVEL → REAL INCREASE OR DECREASE IN AUTONOMY`

Autonomy must change materially, not only emotionally.

---

## S25 — ORBITAL COMING-OF-AGE LOOP

`ORDINARY ORBITAL ROLE → FIRST NONTRIVIAL RESPONSIBILITY → PERSONAL WANT COLLIDES WITH DUTY → WRONG YOUTHFUL STRATEGY → SOMEONE ELSE PAYS PART OF THE PRICE → CHOICE UNDER PRESSURE → NEW DEGREE OF TRUST / WORK / MOBILITY / STATUS`

This is more useful than importing an Earth high-school milestone unchanged.

# YOUTH CHARACTER GATE v0.1

For every major character roughly 13–25, answer:

1. Exact age/developmental situation?
2. What do they want this month?
3. What do they want today?
4. What social group matters most?
5. What embarrasses them?
6. What adult dependency do they resent but still need?
7. What are they unusually competent at?
8. Where are they still immature?
9. What do they hide from friends?
10. What do they hide from family?
11. What attraction/jealousy/status pressure currently distorts attention?
12. What bad decision is attractive to them and why?
13. What ordinary obligation continues during the plot?
14. Who pays when they make a mistake?
15. What concrete independence/status changes by the resolution?

FAIL if the character could be changed to age 40 with almost no story changes.

# RED TEAM — PASS 07

FATAL:
- teenage cast functions as adult policy committee;
- age has no effect on choices, dependence or social stakes;
- youth mistakes occur only because plot requires stupidity.

MAJOR:
- every teen is witty in the same way;
- gifted character is emotionally omniscient;
- parents exist only as obstacles;
- romance is the only meaningful youth relationship;
- school/work/money/housing disappear during the adventure;
- social consequences reset after each chapter.

MEDIUM:
- no boredom;
- no embarrassment;
- no bad timing;
- no status anxiety;
- no ordinary obligations;
- no difference between 14, 17, 20 and 24-year-old behavior.

Story first. Character before ability. Youth must genuinely live inside orbital civilization.
