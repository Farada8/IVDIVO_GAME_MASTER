## B11 — JOHN TRUBY — THE ANATOMY OF GENRES

**PRIMARY ROLE:** Genre Architect / Saga Architect
**BEST FOR:** genre philosophy, mixing genres, identifying what kind of truth/action a form foregrounds.

### Core findings
Truby treats genres not as lists of tropes but as different models of how human beings act, know, organize society and pursue a good life. The book explicitly differentiates families such as Horror/Fantasy/SF, Crime/Detective/Thriller, Action and others by their underlying story view.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Genre = recurring dramatic logic, not decoration.
- Each genre foregrounds a different relationship between mind and action.
- SF can function as social philosophy through invented society/technology.
- Detective privileges investigation and truth-finding; Crime privileges morality/justice and the cost of means.
- Genre mixing works when one form supplies what another lacks, not when tropes are simply stacked.
- Final reveals should be planned as part of an interconnected campaign of information, not attached late.
- Every genre still tracks the price of the hero’s chosen means.

### IVDIVO use
ORBITAL YOUTH can change genre book-to-book while retaining youth/developmental continuity. SMITH can combine procedural/institutional mystery with supernatural/SF anomaly without becoming generic urban fantasy.

### Risk
Truby can over-philosophize genres. Use genre worldview as diagnostic, never as an imposed thesis.

## B12 — ROBERT McKEE — ACTION

**PRIMARY ROLE:** Action Director / Tactical Story Engine
**BEST FOR:** action cores, power relations, tactics, set pieces, pace/progression, action subgenres.

### Core findings
The book separates action form from action cliché. Its architecture moves through core value/cast/event/emotion, then power and the action triad, then inciting incident, spine, MacGuffin, tactics, set pieces, crisis/climax, pace/progression, depth/breadth, subgenres and finally form versus formula.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Action is structured around contested power and consequential doing.
- Tactics matter more than generic motion: characters choose methods, meet counter-methods and adapt.
- Set pieces earn scale by changing the strategic situation.
- Pace is progression, not simply speed.
- Physical stakes need emotional/relational context to have depth.
- Action cast roles should create different forms of pressure rather than duplicate competence.
- Subgenre changes the meaning of action: duel, thriller, epic, adventure produce different expectations.

### IVDIVO use
SMITH action must express jurisdiction, knowledge and competing objectives. ORBITAL emergencies should reveal habitat rules, dependence and relationships while changing what becomes possible next.

### Risk
Spectacle can colonize story. Never let action replace climax choice.

## B13 — JAMES SCOTT BELL — PLOT & STRUCTURE

**PRIMARY ROLE:** Plot Diagnostic / Development Editor
**BEST FOR:** objective, confrontation, structural doorways, middle, ending.

### Core findings
Bell’s LOCK system is explicitly presented as Lead / Objective / Confrontation / Knockout. He reframes confusing terms such as plot point/inciting incident into practical disturbances and structural doorways that commit the character to the next phase.

### Transferable mechanisms
- A plot gains force when Lead + Objective + Confrontation + ending payoff are mutually dependent.
- Early disturbance destabilizes ordinary life before full commitment.
- Structural doorway = event/decision that makes retreat materially harder.
- Objective needs meaningful stakes; ‘death’ is broader than literal death but must feel consequential.
- Middle must intensify commitment and opposition rather than accumulate incidents.
- Ending should feel like the inevitable result of choices but not the most obvious mechanical outcome.
- Plot method is learnable; inspiration is not a substitute for revision and diagnosis.

### IVDIVO use
Use Bell after Truby architecture, not before it. His doorways are useful for checking irreversible commitment in long novels.

### Risk
LOCK/doorways can become visible scaffolding if treated as mandatory page markers.

## B14 — ORSON SCOTT CARD — CHARACTERS & VIEWPOINT

**PRIMARY ROLE:** Character Director / POV Editor
**BEST FOR:** motive, past, reputation, relationships, stereotype-breaking, viewpoint distance.

### Core findings
Card stresses that readers know characters most strongly through what they **do**, why they do it, and what they have done before. Physical inventory is often far less important than action, motive, past, reputation, relationships, habits, talents and tastes. He also explicitly notes that people present different personas in different relationships—particularly visible in adolescence.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Elaborated motive creates credibility; simple motive creates archetype/cliché.
- Multiple motives may conflict without making character incoherent.
- Past matters when it explains present options/expectations, not as biography dump.
- Relationship network reveals different versions of the same person.
- Stereotype may be the reader’s first assumption but should lose power as individual evidence accumulates.
- POV controls reader access, sympathy and interpretation.
- Characters invented merely to convey an idea/history/science need additional life if emotional investment is expected.

### IVDIVO use
Extremely useful for youth: public cohort persona / family persona / friend persona / romantic persona / work persona can all differ without becoming separate characters.

### Risk
Some terminology/social examples reflect the book’s era. Use mechanisms, not dated cultural assumptions.

## B15 — DAVID CORBETT — THE ART OF CHARACTER

**PRIMARY ROLE:** Character Lab / Relationship Director
**BEST FOR:** desire, frustration, wound, secrets, contradiction, physical/psychological/social embodiment.

### Core findings
Corbett builds character from a strong cluster: quality of desire, distortion caused by frustration, wounds, secrets and unavoidable contradiction; then expands into physical, psychological and sociological nature. His table of contents explicitly treats desire/fear/courage/love/hate/shame/guilt/failure/success, family/spouse/friends/class/work/education/home/tribe and protagonist change.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Desire should be specific enough to generate behavior.
- Frustrated desire changes personality and tactics.
- Wound creates vulnerability; it should not merely decorate backstory.
- Secret changes behavior before reveal.
- Contradictions can arise from multiple social roles, competing morals/goals, deceit, conscious/unconscious conflict.
- Character biography is strongest when built from scenes, not dossiers.
- Work/class/family/geography are dramatic identity forces, not metadata.

### IVDIVO use
Character passports must feed scenes. Especially useful for ORBITAL YOUTH ordinary life and SMITH’s professional/private contradiction.

### Risk
Trauma/wound frameworks can overpathologize characters. Ordinary desire and pleasure matter too.

## B16 — GWEN HAYES — ROMANCING THE BEAT

**PRIMARY ROLE:** Relationship/Romance Engine
**BEST FOR:** relationship causality, attraction/withdrawal, emotional progression, integrating romance with external plot.

### Core findings
Hayes explicitly says romance beats must be braided with external plot in longer/complex works, especially romantic suspense. Her sequence organizes relationship movement through attraction, resistance, increasing vulnerability, false highs, doubts/retreat and an eventual fight for the relationship.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Two characters need independent goals/flaws before romance becomes causal.
- First encounter should establish tone and an initial relational pattern.
- Attraction is not yet trust or compatibility.
- Emotional progress needs reversals; uninterrupted closeness is dramatically flat.
- Doubt should be customized to each character’s wound/flaw, not manufactured misunderstanding.
- External events may pressure intimacy; relationship choices must also alter external action.
- Romance payoff requires active choice, not simply removal of obstacle.

### IVDIVO use
Relationship B-story cannot be detachable. In ORBITAL YOUTH, housing, work, transport, status, mixed-species norms, AI/synthetic bodies and family must affect intimacy.

### Risk
Beat labels can turn relationships into formula. Use as diagnostic chronology, never compulsory scene count.

## B17 — CHARLES BAXTER — THE ART OF SUBTEXT

**PRIMARY ROLE:** Dialogue Editor / Scene Director / Literary Depth Pass
**BEST FOR:** unstated emotion, staging, behavior, social signals, indirect meaning.

### Core findings
Baxter treats subtext as something generated by staging and social behavior, not a hidden sentence beneath dialogue. Gesture, face, spacing, objects, silence and the manner of speech can evoke what characters cannot or will not say.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Staging can carry the unsaid through physical arrangement and micro-behavior.
- A detail becomes subtextual when it exposes a tense social position.
- Contradictory signals (eyes/lips, smile/malice, confidence/timidity) create layered meaning.
- What a character cannot say may appear in how they perform ordinary behavior.
- Scene space can become claustrophobic/open depending on relationship pressure.
- Subtext is strongest when reader infers it; explanation destroys part of its force.

### IVDIVO use
Use after McKee Dialogue to prevent dialogue-as-chess from becoming too explicit. Particularly useful for romance, family, youth embarrassment and institutional meetings.

### Risk
Excessive subtlety can obscure actual objective and causal change.

## B18 — MARK DOTY — THE ART OF DESCRIPTION: WORLD INTO WORD

**PRIMARY ROLE:** Prose Style Lab / World Reveal
**BEST FOR:** attention, concrete detail, perception, metaphor, sensory selection.

### Core findings
Doty frames description as the translation of attention into language. His analyses repeatedly move between outward detail and inward association: seeing is selective, interpretive and rhythmic, not camera transcription.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Description reveals the perceiver as much as the object.
- Detail should be selected because attention is drawn to it now.
- Movement of attention can shift between external surface and internal association.
- Precision does not require exhaustive inventory.
- Metaphor can show a mind trying to name experience, not merely beautify prose.
- Sensory specificity earns abstract implication.
- Leaving some meaning open can be stronger than explaining what the description ‘means.’

### IVDIVO use
Core for WORLD THROUGH STORY. Orbital architecture, artificial bodies and nonhuman environments should be experienced through task, body and character-specific attention.

### Risk
Beautiful description can stop the desire line. Story function remains primary.

## B19 — ALICE LaPLANTE — THE MAKING OF A STORY

**PRIMARY ROLE:** Master Craft Integrator / Development Editor / Revision Surgeon
**BEST FOR:** discovery process, concrete detail, character, plot causality, revision, exercises.

### Core findings
This is a broad curriculum rather than a single formula. Its structure repeatedly combines craft explanation, exercises and reading-as-writer examples. Key sections include writing as discovery, concrete details, narration, POV, dialogue, plot/causality, character and revision.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Writing can discover material not fully known before drafting.
- Concrete details are basic building blocks; general meaning grows from particulars.
- ‘Surprising yet convincing’ is a useful quality test.
- Plot discussion explicitly foregrounds causality and character-based plotting.
- Render how events/behavior happen rather than prematurely explaining why.
- Revision should identify ‘hot spots’ and developmental stage, not polish everything equally.
- Exercises can diagnose a problem by rewriting through a different constraint.

### IVDIVO use
Excellent counterweight to architecture-first manuals: lets prose discovery generate evidence that may require architecture revision.

### Risk
As a broad course, it should not be called for narrow tactical problems when a specialist source exists.

## B20 — WRITING FICTION: A GUIDE TO NARRATIVE CRAFT (10th ed.)

**PRIMARY ROLE:** General Fiction Craft / Prose & Narrative Cross-Check
**BEST FOR:** showing/telling, characterization, setting, plot/structure, POV, comparison, revision/theme.

### Core findings
The book’s architecture covers process, showing/telling, two chapters of characterization, fictional setting, plot/structure, POV, comparison, and revision/theme. It is useful precisely because it is broad and classroom-oriented rather than one proprietary story system.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Showing and telling are choices of emphasis, not a moral binary.
- Characterization is distributed across behavior, language, detail and narrative access.
- Setting participates in meaning and pressure.
- Plot and structure connect selection/arrangement of events to causality.
- POV determines access and rhetorical effect.
- Comparison/metaphor shapes perception.
- Revision and theme develop together; theme need not be announced first.

### IVDIVO use
Use as neutral cross-check when Truby/McKee/Bell begin to over-determine architecture. Particularly useful during development edit and line/prose passes.

### Risk
Breadth means lower priority when a specialized diagnostic is available.
