# SOURCE SET F — ROBERT MCKEE / DIALOGUE

## M021 — EVERY LINE IS A VERBAL TACTIC

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Speech is an action a character takes to satisfy a need or desire; a line should do something to someone, even when the ‘someone’ is the speaker’s own inner self.

TRIGGER:
Dialogue sounds intelligent but inert.

SEQUENCE:
Need → intent → chosen verbal tactic → reaction → tactical adjustment.

READER EFFECT:
Conversation moves like action rather than explanation.

FAILURE MODE:
Characters exchange positions or information without trying to alter the situation.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Before writing an important conversation, assign each participant an objective and a current tactic: charm, delay, test, shame, provoke, recruit, conceal, calm, threaten, reframe, bargain, distract, confess, etc.

BEST FOR:
Dialogue Editor; relationship scenes; interrogations; family conflict; Old Earth vs Confederation meetings.

---

## M022 — SAID / UNSAID / UNSAYABLE

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Dialogue gains depth when spoken content sits above private thought and deeper motives the speaker may not fully understand.

TRIGGER:
Characters say exactly what they feel and mean.

SEQUENCE:
Conscious objective → spoken tactic → withheld thought → involuntary contradiction/behavior → reader inference.

READER EFFECT:
Subtext and psychological depth.

FAILURE MODE:
Writer explains the subtext immediately after the line.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
For major dialogue, track three layers: what the character says, what they knowingly hide, and what their behavior reveals despite them.

BEST FOR:
Romance, family, youth shame, interrogations, AI/personhood debates.

---

## M023 — VOICE BEGINS WITH CHARACTER-SPECIFIC VOCABULARY

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Distinct syntax, rhythm, tone and word choice make characters recognizable without dialogue tags.

TRIGGER:
Lines can be swapped between characters without damage.

SEQUENCE:
Life experience/status/work → attention habits → vocabulary → syntax/rhythm → recurring tactics → stress distortion.

READER EFFECT:
Characters feel socially and psychologically separate.

FAILURE MODE:
Giving everyone a superficial catchphrase or accent while their thinking remains identical.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Build a Voice DNA sheet: ordinary vocabulary, taboo words, humor mode, precision level, metaphors, argument style, silence style, stress language, lies, affection language.

BEST FOR:
Orbital Youth ensemble; mixed-species characters; Old Earth professionals; AI/synthetic persons.

---

## M024 — DIALOGUE EXCHANGE MUST CREATE ACTION/REACTION AND CHANGE

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Each exchange progresses the scene; verbal behavior is causally linked to the next beat.

TRIGGER:
A conversation contains five good lines but could be reordered freely.

SEQUENCE:
Line-action → reaction → changed tactic → counteraction → turning point.

READER EFFECT:
Conversation has momentum and consequence.

FAILURE MODE:
Equal-airtime roundtable where everyone offers a polished view in sequence.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Cut or rewrite any exchange whose order can be shuffled without changing the scene.

BEST FOR:
Dialogue Red Team; policy/jurisdiction scenes; group scenes.

# SOURCE SET G — JORDAN ROSENFELD / MAKE A SCENE

## M025 — SCENE = REAL-TIME MOMENTUM + PURPOSEFUL ACTION

SOURCE FUNCTION:
A scene simulates unfolding time through beat-by-beat behavior; momentum is what makes it a scene rather than summary.

TRIGGER:
Draft has paragraphs of thought/exposition with no active change.

SEQUENCE:
Launch → immediate intention → behavior → response → complication → changed condition.

READER EFFECT:
Presence and immersion.

FAILURE MODE:
Mistaking length or dialogue volume for scene-ness.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Ask: what physically/socially/relationally happens during these pages that was not true at entry?

BEST FOR:
Scene Engine; chapter construction; ordinary-life scenes.

---

## M026 — NEW INFORMATION SHOULD BE A CONSEQUENCE, NOT A DELIVERY

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Scenes advance through discoveries, discussions, events or epiphanies that usually grow from prior scenes and current action.

TRIGGER:
Exposition arrives because the author needs it now.

SEQUENCE:
Prior consequence → current attempt → encounter/test → information uncovered → behavior changes.

READER EFFECT:
World knowledge feels earned.

FAILURE MODE:
Briefing-room dialogue or conveniently volunteered lore.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
New civilization/technology/metaphysics information should be obtained by doing a job, making a mistake, breaking a rule, paying, repairing, investigating, dating, travelling or surviving.

BEST FOR:
World Reveal; science/technology; mystery.

---

## M027 — SCENE INTENTION + TENSION + SUBTEXT ARE A SINGLE SYSTEM

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Characters act purposefully; dramatic tension tests that intention; subtext deepens what the overt action means.

TRIGGER:
A scene is technically active but emotionally flat.

SEQUENCE:
Intention → obstacle → tactic → hidden meaning → pressure → decision/turn.

READER EFFECT:
External and emotional stories advance together.

FAILURE MODE:
Atmospheric scenes that deepen mood but leave relationships and plot unchanged.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
For every scene log WHO WANTS WHAT / WHY NOW / WHAT STOPS THEM / WHAT THEY WON’T SAY / WHAT CHANGES.

BEST FOR:
All departments; Story Gate.

---

## M028 — VARIED SCENE TYPES CREATE NARRATIVE MUSIC

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Suspense, dialogue, contemplative, climactic, epiphany and other scene modes combine like different notes; not every scene should use the same intensity pattern.

TRIGGER:
A manuscript feels monotonous despite constant plot activity.

SEQUENCE:
High-pressure scene → aftermath/relationship processing → new social complication → investigation → intimate scene → renewed crisis.

READER EFFECT:
Rhythm, contrast, emotional recovery.

FAILURE MODE:
Using contemplative scenes as consequence-free pauses.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Ordinary youth life is not filler: quieter scenes must alter attachment, status, decision, trust, money, family or future action.

BEST FOR:
Chapter architecture; Reader Advocate; pacing pass.

# SOURCE SET H — VERONICA MARS / PILOT

## M029 — HERO VOICE CAN DOUBLE AS SOCIAL X-RAY

SOURCE FUNCTION:
A protagonist’s subjective voice simultaneously reveals character defense and maps the class/status structure of the setting.

TRIGGER:
Need to explain a school, habitat or city hierarchy.

SEQUENCE:
Concrete social event → protagonist notices winners/losers/rules → biased interpretation → personal wound leaks through → action.

READER EFFECT:
Exposition becomes characterization.

FAILURE MODE:
Voice-over simply tells facts neutrally.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
An orbital teen should notice the world according to what hurts or matters to them: access tiers, body models, transit zones, job placements, oxygen/energy privileges, species/status cliques, family reputation.

BEST FOR:
Orbital Youth; first-person/close POV; world reveal.

---

## M030 — YOUTH MYSTERY WORKS WHEN INVESTIGATION AND SOCIAL SURVIVAL ARE THE SAME PROBLEM

SOURCE FUNCTION:
The protagonist investigates inside a hierarchy where clues, allies and enemies are also classmates, families, institutions and status groups.

TRIGGER:
YA mystery feels like an adult detective plot with younger actors.

SEQUENCE:
Social injury → investigative action → status retaliation → new clue → relationship complication → deeper personal stake.

READER EFFECT:
Mystery cannot be detached from adolescence.

FAILURE MODE:
Case scenes and school/friend scenes run on separate tracks.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
In Orbital Youth, investigating a habitat problem should affect grades/jobs, friendships, family access, housing, reputation or romantic standing.

BEST FOR:
Youth Mystery Architect; Orbital Youth Director.

---

## M031 — COMPETENCE + VULNERABILITY IN THE SAME INTRODUCTION

SOURCE FUNCTION:
The young protagonist is introduced doing unusually competent adult-like work while still carrying unmistakably adolescent obligations and wounds.

TRIGGER:
Gifted teen risks feeling like a miniature 35-year-old professional.

SEQUENCE:
Competent task → mundane youth constraint → social danger → emotional blind spot → active choice.

READER EFFECT:
Capability is impressive without erasing age.

FAILURE MODE:
‘Teen genius’ who never worries about school, sleep, parents, status, attraction, money or embarrassment.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Orbital youths can perform real maintenance, navigation, AI or expedition work, but their competence must coexist with developmental and social dependency.

BEST FOR:
Character Director; Orbital Youth.

# SOURCE SET I — MY SO-CALLED LIFE / “LIFE OF BRIAN”

## M032 — INNER VOICE AND SPOKEN VOICE SHOULD DISAGREE

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Adolescent authenticity emerges from the gap between elaborate inner interpretation and clumsy, defensive or incomplete speech.

TRIGGER:
Teenagers sound too self-aware and verbally perfect.

SEQUENCE:
Private desire/fantasy → rehearsed internal interpretation → social encounter → degraded spoken version → embarrassment/misreading → new private rationalization.

READER EFFECT:
Humor, tenderness and credibility.

FAILURE MODE:
Characters articulate their attachment pattern and moral lesson in polished adult language.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Let young characters think more than they can say; let attraction, jealousy and fear distort timing, vocabulary and tactics.

BEST FOR:
Youth Dialogue Editor; romance/friendship.

---

## M033 — MISREADING IS A STORY ENGINE, NOT A BUG

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Young characters act on partial interpretations of glances, rumors, attraction and social signals; the resulting choices create consequences.

TRIGGER:
All characters understand each other too efficiently.

SEQUENCE:
Ambiguous signal → biased reading → action → unintended reaction → status/emotional consequence → revised belief.

READER EFFECT:
Social suspense and comic/painful realism.

FAILURE MODE:
Misunderstanding sustained by one conversation everyone inexplicably refuses to have.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Misread signals should arise from credible fear/status asymmetry and produce action before clarification becomes possible.

BEST FOR:
Friendship, jealousy, romance, youth ensemble.

---

## M034 — ORDINARY ADULT PROBLEMS SHOULD REFRACT THROUGH YOUTH LIFE

SOURCE FUNCTION:
Family employment, marriage strain, parental worldview and household habits change how young characters interpret their own choices.

TRIGGER:
Parents exist only as permission-givers or obstacles.

SEQUENCE:
Adult problem → youth observes/misunderstands → youth applies analogy to own problem → flawed/insightful choice → family feedback.

READER EFFECT:
Family feels like an ecosystem, not background.

FAILURE MODE:
Parent scenes provide thematic speeches rather than lived pressure.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
Orbital parents’ contracts, shifts, migration status, body choices, species relations, security work and housing debt should shape adolescent decisions indirectly and directly.

BEST FOR:
Family/Relationship Room; Orbital Youth.

# CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS PASS 02

## S04 — DIALOGUE ACTION LOOP

Combine M021 + M022 + M024:

`DESIRE → VERBAL TACTIC → REACTION → SUBTEXT LEAK → TACTIC CHANGE → STATUS/RELATIONSHIP SHIFT`

Red-team test:
If a conversation ends with everyone holding the same status, information and emotional position as at entry, it likely did not need to be a scene.

---

## S05 — YOUTH VOICE LOOP

Combine M023 + M029 + M032 + M033:

`PRIVATE INTERPRETATION → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC LANGUAGE → SOCIAL SIGNAL → MISREADING/DEFENSE → IMPERFECT SPOKEN ACTION → EMBARRASSMENT OR STATUS CHANGE → NEW PRIVATE STORY ABOUT WHAT HAPPENED`

This is an explicit antidote to teenagers speaking like adult policy committees.

---

## S06 — YOUTH MYSTERY / ORBITAL SOCIAL ENGINE

Combine M026 + M030 + M031 + M034:

`REAL YOUTH OBLIGATION → ANOMALY/QUESTION → INVESTIGATION THROUGH ORDINARY SYSTEM → CLUE HAS SOCIAL COST → FAMILY/WORK/SCHOOL PRESSURE DISTORTS CHOICE → COMPETENT ACTION + AGE-SPECIFIC MISTAKE → CONSEQUENCE CHANGES BOTH CASE AND SOCIAL LIFE`

The mystery line and youth-life line must causally touch.

# RED TEAM CHECKS ADDED BY PASS 02

1. Can dialogue lines be reordered? If yes, causal verbal action is weak.
2. Does every important speaker have a different tactic, vocabulary and stress behavior?
3. Does the scene produce new behavior, not merely new information?
4. Do young characters misread, hesitate, posture, overreact, conceal and embarrass themselves in age-credible ways?
5. Does competence erase youth dependency? If yes, redesign.
6. Does family/social status materially affect the genre plot? If no, youth life is decorative.
7. Does subtext remain inferable without an explanatory paragraph after dialogue?
