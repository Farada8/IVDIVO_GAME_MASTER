# IVDIVO REFERENCE STUDY PASS 14 — SCENE / PACING / OPENINGS

Date: 2026-08-17
Status: REFERENCE ONLY / WORKING TOOL
Canon effect: NONE

## PURPOSE
Convert the IVDIVO scene law — `WHO WANTS WHAT? WHY NOW? WHAT STOPS THEM? WHAT CHANGES?` — into a stricter executable scene/pacing/opening system grounded in multiple independent craft sources.

## SOURCES

### 1. Jack M. Bickham — *Scene & Structure*
Drive ID: `1_7jkgrYrGxbmVPXQ0hHG9nIBYZDL2If5`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT/05_SCENE_PACING_OPENINGS`
Text status: FULL EPUB TEXT EXTRACTED
Role: PRIMARY STRUCTURAL SCENE SOURCE

High-value mechanism:
A dramatic scene can be understood as a protagonist pursuing a specific immediate goal against conflict until the outcome forces a worse or more complicated situation. Bickham's scene/sequel distinction is useful when treated as a pressure rhythm rather than a rigid formula.

Operational takeaways:
- scene goal should be concrete enough to generate a scene question;
- conflict is active resistance to that goal, including refusal to answer directly;
- a scene ending should complicate the next action rather than simply stop;
- after high-pressure action, a short reaction/processing/decision unit can convert consequence into the next goal;
- stimulus/response continuity helps prose feel causally linked rather than assembled from disconnected beats.

IVDIVO caution:
Do not mechanically force every scene into identical `goal-conflict-disaster / emotion-thought-decision` blocks. Use the causal logic, not a visible template.

### 2. Sandra Scofield — *The Scene Book*
Drive ID: `1QDZQJeTw4J_6etWSOG___pp2P4i8eJJJ`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT/05_SCENE_PACING_OPENINGS`
Text status: FULL EPUB TEXT EXTRACTED
Role: PRIMARY SCENE / EMOTIONAL PULSE SOURCE

High-value mechanism:
A scene is an event rendered in detail, not description/information/rumination alone. A strong scene has an emotional pulse — a state of desire/need — and tension grows when action pressures that pulse.

Operational takeaways:
- scene = something happening now in enough detail that action/feeling can change;
- description, reflection and exposition may live inside a scene but cannot substitute for event;
- the scene should have a focal point where its elements converge and turn;
- action and emotional meaning should interact;
- summary is legitimate when detail would not generate a meaningful turn.

### 3. Jordan Rosenfeld — *Make a Scene: Revised and Expanded Edition*
Primary Drive ID: `1tZ5l0rDCAyfcBffJX8oDv9AbYUUUVsnk`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT/05_SCENE_PACING_OPENINGS`
Text status: FULL TEXT EXTRACTABLE
Role: PRIMARY SCENE CRAFT SOURCE

High-value mechanism:
Scene beginnings, middles and endings have different jobs: entry must place the reader into active pressure; the middle raises stakes/complication; the ending creates consequence and prepares the next causal unit.

Operational takeaways:
- characters need scene intentions so behavior is purposeful;
- scene tension can come from conflict, suspense, subtext and competing intentions;
- setting should interact with action rather than exist as neutral decoration;
- narrative summary is a compression tool, not automatically a flaw;
- scene endings should leave a changed state, not simply a mood.

### 4. Les Edgerton — *Hooked*
Drive ID: `1nNVCBOk9r5at5Gr9mqsRPkZ8gBYjJYcv`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT/05_SCENE_PACING_OPENINGS`
Text status: FULL TEXT EXTRACTABLE
Role: OPENING / READER-PROPULSION CRAFT SOURCE

High-value mechanism:
An opening needs a present-tense disturbance/problem, not a biography before the story. Edgerton distinguishes the immediate surface problem from the deeper story-worthy problem; the protagonist may not yet understand the deeper issue.

Operational takeaways:
- open near a disturbance that requires attention/choice;
- backstory must be rationed by present need;
- the opening scene should already contain opposition, even if muted/subtextual;
- the immediate problem can be smaller than the eventual core problem but must be causally connected;
- reader orientation does not require explaining the whole world first.

IVDIVO caution:
Do not turn every opening into artificial action spectacle. `Disturbance` may be social, professional, romantic, bureaucratic or domestic.

# PASS 14 — EXECUTABLE SCENE ENGINE

## CORE SCENE LOOP

IMMEDIATE WANT
-> ATTEMPT
-> RESISTANCE
-> TACTIC / ACTION CHANGE
-> ESCALATION
-> TURN
-> CONSEQUENCE
-> NEW REQUIRED ACTION OR DECISION

A scene that ends in the same practical state in which it began is suspect.

## CARD 1 — SCENE QUESTION
Before drafting, be able to phrase a concrete question:
`WILL [CHARACTER] GET / LEARN / PREVENT / HIDE / PERSUADE / ESCAPE / SECURE X IN THIS SCENE?`

Do not confuse this with the novel's thematic question.

## CARD 2 — EVENT, NOT TOPIC
Weak scene premise:
`They discuss orbital law.`

Strong scene premise:
`Maya needs a temporary housing authorization before her shift starts; the clerk refuses because Samir's unauthorized access flag is attached to the shared application.`

A topic does not create a scene. A pressured event does.

## CARD 3 — WHY NOW
Every scene needs a timing trigger:
- deadline;
- arrival/departure;
- new evidence;
- money due;
- shift beginning;
- family call;
- status event;
- injury worsening;
- access expiring;
- somebody else acting first.

Without `WHY NOW`, the scene often feels writer-scheduled.

## CARD 4 — RESISTANCE MUST BE SPECIFIC
Opposition should not simply say no.

Resistance can be:
- another character's conflicting want;
- procedure;
- cost;
- status risk;
- missing information;
- physical environment;
- time;
- protagonist's own wrong strategy;
- relationship obligation.

## CARD 5 — FOCAL TURN
A scene needs a point after which the character cannot continue exactly as planned.

TURN may be:
- new fact;
- refusal;
- arrival;
- exposure;
- mistake;
- status reversal;
- decision;
- unexpected success with hidden cost.

## CARD 6 — CONSEQUENCE BRIDGE
End scenes on a changed condition that creates the next causal unit.

SCENE A RESULT
-> character reacts/decides
-> SCENE B GOAL.

Do not connect chapters only by chronology (`later that day`).

## CARD 7 — SCENE VS SUMMARY TEST
Render as full scene when:
- choice changes;
- relationship changes;
- important evidence is interpreted;
- conflict turns;
- price is paid;
- reader must experience tension/embarrassment/wonder directly.

Prefer summary when:
- routine repeats without a new turn;
- travel/logistics matter only as elapsed time;
- information can be compressed without emotional/causal loss;
- the same conflict has already been dramatized sufficiently.

## CARD 8 — OPENING DISTURBANCE
The first chapter should contain:
ORDINARY LIFE + CURRENT PRESSURE + SMALL/MEDIUM DISTURBANCE + CHARACTER RESPONSE.

Do not require the full main conflict to appear immediately, but the opening pressure must connect causally to the book's engine.

## CARD 9 — BACKSTORY BY DEMAND
Backstory enters when the present scene creates a question whose answer matters now.

Present trigger -> small past fragment -> changed interpretation/action.

Avoid `before we continue, here is the history of...`.

## CARD 10 — EMOTIONAL PULSE
For each scene identify the dominant private pressure:
BELONG / PROVE / HIDE / ESCAPE / BE FORGIVEN / KEEP CONTROL / BE CHOSEN / NOT LOOK WEAK / PROTECT / WIN STATUS.

The pulse is not the same as the external goal, but it should affect how the character pursues it.

# IVDIVO SCENE GATE v0.2

A scene is GREEN only if the important answers are concrete:

1. WHO is the scene owner?
2. WHAT do they want before the scene ends?
3. WHY NOW?
4. WHAT do they do first?
5. WHO/WHAT specifically resists?
6. What private emotional pulse shapes the strategy?
7. Where must the protagonist change tactic?
8. What is the focal turn?
9. What is now different: information / status / relationship / physical state / access / money / decision?
10. What next action becomes necessary because of this result?
11. Why must this be a scene rather than summary?
12. What world detail becomes causal rather than decorative?
13. If the scene is removed, which later event becomes impossible or emotionally unearned?

If #9, #10 and #13 have weak answers, cut or rebuild the scene.

---

# OPENING GATE v0.1

Before a Book/Part/Chapter opening is GREEN:

1. Is a character already inside a life rather than waiting for the plot?
2. What current pressure exists on page one?
3. What disturbance changes the character's near-term plan?
4. What does the character do about it?
5. What does the reader want to know next?
6. Is backstory limited to what the present pressure activates?
7. Does the opening reveal world through action, work, housing, money, transport, relationship or rule?
8. Is the speculative element observable before ontology is explained?

# ORBITAL YOUTH APPLICATION

High-value scene engines:
- missed transport connection threatens first shift;
- housing rule creates roommate/family conflict;
- a sports/social event changes status;
- job error creates debt or access restriction;
- romantic choice collides with work schedule;
- AI interface exposes private information;
- maintenance routine reveals anomaly;
- family permission arrives too late or with conditions.

These are not filler. They are where orbital civilization becomes story.

---

# SMITH APPLICATION

Smith scenes should often turn on:
- jurisdiction refusal;
- evidence contradicting classification;
- old archive access condition;
- witness/nonhuman negotiation;
- procedure failing;
- Confederation model explaining one fact while missing another;
- Smith's old method working but creating a new ethical/legal cost.

Avoid static briefing-room chapters.

# RED TEAM ADDITIONS

FATAL / MAJOR:
- scene has no immediate want;
- no reason it happens now;
- opposition is generic;
- climax scene uses a capability not established through prior scenes;
- chapter architecture is chronological rather than causal.

MAJOR:
- worldbuilding scene has no turn;
- relationship scene repeats known conflict;
- opening spends pages on backstory before current pressure;
- scene ends with mood only and no changed condition;
- full scene dramatizes routine that summary could handle with no loss.

MEDIUM:
- scene begins too early with greetings/setup;
- scene continues after its turn only to explain what happened;
- repeated emotional pulse with no escalation;
- setting remains non-causal.

# RESULT

The IVDIVO scene engine is now:

ORDINARY LIFE / CURRENT PRESSURE -> IMMEDIATE WANT -> WHY NOW -> ATTEMPT -> SPECIFIC RESISTANCE -> TACTIC CHANGE -> FOCAL TURN -> CONSEQUENCE -> NEXT REQUIRED ACTION.

This preserves the primary law: STORY, not word count.
