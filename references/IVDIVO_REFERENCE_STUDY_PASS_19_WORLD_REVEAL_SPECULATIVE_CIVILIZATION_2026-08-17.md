# IVDIVO REFERENCE STUDY PASS 19 — WORLD REVEAL / SPECULATIVE CIVILIZATION

Date: 2026-08-17
Status: REFERENCE ONLY / WORKING PRODUCTION TOOL
Canon effect: NONE

## PURPOSE
Convert the dedicated worldbuilding/speculative craft shelf into a story-first production system for IVDIVO.

Primary problem addressed:
IVDIVO requires a large future civilization, but the novels must not become encyclopedias, technology catalogues, franchise lore dumps, or ordinary Earth life with futuristic wallpaper.

Reference law:
`REFERENCE -> ABSTRACT MECHANISM -> IVDIVO TRANSFORM -> ORIGINAL STORY`.

Story law remains superior:
`HERO -> WANT -> WHY NOW -> OPPOSITION -> ACTION -> RESISTANCE -> CHOICE -> PRICE -> CLIMAX -> CHANGE -> RESOLUTION`.

Worldbuilding is admitted only when it changes that chain.

# SOURCE SET / STUDY DEPTH

## P19-S01 — Orson Scott Card — How to Write Science Fiction and Fantasy
Drive ID: `1Fuu_QaIPrJ3DxUeJ_t_blFgO98yDxFv-`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / WORLDBUILDING_SPECULATIVE_CRAFT`
Status: `PRIMARY_STUDIED / MECHANISMS_EXTRACTED`

Substantial source text inspected, especially world creation, rule formation, societal consequence, story construction, and gradual world reveal.

High-value findings:
- a speculative idea should be dragged through `WHY / HOW / WITH WHAT RESULT`, not merely named;
- major innovations should produce multiple social responses and secondary consequences rather than one unanimous societal reaction;
- a pile of ideas is not yet a world; rules and causal relationships give shape;
- the reader must learn what can and cannot happen through stable world rules;
- speculative rules matter because they constrain what characters can do;
- world information should arrive through the viewpoint character's need and experience rather than narrator-first history summary;
- begin with the small world the protagonist already inhabits, then expand as the protagonist is forced into larger consequences;
- large explanation becomes tolerable only after the reader already cares about the people and has seen the disorder/consequence in action;
- language/cultural difference can be implied through concepts, use and context rather than floods of untranslated invented vocabulary;
- research into real institutions/cultures/material life strengthens invented settings.

IVDIVO transform:
`ORDINARY LIFE -> DISTURBANCE -> LOCAL RULE -> CONSEQUENCE -> LARGER SYSTEM REVEALED BY NEED`.

## P19-S02 — Jeff VanderMeer — Wonderbook: Chapter 6 Worldbuilding (+ relevant exposition/story sections)
Drive ID: `1sHCmQgphlBUD2uGm1wZ0DSRR4JmTmTpM`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / WORLDBUILDING_SPECULATIVE_CRAFT`
Status: `PRIMARY_STUDIED / MECHANISMS_EXTRACTED`

High-value findings:
- every fictional setting is a selective model, not a one-to-one reproduction of a whole world;
- `WORLDVIEW` = what the author knows; `STORYVIEW` = what characters/narration know and believe;
- the gap between worldview and storyview can produce mystery, error, conflict and discovery;
- a native of a culture usually notices deviations from normality, not everything that is normal to them;
- visitors notice different things and can misinterpret local meaning;
- setting rules should create responsibility: once established, the author and characters must live with the constraints;
- specific details persuade more effectively than exhaustive description;
- setting becomes alive when past/present, institutions and material conditions actively complicate character life;
- convincing worlds contain wider cause/effect beyond a single plot incident;
- cultures should not be monolithic; technological, class, ethnic, institutional and historical layers can coexist unevenly;
- `WHAT EVERYONE KNOWS` inside a culture can be false yet still causally real because people organize behavior around it;
- a world should retain unexplored vistas and mystery rather than fill every gap;
- real-world influences should be transformed/cooked into the new context rather than visibly copied.

IVDIVO transform:
`WORLDVIEW > STORYVIEW`.
Author may know the whole system; character only reveals what their life, status, error or need makes available.

## P19-S03 — Mark J. P. Wolf — Building Imaginary Worlds
Drive ID: `1aVMV3G3cS3acQ-jns9A6bKxA2ansO0r0`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / WORLDBUILDING_SPECULATIVE_CRAFT`
Status: `PRIMARY_STUDIED / MECHANISMS_EXTRACTED`

High-value findings:
- story and world are related but not identical; encyclopedic world data can enrich context while also stopping narrative movement;
- secondary-world plausibility depends on invention, sufficient completeness and consistency operating together;
- changing one default creates limits and consequences for further invention;
- small specific changes can imply larger systems (`different object/architecture -> power/space/use assumptions -> social context`);
- world systems can be understood through layered infrastructures: space, time/history, relationships, nature/physics, culture, language, mythology, philosophy;
- narrative should usually determine which world components receive foreground definition;
- audiences fill many gaps from real-world defaults until the work teaches them new secondary-world defaults;
- once a secondary-world default is learned, later details can be inferred rather than repeatedly explained;
- the necessary details are those that establish the structures holding the world together; beyond that, selective gaps create a sense of extension;
- too much completion can become counterproductive; believable worlds retain enigmas and implied areas beyond the frame;
- world continuity becomes increasingly difficult as invention expands, so continuity control is not optional in a multi-book saga.

IVDIVO transform:
`ESTABLISH DEFAULT -> REPEAT THROUGH USE -> READER LEARNS -> STOP EXPLAINING -> EXPLOIT DEFAULT AS STORY TOOL`.

# CORE PASS 19 MECHANISMS

## P19-M01 — WORLD MODEL, NOT WORLD INVENTORY
The author may know ten thousand facts.
The scene gets only the facts needed to:
- cause action;
- constrain choice;
- locate bodies/social roles;
- reveal character/status;
- create clue/misunderstanding;
- set up/pay off later action.

Diagnostic:
`If this fact disappeared, what action or interpretation would change?`
If nothing changes, it is probably reference-bible material, not scene material.

## P19-M02 — WORLDVIEW > STORYVIEW
Maintain three layers:
1. what IVDIVO canon/world bible says is true;
2. what institutions publicly claim;
3. what current POV believes/knows.

They need not match.

This allows:
- legitimate error;
- institutional propaganda;
- old/new-system disagreement;
- youth misunderstanding;
- nonhuman ontology conflict;
- gradual revelation without authorial cheating.

## P19-M03 — NATIVE NORMALITY LAW
A person raised inside orbital civilization should not tour the reader through normal orbital life.

They notice:
- a failure;
- a queue;
- a price;
- an exception;
- an embarrassing rule;
- an upgrade they cannot afford/access;
- a social status signal;
- a dangerous deviation;
- a changed route;
- something an outsider gets wrong.

World reveal arrives through friction with normality.

## P19-M04 — DEFAULT CHANGE -> CASCADE
Any major technology or institutional rule must be tested for adjacent consequences.

At minimum ask:
- WORK: what jobs appear/disappear/change?
- MONEY: who pays, owns, rents, insures, subsidizes?
- HOUSING: what space becomes valuable/possible/illegal?
- TRANSPORT: what routes, timing, safety or access change?
- FAMILY: what becomes easier/harder for parents, teens, partners, elders?
- STATUS: who gains prestige/access and who loses it?
- LAW: what new rights/liabilities/jurisdiction appear?
- MAINTENANCE: who keeps the system alive at 03:00?
- EDUCATION: what must people learn and at what age?
- CULTURE: what jokes, shame, etiquette, habits and slang emerge?
- FAILURE: what happens when the system is unavailable or wrong?

If none of these change, the technology is probably decoration.

## P19-M05 — RULE -> CONSTRAINT -> PRICE
A world rule matters only when the story cannot casually ignore it.

`RULE -> HERO WANTS SOMETHING -> RULE BLOCKS/COMPLICATES -> TACTIC -> PRICE/TRADEOFF`.

Do not invent a restriction and then waive it because the protagonist needs the plot to move.
Exceptions must themselves have cost, authority and consequence.

## P19-M06 — MULTIPLE SOCIAL RESPONSES
No major contact, technology, migration, disaster or policy should produce one civilization-wide opinion.

Map at least:
- beneficiary;
- loser;
- professional operator;
- regulator/institution;
- ordinary user;
- skeptic/resister;
- opportunist;
- person who simply has other priorities.

Not all need a scene. The world should show enough incompatible behavior to avoid policy-committee unanimity.

## P19-M07 — SPECIFIC DETAIL AS SYSTEM PORTAL
Prefer one implication-rich object/action over a paragraph of generic futurism.

Examples of IVDIVO mechanism, not mandatory inventions:
- a delayed pressure-door cycle reveals capacity limits;
- a rent surcharge reveals scarce radial orientation;
- a maintenance patch reveals habitat age/class;
- school gear clipped differently for spin gravity reveals embodied normality;
- a transit access colour reveals age/work permissions;
- a household schedules laundry around reclaimed-water load rather than receiving a lecture on water systems.

`OBJECT -> USE -> CONSTRAINT -> SOCIAL IMPLICATION`.

## P19-M08 — LEARNED DEFAULT, THEN STOP EXPLAINING
First appearance:
show enough context for the reader to infer a rule.

Second/third use:
reinforce through consequence.

After the default is stable:
use it without explanation.

Repeated re-explanation makes residents sound like tour guides and slows the story.

## P19-M09 — OLD + NEW COEXIST
Real civilizations are layered.

Do not make the future uniformly new.
Allow:
- old modules beside new ones;
- retrofit infrastructure;
- incompatible standards;
- inherited bureaucracies;
- obsolete slang still used by adults;
- premium and cheap versions of the same technology;
- laws written for conditions that changed;
- historical crimes/successes embedded in current institutions.

This directly supports:
`OLD EARTH SECURITY <-> NEW CONFEDERATION SECURITY`.

## P19-M10 — MYSTERY FIELDS / FALLOW LORE
Not every canon fact should be revealed because it exists.

Leave unknown when:
- current story does not need the answer;
- characters plausibly cannot know;
- the gap creates legitimate curiosity;
- premature answer would lock future books without payoff.

But the unknown should feel bounded by an underlying system, not arbitrary author withholding.

IVDIVO labels help:
CANON / WORKING / OPTION / UNKNOWN / REFERENCE ONLY / REJECTED.

## P19-M11 — WORLD REVEAL THROUGH CONSEQUENCE
Preferred reveal engine:
`CHARACTER WANTS ORDINARY THING -> USES SYSTEM -> SYSTEM RULE/STATUS INTERVENES -> CHARACTER ADAPTS -> CONSEQUENCE REVEALS WORLD`.

Examples of ordinary wants:
- get home;
- make a shift;
- afford a room;
- meet someone privately;
- avoid embarrassment;
- get food;
- join sport/training;
- help family move;
- keep a job;
- cross a jurisdiction boundary.

This is the main anti-lore-dump engine for Orbital Youth.

# ORBITAL YOUTH WORLD-REVEAL GATE

Before accepting a futuristic element into a chapter, ask:

1. WHO is using it and what do they want right now?
2. Is this element normal to the POV, new to them, or malfunctioning?
3. What does it COST in money/time/status/access/attention?
4. Who OWNS / MAINTAINS / REGULATES it?
5. What does it prevent the character from doing?
6. What happens when it fails or is unavailable?
7. Does it alter youth independence relative to Earth adolescence?
8. Does it affect family, friendship, attraction, embarrassment, work or status?
9. Is there social inequality or different access around it?
10. Would the scene still work unchanged in an ordinary Earth school/city?

If answer to 10 = yes, test for `EARTH LIFE WITH SPACE WALLPAPER`.

## ORBITAL YOUTH REQUIRED REVEAL SURFACES
Across the book, civilization should become legible through causal use of:
- housing;
- money;
- jobs / first jobs;
- education/training;
- transit;
- food/water;
- maintenance;
- sport/entertainment;
- family structure;
- friendship/romance;
- status/access;
- AI/synthetic systems;
- mixed-species/nonhuman contact where earned;
- law/bureaucracy;
- emergency procedure.

No chapter must include all of them.

# SMITH / OLD EARTH SECURITY WORLD-REVEAL GATE

Smith worldbuilding should emerge through operational conflict.

Ask:
1. What does Old Earth Security treat as normal procedure that Confederation finds archaic/opaque?
2. What Confederation measurement makes an old category obsolete or incomplete?
3. What old practice worked historically, and what did it cost?
4. Which rule/jurisdiction blocks the obvious action?
5. Which archived agreement still creates present legal/ontological consequences?
6. What mundane institutional artifact reveals history: access seal, obsolete room, form, chain of custody, old vocabulary, restricted corridor, maintenance practice?
7. Where do old and new systems have incompatible defaults?
8. Does the case require both systems rather than proving one side universally superior?

Do not deliver institutional history as a lore lecture if a procedure, dispute or failed action can reveal it.

---

# CONFEDERATION / CONTACT GATE

A new nonhuman fact must produce more than wonder.

Test consequences across:
- science;
- law;
- security;
- religion/metaphysics;
- media;
- diplomacy;
- markets/resources;
- ordinary households;
- youth aspiration/status.

Do not show every reaction in one chapter.
Do not make every institution react the same way.

Use `WORLDVIEW > STORYVIEW`: Confederation may know things Earth institutions do not; Confederation itself may also hold incomplete or contested models.

# RED TEAM FAILURE CLASSES

## FATAL / MAJOR
- a world rule is broken solely to rescue the plot with no established exception/cost;
- technology that controls climax has not been established or inferable before needed;
- POV suddenly possesses world knowledge they could not plausibly have;
- current plot depends on social/legal consequences that contradict established civilization rules;
- Orbital Youth society could be replaced by present-day Earth with cosmetic nouns and scene causality would remain unchanged.

## MEDIUM
- lore paragraph stops action without present need;
- residents explain ordinary normality to each other for reader benefit;
- future technology has no ownership/maintenance/cost/failure ecology;
- society reacts unanimously to major change;
- all habitats/classes use identical infrastructure and culture;
- repeated terminology introduced before observable phenomenon;
- invented word performs no causal/cultural work;
- backstory arrives before reader has a reason to want it;
- speculative object is described but never used to pressure choice.

## POLISH
- one generic future detail;
- local over-explanation after default already learned;
- unnecessary proper noun;
- redundant glossary-like phrase;
- minor world texture that can be made more POV-specific.

---

# APPLICATION LAW
1. Story/character need first.
2. World bible may be larger than manuscript storyview.
3. Observable phenomenon before terminology/ontology when possible.
4. Native characters notice deviations, prices, failures and status—not every normal mechanism.
5. Once a default is learned, trust the reader.
6. Every major innovation should have secondary consequences somewhere in the saga architecture.
7. Do not force every consequence into the same book.
8. Preserve mystery fields for future books when current story gains nothing from disclosure.
9. Current-book closure outranks franchise lore expansion.
10. A world detail earns prose by changing action, inference, emotion, status or consequence.
