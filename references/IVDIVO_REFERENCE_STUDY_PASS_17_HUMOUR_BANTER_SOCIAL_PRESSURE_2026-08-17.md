## PURPOSE
Prevent Orbital Youth and ensemble prose from becoming uniformly serious or uniformly witty. Build a practical humour/b​​anter control in which comedy grows from character, status, embarrassment, relationships and world rules rather than detachable jokes.

## SOURCE LIMITATION
The dedicated humour shelf currently contains two books by the same author, John Vorhaus. They are therefore **not treated as two independent authorities**.

Their mechanisms are cross-checked against previously studied fiction/screen sources:
- Rob Thomas YA (`Rats Saw God`, `Satellite Down`) — humour/irony as youth defense and voice;
- *The Perks of Being a Wallflower* — social awkwardness and belonging pressure;
- Pass 12 television scripts — humour as relationship/status action inside active scenes;
- Pass 13 dialogue gate — a joke is a verbal tactic, not decoration.

## High-value mechanisms

### COMIC CHARACTER FOLLOWS A PRIVATE RULE
Vorhaus's useful abstraction is that a comic character often behaves according to a strong internal rule/filter that clashes with the situation or with other people's rules.

Examples at the abstract level:
- I must always be right.
- Nothing can embarrass me.
- Every rule is negotiable.
- If adults care about it, it is probably stupid.
- Everything is a competition.
- Never let anyone know I need help.

For IVDIVO, the point is not to make characters caricatures. It is to identify a pressure tendency that predicts how they will joke, defend themselves and create trouble.

### CONFLICTING FILTERS CREATE BANTER
Humour can emerge when two characters interpret the same situation through incompatible personal rules. The joke therefore grows out of conflict already present in the story.

### COMEDY GROUNDED IN REAL PRESSURE
The source repeatedly returns to `truth/pain`: comedy has more force when it touches something recognizably uncomfortable rather than floating as wordplay.

For youth fiction this maps well to:
- attraction;
- being broke;
- parents;
- status loss;
- bad jobs;
- being new;
- being visibly incompetent;
- trying too hard;
- privacy;
- body embarrassment;
- friendship exclusion.

## High-value mechanisms

### BREAK A SOCIAL RULE / REVEAL THE CHARACTER'S RULE
A comic action often violates what the surrounding group considers normal because the character is following a different internal logic.

IVDIVO use:
The humour is strongest when the reader understands both:
- the local/orbital/social rule;
- why this particular character behaves differently.

This means worldbuilding can create comedy causally without `future jokes` pasted onto the setting.

### CHARACTER KEY
Small repeated behavior can predict larger behavior. A tiny comic habit becomes useful when it is the miniature form of a real character strategy.

Example abstraction:
- always corrects technical terminology when nervous;
- pretends every missed transit connection was intentional;
- refuses to ask for directions and turns route failure into performance;
- converts embarrassment into exaggerated confidence.

The key should later matter beyond the joke.

### EXPECTATION GAP
Comedy often depends on the distance between:
- what the situation appears to demand;
- what the character actually does/says because of their private rule.

Do not confuse this with randomness. The surprising response should feel specific to the character.

# FICTION / SCREEN CROSS-CHECK

## Rob Thomas control
Youth irony can protect against shame, family hurt, romantic vulnerability or status loss. If the underlying pressure changes, the humour should change too.

## Pass 12 screenplay control
A joke is strongest when it changes relationship/status or exposes a point of view. Humour should not stop the scene merely so everyone can appreciate the line.

## Pass 13 dialogue control
Treat joke as tactic:
DEFLECT / FLIRT / PROVOKE / REPAIR STATUS / SIGNAL BELONGING / ATTACK / ASK FOR HELP WITHOUT ASKING / HIDE FEAR.

# PASS 17 — EXECUTABLE HUMOUR ENGINE

`SOCIAL / EMOTIONAL PRESSURE`
-> `CHARACTER PRIVATE RULE`
-> `JOKE / BEHAVIORAL TACTIC`
-> `OTHER PERSON REACTS`
-> `STATUS / AFFILIATION / EMBARRASSMENT CHANGES`
-> `SCENE CONTINUES`.

A joke that changes nothing is optional decoration.

---

# CARD 1 — WHAT IS THE JOKE DOING?

For an important humorous line assign a function:
- defend status;
- flirt;
- test intimacy;
- hide need;
- reduce another person's authority;
- signal group membership;
- exclude somebody;
- repair embarrassment;
- provoke;
- delay a serious answer;
- make pain speakable.

If the answer is only `be funny`, the line is suspect.

---

# CARD 2 — PRIVATE COMIC RULE

For each major youth character, optionally identify one pressure rule:

`WHEN EMBARRASSED / THREATENED / ATTRACTED, I TEND TO...`

This is not a catchphrase.
It is a behavior generator.

The rule may evolve across the book.

---

# CARD 3 — BANTER REQUIRES ASYMMETRY

Strong banter is rarely two equally witty people taking turns.

Track:
- who has higher status right now;
- who is trying to impress whom;
- who is actually hurt;
- who does not understand the joke;
- who refuses to participate;
- who changes the subject;
- whose joke lands badly.

Unequal reactions keep the scene human.

---

# CARD 4 — HUMOUR MAY FAIL

A joke can:
- embarrass the wrong person;
- expose attraction;
- insult a local norm;
- reveal ignorance;
- worsen an argument;
- make an authority figure less cooperative;
- create a nickname/status that the speaker did not want.

Failed humour is useful character action.

---

# CARD 5 — WORLD RULE -> COMIC CONSEQUENCE

Orbital comedy should arise from lived rules:
- gravity transitions;
- movement etiquette;
- housing privacy;
- transit timing;
- work certification;
- maintenance culture;
- AI interfaces;
- mixed-species norms;
- sports;
- Earth-born misunderstandings.

But the joke must belong to a person and situation. Do not write `look how funny the future is`.

---

# CARD 6 — DO NOT PUNCH DOWN BY DEFAULT

Nonhuman bodies, disability, synthetic embodiment, accents, poverty, novice status or cultural difference should not automatically be the joke object.

Humour may come from:
- conflicting assumptions;
- status overconfidence;
- procedural absurdity;
- character vanity;
- self-protection;
- institutional contradiction;
- mutual misunderstanding where characters retain agency.

The target of the joke matters socially inside the scene.

---

# CARD 7 — COMEDY DOES NOT ERASE CONSEQUENCE

After injury, betrayal, death, serious privacy harm or displacement, jokes may still occur — people use humour under stress — but they must not magically cancel the cost.

Ask:
`Does the joke reveal coping, or does the author use it to escape emotional consequence?`

If the latter, cut/rewrite.

---

# CARD 8 — RECURRING JOKE MUST EVOLVE

A recurring bit should change with relationship/status:
- first use = attack;
- later = private affiliation signal;
- later = painful reminder;
- later = repair or farewell.

If repeated identically, it becomes generated banter.

This directly reinforces the existing Ch21 `face`-joke polish watch.

---

# ORBITAL YOUTH HUMOUR GATE v0.1

Before a youth ensemble section is GREEN, ask:

1. Does at least one character have a distinct humour strategy under pressure?
2. What embarrassment/status pressure makes the joke useful now?
3. Who is the joke aimed at?
4. What does the speaker actually want from the listener?
5. Does the listener laugh, resist, miss it, get hurt, retaliate or ignore it?
6. Does the joke change status/affiliation/tension?
7. Could the same joke be spoken by any other character? If yes, voice is weak.
8. Is local/orbital specificity causal rather than novelty garnish?
9. Does humour survive ordinary youth life — jobs, money, romance, family, sport — rather than appearing only in emergencies?
10. Does humour disappear appropriately when this particular character can no longer hide behind it?
11. Are serious consequences still emotionally real afterward?
12. Has any recurring joke changed meaning across the arc?

# SMITH HUMOUR CONTROL

Smith does not need to become a quip machine.
His humour, if used, should reveal professional stance:
- dry shorthand;
- institutional skepticism;
- deflection from personal vulnerability;
- status-testing between professionals;
- reaction to absurd bureaucracy;
- old-system/new-system cultural friction.

Enia should not become a dispenser of mystical aphorisms or comic wise-man lines.

# RED TEAM ADDITIONS

## MAJOR
- every young character is equally witty;
- humour repeatedly cancels emotional consequence;
- banter replaces actual disagreement;
- jokes target identity difference without agency/context;
- characters joke during danger in a way that contradicts their established fear/competence only because the author wants levity.

## MEDIUM
- joke could be reassigned to anyone;
- recurring bit repeats without changed meaning;
- humour exists only in dialogue, never behavior/situation;
- every joke lands successfully;
- humorous character never pays a social price.

## POLISH
- too many lines built around the same reversal rhythm;
- overuse of sarcastic one-liners;
- repeated self-deprecation;
- characters verbally explaining why something was funny.

# RESULT

IVDIVO humour engine:

`REAL PRESSURE -> CHARACTER-SPECIFIC RULE -> HUMOROUS TACTIC/BEHAVIOR -> UNEQUAL REACTION -> STATUS/RELATIONSHIP CHANGE -> STORY CONTINUES`.

Humour is not a break from character.
It is one of the ways character acts under pressure.
