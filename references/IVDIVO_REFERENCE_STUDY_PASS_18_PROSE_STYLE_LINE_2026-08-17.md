## SOURCE SET / STUDY DEPTH

### P18-S01 — Ursula K. Le Guin — Steering the Craft
Drive ID: `1RkqyP4EVgPpHE9vjuPmkhycrLPF48RsH`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / PROSE_STYLE`
Status: `PRIMARY_STUDIED / MECHANISMS_EXTRACTED`
Substantial source text inspected, especially chapters on sound, sentence length/complex syntax, POV/voice, and crowding/leaping.

High-value findings:
- narrative prose is heard as well as decoded; sound, silence and rhythm carry meaning and pace;
- a narrative sentence must lead into the next sentence rather than stop the story to display itself;
- all-short-sentence prose becomes monotonous just as all-long-sentence prose becomes clogged;
- sentence length and syntax should follow pressure, thought complexity and movement;
- limited POV is an information restriction, not merely a pronoun choice;
- voice and POV are tightly coupled because the story is filtered through what this narrator can know, notice and misinterpret;
- vivid prose requires both `CROWDING` and `LEAPING`: exact implication-rich detail where it matters, aggressive omission where it does not;
- listing everything is not description;
- action becomes fast when spatial orientation is clear and sensory detail is brief, exact and causal rather than blow-by-blow.

IVDIVO translation:
`PRECISE CAUSAL DETAIL -> ORIENTATION -> SELECTIVE LEAP -> NEXT CHANGE`.

### P18-S02 — Gary Provost — 100 Ways to Improve Your Writing
Drive ID: `1rQjSJWOvEHNUDf5NKo2KIuCiVtL9XZST`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / PROSE_STYLE`
Status: `PRIMARY_STUDIED / MECHANISMS_EXTRACTED`

High-value findings:
- read prose aloud to detect false rhythm, tonal jumps, breathless clauses and distracting sound collisions;
- vary sentence length and construction rather than building pages from one repeated syntax;
- strong verbs and specific nouns normally carry more energy than modifier piles;
- transitions should move the reader quickly unless the transition itself contains a causal event;
- do not explain travel, handling, movement or connective procedure when nothing important happens there;
- every word should perform work; repeated or already-implied information slows the reader;
- line rules are communication tools, not mechanical commandments.

IVDIVO translation:
`IF NOTHING CHANGES -> LEAP`.
`IF THE IMPLICATION IS ALREADY LEGIBLE -> DO NOT EXPLAIN IT AGAIN`.

### P18-S03 — Joseph M. Williams / Joseph Bizup — Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace
Drive ID: `1gTLSEGpvJFH4VrdIl5GBYMNJsxEHLvoI`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / PROSE_STYLE`
Status: `PRIMARY_STUDIED / MECHANISMS_EXTRACTED`

Scope warning:
This is primarily a clarity/style text for expository writing. Its mechanisms are useful; its surface style is NOT an authority for fictional voice.

High-value findings:
- when agency matters, make central characters/actors grammatical subjects and important actions verbs;
- hidden action inside abstract nouns can make causal prose feel bureaucratic or evasive;
- sentence flow improves when familiar/established information frames new information;
- readers naturally place stress toward sentence endings, so important new image/information can be positioned there intentionally;
- concision means enough words, not minimum words;
- excessive terseness is as damaging as verbal flab;
- long openings before the subject/verb and sprawling uncontrolled endings burden comprehension;
- sentence length and rhythm can be managed deliberately to embody motion or thought.

IVDIVO translation:
`AGENT -> ACTION -> OBJECT/CHANGE` when causal clarity matters.
Do not apply this so rigidly that interior voice or deliberate ambiguity is flattened.

### P18-S04 — Jordan Rosenfeld — The Sound of Story
Drive ID: `1PvVRn0bDa0X-BFHydhNXaIZRQThPRimJ`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / PROSE_STYLE`
Status: `PRIMARY_STUDIED / MECHANISMS_EXTRACTED`

High-value findings:
- voice differentiation comes from specificity of perception and selection, not merely slang/tics;
- voice operates in observation, thought, action and feeling, not just dialogue;
- syntax and vocabulary are part of character architecture;
- concrete nouns carry different emotional associations for different characters;
- rhythm and musicality can reinforce voice, but ornamental sound can also clutter or distance;
- clutter includes unintended repetition, generic cliche, unnecessary filters and overworked modifiers;
- tone is the reader-facing emotional effect produced by conflict, imagery, detail and arrangement, not simply a character announcing an attitude;
- a voice should be refined in revision without normalizing every character into the same polished authorial voice.

IVDIVO translation:
`SAME EVENT -> DIFFERENT POV -> DIFFERENT SELECTED DETAILS / WORD VALUES / RHYTHM`.

# PROSE MECHANISM CARDS

## P18-M01 — SELECTED PERCEPTION CREATES VOICE
Do not ask only: `What is in the room?`
Ask: `What would THIS POV notice first, name precisely, misread, ignore, resent, optimize, fear, desire or joke about?`

Orbital Youth examples of transform, not prose prescriptions:
- Ollie notices system relation, sequence, fit, timing, permissions and anomalies;
- Aoife notices framing, omission, ownership, wording, recording boundaries and what an edit would imply;
- Ethan notices dependencies, options, tradeoffs, status pathways and what can be optimized;
- Maya notices social permission, embarrassment, attention, autonomy and who is watching;
- Samir notices body timing, balance, movement, space, practical awkwardness and competitive/social rhythm.

A paragraph that could be moved unchanged between POVs fails this card unless neutral narration is deliberately required.

## P18-M02 — OBJECT -> ACTION -> IMPLICATION
Prefer observable causal prose:
`OBJECT/CONDITION -> ACTION -> RESULT/IMPLICATION`.

Watch for generated inverse:
`ABSTRACT REALIZATION -> EXPLANATION -> MORAL`.

A character need not explain what the action already proves.

## P18-M03 — CROWD AND LEAP
CROWD where reader orientation, emotion, clue, physical causality or character selection needs exact detail.
LEAP over repeated handling, routine transit, procedure and micro-actions that do not change state.

`DETAIL COUNT` is not richness.
`CAUSAL / CHARACTER-SELECTED DETAIL` is richness.

## P18-M04 — SENTENCE RHYTHM FOLLOWS PRESSURE
Short sentence = one tool.
Long/complex sentence = one tool.
Neither is default quality.

Use variation to follow:
- speed;
- hesitation;
- thought complexity;
- sensory overload;
- confidence/uncertainty;
- character voice;
- scene pressure.

Failure mode: paragraphs composed almost entirely of one-sentence fragments because the prose is trying to imitate intensity.

## P18-M05 — AGENCY MUST REMAIN LEGIBLE
When the reader must understand who caused what, do not bury the event in abstractions or passive institutional fog unless concealment itself is the point.

Diagnostic transform:
`Who/what acted? -> what did they do? -> what changed?`

Especially important in:
- Smith jurisdiction/action scenes;
- orbital maintenance;
- incident reconstruction;
- safety decisions;
- political/institutional consequence.

## P18-M06 — STRESS THE RIGHT WORD
Sentence/paragraph endings naturally carry weight.
Check whether the line accidentally ends on a weak qualifier, procedural filler or explanation after the actual image/turn.

Do not turn every line into a punchline. Use emphasis selectively.

## P18-M07 — READ-ALOUD DIAGNOSTIC
A line pass should flag:
- repeated cadence;
- repeated sentence openings;
- accidental rhyme/alliteration;
- breathless clause chains;
- hard-to-say dialogue;
- repeated filler beats;
- monotone short-sentence hammering;
- sudden authorial register that does not belong to POV.

## P18-M08 — CONCISION != TERSE
Cut redundancy, not life.

Keep detail when it does at least one of:
- causes action;
- locates the body in space;
- differentiates POV;
- creates social/emotional pressure;
- reveals world through use;
- sets up/payoffs;
- creates necessary rhythm/beauty.

Cut when it merely repeats the previous implication or reports an unimportant connective step.

# IVDIVO PROSE / LINE RED TEAM

## FATAL / MAJOR-CLASS LINE FAILURE ONLY WHEN IT DAMAGES STORY
A line-level issue becomes MAJOR when it systematically makes scenes unreadable, destroys POV knowledge boundaries, hides causality required for the plot, or gives multiple protagonists the same narrative consciousness.

Examples:
- narrator states knowledge the current limited POV cannot have and plot relies on it;
- emergency action is spatially/causally incomprehensible because agency is hidden;
- the same polished thematic voice overwrites distinct characters across many chapters;
- explanation after every event destroys dramatic implication and pacing across the manuscript.

## MEDIUM
- repeated generated cadence across a scene/chapter;
- too many isolated microparagraphs without pressure function;
- transition logistics narrated despite no event;
- line endings routinely explain the stronger preceding image/action;
- generic description not filtered by POV;
- dialogue surrounded by redundant interpretation;
- action crowded with noncausal micro-movement;
- important action buried in abstract noun-heavy construction.

## POLISH
- local repeated words/gestures;
- accidental sound collision;
- weak sentence stress;
- unnecessary filter words;
- generic cliche where character-specific perception would be better;
- one or two cadence repetitions.

# GENERATED-TEXT PATTERN WATCH — IVDIVO SPECIFIC
Existing Founder law reinforced:
- repeated syntax;
- excessive tiny paragraphs;
- `There it was`;
- `That mattered`;
- `That was worse`;
- repeated `X looked at Y`;
- moral explanation after action;
- symmetrical group dialogue;
- perfect thematic formulations.

Pass 18 adds:
- repeated `Not X. Not Y. Z.` structures;
- repeated diagnostic fragments used as artificial intensity;
- repeated one-line aphoristic closers;
- every POV observing with the same metaphor density and sentence rhythm;
- automatic deletion of long sentences merely because they are long.

# APPLICATION LAW
1. Story/scene/character gates come first.
2. Apply Pass 18 after prose exists.
3. Do not flatten deliberate voice irregularity.
4. Do not rewrite accepted GREEN chapters wholesale because a style manual exists.
5. Repair concrete local/systemic problems only.
6. Read-aloud/sound control is diagnostic, not a demand for ornamental prose.
7. If a beautiful line stops the story merely to be admired, test whether it belongs.
8. If a plain line carries action perfectly, do not decorate it to prove craft.
