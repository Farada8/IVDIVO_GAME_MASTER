## 1. WILL STORR — THE SCIENCE OF STORYTELLING

Drive ID: `1v_IPZ-yEvdbvo-WpmRJLDcW974LJL549`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / Narrative Theory & Cognition`
Quality: PRIMARY / READABLE

### Extracted mechanisms

1. **CHANGE CAPTURES ATTENTION** — stable reality becomes narratively active when something changes; change creates an information/control problem.
2. **THE FLAWED MODEL DEFENDS ITSELF** — once a person has built a model of self/world, contradictory evidence can trigger model-defense rather than rational updating.
3. **STATUS PLAY IS STORY MATERIAL** — hierarchy, humiliation, exclusion, recognition and reversals of rank are naturally dramatic.
4. **CHARACTER-FIRST STRUCTURE** — structure should support the cast; unique plots are more likely to grow from specific flawed people than from a generic beat list.
5. **MULTIPLE ATTENTION SYSTEMS** — curiosity, specificity, moral outrage, status, threat, unfairness and attraction can work together rather than one plot formula doing all the work.

### IVDIVO use

- Smith should not merely solve external facts. His current model of an anomaly, institution or agreement should be capable of being wrong and defended.
- Orbital Youth scenes need status changes: who belongs, who is embarrassed, who has access, who gets the better shift, room, route, scholarship, body, invitation or romantic attention.
- Character architecture must include a defended self/world model, not only a WANT.

### Do not copy

Do not import Storr’s examples, terminology as fictional lore, or turn psychology into exposition. Use only the causal mechanism.

## 2. LAURENCE STEINBERG — ADOLESCENCE, 13TH EDITION

Drive ID: `1K4KQhnH4Vks9P7919IrUJ8xqdUsLcqqp`
Shelf: `08_YOUTH_PSYCHOLOGY`
Quality: PRIMARY SCIENTIFIC REFERENCE / READABLE

### Structural value for Orbital Youth

The book treats adolescence across three interacting layers:

- fundamental changes: biological, cognitive, social transitions;
- contexts: families, peer groups, schools, work/leisure/media;
- psychosocial development: identity, autonomy, intimacy, sexuality, achievement, psychosocial problems.

This is a better control grid for Orbital Youth than a generic “teen voice” checklist.

### Extracted mechanisms

1. **ADOLESCENCE IS A SOCIAL STATUS TRANSITION** — legal/status boundaries and social redefinition matter, not only age.
2. **PEER ECOLOGY HAS STRUCTURE** — friends, cliques, crowds, popularity, rejection and bullying are different social mechanisms.
3. **WORK CHANGES DEVELOPMENT** — adolescent employment affects money, independence, friendships, time, aspirations and school pressure. The text notes that heavy school-year work can have different effects from lighter or summer employment.
4. **AUTONOMY IS NEGOTIATED** — independence is not simply “leaving parents”; it changes authority, responsibility and emotional dependence.
5. **INTIMACY CHANGES TARGETS AND FORMS** — friendship, mixed-gender friendship, dating and romantic relationships develop differently and carry different stakes.
6. **CONTEXT MATTERS** — neighborhood, economic stress, migration, school organization and media environment shape adolescent behavior.

### IVDIVO use

Every major Orbital Youth character should have a living matrix across:
FAMILY / PEER GROUP / SCHOOL OR TRAINING / WORK / MONEY / LEISURE / MEDIA / IDENTITY / AUTONOMY / INTIMACY-ROMANCE / STATUS.

Not every element must appear in every chapter, but if several are missing from the book architecture the character risks becoming a plot-function adult in a young body.

### Special work/money rule

A youth job must produce at least two consequences beyond exposition, e.g.:
- schedule conflict;
- fatigue;
- money or debt;
- access;
- status;
- new peer group;
- dependence or independence from family;
- professional mistake;
- romantic/social opportunity;
- ethical pressure.

### Do not copy

Do not freeze 2023 U.S. adolescent institutions into future orbital society. Transfer developmental mechanisms, then rebuild the institutions for orbital civilization.

## 3. KARL IGLESIAS — WRITING FOR EMOTIONAL IMPACT

Primary Drive ID: `1AWCqqiRZEQCqdJ1N3mWTP9-nPjMMwefG`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / 20_EMOTIONAL_IMPACT_READER_ENGAGEMENT`
Quality: PRIMARY / CLEAN READABLE TEXT

Alternate scans:
- `16Tco9hWzOKum_cKASKrEYEz8wkQakvVU`
- `1KmOFBBWDkvFPxoVhUkY1c8O1WFLufxCl` — degraded text extraction in places

All three appear to represent the same 2005 first edition; sizes differ, so they are NOT declared byte-identical duplicates.

### Extracted mechanisms

1. **CHARACTER EMOTION != READER EMOTION** — a calm character may create reader fear; a distressed character may fail to create reader sympathy.
2. **READER EMOTION MUST BE DESIGNED** — attraction, curiosity, anticipation, worry, fear, tension, surprise, amusement, sadness, triumph etc. are reader-experience variables.
3. **SUBTEXT MAKES THE READER ACTIVE** — surface text and underlying emotional truth can differ; the reader participates by inferring.
4. **ACTION IS STRONGER THAN LABELLED EMOTION** — avoid explaining “she is angry” after behavior has already shown it.
5. **SCENE AS MINI-STORY** — a dramatic scene needs its own objective/resistance/change and emotional movement.

### IVDIVO use

Add two separate fields to Reader Advocate / scene review:
- CHARACTER EMOTION NOW
- DESIRED READER EMOTION NOW

If both are left implicit, the scene is not emotionally designed enough for a late-stage pass.

### Do not copy

Do not convert the emotional palette into a mechanical quota (“one surprise every N pages”). Use it diagnostically.

## 4. ROBERT McKEE — STORY

Primary source used for this pass: `1Fj6I5T4D8c_nvb9DzcqZKZ-PyNDSW3Ht`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / 01_STORY_STRUCTURE_PLOT`
Quality: PRIMARY CRAFT REFERENCE

Other Drive formats/copies exist and are not treated as independent mechanism sources.

### Extracted mechanisms

1. **PRINCIPLES, NOT FORMULAS** — form is functional, but no single commercial recipe guarantees story.
2. **ARCHETYPE THROUGH SPECIFICITY** — universal conflict works best inside a highly specific world rather than generic stereotypes.
3. **STRUCTURE / CHARACTER / MEANING ARE INTERLOCKED** — story design cannot be repaired by moving beats alone if motivation is false.
4. **ANTAGONISM REVEALS CAPACITY** — stronger, more specific opposition forces more meaningful choices.
5. **CRISIS -> CLIMAX -> RESOLUTION** — climax should resolve the current dramatic action; resolution is not a trailer for the sequel.
6. **RESPECT THE AUDIENCE** — audience anticipation is part of design; predictability and explanatory overkill are failures.

### IVDIVO use

- Red Team should distinguish missing structure from false motivation.
- Story Gate should ask whether the opposition forces the hero to reveal character through costly choice.
- Worldbuilding should become highly specific daily life while conflict remains human-readable.

## 5. JOHN TRUBY — THE ANATOMY OF STORY

Primary Drive ID: `1YlRpbL0f-9vBC86UcC62arAlbEpfUhn2`
Shelf: `01_WRITING_CRAFT / 01_STORY_STRUCTURE_PLOT`
Quality: PRIMARY CRAFT REFERENCE

Alternate Drive edition/copy: `1gy2MalLc0b2ag-TOAWZJPFvZugCnmnyq`

### Extracted mechanisms

1. **ORGANIC CAUSALITY OVER EXTERNAL ACT BREAKS** — the story should feel like one growing organism rather than boxes connected by page numbers.
2. **DESIRE -> ACTION -> LEARNING -> DECISION -> NEW ACTION** — useful core loop for causal chapter maps.
3. **REVEAL / WITHHOLD** — the audience should be emotionally living the story and intellectually figuring something out.
4. **CHARACTER WEB** — characters define and pressure one another; a hero cannot be fully designed in isolation.
5. **STORY SUBSYSTEMS MUST INTERLOCK** — character, plot, revelation, world, moral argument, symbols, scene weave and dialogue should support rather than contradict each other.

### IVDIVO use

Causal map default:
WANT -> ACTION -> RESISTANCE -> INFORMATION/REVELATION -> DECISION -> COST -> NEW SITUATION.

If chapters can be reordered without breaking this chain, architecture is probably episodic.

## 6. ANGIE THOMAS — THE HATE U GIVE

Drive ID: `1E5nOrLUNWf77RsZuvHlgntxiuN9y1gRl`
Shelf: General YA Literary Reference
Quality: PRIMARY FICTION REFERENCE

### Extracted mechanisms

1. **TWO SOCIAL SELVES / CODE-SWITCHING PRESSURE** — the protagonist belongs to more than one social world and adjusts behavior between them.
2. **STATUS BEFORE CRISIS** — party discomfort, clothes, school identity, reputation, friendships and attraction exist before the major public event.
3. **FAMILY / WORK / NEIGHBORHOOD / SCHOOL INTERLOCK** — youth life is not confined to school.
4. **VOICE CARRIES SOCIAL POSITION** — vocabulary, jokes, irritation, embarrassment and observation differentiate lived identity.

### Orbital Youth transformation

A young orbital citizen may speak/behave differently across habitat, Earth family, professional shift, mixed-species group, elite training cohort or planetary visit. The friction between these identities can itself generate story.

## 7. STEPHEN CHBOSKY — THE PERKS OF BEING A WALLFLOWER

Primary Drive ID: `1QCao7Ar6-C59LlKGcsYrjV9sK-Lo6n0L`
Shelf: General YA Literary Reference

Alternate/legacy text copy: `1Z2YiAqLsCSyh2TmiCMoCBmiP43znUwgK`
Same work, different file/format quality; not declared byte duplicate.

### Extracted mechanisms

1. **PASSIVITY VS PARTICIPATION CAN BE AN ARC ENGINE** — a character’s relationship to participation may be more important than an external “special ability.”
2. **VOICE IS SOCIAL PERCEPTION** — the narrator notices grammar, embarrassment, family silences, school naming, music, routines and social signals in a distinctive way.
3. **ORDINARY LIFE CARRIES HEAVY EMOTION** — dishes, cars, classes, tapes, siblings, teachers, money and small favors carry relational meaning.
4. **RELATIONSHIPS CREATE THE WAITING QUESTION** — reader engagement can come from “will this person enter life / connect / speak / choose?” rather than mystery alone.

### Orbital Youth transformation

Use orbital equivalents of mundane culture — transport habits, shared rooms, playlists/immersive media, sports, maintenance shifts, meal routines, public/private channels — as emotional carriers.

# CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

## A. CHARACTER MODEL ENGINE

STORR + TRUBY + McKEE

Hero has:
- external WANT;
- defended model of self/world;
- status position;
- action strategy.

Opposition attacks both the WANT and the model.
New information should not automatically enlighten the hero. It may trigger defense, rationalization, escalation or a wrong choice.

## B. ORBITAL YOUTH REALITY GRID

STEINBERG + THE HATE U GIVE + PERKS + SCYTHE + LITTLE FIRES

Before a large speculative plot takes over, architecture should know:
- home / housing;
- family authority;
- friends / clique / crowd;
- school/training;
- job/shift;
- money;
- leisure/media/sport;
- social status;
- identity across contexts;
- autonomy conflict;
- intimacy/romance;
- ordinary institutional rules.

## C. READER EMOTION ENGINE

IGLESIAS + STORR + McKEE

For major scenes distinguish:
1. What does the character feel?
2. What should the reader feel?
3. What information gap produces that response?
4. What changes the reader’s prediction?
5. What relationship/status shift makes the scene matter?

## D. CAUSAL CHAPTER LOOP

TRUBY + McKEE

WANT -> ACTION -> RESISTANCE -> REVELATION/INFORMATION -> DECISION -> PRICE -> CHANGED SITUATION.

A chapter that contributes only information but no changed situation is suspect.

# NEW STORY GATE QUESTIONS — PASS 09

1. What model of self/world is the protagonist defending?
2. What evidence threatens that model?
3. Does the protagonist update, deny, rationalize or escalate?
4. What is the protagonist’s status at scene entry and exit?
5. What does the character feel, and separately what should the reader feel?
6. What information gap keeps the reader active?
7. For youth: where are family, peers, work/money, autonomy and intimacy affecting decisions?
8. Does the youth job change schedule/money/status/relationships, or is it decoration?
9. Does speculative worldbuilding enter through ordinary life before terminology?
10. Does each major revelation cause a decision or altered action?
11. Can chapters be reordered without breaking causality? If yes, investigate episodic structure.
12. Does climax resolve the present book’s main dramatic action?

# RED TEAM WARNINGS ADDED

MAJOR:
- mechanically correct architecture with no designed reader emotion;
- youth character with no peer/status/money/autonomy ecology;
- revelation that changes knowledge but not action;
- protagonist accepts contradictory evidence too cleanly despite strong prior identity investment;
- speculative institution explained before it materially affects ordinary life.

MEDIUM:
- job/housing/school exists only as worldbuilding wallpaper;
- all characters experience the same social identity across every context;
- important dialogue states relationship conflict instead of letting action/subtext carry it;
- scene changes plot information but not relationship/status/emotional prediction.
