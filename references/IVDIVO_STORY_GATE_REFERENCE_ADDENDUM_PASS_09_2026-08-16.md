# IVDIVO STORY GATE — REFERENCE ADDENDUM PASS 09

Date: 2026-08-16
Status: WORKING PRODUCTION TOOL
Authority: below canon; supplements existing Story Gate; does not replace Founder instructions or IVDIVO Production Canon.

## PURPOSE

Operationalize findings from Reference Study Pass 09. Apply during Story Core, Architecture, Causal Chapter Map, Red Team, Character/Relationship Pass and Reader Advocate.

---

## GATE A — CHARACTER MODEL

For protagonist and major opposition answer:

- WANT:
- WHY NOW:
- DEFENDED MODEL OF SELF:
- DEFENDED MODEL OF WORLD:
- STATUS POSITION:
- WRONG STRATEGY:
- EVIDENCE THAT THREATENS MODEL:
- FIRST RESPONSE TO CONTRADICTION: update / denial / rationalization / escalation / concealment
- PRICE OF DEFENDING MODEL:
- CLIMAX MODEL CHANGE OR REFUSAL:

FAIL if new evidence simply makes the character instantly correct without psychological/social resistance where prior identity investment is strong.

---

## GATE B — CAUSAL ACTION LOOP

Every major sequence must support:

WANT -> ACTION -> RESISTANCE -> INFORMATION/REVELATION -> DECISION -> PRICE -> CHANGED SITUATION.

FAIL if:
- revelation changes only knowledge;
- chapter can be removed with no downstream causal damage;
- chapters can be freely reordered;
- protagonist remains observer while others generate all decisive action.

---

## GATE C — ORBITAL YOUTH REALITY

For every major young character establish at architecture level:

- HOME / HOUSING
- FAMILY AUTHORITY
- FRIENDS
- CLIQUE / CROWD / STATUS GROUP
- SCHOOL / TRAINING
- JOB / SHIFT
- MONEY / DEPENDENCE
- LEISURE / SPORT / MEDIA
- IDENTITY ACROSS CONTEXTS
- AUTONOMY CONFLICT
- INTIMACY / ROMANCE
- EMBARRASSMENT / EXCLUSION RISK
- ORDINARY ORBITAL RULE THAT AFFECTS LIFE

Not all fields need equal page-time.

FAIL if youth life can be moved unchanged to an ordinary present-day Earth school.
FAIL if job, housing or money exists only as exposition.

### JOB CONSEQUENCE TEST

A youth job should normally affect at least two of:
TIME / FATIGUE / MONEY / ACCESS / STATUS / FRIENDS / FAMILY DEPENDENCE / ROMANCE / PROFESSIONAL RISK / ETHICS.

## GATE D — STATUS MOVEMENT

For important scenes:

- STATUS AT ENTRY:
- WHO THINKS THEY HAVE POWER:
- WHO ACTUALLY HAS LEVERAGE:
- STATUS THREAT:
- HUMILIATION / RECOGNITION / EXCLUSION / ACCESS CHANGE:
- STATUS AT EXIT:

FAIL if repeated dialogue scenes leave the same relationship hierarchy untouched.

---

## GATE E — READER EMOTION

For every major scene specify separately:

- CHARACTER EMOTION:
- DESIRED READER EMOTION:
- INFORMATION GAP:
- READER PREDICTION:
- WHAT CHANGES THAT PREDICTION:
- EMOTIONAL PAYOFF OR NEW WAITING QUESTION:

Possible reader responses include curiosity, anticipation, hope, worry, fear, tension, surprise, amusement, compassion, admiration, contempt, sadness, relief, triumph, wonder.

Do not quota emotions mechanically.

FAIL if technically correct scene produces no intended reader experience.

---

## GATE F — SUBTEXT / DIALOGUE ACTION

For important conversations:

- SPEAKER OBJECTIVE:
- LISTENER RESISTANCE:
- SURFACE SUBJECT:
- ACTUAL RELATIONSHIP ISSUE:
- WITHHELD INFORMATION:
- STATUS ASYMMETRY:
- ACTION THAT CONTRADICTS OR QUALIFIES WORDS:
- CHANGE AT EXIT:

FAIL if scene says directly what both characters already know and neither status nor decision changes.

---

## GATE G — WORLD THROUGH ORDINARY LIFE

Before explaining a speculative institution/technology, show at least one concrete consequence in:

JOB / HOME / TRANSPORT / FOOD / SCHOOL / MONEY / LAW / MAINTENANCE / ENTERTAINMENT / RELATIONSHIP / BUREAUCRACY / EMERGENCY.

FAIL if terminology arrives before observable phenomenon without story need.

---

## GATE H — SPECIFIC WORLD / HUMAN CONFLICT

Test:

1. Is the conflict emotionally human-readable?
2. Is its expression specific to this future civilization?
3. Would changing the orbital setting to a generic modern city leave most scenes intact?

If answer to 3 is YES, world integration is weak.

---

## GATE I — READER ADVOCATE WAITING QUESTIONS

At chapter exit answer:

- Why turn the page?
- What am I emotionally waiting for?
- What prediction am I testing?
- Which relationship is unresolved?
- Which cost has increased?
- Which ordinary-life problem remains active alongside the main plot?

FAIL if only answer is “to learn more lore.”

---

## GATE J — CLIMAX / RESOLUTION

- What current-book action reaches crisis?
- What costly choice belongs specifically to this hero?
- What becomes impossible after the choice?
- What main conflict is actually resolved?
- What permanent consequence survives?
- Only after resolution: what series hook remains?

FAIL if climax merely reveals franchise information.
FAIL if Book N ends by transferring its unresolved main conflict to Book N+1.

---

## RED TEAM FLAGS ADDED BY PASS 09

FATAL:
- no causal decision chain from protagonist to climax;
- current-book conflict not resolved.

MAJOR:
- character has WANT but no defended model/contradiction;
- youth exists outside believable family/peer/work/money/autonomy ecology;
- technically correct structure with no reader-emotion design;
- revelations do not change action;
- speculative institution explained rather than lived;
- opposition does not force costly character revelation.

MEDIUM:
- status unchanged across repeated scenes;
- job/housing/school functions as wallpaper;
- important dialogue lacks subtext or asymmetry;
- every social context produces the same character voice/behavior.

POLISH:
- emotional state explained after already being shown;
- abstract theme stated where action could carry it.

---

## USAGE

This is a diagnostic layer, not a formula. It must strengthen STORY FIRST and CHARACTER SECOND, not create checklist fiction.
