## Executive extraction

This batch materially strengthens five Writers’ Room systems:
1. SMITH / OLD EARTH SECURITY: hypothesis-driven mystery; institutional lies; incompatible ontologies; investigator method as personal cost.
2. ORBITAL YOUTH: ordinary life before anomaly; work/money/friends/family as world reveal; bounded real social networks; youth ability rooted in shame/status/relationships.
3. FIRST CONTACT / CONFEDERATION: contact as scientific, political, religious, media and jurisdictional event rather than a single revelation scene.
4. WORLD REVEAL: institutions made visible through transactions, jobs, bureaucracy, transport, labor, status and everyday humiliation.
5. CHARACTER / VOICE: self-invention, unreliable self-justification, friendship contrasts, mixed motives inside groups.

## Cross-source mechanisms to operationalize

### SMITH
- CLOSED CASE → NEW EVIDENCE → SOCIAL/INSTITUTIONAL DESTABILIZATION (Christie)
- COMPETING HYPOTHESES, not monster-of-the-week taxonomy (Eco)
- OPERATIONAL COMPARTMENTALIZATION and institutional deception (le Carré)
- INVESTIGATOR METHOD HAS A PRICE (Harris)
- INCOMPATIBLE DEFINITIONS OF PERSONHOOD/HELP/ATTACK (Watts)
- BELIEF CAN BE OPERATIONALLY REAL EVEN IF ONTOLOGY IS WRONG (Eco legendary lands)

### ORBITAL YOUTH
- ORDINARY LIFE FIRST: jobs, money, family, friends, school/work, pets, status (Rowling, Bradbury, Stephenson)
- MYSTERY CLUES LIVE INSIDE ORDINARY LIFE, not separate exposition track (Rowling)
- ABILITY AFTER WOUND / DESIRE / STATUS (Marie Lu)
- SOCIAL WORLD MUST HAVE A CORE NETWORK (Dunbar)
- EMERGENCY PRESSES AN EXISTING CIVILIZATION (Seveneves)

### CONFEDERATION / FIRST CONTACT
- CONTACT CHANGES INSTITUTIONS BEFORE PHYSICAL ENCOUNTER (Sagan)
- DIFFERENT ONTOLOGIES CAN MAKE BOTH SIDES RATIONALLY TERRIFIED (Watts)
- SCIENCE/IDEA MUST BECOME CHARACTER CHOICE AND PRICE (Chiang)

### GENERAL STORY GATE ADDITIONS
For any high-concept IVDIVO story, ask:
1. What ordinary life exists before the concept arrives?
2. What does the protagonist risk losing besides survival?
3. What hypothesis does the protagonist currently believe?
4. What evidence would force that hypothesis to change?
5. What institution has incentives to preserve a wrong model?
6. What does the threat believe it is doing?
7. Which mundane object/job/relationship is secretly causal?
8. Does the climax resolve the current book’s action even if ontology stays partially open?
