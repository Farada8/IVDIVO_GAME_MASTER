### 1. CHARMED (2018) — PILOT

#### Observed construction
The pilot does not begin with a lecture about magic. It begins with sisters, borrowed boots, a party, ideology, family humor, a mother's work conflict and ordinary movement through town. The supernatural rupture enters that existing family/social system.

Act progression is especially useful:
- Act One ends with Macy crossing the threshold and revealing evidence that she may be their sister.
- Act Two ends with the sisters being told what they are and what larger danger exists.
- Later acts keep converting abstract mythology into personal danger, social consequences and family choices.
- The episode ending resolves the immediate pilot movement enough to establish the new family unit but opens a new trust question around Harry.

#### Abstract mechanisms
**RELATIONSHIP BEFORE ONTOLOGY**
The audience understands who annoys, protects, judges and loves whom before it understands the full supernatural system.

**PROGRESSIVE REVEAL LADDER**
Do not reveal family secret + ability + institution + enemy + cosmology in one scene. Each new fact should force a new action or relationship interpretation.

**ACT-OUT = NEW OPERATING REALITY**
A good act-out is not merely danger. It changes what the characters now believe they must do.

**HUMOR AS RELATIONSHIP ACTION**
Jokes establish sibling status, ideology, attraction, embarrassment and resistance; they are not detachable garnish.

#### IVDIVO use
Orbital Youth openings should prefer:
ordinary social friction -> local rule/mistake -> relationship turn -> first speculative evidence -> changed action.

Do not open with orbital history, Confederation policy or AI ontology unless a character is actively fighting over it for an immediate reason.

### 2. CHARMED (2018) 1x15 — “SWITCHES & STONES”

#### Observed construction
An ongoing-series episode can carry several active pressures without turning into equal-airtime committee dialogue.

The episode opens with Macy seeking institutional help for a dangerous condition. That scene has a clear objective, resistance, partial permission, withholding and interruption. The story then cuts into the sisters' domestic/magical problem and Mel's romantic/political mission.

Crucially, exposition is delivered through conflicts of interest:
- Macy wants help now; the Elders want procedure/control.
- Mel wants truth now; Jada controls information.
- Maggie's concern about Mel is simultaneously sisterly, social and strategic.
- Harry/Charity institutional knowledge is not neutral because relationships and past decisions contaminate it.

The late episode reveal does not arrive as encyclopedic explanation. Macy receives evidence through a dangerous procedure, which changes who she trusts and immediately produces lethal resistance.

#### Abstract mechanisms
**EXPOSITION NEEDS AN OWNER**
Every important fact should be controlled, resisted, misunderstood or strategically released by someone.

**THREADS CUT ON CHANGE**
Switch storylines when a thread gains a new objective, danger, fact or relationship state — not just because another character has been absent for three pages.

**INSTITUTION + RELATIONSHIP OVERLAP**
The same person can be mentor, authority, suspect, lover, sibling or ally. Those roles should interfere with procedure.

**INFORMATION REVEAL MUST CHANGE TRUST**
A reveal that only increases lore but changes no relationship/action is weak.

#### IVDIVO use
Book 4 crossover and Smith/Confederation scenes should avoid five experts explaining the world. Give each institution partial knowledge, a procedural objective and something it does not want disclosed.

Orbital Youth ensemble scenes should preserve unequal objectives. One character wants to leave, one wants status, one wants information, one wants to hide embarrassment. Do not give them equal explanatory turns.

### 3. SMALLVILLE 5x08 — “SOLITUDE”

Drive shelf: `06_SCREENPLAYS_TV_AUDIO/SMALLVILLE`
Folder ID: `1JI4bUJW130dLvvBm8nKE1HsqxoOqrHFh`
Draft: Goldenrod / production revision chain, 2005
Text status: FULL TEXT EXTRACTABLE
Rough scene slug count: 35

#### Observed construction
The teaser begins with ordinary adult/youth life: a new car, groceries, work/housing jokes, family affection. The speculative attack then strikes Martha inside that lived routine.

Act One does not immediately name the correct cause. Clark investigates an anomalous event through weather data, medical results, family knowledge and a suspect explanatory model. He is vulnerable because he already fears an old warning.

Professor Fine succeeds partly by exploiting that fear. Information is not neutral: he gives Clark a model that is emotionally convenient and operationally dangerous.

Parallel ordinary-life scenes — Lois at work, political campaigning, family conversation — continue while the main threat escalates. They are not random filler; they preserve relationships, status and ongoing consequences.

Act progression:
- Teaser: unexplained attack.
- Act One: visible physical evidence proves the anomaly is not over.
- Act Two: a false/hostile explanation predicts Martha's death, forcing urgency.
- Act Three: Clark commits to the wrong guide and enters the trap.
- Act Four: deception is exposed and immediate physical conflict resolves.
- Final scenes return to ordinary relationships and future consequence rather than ending on spectacle alone.

#### Abstract mechanisms
**EMOTIONAL PRIORS DISTORT HYPOTHESIS TESTING**
A protagonist chooses which explanation to believe partly because of fear, guilt, hope or prior conflict.

**ORDINARY THREADS KEEP THE WORLD ALIVE**
Jobs, cars, elections, family and friendship continue while the speculative plot advances.

**FALSE MODEL -> REAL ACTION -> PRICE**
A wrong hypothesis should produce a consequential action, not just a corrected conversation.

**AFTERMATH RETURNS TO RELATIONSHIPS**
After the action climax, give the story room to register what the event changed in trust, fear, family or future choice.

#### IVDIVO use
Strong Smith mechanism:
ANOMALY -> FIRST MODEL -> PARTIAL EVIDENCE -> EMOTIONAL/INSTITUTIONAL BIAS -> WRONG GUIDE OR WRONG INTERPRETATION -> ACTION -> TRAP/PRICE -> MODEL REVISION -> PROCEDURAL CLIMAX -> RELATIONAL AFTERMATH.

# PASS 12 — EXECUTABLE MECHANISM CARDS

## CARD 1 — ACT-OUT CHANGES THE OPERATING REALITY
Weak act-out: louder danger.
Strong act-out: the hero now has a different problem, model, relationship state or required action.

Test every major chapter/act ending:
WHAT NEW FACT OR CONSEQUENCE MAKES THE NEXT ACTION NECESSARY?

## CARD 2 — EXPOSITION NEEDS AN OWNER
For every nontrivial fact:
WHO KNOWS IT?
WHO WANTS IT KNOWN?
WHO WANTS IT HIDDEN?
WHO MISINTERPRETS IT?
WHAT DOES REVEALING IT COST?

If everyone can state the same clean explanation, the scene is probably dead.

## CARD 3 — THREADS CUT ON CHANGE
Multi-POV/ensemble cutting rule:
OBJECTIVE -> RESISTANCE -> TURN -> CUT.
Do not cut because the page quota for that character has expired.

## CARD 4 — ORDINARY LIFE SURVIVES THE SPECULATIVE PLOT
Every 3–5 high-concept scenes ask whether jobs, housing, money, family, friendship, romance, status or transport have materially changed.
If not, civilization is becoming a backdrop.

## CARD 5 — WRONG MODEL MUST CAUSE DAMAGE
A mistaken theory is dramatically valuable only if the protagonist acts on it and pays.

MODEL -> DECISION -> ACTION -> CONSEQUENCE -> REVISED MODEL.

## CARD 6 — DIALOGUE STATUS ASYMMETRY
In important group dialogue, assign each speaker:
OBJECTIVE / STATUS / WITHHELD INFO / TACTIC / EXIT CONDITION.

Ban default round-robin: five characters each give one correct answer.

## CARD 7 — AFTERMATH BEFORE SERIES HOOK
After the immediate climax:
RESULT -> RELATIONSHIP CONSEQUENCE -> ORDINARY-LIFE CONSEQUENCE -> PERMANENT CHANGE -> ONLY THEN SERIES HOOK.

# ADDITIONS TO IVDIVO STORY / SCENE GATE

Before GREEN:
1. Does each chapter/act end change the next required action?
2. Who owns every important piece of exposition?
3. Which important conversation has unequal information/status?
4. Are storyline cuts triggered by turns rather than equal airtime?
5. What ordinary-life system continues moving while the speculative conflict escalates?
6. Which wrong model causes a real action and price?
7. After climax, what relationship/ordinary-life consequence is shown before the series hook?
8. In ensemble dialogue, can any character's lines be reassigned to another without damage? If yes, voices/functions are insufficiently distinct.

# RED TEAM ADDITIONS

MAJOR if:
- lore is explained by a neutral spokesperson with no resistance;
- parallel threads do not causally change anything;
- chapter endings are only cliffhanger noise;
- protagonist's wrong theory has no cost;
- ordinary life disappears once the anomaly begins;
- climax ends the book immediately without relational/ordinary-life aftermath.

MEDIUM if:
- cuts happen by rotation rather than change;
- humor can be deleted without changing status/relationship;
- group dialogue distributes information too evenly.

# RESULT

Pass 12 converts screenplay craft into IVDIVO production rules without importing television pacing mechanically into novels.

The most useful synthesis is:
RELATIONSHIP / ORDINARY LIFE -> DISTURBANCE -> PARTIAL MODEL -> OWNED/CONTESTED INFORMATION -> ACTION -> ACT/CHAPTER TURN -> PARALLEL LIFE PRESSURE -> WRONG MODEL COST -> REVEAL THAT CHANGES TRUST -> CLIMAX -> RELATIONAL AFTERMATH -> SERIES HOOK.
