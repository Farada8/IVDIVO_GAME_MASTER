# EPISODE 114 — NO TRADE

Status: WORKING / TEXT COMPLETE / NOT FOUNDER-LOCKED

## SCENE 1 — HERITAGE GOVERNANCE ROOM — MORNING

[SFX: Glass door closes. Paper packets are placed on a table.]

GRACE KELLAN: The board authorized a narrower proposal.

MARA: Read it.

GRACE KELLAN: Heritage will file non-opposition to separate late registration, fund archive certification, maintain internal continuity across Vale-controlled banking, housing and medical systems during migration, and provide a limited correction reserve for third-party administrative costs.

EVELYN: Better.

MARA: In exchange?

GRACE KELLAN: Confidentiality for operational protection details not required by the court. A release of claims based on lawful implementation of the final correction order. And a waiver of claims asserting that the new birth record itself creates automatic entitlement to existing Vale trusts.

MARA: The last part is still broader than it needs to be.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: We do not want a civil-record correction to become an unexamined property ruling.

MARA: Neither do I.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Then why object?

MARA: Because your draft waives claims “arising from historical identity status,” not just claims created by the correction order.

GRACE KELLAN: That language protects the trust from retroactive theories.

MARA: It also protects Heritage from theories nobody has mapped yet.

EVELYN: Including mine?

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Potentially.

Evelyn sits back.

EVELYN: At least that is clear.

MARA: Separate the issues. I will stipulate that late registration alone does not award property. I will not sign away unknown historical claims as the price of having a birth record.

GRACE KELLAN: The board may withdraw the correction reserve.

MARA: Then let them.

## SCENE 2 — SAME — LATER

GRACE KELLAN: That reserve covers counsel, certified archives, agency fees and potentially lost work during migration. You are not refusing a symbolic benefit.

MARA: I know.

GRACE KELLAN: Your current client has already paused a significant part of your contract.

MARA: I know that too.

GRACE KELLAN: Rent?

MARA: Current.

GRACE KELLAN: For now.

MARA: Grace.

GRACE KELLAN: I am not threatening you. I am explaining why the board thinks the package is reasonable.

MARA: And I am explaining why bundling legal existence with a liability release is unacceptable.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Legal existence is not contingent on the release. Heritage has already stated it will obey the court.

MARA: Good. Then remove any language that makes your cooperation contingent on it.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Cooperation costs money.

MARA: So does litigation.

EVELYN: There we are.

MARA: What?

EVELYN: Two institutions discussing price without pretending price is morality.

GRACE KELLAN: Heritage will pay for what it is legally required to provide. Voluntary funding beyond that requires consideration.

MARA: Fine. I can accept less money.

GRACE KELLAN: Can you?

Mara does not answer immediately.

MARA: Yes. But I need a different strategy.

## SCENE 3 — CELESTE’S OFFICE — AFTERNOON

[SFX: Calculator keys. Printer.]

CELESTE: If Heritage withdraws the reserve, your likely short-term exposure is archive certification, filing costs, outside data-migration counsel and lost billable time.

MARA: Range.

CELESTE: Not a promise. Low five figures if the migration is boring. More if agencies fight.

MARA: My held work is twelve thousand eight hundred.

CELESTE: Which could return after the court order.

MARA: Or not.

CELESTE: Correct.

EVELYN: Could we seek costs later?

CELESTE: Maybe. Different proceeding. Different standard. Do not finance today with a future award.

MARA: What can we do now?

CELESTE: Ask the court to order custodians to provide the evidence necessary for correction at ordinary administrative cost. Use the public-interest legal clinic for the narrow records issue. Keep Heritage’s voluntary continuity guarantees if they are not tied to releases. Defer every property question.

MARA: And if Heritage refuses the guarantees without the waiver?

CELESTE: Then you ask the court for temporary continuity protection based on the standing finding.

MARA: That is slower.

CELESTE: Yes.

MARA: More expensive.

CELESTE: Probably.

MARA: Less compromising.

CELESTE: That is your judgment, not mine.

MARA: Draft it.

CELESTE: You are paying me to say that.

MARA: I am paying you to stop me when I confuse stubbornness with strategy.

CELESTE: Then this is strategy. Barely.

EVELYN: What is the worst version?

CELESTE: Mara refuses the package, the court grants the registration, and the migration costs more than expected while her work remains partially restricted. She could win the legal issue and still spend months financially weaker.

MARA: Put that in the decision memo.

EVELYN: Why?

MARA: Because if I regret this later, I want a record of what I knew now.

CELESTE: That is sensible.

MARA: Do not sound surprised.

CELESTE: I am surprised you are making a personal decision with the same documentation standard you use for clients.

MARA: That is not surprising.

CELESTE: The personal part is.

MARA: Fair.

EVELYN: There should be a bell every time you admit somebody else is right.

MARA: We would all go deaf.

## SCENE 4 — VALE BOARD VIDEO SESSION — EVENING

[SFX: Conference system tone.]

BOARD CHAIR: Ms. Quinn, we have your counterproposal.

MARA: Then you know I will not sign the historical-claims waiver.

BOARD CHAIR: We do.

MARA: I will agree to narrow operational confidentiality, protection of unrelated minors, phased correction notice, and an express statement that late registration itself does not determine trust entitlement.

BOARD CHAIR: You also request our internal continuity resolution without the release.

MARA: Yes.

BOARD CHAIR: Why should Heritage give that voluntarily?

MARA: Because internal continuity reduces your own error exposure. Freezing me in one Vale-controlled system while your records prove I am in court-supervised correction creates avoidable harm and avoidable liability.

GRACE KELLAN: That is accurate.

The chair glances at her.

BOARD CHAIR: You are advising the board to accept?

GRACE KELLAN: I am advising that the continuity resolution is operational risk control, not settlement consideration. The correction reserve is different.

BOARD CHAIR: Ms. Quinn, the reserve is withdrawn.

MARA: Understood.

BOARD CHAIR: You may reconsider before the final order.

MARA: If the terms change, I will reconsider the terms. I will not reconsider the principle because I get tired.

BOARD CHAIR: Fatigue changes many settlements.

MARA: Then document that I was tired when I said no.

GRACE KELLAN: It is being recorded.

MARA: Good.

BOARD CHAIR: The continuity resolution and archive cooperation are approved, subject to the confidentiality limits you proposed.

MARA: Thank you.

BOARD CHAIR: Do not mistake this for agreement on liability.

MARA: I do not.

EVELYN: Or property.

MARA: Evelyn.

EVELYN: Just helping.

BOARD CHAIR: I noticed.

## SCENE 5 — STREET OUTSIDE CELESTE’S OFFICE — NIGHT

[SFX: Light rain. Bus doors hiss.]

ADRIAN: Grace told me the board voted.

MARA: Of course she did.

ADRIAN: I did not vote. I did not lobby.

MARA: Good.

ADRIAN: You sound disappointed.

MARA: I am tired.

ADRIAN: Dinner?

MARA: Not tonight.

ADRIAN: All right.

MARA: Friday still exists.

ADRIAN: I know.

MARA: You are allowed to be pleased.

ADRIAN: Quietly?

MARA: Very.

He smiles.

ADRIAN: Did you get what you needed?

MARA: I got cooperation without buying it with my unknown claims. I lost the reserve.

ADRIAN: How much?

MARA: Enough to hurt.

ADRIAN: Do you want help?

MARA: No.

ADRIAN: All right.

MARA: Ask again after the order, if I am complaining about a filing fee instead of my existence.

ADRIAN: I can do that.

MARA: And do not quietly pay anything through a Vale account.

ADRIAN: I was not planning to.

MARA: Your face was.

ADRIAN: My face has no banking authority.

MARA: It has caused administrative problems before.

ADRIAN: That feels defamatory.

MARA: Sue me after Friday.

ADRIAN: There is the most optimistic thing you have said all week.

MARA: Do not make it romantic.

ADRIAN: Too late.

MARA: Adrian.

ADRIAN: Quietly romantic. I heard the instruction.

[SFX: Mara’s phone buzzes.]

CELESTE: Court accepted the remedy schedule. One more evidentiary issue before the final hearing.

MARA: Which one?

CELESTE: Helena’s last preserved decision packet. Heritage found the original office copy.

MARA: Authenticated?

CELESTE: Not yet.

MARA: Then we start there.

NARRATOR: Mara had refused the money and kept the cooperation.

The price did not disappear. It became hers to carry.

And the last unresolved question was no longer what Heritage would trade.

It was what Helena had chosen to preserve.
