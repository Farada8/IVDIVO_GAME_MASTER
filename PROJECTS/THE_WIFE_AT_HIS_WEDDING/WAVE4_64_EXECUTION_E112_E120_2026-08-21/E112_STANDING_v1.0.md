## SCENE 1 — FAMILY COURT — MORNING

[SFX: Courtroom murmur settles. A binder closes.]

NARRATOR: The judge had asked for a source graph. Heritage arrived with colored tabs and three people whose job descriptions sounded like objections. Mara arrived with one notebook and a rule she had written on the first page: do not use the answer to prove the answer.

JUDGE: We are not deciding the registration remedy today. We are deciding whether Ms. Quinn has shown enough non-circular continuity to ask for it.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Heritage does not dispute two live births. We dispute one transition in the chain.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Which one?

HERITAGE COUNSEL: The move from the protected infant designation B-Q-seven to the later juvenile subject number. If that bridge is derivative of a later Mara Quinn file, the chain loops.

MARA: Then test the bridge.

JUDGE: Ms. Quinn, let counsel do the speaking until you are asked.

MARA: Yes, Your Honor.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: First custodian.

PLACEMENT LIAISON: The pediatric transfer ledger assigns Infant B the temporary protection designation B-Q-seven. My office received that designation before any Quinn identity appears in our records.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: How do you know the later subject is the same child?

PLACEMENT LIAISON: We do not, from my ledger alone.

Mara’s pen stops for half a second.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Thank you.

PLACEMENT LIAISON: We know only that B-Q-seven entered our system alive and unnamed.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Which is exactly the ceiling of that record.

JUDGE: Continue.

## SCENE 2 — SAME — LATER

[SFX: Scanner tone. Paper is passed to the witness.]

ARCHIVE CUSTODIAN: This is the annual migration sheet from the protected-placement index. It maps B-Q-seven to juvenile subject J-14 during a system migration.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Created when?

ARCHIVE CUSTODIAN: Eleven years before the current litigation. Three years before the Quinn shell appears in our connected archive.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Could a later clerk have backfilled it?

ARCHIVE CUSTODIAN: The physical sheet is contemporaneous. The ink batch and paper stock are consistent with that year. More important, the same migration appears in a separate education-continuity roster held by a different custodian.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Different institution, or just a different room?

ARCHIVE CUSTODIAN: Different institution. Different retention system. Different staff.

MARA’S COUNSEL: May we call the education custodian?

JUDGE: Do it.

EDUCATION CUSTODIAN: Our roster shows J-14 receiving continuity services under a protected-child funding code. No civil name. The school-health fragment later converts J-14 to subject M-22 after another protection transfer.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: And M-22 eventually becomes Mara Quinn.

EDUCATION CUSTODIAN: Eventually. My record does not say when or why.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: So we still have another transition.

MARA: Good.

JUDGE: Ms. Quinn.

MARA: Sorry.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: She is right, though. We do.

NARRATOR: Heritage was not denying Mara. That would have been easier to fight. They were doing something more dangerous: asking each bridge to carry only its own weight.

## SCENE 3 — COURTHOUSE CORRIDOR — MIDDAY

[SFX: Vending machine hum. Coins drop.]

EVELYN: You said good in court again.

MARA: I was reprimanded.

EVELYN: I enjoyed it.

MARA: Naturally.

Evelyn hands her a paper cup.

MARA: What is this?

EVELYN: Tea.

MARA: From that machine?

EVELYN: Legally, probably.

MARA: I have a client call in six minutes.

EVELYN: The financing scope?

MARA: They extended the hold until tomorrow morning. If standing fails, the insurer will probably reassign it.

EVELYN: Do you want me to say that is unfair?

MARA: No.

EVELYN: Do you want me to say you will win?

MARA: Definitely not.

EVELYN: Excellent. I have nothing.

MARA: Stay anyway.

Evelyn leans against the wall beside her.

EVELYN: That I can do.

Mara checks her phone, then puts it away without opening the client email.

MARA: I keep wanting one document to end this.

EVELYN: Because you are tired.

MARA: Because one document would be efficient.

EVELYN: Same diagnosis, different vocabulary.

Mara drinks the terrible tea.

MARA: I hate this.

EVELYN: That is the first unqualified statement you have made all day.

MARA: Do not make it meaningful.

EVELYN: Too late. I am your sister. I make ordinary things inconvenient.

MARA: You are very good at it.

EVELYN: I also brought the lease letter.

MARA: Why?

EVELYN: Because your landlord sent the corrected wording. Tenancy active, identity review pending, no action required.

MARA: You opened my email?

EVELYN: You forwarded it to me at two in the morning with the subject line “if I forget.”

MARA: That sounds plausible.

EVELYN: You forgot.

MARA: Apparently.

EVELYN: One problem did not get worse while we were in court.

MARA: That is almost suspicious.

EVELYN: Take the win.

MARA: I am rationing them.

## SCENE 4 — FAMILY COURT — AFTERNOON

[SFX: Recorder resumes.]

FORENSIC TECH: Because the identifier transition from M-22 to the later subject-control number is recorded in the index independently of the name field, and the Hawthorne withdrawal record carries the same control number. Mara looks at Heritage counsel. For the first time that day, she does not look angry.

## SCENE 5 — RULING

JUDGE: The court is not being asked to create a person out of a theory. The record already contains evidence of a living second infant, a protected unnamed child, a juvenile continuity chain, a later protected teenage subject, and a current adult whose body maps to that subject.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Our objection is preserved.

JUDGE: It is. And it improved the record.

The judge looks at Mara.

JUDGE: Standing is established for purposes of seeking a late registration remedy. That is not a final finding on every requested correction, name issue, property consequence, or collateral record.

MARA: Understood.

JUDGE: I suspect you do.

EVELYN exhales beside her.

JUDGE: Next hearing: remedy structure. I do not want a proposal that solves one woman by rewriting the other. Bring me a design that treats the existing registered birth and the unregistered second birth as separate problems.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Yes, Your Honor.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Heritage will submit an alternative.

MARA: Of course you will.

The judge raises an eyebrow.

MARA: Sorry.

[SFX: Gavel tap. Chairs move.]

NARRATOR: Mara won the right to ask for a birth record. Nothing in the room changed her bank flag, her client deadline, or the name on the documents she already carried.

EVELYN: What happens tomorrow?

MARA: We stop trying to correct one record.

EVELYN: And?

MARA: We ask for two people to have two foundations.

NARRATOR: Standing had answered who was allowed to ask the question.

The harder argument began with what the answer was allowed to change.
