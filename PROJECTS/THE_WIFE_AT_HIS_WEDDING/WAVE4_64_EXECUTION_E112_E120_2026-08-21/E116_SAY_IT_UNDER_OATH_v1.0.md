# EPISODE 116 — SAY IT UNDER OATH

## SCENE 1 — FAMILY COURT — MORNING

[SFX: Recorder tone. Chair adjusts.]

CLERK: Raise your right hand.

MARA does.

CLERK: Do you swear or affirm that the testimony you give will be true to the best of your knowledge and belief?

MARA: I do.

MARA’S COUNSEL: State your current name.

MARA: Mara Quinn.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Is that the name on your original birth certificate?

MARA: There is no original birth certificate that identifies me as the second live birth.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Did the name Mara Quinn originate with you?

MARA: No. The Quinn source came from the compromised record of a deceased child, Mara Elise Quinn.

A rustle moves through the room.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Did Noah Quinn create that source identity for you?

MARA: No. The shell existed before his later involvement.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Did Noah later help keep it operational?

MARA: Yes. After he knew the provenance, he supplied a physical vaccination card connected to his sister’s record.

NOAH sits behind counsel, expression fixed.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Did you know the source was compromised when you first used the name?

MARA: No.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Did you later learn?

MARA: As a protected teenager, yes.

MARA’S COUNSEL: What did you do then?

MARA: The authenticated protected-file interview shows that the teenage subject using Mara Quinn was told the source came from a deceased child. She rejected another temporary name selected by the program and asked to continue using Mara Quinn until she could have a lawful separate identity.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Why do you say “the teenage subject” instead of “I”?

MARA: Because I do not remember every event in that record. The body mapping supports continuity between that subject and me. Memory does not fill the gaps.

## SCENE 2 — CROSS-EXAMINATION

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Ms. Quinn, you want the court to recognize Mara Quinn as your adult legal name.

MARA: Yes.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Even though you just testified that the name entered your life through theft from a dead child.

MARA: Yes.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Why not take the maternal surname from the late birth record and end the contamination?

MARA: Because provenance and present choice are different questions.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: That sounds rehearsed.

MARA: It is a distinction I have had to make repeatedly.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Answer without the distinction.

MARA pauses.

MARA: I have lived as Mara Quinn for most of my adult life. I built work, friendships, contracts, mistakes and ordinary habits under that name. I did not choose how it first reached me. I am choosing now whether to keep it.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Does keeping it honor the deceased Mara Elise Quinn?

MARA: I am not claiming that.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Does it dishonor her?

MARA: I cannot answer for her family.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Noah Quinn is here.

MARA: He does not own my adult choice. He does have an interest in how his sister’s source record is severed from mine.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: So you want the name but not the record.

MARA: I want the court to recognize my adult legal name and stop dependent systems from treating the deceased child’s record as my foundation.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Convenient.

MARA: Necessary.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: You understand some agencies will see a name order and assume the Quinn source was validated retroactively.

MARA: Then the order should say the opposite.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: You want a legal name with an attached warning label.

MARA: I want a legal name with accurate provenance. If a system needs a plain sentence, give it one: adult name recognized; deceased-child source record not foundational identity.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: And if that makes migration slower?

MARA: Then slower is the price of not lying.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: You seem comfortable assigning costs to institutions.

MARA: They have assigned some to me.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: That was almost a speech.

MARA: You asked a broad question.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Ms. Quinn, are you certain you were Baby B?

MARA: No.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Yet you ask for a birth record.

MARA: I ask the court to make a finding from cumulative evidence. I am not testifying to certainty I do not have.

The judge looks up.

JUDGE: Keep going.

MARA takes a breath and keeps both hands flat on the witness rail.

## SCENE 3 — HALLWAY — MIDDAY

[SFX: Door closes. Low courthouse traffic.]

NOAH: You did not need to say my part that plainly.

MARA: Yes, I did.

NOAH: I know.

MARA: Do not say that if you are angry.

NOAH: I am angry at myself, which is annoyingly compatible with knowing.

MARA: That sentence was terrible.

NOAH: Family resemblance.

MARA: We are not doing that.

NOAH: Right.

A beat.

NOAH: For what it is worth, I want the source severed too. My sister should not remain a hidden root under your passport, and you should not need her vaccination record to remain employed.

MARA: Put that in your own testimony if counsel asks.

NOAH: I will.

MARA’S phone vibrates.

CLIENT: We got the licensing-board letter.

MARA: And?

CLIENT: Insurer released half of the held financing scope pending the final order. Six thousand four hundred is back on the schedule.

MARA closes her eyes for a moment.

CLIENT: Mara?

MARA: I am here.

CLIENT: Is that good?

MARA: It is billable.

CLIENT: I was told that means yes.

MARA: It means I will be on site Wednesday if the court does not create a new disaster.

CLIENT: I will put Wednesday in pencil.

MARA: Wise.

NARRATOR: Half the work returned before the hearing was over. The other half remained behind a legal finding that did not exist yet.

## SCENE 4 — COURT — AFTERNOON

MARA’S COUNSEL: One final question. If the court recognizes the late second birth but refuses the name Mara Quinn, what will you do?

MARA: Comply with the birth finding and appeal or separately petition the name issue if permitted.

MARA’S COUNSEL: You would not reject the birth record?

MARA: No.

MARA’S COUNSEL: Why?

MARA: Because the fact of a second birth does not become false because I dislike the name entered on the first version of the correction.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: And if the court recognizes the name but denies any property consequence?

MARA: That is a different dispute.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: You refuse to waive it today.

MARA: I refuse to resolve unknown property claims as consideration for identity correction.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Could you later sue Heritage?

MARA: If counsel advises that a supported claim exists, yes.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Could Evelyn?

MARA: Ask Evelyn.

EVELYN looks down, almost smiling.

JUDGE: Sensible answer.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: No further questions.

JUDGE: Ms. Quinn, you may step down.

MARA: Thank you.

She stands, then stops.

MARA: Your Honor?

JUDGE: You are no longer under questioning.

MARA: Then never mind.

JUDGE: Sit down before you volunteer a closing argument.

MARA: Yes, Your Honor.

## SCENE 5 — COURTHOUSE CAFETERIA — LATE AFTERNOON

[SFX: Fork against plate. Espresso machine.]

EVELYN: What were you going to say?

MARA: Nothing useful.

EVELYN: That means something emotional.

MARA: I was going to say I am tired of proving I exist without overclaiming what existence means.

EVELYN: That is a closing argument.

MARA: I know.

EVELYN: Good thing the judge stopped you.

MARA: Yes.

EVELYN: My turn tomorrow.

MARA: You do not have to protect me.

EVELYN: I am not planning to.

MARA: Good.

EVELYN: Stop.

MARA: Sorry.

EVELYN: I am going to say what I did, what I knew, and what I was afraid of. Some of it will not help you.

MARA: Then say it.

EVELYN: You mean that?

MARA: Yes.

EVELYN: That is new.

MARA: So is being a person with standing and no final order.

EVELYN: Very glamorous.

NARRATOR: Mara had said the name under oath without cleaning its origin or surrendering it to that origin.

The court had heard her choice.

Next it would hear the sister who had spent years being treated as the only one.
