## SCENE 1 — VALE GOVERNANCE ARCHIVE — MORNING

[SFX: Archive cart. Cotton gloves. Recorder tone.]

ARCHIVIST: The packet came from the sealed governance series, box nineteen, folder six. Original paper, not a later scan.

MARA: Chain.

ARCHIVIST: Sealed by Helena Vale’s governance office, transferred to Heritage legal custody after her death, inventoried twice, never opened for this litigation until yesterday.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: We invited an outside examiner before unsealing.

INDEPENDENT EXAMINER: Paper, toner, office embossing and staple oxidation are consistent with the date. Handwritten margin marks match authenticated Helena samples at a level sufficient to attribute the annotations to her, with the usual limitation that handwriting comparison is not mind-reading.

EVELYN: Thank you for saying that before Mara did.

MARA: I was waiting.

ARCHIVIST: The packet contains the threat review, outside-counsel repair memorandum, Victoria’s objection, and Helena’s final governance instruction.

MARA: We have copies of three of those. What is new?

ARCHIVIST: The routing cover and an attached review note.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: The note was not in the litigation copy because it was filed under governance continuity, not identity correction.

MARA: Read only what is authenticated.

ARCHIVIST: “Second subject correction remains legally available. Immediate disclosure increases convergence risk while threat actor remains active. Do not register during active phase.”

EVELYN: That part is safety.

MARA: Continue.

ARCHIVIST: “After stabilization, reassess. Preserve one-public-heir structure pending trust review and succession exposure.”

The room changes.

EVELYN: That part is not.

## SCENE 2 — DOCUMENT TABLE — LATER

MARA: Correct.

EVELYN: It says reassess after stabilization.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Yes.

EVELYN: Did she?

ARCHIVIST: There is a later governance tick-box review six years after the threat period ended. The second-subject issue is listed as “defer — succession exposure unresolved.”

EVELYN: No safety language.

ARCHIVIST: Not in that line.

MARA: Any supporting memo?

ARCHIVIST: A trust-impact note. It says recognition of a second surviving child could create litigation over heir status, prior distributions and governance legitimacy.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: It does not say those claims would succeed.

MARA: Nobody said they would.

EVELYN: So Helena started with a real threat and kept the concealment after the threat changed because the structure had become useful.

VOSS: That is how I read it.

MARA: Your reading is not the finding.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Nor is ours. The court can find that governance considerations became part of the deferral without finding Helena personally intended every downstream consequence.

MARA: Correct.

EVELYN: I hate how careful that is.

HERITAGE COUNSEL: Careful is what keeps a dead woman from becoming whatever the living need her to be.

EVELYN looks at her.

EVELYN: I did not expect that from you.

MARA: Put the chronology beside the motives. Threat active. Threat reduced. Trust concern remains. Repair route known. No proof of Quinn source selection.

ARCHIVIST: I can prepare that table.

EVELYN: Progress.

EVELYN: Mara.

MARA: Sorry.

EVELYN: I was not asking Voss.

## SCENE 3 — ARCHIVE BREAK ROOM — MIDDAY

[SFX: Kettle. Old refrigerator hum.]

EVELYN: I need five minutes where this is not your case.

MARA: All right.

EVELYN: She raised me to believe I was the only child who could safely exist in public. I spent years thinking that meant she chose me.

MARA says nothing.

EVELYN: Then I learned there was another girl. Then I thought maybe she chose the system. Then I thought maybe she chose survival. Now I have a note saying she chose the trust structure too.

MARA: Yes.

EVELYN: I do not know which version hurts more.

MARA: You do not have to pick.

EVELYN: Stop being reasonable. It is unsettling.

MARA: I can leave.

EVELYN: No. Sit there and be my sister without fixing the sentence.

Mara sits.

EVELYN: Did she love me?

MARA: I cannot know.

EVELYN: I know.

MARA: Did she use you inside a structure that benefited from one public heir? The records support that.

EVELYN: I know.

MARA: Those two things can both be true.

EVELYN: That is worse.

MARA: Probably.

EVELYN: Thank you.

MARA: For what?

EVELYN: Not turning her into a mastermind. I wanted one for about thirty seconds.

MARA: It would be easier.

EVELYN: Yes.

They sit with the kettle clicking itself cold.

EVELYN: Lily has a parent meeting tonight.

MARA: Go.

EVELYN: She says I do not need to. It is about elective subjects next year.

MARA: That means she wants you to go.

EVELYN: How do you know?

MARA: Because teenagers say the opposite thing when the answer matters.

EVELYN: You have no children.

MARA: I have clients.

EVELYN: Not the same.

MARA: Often louder.

EVELYN: I am going.

MARA: Good.

EVELYN: Do not use that word like permission.

MARA: Fine.

EVELYN: Worse.

Mara almost smiles.

MARA: Go to the meeting. I will finish the source reconciliation with counsel.

EVELYN: That is not you taking over?

MARA: It is me doing my part while you do yours.

EVELYN: Better.
