THE WIFE AT HIS WEDDING
EPISODE 98 — NO MORE DIRECT SUBSTITUTIONS
Status: WORKING

SCENE 1 — ROUTE-CONTINUITY REVIEW — MORNING
[SFX: Digital timeline loads.]
FORENSIC TECH: We tested Victoria’s prohibition against preserved calendars, security routes, travel manifests, household entries and marriage-preparation records.
MARA: Result first.
FORENSIC TECH: After the greenhouse incident, current Mara’s historical subject does not appear in direct private Adrian routes. Current Evelyn’s historical subject does.
EVELYN: Engagement trip?
FORENSIC TECH: Evelyn.
ADRIAN: Private hospital visit before the engagement announcement?
FORENSIC TECH: Evelyn.
MARA: Marriage-license appointment?
FORENSIC TECH: Evelyn.
EVELYN: House staff onboarding?
FORENSIC TECH: Evelyn.
MARA: My routes?
FORENSIC TECH: Public gala visibility, medical-access cover, two controlled arrival decoys, one nursery-adjacent emergency task years later. No supported private marital substitution.
PRICE: Heritage wants the phrase “no supported” rather than “none occurred.” There are archive gaps.
MARA: Agreed.
EVELYN: I hate the gaps.
MARA: Gaps are not permission to invent the worst answer.
EVELYN: I am working on it.
ADRIAN: And this means the marriage memories I have after seventeen—
MARA: Belong to the person the records map there. Do not finish it as a romantic verdict.
ADRIAN: I was going to say they stop being a fog.
MARA: That lands differently.
PRICE: There is a separate privacy question. If the court already has enough to resolve direct-substitution scope, we should not unseal intimate household detail merely to make the emotional answer feel complete.
EVELYN: I support that.
MARA: So do I.
ADRIAN: Me too.
FORENSIC TECH: Then the report can rely on route authentication, access logs and event markers without narrative household content.
MARA: Do that.

SCENE 2 — MARA’S FORENSIC OFFICE — NOON
[SFX: File cabinet. Office phone.]
DIRECTOR: Compliance cleared your warehouse review.
MARA: Finally.
DIRECTOR: New external cases still require manual identity check.
MARA: How many did I lose?
DIRECTOR: Two inquiries went elsewhere. One client asked whether you are still licensed.
MARA: What did you say?
DIRECTOR: Yes, with court-documented identity review unrelated to competence.
MARA: Send me the written restriction.
DIRECTOR: You could issue a public statement.
MARA: About what?
DIRECTOR: “I remain fully licensed and—”
MARA: No slogan. If a client asks, give them the verification letter.
DIRECTOR: Reputation does not work that way.
MARA: It is how evidence works.
DIRECTOR: Reputation also pays rent.
[Beat.]
MARA: I hear you.
DIRECTOR: You can dislike that and still plan for it.
MARA: Fine. Put a neutral notice on my professional page: license active, administrative identity review pending, no effect on prior work validity.
DIRECTOR: You nearly wrote a statement.
MARA: Do not celebrate.
DIRECTOR: I would never.
NARRATOR: Mara could not keep ordinary life pure from the case. She could choose how much of herself became a press release. That was a smaller form of control and a more useful one.

SCENE 3 — EVELYN’S LAWYER’S OFFICE — AFTERNOON
[SFX: Legal pad. Door closes.]
EVELYN’S COUNSEL: Evelyn wants her marriage and Lily’s records formally severed from the Baby B identity proceeding except where necessary to answer a specific mapping question.
MARA: I support that.
EVELYN: You do?
MARA: Lily is not evidence. Your marriage is not a container for my birth record.
EVELYN: Yesterday you wanted the route history.
MARA: Because we needed to know whether my body was used in private Adrian paths. We have that answer to a defensible confidence level.
EVELYN: So you are done with my marriage file.
MARA: Unless a new contradiction directly requires it.
EVELYN: That sounded cold.
MARA: It was supposed to sound bounded.
EVELYN: I am trying not to hear “your life is irrelevant.”
MARA: Your life is relevant to you; I want it out of my case for exactly that reason.
[Silence.]
EVELYN: Closer.
EVELYN’S COUNSEL: There is one complication. The pre-wedding identity verification file is already in evidence from the earlier proceedings.
MARA: The thumbprint file.
EVELYN’S COUNSEL: Yes. It can now be read against the biometric cards.
EVELYN: I want that done.
MARA: Are you sure?
EVELYN: I want to know whether I was the legal bride. Not because I want Adrian back. Because I want one part of my adult life to stop being a question somebody else asks for me.
MARA: Then we do it.

SCENE 4 — HERITAGE CONFERENCE — LATE AFTERNOON
PRICE: Heritage supports severance of Lily’s records from general identity discovery.
MARA: Put that in the motion.
PRICE: We also support limiting marital evidence after the pre-wedding verification question is resolved.
EVELYN: You are agreeing with us too much.
PRICE: Do not worry. I still oppose public release of the Quinn source file.
EVELYN: Closer.
MARA: What do you want in exchange for severance?
PRICE: Nothing. Fewer contaminated proceedings help my institution too.
MARA: There it is.
PRICE: You keep acting surprised that self-interest and good procedure can overlap.
MARA: I am trying to recover from a system where self-interest wore the mask of care.
PRICE: Fair.
SHAW: Pre-wedding verification can be tested tomorrow. Asset One thumbprint, registrar attendance, ceremony security and travel continuity.
ADRIAN: Do I need to be there?
EVELYN: Not for the lab work.
ADRIAN: Thank you.
MARA: That was efficient.
EVELYN: I am learning to own my evidence.

SCENE 5 — EVENING SEQUENCE — MARA’S APARTMENT / COUNSEL / EVELYN’S CAR / MARA’S DESK
[SFX: Takeout carton closes.]
ADRIAN: I do not know how to feel about tomorrow.
MARA: You are allowed not to know.
ADRIAN: If it proves Evelyn was the bride, I lose nothing I did not already know intellectually.
MARA: But emotionally?
ADRIAN: Emotionally, it makes the years real again. The marriage was not an administrative mistake. It was mine. Hers. Then it ended in catastrophe and absence.
MARA: You are not asking me to fix that.
ADRIAN: No.
MARA: Stay there.
ADRIAN: And if the file somehow contradicts the mapping?
MARA: Then we follow the contradiction.
ADRIAN: Even if it hurts us.
MARA: Especially then.
[Beat.]
ADRIAN: Friday still happened. It was good.
MARA: Yes.
ADRIAN: You used present tense.
MARA: Do not become poetic.
ADRIAN: I was making a grammar observation.
MARA: Worse.
[SFX: Phone chime.]
MARA: Pre-wedding file is released for biometric comparison.
ADRIAN: Tomorrow.
MARA: Tomorrow.
NARRATOR: The new evidence did not erase the old marriage. It made it possible to stop using uncertainty as emotional currency. Mara had one teenage memory. Evelyn had a protected adult life that was increasingly her own to name. The next test would decide whether the woman the law married had been Evelyn all along.
[TRANSITION: Later, independent counsel call.]
COUNSEL: Severing Evelyn’s marriage and Lily’s records is strategically useful for you too.
MARA: Because it narrows the case.
COUNSEL: Because it prevents Heritage from arguing every uncertainty about your birth destabilizes every Vale family record.
MARA: Make the disclaimer explicit.
COUNSEL: There is a cost. Once Evelyn’s marriage is separated, you cannot use marital equities as leverage for your own identity remedy.
MARA: I was never entitled to them.
COUNSEL: I believe you. I need it stated because opposing counsel may assume claimants maximize leverage.
MARA: Put in our filing that I disclaim any request to obtain Evelyn’s marriage, maternity status or trust identity as a condition of recognition.
COUNSEL: That narrows the claim significantly.
MARA: It is an accurate one.
COUNSEL: Price may support it.
MARA: She will enjoy how much liability it removes.
COUNSEL: You can let a good procedural result have an annoying beneficiary.
MARA: Apparently that is the theme of the week.

[TRANSITION: Same night, Evelyn’s car.]
[SFX: Parked car. Turn signal ticking, then stops.]
EVELYN: I read the draft severance motion.
MARA: And?
EVELYN: You gave up any argument that my marriage should be transferred to you.
MARA: I never wanted it transferred.
EVELYN: Seeing it in writing still did something.
MARA: Good or bad?
EVELYN: Both. It made me trust you and mourn the version of events where one answer fixed everything.
MARA: That version was always a trap.
EVELYN: I still miss the version where one answer fixed everything.
MARA: You can miss it without choosing it.
EVELYN: Unfortunately, yes.

[TRANSITION: Before bed, Mara’s desk.]
NARRATOR: Mara signed the severance draft under her own name. It removed leverage she could have exploited and protected lives she was no longer willing to treat as evidence. The case became narrower. Her claim became cleaner. Neither made tomorrow easier.

END OF EPISODE
