THE WIFE AT HIS WEDDING
EPISODE 91 — TWO WRISTBANDS
Status: WORKING
Arc: THE SECOND BIRTH / THE GIRL WITHOUT A CERTIFICATE
Romance Weight: BALANCED

SCENE 1 — MARA’S APARTMENT — MORNING
[SFX: Radiator knocks. Kettle. Phone vibrates once.]

NARRATOR: Mara had slept in her own apartment for three nights. The radiator had developed two opinions about dawn. The court server had developed a third.
MARA: Shaw said authenticated copy, not interpretation.
EVELYN: I know.
MARA: You have said that twice.
EVELYN: Because you have said the rule three times.
MARA: Then we are both repetitive before coffee.
EVELYN: Progress. We used to be repetitive with lawyers present.
[SFX: Mug placed down.]
MARA: Breakfast was supposed to be ordinary.
EVELYN: It can still contain toast and evidence.
MARA: Ordinary breakfast was the deal.
EVELYN: I ordered eggs.
MARA: Eggs help.
NARRATOR: The maternity ledger sat unopened on Mara’s tablet. Evelyn had arrived nine minutes early and then waited in the hall because Mara had not answered the first knock. Neither mentioned that as progress.
EVELYN: Open it when you want.
MARA: I want to open it before somebody turns two wristbands into a theory about which of us deserves to exist.
EVELYN: That sentence does not sound like wanting.
MARA: No. It sounds like timing.
[SFX: Tablet unlock.]
MARA: Admission page. Mother’s name redacted under old protection order. Two neonatal identifiers. Baby A. Baby B.
EVELYN: Weights?
MARA: Different. Six pounds eleven. Six pounds four.
EVELYN: So not duplicate paperwork.
MARA: Not by itself. Same-day twins can be entered wrong. We stay inside the record.
EVELYN: Right.
MARA: Baby A: live birth. Nursery transfer. Discharge with mother. Baby B: live birth. Nursery transfer—
[Beat.]
EVELYN: Mara?
MARA: Separate discharge entry.
EVELYN: Alive?
MARA: It says live discharge. Different time. Different receiving code.
EVELYN: And the certificate?
MARA: One certificate number attached to Baby A.
[Silence.]
EVELYN: Two babies.
MARA: The ledger records two live births and two discharges. We do not yet know whether it is complete, corrected later, or wrong.
EVELYN: You always do that.
MARA: Do what?
EVELYN: Build a fence around the sentence before you let yourself stand inside it.
MARA: It keeps evidence from becoming family mythology.
EVELYN: It also keeps you from saying you are scared.
MARA: I am scared.
[Evelyn stops moving.]
EVELYN: All right.
MARA: Do not make it bigger because I said it.
EVELYN: I was going to ask if you wanted more coffee.
MARA: I do.

SCENE 2 — SPECIAL MASTER’S EVIDENCE ROOM — LATE MORNING
[SFX: Evidence seal photographed. Scanner hum.]
SHAW: Chain report first.
FORENSIC TECH: Hospital archive export was made under the unsealing order. Hash matches the archive image. Paper microfilm index independently lists two neonatal bands on the same admission.
MARA: Read identifiers, not conclusions.
FORENSIC TECH: Baby A band ends 4412. Baby B band ends 4413. Separate weights. Separate nursery medication logs. Separate live-discharge lines.
HERITAGE COUNSEL: Eleanor Price, for Heritage Trust. We object to any inference beyond authentication.
MARA: So do I.
PRICE: Opposing counsel does not usually begin by agreeing.
MARA: You are opposing release. I am opposing bad logic.
SHAW: Ms. Price, your objection to authentication?
PRICE: None. My objection is scope. These records were sealed because the original protective case involved minors, threats and identity compromise. If the court treats a neonatal ledger as public identity adjudication, it can expose people who never consented to reopening the file.
MARA: Which people?
PRICE: Some are living. Some are dead. Some are unrelated to you.
MARA: Then narrow the release. Do not erase the fact.
PRICE: I did not say erase.
MARA: Then keep that verb.
FORENSIC TECH: There is also a vital-record cross-reference.
SHAW: Read it.
FORENSIC TECH: Baby A links to certificate 89-14-771. Baby B field says “correction packet pending.”
MARA: Pending when?
FORENSIC TECH: Entered two days after discharge.
PRICE: That packet is not in the unsealed production.
MARA: Why?
PRICE: Because it was later associated with a protected juvenile case.
MARA: You know that from where?
PRICE: Heritage has the case index, not the packet contents.
MARA: Then produce the index entry under seal.
PRICE: I can seek authority.
MARA: Today.
PRICE: Ms. Quinn, I am not your staff.
MARA: No. You are counsel for the institution asking the court to keep a missing child’s civil record sealed. I am asking you to identify the legal basis before the next hearing.
PRICE: I can do that.
SHAW: Good. Noon filing.

SCENE 3 — VITAL RECORDS SERVICE COUNTER — AFTERNOON
[SFX: Ticket number chime. Printer.]
VITAL RECORDS OFFICER: I can confirm issuance history, not protected-case contents.
MARA: Enough for issuance history. Certificate 89-14-771.
OFFICER: Issued for one female child. No twin marker on the public certificate.
MARA: Was a second certificate ever issued under a different number that day?
OFFICER: Search by maternal admission shows one completed certificate.
EVELYN: Could Baby B have been registered later?
OFFICER: Possible. Different jurisdiction, delayed filing, adoption process, protected registration. I would need another identifier.
MARA: We have the correction-packet note.
OFFICER: Then there should be a workflow event.
[SFX: Keyboard.]
OFFICER: Yes. “Baby B correction packet created.” Then—one moment.
MARA: Take your time.
OFFICER: Packet status changes from pending to “removed to protected case.” No final issuance event.
EVELYN: Removed by whom?
OFFICER: The user field is not a person. It is a protected-case gateway.
MARA: Reference?
OFFICER: I can read the code. HRT-JV-204.
EVELYN: Heritage.
PRICE: Heritage is a later administrator. The code does not prove Heritage removed anything at birth.
MARA: She is right.
EVELYN: I hate when that happens.
PRICE: I am becoming used to it.
MARA: Do not.
OFFICER: There is one more thing. Baby B’s discharge record has a destination class.
MARA: Not a name?
OFFICER: No permanent name. “Protective transfer — juvenile placement intake.”
EVELYN: A baby left the hospital with no birth certificate and no civil name?
OFFICER: Those linked entries suggest exactly that. I would not call it final without the transfer ledger.
MARA: Neither will we.

SCENE 4 — STREET OUTSIDE VITAL RECORDS — LATE AFTERNOON
[SFX: Traffic. Crosswalk signal.]
ADRIAN: I came because Shaw said you might need a ride.
MARA: Did Shaw ask me?
ADRIAN: No. I texted you. You did not answer. So I stayed across the street.
MARA: Oddly specific restraint.
ADRIAN: I am learning in embarrassing increments.
EVELYN: I am taking the train.
MARA: Breakfast tomorrow?
EVELYN: Unless we discover a third newborn before nine.
MARA: Do not joke that into the universe.
[Evelyn leaves.]
ADRIAN: How bad?
MARA: Two live-birth bands. Two live discharges. One certificate. A Baby B correction packet existed and was removed into a protected case.
ADRIAN: What does that mean?
MARA: It means exactly that.
ADRIAN: Right.
MARA: Ask the next useful question.
ADRIAN: What are you doing?
MARA: Getting the transfer ledger before Price can make “protection” into a reason nobody is allowed to know whether Baby B had a legal life.
ADRIAN: Can she stop you?
MARA: She can slow access. She can ask other institutions not to rely on disputed identity material until the court decides scope.
ADRIAN: Your license.
MARA: My license. My lease verification. Bank compliance. Client portals.
ADRIAN: Do you want Vale counsel to—
MARA: No.
ADRIAN: I stopped before the verb.
MARA: You did.
[SFX: Phone notification.]
MARA: Price filed.
ADRIAN: Already?
MARA: “Temporary identity-integrity notice pending protected-record adjudication.”
ADRIAN: Is that legal?
MARA: Probably. It is also leverage.
ADRIAN: Against you?
MARA: She will say against bad records.
ADRIAN: And you?
MARA: I say both can be true.
[SFX: Another notification.]
MARA: Shaw got the protected index.
ADRIAN: What does it say?
MARA: “Baby B correction packet — transferred to juvenile protection file HRT-JV-204.”
ADRIAN: Anything else?
MARA: Yes.
[Beat.]
MARA: “Disposition: removed from ordinary vital-record workflow.”
NARRATOR: Two bands had become two discharges. The missing certificate was no longer an empty space. Someone had taken a correction packet out of the ordinary system and put it behind a protected door.

SCENE 5 — MARA’S APARTMENT LOBBY — EVENING
[SFX: Building intercom.]
LETTING AGENT: One administrative thing. The payment portal flagged the court notice, but your lease remains valid.
MARA: What do you need?
LETTING AGENT: The temporary living-claimant verification when it issues.
MARA: I will send it. Do not call Vale House for a guarantor.
LETTING AGENT: I was not planning to.
MARA: Sorry. Reflex.
LETTING AGENT: I noticed.

END OF EPISODE
