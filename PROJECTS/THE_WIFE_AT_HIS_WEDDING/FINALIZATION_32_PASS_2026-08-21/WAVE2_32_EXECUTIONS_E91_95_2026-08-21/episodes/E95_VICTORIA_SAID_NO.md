THE WIFE AT HIS WEDDING
EPISODE 95 — VICTORIA SAID NO

SCENE 1 — SEALED SOURCE REVIEW — MORNING
[SFX: Court audio channel opens. Paper sleeve lifted.]

NARRATOR: Victoria Arden had been dead long enough to become useful to everybody. That made Mara suspicious of every sentence left in Victoria’s voice.

SPECIAL MASTER: Limited disclosure remains in force. We are reading only material necessary to determine the Quinn-source decision and later subject choice.

MARA: Agreed.

FORENSIC TECH: First document: Victoria’s objection to active use of the deceased Mara Elise Quinn record. Authenticated.

MARA: We read that yesterday.

FORENSIC TECH: Today we have the response chain.

SPECIAL MASTER: Proceed.

FORENSIC TECH: Helena Vale’s risk office: “Objection acknowledged. Current threat environment does not permit open civil correction without exposing subject count and location. Temporary shell may remain operational pending separate-identity solution.”

EVELYN: Temporary.

MARA: Date.

FORENSIC TECH: Eleven years after birth.

MARA: Any deadline on “temporary”?

FORENSIC TECH: None.

SHAW: That matters.

MARA: It matters if later actors relied on it. Keep going.

FORENSIC TECH: Voss comment: “Legal repair can follow stabilization. Immediate priority is prevent convergence exposure.”

EVELYN: There is the whole disease.

MARA: It is the operating logic. Do not make it bigger than the words.

EVELYN: I am not in court.

MARA: We are on a court channel.

EVELYN: Technically irritating.

SPECIAL MASTER: Ms. Vale.

EVELYN: Sorry.

FORENSIC TECH: Victoria responds: “A temporary identity without a termination date becomes ownership. If the living subject is old enough to understand the risk, she must be told what name is being used and why.”

[Silence.]

MARA: That is a rule proposal. Not proof it happened.

FORENSIC TECH: Correct.

SCENE 2 — HERITAGE RECORDS CONFERENCE — LATE MORNING
[SFX: Conference speaker. Folder clips.]

GRACE KELLAN: Before you ask, Heritage is not taking a position on whether Victoria’s rule was morally sufficient.

MARA: What position are you taking?

GRACE KELLAN: That the current system cannot safely “correct” every downstream record the moment one historical source changes.

EVELYN: You keep returning to safety.

GRACE KELLAN: Because I have watched a bank freeze the wrong account and a hospital merge the wrong patient file. Administrative harm is still harm.

MARA: What is your proposed sequence?

GRACE KELLAN: Establish historical facts. Identify which current person maps to which protected subject. Preserve both current women’s access to money, housing, healthcare and parental records. Then make foundational corrections through one supervised order.

MARA: One supervised process is not the same as one identity.

GRACE KELLAN: Correct.

EVELYN: And if the supervised process decides one certificate is easier?

GRACE KELLAN: Then I expect you to fight it.

EVELYN: You would represent the institution on the other side.

GRACE KELLAN: Probably.

EVELYN: That is bizarrely honest.

GRACE KELLAN: It is cheaper than pretending.

MARA: What does Heritage want in exchange for cooperation?

GRACE KELLAN: At this stage? Redaction of unrelated protected people and notice before public release of operational source files.

MARA: No waiver.

GRACE KELLAN: Not in this request.

MARA: No confidentiality over facts already admitted in court.

GRACE KELLAN: Agreed.

MARA: No preapproval of our evidence requests.

GRACE KELLAN: Agreed.

EVELYN: I am waiting for the trap.

GRACE KELLAN: The trap is that if the evidence later threatens thousands of linked records, I will argue for the narrowest remedy that preserves continuity.

MARA: There it is.

GRACE KELLAN: I told you I would remain inconvenient.

NARRATOR: Kellan did not need to hate Mara to oppose her. She only needed to believe that a system’s cleanest repair could be more important than a person’s preferred truth.

SCENE 3 — VICTORIA INTERVIEW ARCHIVE — AFTERNOON
[SFX: Old recorder hiss. Authentication tone.]

FORENSIC TECH: This interview was recorded six years after Victoria’s first objection. Subject is a teenage protected girl using the name Mara Quinn. Face is not available in this copy. Voice and file chain are authenticated as the historical interview, but this recording alone does not map the teen to current Mara.

MARA: Good. Play only the name section.

VICTORIA, RECORDING: You know Quinn is not your birth name.

TEEN MARA, RECORDING: I know it belonged to a child who died.

VICTORIA, RECORDING: You know using it creates legal problems later.

TEEN MARA, RECORDING: Everything creates legal problems later.

VICTORIA, RECORDING: That is not an answer.

TEEN MARA, RECORDING: It is the answer I have.

VICTORIA, RECORDING: I can ask them to assign another temporary name.

TEEN MARA, RECORDING: Chosen by who?

VICTORIA, RECORDING: The protection office.

TEEN MARA, RECORDING: Then no.

[Brief tape hiss.]

VICTORIA, RECORDING: Why keep this one if you know where it came from?

TEEN MARA, RECORDING: Because it is the first name they gave me that does not sound like I am waiting to become someone else again.

[Silence in present room.]

EVELYN: She chose it.

MARA: The historical teen chose it.

EVELYN: Right.

MARA: We still need to map the teen.

EVELYN: I said right.

MARA: I heard you.

[Beat.]

VICTORIA, RECORDING: I will record that the choice is informed and that lawful separate identity remains required. This does not make the source theft acceptable.

TEEN MARA, RECORDING: Fine.

VICTORIA, RECORDING: You can change your mind later.

TEEN MARA, RECORDING: So can everyone else, apparently.

[SFX: Recording stops.]

SHAW: That line sounds familiar.

MARA: It can just be a familiar sentence.

SHAW: I was going to say “difficult.”

MARA: Fine.

SCENE 3B — COURTHOUSE STAIRWELL — LATER
[SFX: Heavy door closes. Footsteps stop on concrete.]

EVELYN: I want to file something today.

MARA: What?

EVELYN: A notice that there were two living children and the second record is unresolved. Not a name. Not a correction. Just stop every agency from acting like the single certificate is complete truth.

MARA: That could trigger exactly the downstream review Kellan warned about before we know which records attach to whom.

EVELYN: So we wait again.

MARA: We preserve first.

EVELYN: You have said “preserve first” for months.

MARA: Because evidence disappears when people act before they understand it.

EVELYN: People disappear too.

[Beat.]

MARA: I know.

EVELYN: Do you?

MARA: I spent five years as a person whose documents were more stable than her history. Yes.

EVELYN: Then why are you comfortable letting the old certificate sit there like it is the only birth that counts?

MARA: I am not comfortable.

EVELYN: You sound comfortable.

MARA: I sound controlled.

EVELYN: Same difference from here.

[Mara exhales.]

MARA: If we file your notice, I want language saying two live births are supported and current identity mapping remains unresolved. No implication that your certificate is invalid. No implication that I am automatically the missing registration.

EVELYN: I can live with that.

MARA: And independent counsel files it, not Vale, not Heritage.

EVELYN: Fine.

MARA: You wanted a fight.

EVELYN: I wanted movement.

MARA: Sometimes those look similar.

EVELYN: From here, so do control and fear.

[Mara is quiet.]

MARA: Also fair.

EVELYN: That one cost you.

MARA: Enjoy that quietly.

NARRATOR: It was their first disagreement about remedy instead of history. Evelyn wanted the public record to admit incompleteness now. Mara wanted every new sentence to survive the evidence that came next. Neither woman was wrong enough to make the argument easy.

SCENE 3C — MARA’S PHONE — CONTINUOUS
[SFX: Email notification.]

MARA: My licensing letter.

EVELYN: Good?

MARA: Credential remains active during identity review. They will not certify the disputed civil chain beyond continuity of practice.

EVELYN: Is that good?

MARA: It is accurate.

EVELYN: You really do use that word as comfort.

MARA: Sometimes accuracy is the only comfort that does not send an invoice later.

EVELYN: Client coming back?

MARA: Not yet.

EVELYN: I am sorry.

MARA: Me too.

[Mara locks the phone.]

MARA: Come on. Let us see what Victoria actually kept.

EVELYN: Two cards, you said.

MARA: Two cards. Separate sleeves. Separate questions. That is enough for today.

SCENE 4 — SOURCE GUARDIAN INTAKE BOX — LATE AFTERNOON
[SFX: Two rigid cards slide from archival sleeves.]

FORENSIC TECH: Victoria’s dual-subject intake. Two biometric cards, same intake date, separate subject numbers.

MARA: Labels.

FORENSIC TECH: Card one: protected subject “Mara Quinn.” Card two: protected subject “Evelyn.” Each contains thumbprint, palm edge, scar map and height.

EVELYN: Can they be compared to us?

FORENSIC TECH: Yes, with current consent and an independent examiner.

MARA: No comparison in this room.

SHAW: Why wait?

MARA: Because first we authenticate the cards as source objects. Then we decide what question a comparison can answer.

EVELYN: Body continuity.

MARA: Exactly.

EVELYN: Not birth certificate. Not marriage. Not motherhood.

MARA: Exactly.

EVELYN: You are rubbing off on me.

MARA: Seek treatment.

FORENSIC TECH: Card seals are intact. Repository hashes match the Source Guardian index.

SHAW: If the prints match—

MARA: Tomorrow.

SHAW: Right.

MARA: Today’s finding is enough. Victoria opposed using a dead child’s identity as a living container. Later she recorded that the protected teen understood the source and chose to keep the name anyway. Neither fact tells us yet which woman that teen became.

EVELYN: But the cards might.

MARA: The cards might map bodies.

[SFX: Two archival sleeves close.]

NARRATOR: For the first time, the final question was no longer hidden in a story somebody had told about the sisters. It was sitting on the table in two separate sleeves.
END OF EPISODE
