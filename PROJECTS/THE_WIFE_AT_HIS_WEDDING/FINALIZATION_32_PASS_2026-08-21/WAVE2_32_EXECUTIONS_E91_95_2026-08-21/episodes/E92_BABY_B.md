THE WIFE AT HIS WEDDING
EPISODE 92 — BABY B

SCENE 1 — STATE ARCHIVE RECEIVING ROOM — MORNING
[SFX: Metal cart wheels. Box latch. Paper seal photographed.]

NARRATOR: The Baby B packet had not been destroyed. It had been moved so many times that each institution could truthfully say it did not have the original problem.

ARCHIVIST: Box H-P-J four-four-seven. Juvenile transfer series. Court order permits inspection of records necessary to identify the civil-correction chain. Names of unrelated minors remain masked.

MARA: Good.

EVELYN: You always sound happier when somebody says what we cannot see.

MARA: Boundaries save time.

ARCHIVIST: Before I break the inner seal, I need both counsel acknowledgments.

SHAW: Filed.

EVELYN’S COUNSEL: Filed.

[SFX: Seal tears.]

ARCHIVIST: First folder is the Vital Records correction packet.

MARA: Page sequence before content.

FORENSIC TECH: Twelve pages, one missing attachment referenced as Form C, later copy inserted from microfilm. Seal history consistent with transfer log.

EVELYN: Missing attachment.

MARA: Referenced, not necessarily lost. Do not make a mystery before breakfast.

EVELYN: It is ten forty.

MARA: Still applies.

ARCHIVIST: The cover sheet says: “Second live-born female. Civil registration incomplete. Emergency pediatric transfer before registrar completion.”

MARA: What supports live-born?

FORENSIC TECH: The maternity source from yesterday, plus this packet references a pediatric receiving note and a six-hour observation entry.

EVELYN: So Baby B did not die after delivery.

MARA: If the receiving note authenticates.

ARCHIVIST: It is in the next folder.

SCENE 2 — ARCHIVE TABLE — LATER
[SFX: Microfilm reader starts. Paper sleeves turn.]

FORENSIC TECH: Pediatric receiving note. Time stamp matches the hospital transfer by twenty-seven minutes. Patient designation: “Baby B, female twin, live transfer, stable.”

EVELYN: Stable.

MARA: Keep going.

FORENSIC TECH: Weight matches within normal scale variance. Wristband serial four-two-two copied into the receiving line. Feeding and temperature checks continue for six hours.

MARA: Independent staff signatures?

FORENSIC TECH: Three. Two can be matched to employment records. One signature is unreadable but appears on other ward pages.

SHAW: Enough to establish continuity?

FORENSIC TECH: Strongly supports continuity from hospital Infant B to this protected pediatric placement. It does not map that infant to either current adult woman.

MARA: Good.

ARCHIVIST: Transfer ledger is after the observation note.

[SFX: Heavy ledger opens.]

ARCHIVIST: “Release from pediatric hold to protected placement liaison.” No permanent civil name. Temporary designation B-Q-seven.

EVELYN: Q.

MARA: A letter is not a surname yet.

EVELYN: I did not say Quinn.

MARA: Your face did.

EVELYN: My face is not evidence.

MARA: Improving.

ARCHIVIST: There is a placement authority code and a note: “Civil correction deferred pending threat review.”

SHAW: Who had authority to defer it?

ARCHIVIST: The ledger does not say.

MARA: Then we do not know.

EVELYN: Somebody chose it.

MARA: Somebody recorded that it was deferred. That is what we know today.

NARRATOR: Evelyn pushed back from the table. Not because Mara was wrong. Because sometimes precision sounded like the same old machine telling a person to wait until the paperwork was comfortable.

EVELYN: I need five minutes.

MARA: Take ten.

EVELYN: You are not coming?

MARA: No.

EVELYN: Good.

SCENE 2B — ARCHIVE COURTYARD — EARLY AFTERNOON
[SFX: Paper cup lid. Wind against stone. Distant traffic.]

EVELYN: I said I needed ten minutes and then spent eight of them trying to decide whether leaving the building would look dramatic.

MARA: Did you leave?

EVELYN: No.

MARA: Then the experiment failed.

EVELYN: I bought terrible tea.

MARA: Evidence of distress.

EVELYN: Do not.

MARA: Sorry.

[Beat.]

EVELYN: You keep correcting every sentence before it can become anything.

MARA: Today that is my job.

EVELYN: I know. I also know what it feels like from this side.

MARA: Say it.

EVELYN: Like if I say “they erased a baby,” you will tell me the record only shows delayed registration.

MARA: I might.

EVELYN: Exactly.

MARA: Because if we call it erasure before we prove intent, the first competent lawyer can make the whole thing sound emotional and careless.

EVELYN: I am emotional.

MARA: You are allowed.

EVELYN: You are too.

MARA: Less operationally useful.

EVELYN: That was a joke.

MARA: Weak one.

EVELYN: Still counts.

[SFX: Cup sets down.]

EVELYN: I am afraid the answer is going to be that one of us has always had the real life and the other one has been borrowing it.

MARA: I do not accept the premise.

EVELYN: That is not the same as not being afraid of it.

[Mara takes a breath.]

MARA: Fine. I am afraid the documents will make somebody offer us exactly that choice.

EVELYN: Better.

MARA: No scorecard.

EVELYN: I learned from you.

MARA: Then learn restraint.

EVELYN: I tried restraint for five years. It made me very well behaved and extremely dishonest.

MARA: Fair.

EVELYN: That sounded painful.

MARA: It was. Do not expect a second one before lunch.

EVELYN: I will ration my victories.

EVELYN: Impossible.

NARRATOR: They went back inside without solving anything. The useful change was smaller: Evelyn stopped asking Mara to make uncertainty painless, and Mara stopped pretending precision made her immune to it.

SCENE 3 — ARCHIVE CORRIDOR / RECORDS CONFERENCE CALL — MIDDAY
[SFX: Vending machine hum. Conference call connects.]

GRACE KELLAN: I reviewed the preservation order. Heritage will not oppose access to the placement chain that is within the court’s scope.

MARA: What will you oppose?

GRACE KELLAN: Public release of the juvenile placement file before unrelated protected identities are separated.

SHAW: Reasonable if the redactions are narrow.

GRACE KELLAN: Also, any immediate civil amendment based only on this packet.

EVELYN: There it is.

GRACE KELLAN: Ms. Vale, this packet establishes a second child remained alive after hospital discharge. It does not yet establish whether you or Ms. Quinn is that child, and it does not tell a registrar what to do with decades of later records.

EVELYN: So your solution is wait.

GRACE KELLAN: My solution is do not create a second error while correcting the first.

MARA: And Heritage’s exposure if a second live birth was knowingly left unregistered?

GRACE KELLAN: Potentially large. That is not a reason for me to give you a bad evidentiary answer.

MARA: Good.

GRACE KELLAN: You sound disappointed.

MARA: I prefer my opposition competent.

GRACE KELLAN: I will try to remain inconvenient.

EVELYN: She is going to fit in terribly.

[SFX: Call ends.]

SHAW: You realize she is going to use administrability against us later.

MARA: Probably.

SHAW: You do not sound worried.

MARA: I am. I am also listening.

SCENE 4 — PROTECTED PLACEMENT INDEX ROOM — AFTERNOON
[SFX: Card drawers. Scanner beeps.]

ARCHIVIST: The juvenile placement authority kept a separate index because the child had no permanent civil name. We can inspect references tied to B-Q-seven.

MARA: Read chronologically.

ARCHIVIST: First entry: pediatric transfer. Second: emergency placement. Third: threat-review renewal. Fourth: educational continuity referral.

EVELYN: Years?

ARCHIVIST: The sequence covers nine years.

MARA: That means the lack of civil name continued.

ARCHIVIST: Yes.

FORENSIC TECH: There is one cross-reference created later by Bellweather Protective Services.

MARA: Date.

FORENSIC TECH: Eleven years after birth.

EVELYN: Bellweather knew about Baby B.

MARA: Bellweather accessed the placement index. We need the access basis before we say what they knew.

FORENSIC TECH: Cross-reference field is partially redacted by the older protective order.

SHAW: Can the court lift it?

ARCHIVIST: Possibly. But there is an unredacted category label.

MARA: Category only.

ARCHIVIST: “Identity continuity resource.”

EVELYN: That sounds like a person turned into office supplies.

MARA: It sounds like Bellweather.

ARCHIVIST: There is another field.

MARA: Read it.

ARCHIVIST: “Source class: dormant identity.”

[Silence.]

SHAW: And the source name?

ARCHIVIST: The index does not give a full name. It gives a family tag.

MARA: Which is?

ARCHIVIST: Quinn.

[Beat.]

EVELYN: Noah.

MARA: No.

EVELYN: The name is Quinn.

MARA: The name existed in the file before we know Noah was involved. We authenticate first.

FORENSIC TECH: There is a linked case number from a separate sealed archive.

MARA: Label.

FORENSIC TECH: “DORMANT IDENTITY SOURCE — QUINN.”

[SFX: Scanner locks.]

NARRATOR: Baby B had left the hospital alive. She had entered protection without a permanent civil name. And years before Noah Quinn entered Mara’s known story, somebody had already attached his family name to the machinery waiting for her.
END OF EPISODE
