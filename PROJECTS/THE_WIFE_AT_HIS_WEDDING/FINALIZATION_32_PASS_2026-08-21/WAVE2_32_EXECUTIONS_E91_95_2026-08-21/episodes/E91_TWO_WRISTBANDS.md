THE WIFE AT HIS WEDDING
EPISODE 91 — TWO WRISTBANDS

SCENE 1 — SMALL CAFE — MORNING
[SFX: Cups. Street traffic. A spoon taps porcelain.]

NARRATOR: Mara arrived six minutes early because arriving exactly on time would have looked deliberate. Evelyn was already there, which meant either she had arrived ten minutes early or had been sitting in the car rehearsing how not to say anything first.

EVELYN: I ordered coffee. I did not order for you.

MARA: Congratulations.

EVELYN: I knew you would make that sound insulting.

MARA: It was mostly observational.

EVELYN: Mostly.

[SFX: Chair pulls out.]

MARA: You said breakfast. This is coffee.

EVELYN: I panicked at the menu.

MARA: About eggs?

EVELYN: About choosing something you would think was too familiar.

MARA: Eggs are not intimate.

EVELYN: Good to know.

[Beat.]

EVELYN: Did you read the maternity ledger?

MARA: No.

EVELYN: Shaw called me too.

MARA: I told him not to interpret it.

EVELYN: He did not. He sounded almost proud of himself.

MARA: Dangerous.

EVELYN: Do you want to read it here?

MARA: No. I want to finish the breakfast you have not ordered.

EVELYN: Right.

[SFX: Menu opens.]

EVELYN: I like the pancakes.

MARA: That was information.

EVELYN: I am practicing.

MARA: Keep going.

EVELYN: I am going to order the pancakes.

MARA: Excellent.

EVELYN: You are unbearable.

MARA: Also information.

NARRATOR: They ordered separately. Nobody asked what sisters were supposed to order when the court had just unsealed proof that their first day of life might have been recorded as one.

SCENE 2 — INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE INTAKE ROOM — LATE MORNING
[SFX: Security envelope seal breaks. Camera shutter. Scanner hum.]

SHAW: Authenticated copy from the court repository. Original remains sealed in controlled storage.

FORENSIC TECH: I am logging the image set, page sequence, repository hash and prior seal history before we read content.

MARA: Good. No annotations on the source image.

EVELYN: You say that like somebody might write on a birth record with a pen.

FORENSIC TECH: You would be surprised.

MARA: Please ignore her.

SHAW: Ready?

MARA: Read only what is on the page.

FORENSIC TECH: Maternity admission, twin delivery. Infant A: female. Birth weight six pounds, four ounces. Wristband serial ends four-one-seven. Infant B: female. Birth weight five pounds, eleven ounces. Wristband serial ends four-two-two.

EVELYN: Two serials.

MARA: Keep going.

FORENSIC TECH: Infant A post-delivery observation, pulse and temperature entries. Infant B post-delivery observation, pulse and temperature entries. Separate feeding notes.

SHAW: Separate live records.

MARA: They prove two infants were treated after delivery. They do not prove which adult became which infant.

FORENSIC TECH: Correct.

EVELYN: You did not need to say that for me.

MARA: I needed to say it for the room.

EVELYN: Fine.

FORENSIC TECH: Discharge section. Infant A discharged with maternal patient. Infant B discharged through protected pediatric transfer.

MARA: Date and time.

FORENSIC TECH: Same day. Infant B transfer logged forty-three minutes before maternal discharge.

EVELYN: Protected by whom?

FORENSIC TECH: Not on this page.

MARA: Certificate entries.

FORENSIC TECH: One civil certificate number appears in the maternity closeout. It is attached to Infant A.

[Silence.]

EVELYN: And B?

FORENSIC TECH: No certificate number in the closeout.

MARA: Do not call that no certificate yet. It is no certificate number on this record.

SHAW: Agreed.

EVELYN: Mara.

MARA: What?

EVELYN: Your hand.

MARA: What about it?

EVELYN: You are folding the napkin from breakfast into a square.

[Mara stops.]

MARA: I brought it by accident.

EVELYN: I know.

MARA: Leave the napkin alone.

EVELYN: I was not going to.

MARA: Good.

FORENSIC TECH: There is a footer entry. “Correction pending — Baby B.”

MARA: Image it separately. Hash the crop and the full page. Then preserve the repository access log from the date this was unsealed.

SHAW: Why the access log now?

MARA: Because the record just became useful to several people for several different reasons. I want to know who touches it after today.

SHAW: I can request preservation.

MARA: Request it before anyone asks why.

SCENE 3 — VITAL RECORDS SERVICE DESK — AFTERNOON
[SFX: Number ticket printer. Low public-office murmur.]

REGISTRAR CLERK: I can confirm the court order allows inspection of the historic index, not amendment.

MARA: We are not requesting amendment.

REGISTRAR CLERK: Good.

EVELYN: You sound relieved.

REGISTRAR CLERK: People arrive here after a court hearing and assume every database changes before lunch.

MARA: We only want the index history attached to “correction pending — Baby B.”

REGISTRAR CLERK: That entry is old enough to be in the paper-to-digital migration layer. I can search the reference field.

[SFX: Keyboard.]

REGISTRAR CLERK: There is a protected-case code.

MARA: Read the code, not the protected content.

REGISTRAR CLERK: H-P-J four-four-seven. Juvenile protected file. The correction packet itself is not in vital records.

EVELYN: Removed?

REGISTRAR CLERK: Transferred. That is what the index says.

MARA: When?

REGISTRAR CLERK: Seven years after the birth.

MARA: Destination?

REGISTRAR CLERK: Restricted.

[SFX: Phone buzzes.]

SHAW: Mara, Heritage counsel is asking the court to route all identity-record requests through one coordinated channel.

MARA: Why?

SHAW: They say multiple agencies acting independently could create inconsistent corrections.

REGISTRAR CLERK: They are not wrong about that risk.

EVELYN: That is how it starts.

MARA: Not necessarily.

EVELYN: One channel. One clean version.

MARA: Or one way to keep five databases from contradicting each other. We ask what control they want, not what phrase scares us.

EVELYN: You are defending them?

MARA: No. I am refusing to save time by guessing motive.

[Beat.]

MARA: Shaw, tell Heritage we will accept a shared preservation index. We will not accept exclusive access or preclearance of our requests.

SHAW: I will put it in writing.

EVELYN: That sounded like a compromise.

MARA: It was.

EVELYN: I dislike it.

MARA: Also information.

SCENE 3B — HALLWAY OUTSIDE VITAL RECORDS — CONTINUOUS
[SFX: Public-office door closes. Footsteps.]

GRACE KELLAN: Ms. Quinn.

MARA: You are Heritage counsel.

GRACE KELLAN: Grace Kellan. Records and legacy-risk counsel, not litigation on the fire file.

EVELYN: Different file. Same building.

GRACE KELLAN: Different authority too. I asked for coordinated routing because if Vital Records, licensing, a bank and a family court all react to partial facts in different orders, both of you can be harmed before anyone knows what the final correction should be.

MARA: What do you want controlled?

GRACE KELLAN: Distribution of unverified identity conclusions. Not your access to evidence.

MARA: Put that distinction in the proposed order.

GRACE KELLAN: I will.

EVELYN: And if your draft says Heritage reviews our requests first?

GRACE KELLAN: Then strike it.

[Evelyn pauses.]

MARA: That was faster than expected.

GRACE KELLAN: I am not asking you to trust Heritage. I am asking you not to let five institutions invent five versions of you while this is unresolved.

MARA: Shared index. Independent access. No exclusive gatekeeper. No correction without notice to both women.

GRACE KELLAN: That is administrable.

EVELYN: I hate that word.

GRACE KELLAN: Most people do until they need something to work twice.

MARA: Send the draft to Shaw and both independent counsel teams.

GRACE KELLAN: Today.

NARRATOR: Kellan did not threaten them. That made her more useful as opposition, not less. A system could protect people and still prefer a solution clean enough to manage.

SCENE 4 — MARA’S APARTMENT — EVENING
[SFX: Radiator knocks twice. Laptop opens.]

NARRATOR: The apartment had acquired a kettle, two plates and one plant that Lily had already named without permission. Mara had acquired exactly one new rule: evidence did not get to colonize every room.

[SFX: Phone rings.]

MARA: Shaw.

SHAW: Preservation order is entered. Repository access logs are frozen as of this afternoon.

MARA: Good.

SHAW: Vital Records located the transfer notation for the Baby B correction packet.

MARA: Not the packet itself?

SHAW: Not yet.

MARA: Read the notation.

SHAW: “Unfiled correction packet removed from civil intake. Protected-case reference H-P-J four-four-seven. Forward to juvenile placement authority.”

MARA: That tells us there was an attempted correction.

SHAW: Yes.

MARA: It does not tell us it was accurate.

SHAW: Correct.

MARA: It does not tell us which woman was Baby B.

SHAW: Correct.

MARA: It does tell us somebody carried the problem out of Vital Records instead of resolving it there.

SHAW: Yes.

[Beat.]

MARA: Get the transfer ledger before anyone gives me a theory.

SHAW: Already requested.

MARA: Good.

SHAW: Mara?

MARA: What?

SHAW: The protected-case reference has an index label.

MARA: If it is interpretive, do not read it.

SHAW: It is administrative.

MARA: Read it.

SHAW: “Baby B correction packet — transferred, no permanent civil record assigned.”

[Silence.]

MARA: Tomorrow we find out where the baby went.

NARRATOR: Two wristbands did not tell Mara who she was. They did something less comforting and more useful. They proved the record had once known there were two.
END OF EPISODE
