### SCENE 1 — MARA'S FLAT / HOME OFFICE — EARLY MORNING

[SFX: Kettle clicks off. Laptop notification. Printer feeds one page.]

NARRATOR: The form asked for one number Mara did not have.

MARA: Original birth registration number.

[She types. Stops. Deletes.]

MARA: Not “unknown.” Missing.

[SFX: Phone vibrates.]

CLIENT: Morning. I got a notice from compliance saying your professional credential is under manual review.

MARA: It is.

CLIENT: Does that stop the site package?

MARA: It stops me from certifying the final submission until they tell us otherwise. It does not stop design work.

CLIENT: Can someone else sign?

MARA: My colleague Lena can sign within her scope. I will send a responsibility split before nine.

CLIENT: Is this fraud trouble?

[Mara is silent for half a second.]

MARA: No allegation of fraud has been made. My foundational civil record is being reviewed because historical evidence does not fit the current registration chain.

CLIENT: I understood perhaps six words of that.

MARA: My paperwork is complicated. I am still me. The office is checking how to document that.

CLIENT: All right. Send the split.

MARA: In writing.

CLIENT: I knew you were going to say that.

[Call ends.]

NARRATOR: Mara stared at the form again. It had boxes for married name, previous name, lost certificate, amended certificate, overseas certificate. It did not have a box for a living woman whose second birth had never received its own original number.

[SFX: Knock at door.]

EVELYN: I brought coffee.

MARA: I have coffee.

EVELYN: You have hot brown punishment.

MARA: That is coffee.

EVELYN: Your printer looks frightened.

MARA: It should be.

EVELYN: What does the form want?

MARA: A number.

EVELYN: Which one?

MARA: The one the whole problem is that I never had.

### SCENE 2 — PROFESSIONAL CREDENTIAL REVIEW OFFICE — LATE MORNING

[SFX: Public-office murmur. Ticket printer. Door opens.]

REVIEW OFFICER: Ms. Quinn?

MARA: That is the name I use, yes.

REVIEW OFFICER: Before we start, this is a record-consistency review. It is not a fraud interview and we are not making a finding about criminal conduct.

[Mara's shoulders drop slightly.]

MARA: Thank you for saying that first.

REVIEW OFFICER: Your current credential was issued from documents that passed the checks available at the time. New material now indicates the foundational chain may be incomplete.

MARA: Incomplete is accurate.

REVIEW OFFICER: You submitted the Vital Records response, historical subject records, current biometric comparison report, and the statement explaining the Mara Quinn chain.

MARA: Yes.

REVIEW OFFICER: The difficulty is that our system expects one foundational civil event to anchor later names.

MARA: Mine expects me to have had one too.

REVIEW OFFICER: I cannot decide a late registration question. That is outside this office.

MARA: What can you decide?

REVIEW OFFICER: Whether your professional file can remain active while the civil-status question is unresolved, and what work requires a second signatory during review.

EVELYN: She can keep working?

REVIEW OFFICER: Some work, likely. I need to finish the file assessment before I say exactly what.

MARA: No promises before evidence. I approve.

REVIEW OFFICER: You are making this easier than most people do.

MARA: I have had practice being inconvenient to databases.

[Paper slides across desk.]

REVIEW OFFICER: This supplemental statement has an optional field for “reason foundational number unavailable.”

MARA: Optional?

REVIEW OFFICER: The software says optional. For your file, I would explain it.

MARA: The software and I are already enemies.

EVELYN: She has several.

MARA: Do not help.

REVIEW OFFICER: Use plain facts. Do not try to solve the legal question in this form.

MARA: “Contemporaneous evidence supports a second live birth. No separate original civil registration number has yet been located or established. Current identity continuity is under formal clarification.”

REVIEW OFFICER: That works as a factual statement.

MARA: It sounds like a person fell out of a filing cabinet.

REVIEW OFFICER: Government forms have limited poetry.

MARA: Tragic.

REVIEW OFFICER: One more thing. Do not surrender your current credential card today. The review notice did not cancel it.

MARA: The client thought it had.

REVIEW OFFICER: Then give them this status letter. It says active under review, with final certification restricted pending assessment.

MARA: Restricted is expensive.

REVIEW OFFICER: I know.

MARA: No. You know the word. I know which invoice it touches.

[Pause.]

REVIEW OFFICER: Fair correction.

MARA: Sorry.

REVIEW OFFICER: You do not need to be.

MARA: I do when I turn every sentence into a deposition.

EVELYN: Progress.

[Mara gives her a look.]

### SCENE 3 — ACTIVE DESIGN SITE — AFTERNOON

[SFX: Wind against temporary fencing. Distant drill. Tablet unlock tone.]

LENA: I got your responsibility split.

MARA: Any problem with it?

LENA: Only that you wrote three pages to explain a one-page handoff.

MARA: Two and a half.

LENA: I rounded for emotional accuracy.

CLIENT: Can we still issue the package Friday?

LENA: If I take final certification and Mara stays on design coordination, yes.

CLIENT: And the authority accepts that?

MARA: The status letter permits work within the review conditions. Lena is not lending me her signature. She owns the certification decision.

LENA: Including the right to reject your details.

MARA: Especially that.

CLIENT: This costs us another review cycle.

MARA: I know.

CLIENT: I am not trying to punish you. I need a number for the variation.

MARA: Send the actual extra hours. I will absorb the first two from my fee. Anything beyond that we discuss before it happens.

LENA: You do not have to eat all of it.

MARA: I said two hours, not my organs.

CLIENT: Fine.

CLIENT: One practical question. If the review clears next week, do we switch certification back to you?

MARA: Only after the office confirms it in writing. Until then, Lena keeps the role.

LENA: I enjoy power.

MARA: You enjoy red pens.

LENA: Same profession, different branding.

[SFX: Tablet stylus.]

NARRATOR: Nobody fired her. Nobody accused her of being an impostor. The consequence was smaller and therefore more believable: another reviewer, another delay, two hours she could not invoice, and one signature she was temporarily not allowed to place.

LENA: Come look at the north elevation.

MARA: Why?

LENA: Because your window line is wrong.

MARA: Impossible.

LENA: Excellent. You are cured.

[Footsteps on gravel.]

MARA: It is not wrong.

LENA: The steel moved forty millimeters.

MARA: Then the building is wrong.

LENA: Tell it.

MARA: I intend to.

NARRATOR: For eleven minutes, Mara argued with steel, glass and a tape measure. It was the best part of her day.

### SCENE 4 — STREET OUTSIDE THE SITE — EVENING

[SFX: Traffic. Pedestrian signal. Paper envelope opened.]

EVELYN: You look less furious.

MARA: The building gave me something honest to fight.

EVELYN: How much did today cost?

MARA: Two unbilled hours. Maybe more later. A delayed signature. Nothing catastrophic.

EVELYN: But real.

EVELYN: Heritage asked for my ceremony packet this morning. I refused blanket access. If they want it for your file, they can identify the exact purpose and ask me directly.

MARA: You did not have to do that.

EVELYN: I did it for my record. You are simply benefiting.

MARA: Yes.

ADRIAN: Shaw sent the civil-status options memo.

MARA: Options or law?

ADRIAN: Options. He put “jurisdiction-specific counsel required” on every page because he is afraid of you now.

MARA: Healthy.

EVELYN: What are the options?

ADRIAN: Late foundational registration, declaratory identity proceeding, administrative correction route, or a staged combination. None is confirmed yet.

MARA: Then none is the answer.

ADRIAN: Right.

[Mara opens another envelope.]

EVELYN: What is that?

MARA: My bank.

ADRIAN: Please tell me they are not closing your account.

MARA: They are not.

EVELYN: You sounded disappointed by the lack of disaster.

MARA: They want an updated identity document at the next scheduled business-account review. Sixty days.

ADRIAN: Can the current documents satisfy it?

MARA: Maybe. The letter says manual review if the foundational discrepancy remains open.

EVELYN: Another form.

MARA: Another box.

[Pedestrian signal changes.]

ADRIAN: What do you want us to do tonight?

MARA: Nothing.

EVELYN: That is suspicious.

MARA: I want takeaway. I want to send Lena the corrected elevation. Then I want one hour in which nobody asks what my original number was.

ADRIAN: Done.

MARA: You cannot make other people stop asking.

ADRIAN: I can stop asking.

[Mara looks at him.]

MARA: That counts.

NARRATOR: She had spent weeks believing the final answer would be a perfect reconstruction of the past. Now the past was becoming almost painfully clear. The problem had moved.

NARRATOR: Mara knew who she had been. The next fight was whether ordinary systems could recognize who she was without forcing her to disappear into someone else's record.

MARA: Tomorrow we deal with the life I already have.
