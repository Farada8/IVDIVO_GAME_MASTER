# EPISODE 101 — THE PERSON WITH NO ORIGINAL NUMBER

## SCENE 1 — REGISTRAR CONFERENCE ROOM — MORNING

[SFX: Ventilation. Paper notice placed on table.]

NARRATOR: Mara had spent half her adult life correcting other people’s assumptions. The notice in front of her was the first official document she had seen that seemed determined not to assume anything at all.

REGISTRAR: I want to be clear about the administrative posture. Your current documents remain usable unless a specific agency has a lawful reason to suspend reliance. This office is not declaring you fraudulent or nonexistent.

MARA: You are declaring the foundational record unresolved.

REGISTRAR: Correct.

MARA: What can I do today?

REGISTRAR: Request protective continuity letters for essential functions. Ask the court for a supervised identity-remedy proceeding. Preserve the current record while the birth chain is litigated. Do not make voluntary amendments across multiple systems independently.

EVELYN: Because five agencies could create five versions of her.

REGISTRAR: Exactly.

MARA: Grace Kellan said the same thing.

EVELYN: I still hate it when Heritage is right about something.

REGISTRAR: Competent institutions are often inconvenient in more than one direction.

MARA: What can this office not do?

REGISTRAR: I cannot backfill an original certificate simply because current Mara maps to historical Mara Quinn. I cannot declare you Baby B without the birth-to-protected-placement chain. I cannot decide which name a late registration should carry without the legal basis for that name.

MARA: Good.

EVELYN: We are going to have to retire that word.

MARA: Not today.

## SCENE 2 — PROFESSIONAL LICENSING BOARD VIDEO CALL — LATE MORNING

[SFX: Laptop chime.]

LICENSING OFFICER: Ms. Quinn, your credential remains active. The new Vital Records notice means we need a continuity packet before renewal or any new high-reliance certification.

MARA: Define continuity packet.

LICENSING OFFICER: Prior credential file, tax registration history, professional insurance, court docket, and a statement from Vital Records that the issue is foundational identity review rather than a finding of professional fraud.

MARA: I can provide all of that today.

LICENSING OFFICER: Good.

MARA: What happens to current clients?

LICENSING OFFICER: We are not ordering them to stop using you.

MARA: That is not the same as saying insurers will accept the risk.

LICENSING OFFICER: No. I cannot promise that.

MARA: Then do not soften it. My credential is active. Commercial reliance may still narrow.

LICENSING OFFICER: That is accurate.

MARA: Send me that sentence in writing.

LICENSING OFFICER: You really like written sentences.

MARA: Spoken ones mutate.

NARRATOR: The officer smiled. Mara did not. She had already lost half a workstream to somebody else’s uncertainty. Accuracy was not comfort anymore. It was inventory.

## SCENE 3 — CLIENT SITE — AFTERNOON

[SFX: Warehouse echo. Tape measure. Forklift reversing in distance.]

CLIENT: I did not expect you to come in person.

MARA: Your insurer paused half the scope, not my legs.

CLIENT: The structural inventory still needs reconciliation.

MARA: Then show me the discrepancy.

NARRATOR: Mara spent forty minutes on a loading dock checking serial plates against a disputed asset schedule. No sealed identities. No dead children. Just six machines, one missing lease, and a supervisor who had copied the same number twice.

CLIENT: Found it?

MARA: Two forklifts were entered under one serial suffix. Your insurer’s concern is real. This one is unrelated.

CLIENT: I can authorize the unaffected half of the engagement.

MARA: In writing.

CLIENT: Of course.

MARA: And do not describe the other half as suspended by my license. My license is active.

CLIENT: Understood.

MARA: It is paused by your insurer pending identity review.

CLIENT: You want the distinction on the invoice too?

MARA: Especially on the invoice.

NARRATOR: The work did not restore her life. It did something better for one afternoon. It required her competence instead of her biography.

## SCENE 4 — MARA’S APARTMENT — EVENING

[SFX: Kettle. Laptop keys. Envelope through mail slot.]

MARA: That is fast.

EVELYN: What?

MARA: Licensing continuity request. They want certified copies within ten business days.

EVELYN: Can you get them?

MARA: Most of them.

EVELYN: Most?

MARA: Tax history is easy. Insurance is easy. Prior credential file is easy. Current government identification is the disputed chain.

EVELYN: Circular.

MARA: Yes.

EVELYN: I learned a useful word.

MARA: Do not get attached.

[SFX: Phone rings.]

GRACE KELLAN: Mara. Heritage counsel received notice of the supervised identity-remedy petition.

MARA: Through what authority?

GRACE KELLAN: We are an interested records custodian and potential liability party. I am calling before somebody sends you a proposal that looks more hostile than it is.

MARA: That is a promising beginning.

GRACE KELLAN: Heritage can provide administrative support, archive certification, funded independent counsel for the registration process, and interim guarantees against internal Vale-system record freezes.

EVELYN: In exchange for what?

GRACE KELLAN: There will be terms. I am not asking you to accept them on a phone call.

MARA: Send the full draft. No summary.

GRACE KELLAN: I expected that.

MARA: Then save us both time.

NARRATOR: The registrar had given Mara a name for her problem: living person, defective foundation. Heritage had apparently found a price for making the defect easier to live with.

NARRATOR: The proposal arrived eleven minutes later.

END OF EPISODE

# EPISODE 102 — RENT, LICENSE, NAME

## SCENE 1 — MARA’S APARTMENT — MORNING

[SFX: Laptop notification. Radiator knock.]

NARRATOR: Mara made a list before breakfast. Rent. License. Bank. Client. She left “identity” off it because identity was not an errand.

LANDLORD: I am not trying to evict you.

MARA: Good start.

LANDLORD: The building insurer asked whether the name on your lease is subject to a court dispute.

MARA: The foundational record is under review. The leaseholder is not disputed as a living occupant.

LANDLORD: I do not know how to put that in the portal.

MARA: Put “court-supervised identity continuity review; tenancy remains active.”

LANDLORD: That sounds like lawyer language.

MARA: It is shorter than losing the rent.

LANDLORD: Fair.

[SFX: Call ends. Another notification.]

BANK OFFICER: Your account is not frozen. We have added a manual review flag for changes to legal name or tax identity.

MARA: Can I pay bills?

BANK OFFICER: Yes.

MARA: Receive client transfers?

BANK OFFICER: Yes.

MARA: Travel internationally using the current passport?

BANK OFFICER: That is outside my authority.

MARA: Good answer.

BANK OFFICER: I do not hear that often.

MARA: You probably hear more expensive answers.

## SCENE 2 — STREET OUTSIDE LICENSING OFFICE — LATE MORNING

[SFX: Wind. Traffic crossing signal.]

ADRIAN: I brought coffee.

MARA: Why?

ADRIAN: Because you are standing outside a licensing office at eleven in the morning and your texts have become punctuation-only.

MARA: That is not evidence of thirst.

ADRIAN: It is evidence that I bought too much coffee.

[Mara takes one.]

ADRIAN: Do you want me to call the Vale bank contact about the review flag?

MARA: No.

ADRIAN: All right.

MARA: That was fast.

ADRIAN: You said no.

MARA: I am noticing progress.

ADRIAN: Dangerous.

[Mara sips.]

ADRIAN: Do you want me to wait here?

MARA: Also no.

ADRIAN: All right.

MARA: You can walk one block with me.

ADRIAN: I can do one block.

NARRATOR: He did not turn one block into lunch, a car, a lawyer, or a rescue. Mara noticed that too and hated that she was grateful for something so basic.

## SCENE 3 — CLIENT VIDEO CALL — AFTERNOON

CLIENT: The insurer reviewed the continuity letter.

MARA: And?

CLIENT: They are allowing the field work already restarted. They are pausing any report that has to be relied on in financing until your foundational review has a court order.

MARA: How much of the remaining contract?

CLIENT: About forty percent.

[Beat.]

MARA: Give me the amount.

CLIENT: Twelve thousand eight hundred in billable work over the next month.

MARA: Thank you.

CLIENT: I am sorry.

MARA: I know you are not the insurer.

CLIENT: That does not make it less ugly.

MARA: No.

CLIENT: Do you want us to hold the work for you or reassign it?

MARA: Hold for ten business days. After that, reassign if I cannot give you a usable order. I will not make your deadline part of my legal strategy.

CLIENT: Understood.

MARA: Put the hold date in writing.

CLIENT: Already drafting it.

NARRATOR: Twelve thousand eight hundred was not symbolic. It was rent, tax, insurance, and the difference between choosing a case strategy and being cornered into one.

## SCENE 4 — MARA’S APARTMENT — EVENING

[SFX: Pages on table.]

EVELYN: Heritage will pay your independent counsel.

MARA: If I sign.

EVELYN: They guarantee no internal banking, housing, or medical access freeze in Vale-controlled systems while the registration case runs.

MARA: If I sign.

EVELYN: They offer a litigation fund.

MARA: If I sign.

EVELYN: Fine. What do they want?

MARA: Confidentiality over operational records not already public. Release of claims arising from continuity-program identity use. Waiver of property claims tied to any historical Vale identity. Agreement that the remedy is administrative only and creates no trust or ownership entitlement.

EVELYN: That last part sounds designed to keep you out of my life financially.

MARA: It is designed to keep uncertainty out of Heritage financially.

EVELYN: Same thing from their side.

MARA: Not exactly.

EVELYN: Do not start.

[Mara stops.]

EVELYN: Can you afford to refuse it?

MARA: That is the question they priced correctly.

[SFX: Email notification.]

MARA: Grace wants a meeting tomorrow.

EVELYN: Are you going?

MARA: Yes.

EVELYN: Why?

MARA: Because a strong bad offer is more dangerous than a stupid one. I want to know exactly what problem they are willing to spend money to make disappear.

NARRATOR: Mara closed the proposal without signing it.

NARRATOR: Her rent was due in nine days.

END OF EPISODE

# EPISODE 103 — THE PRICE OF A BIRTH CERTIFICATE

## SCENE 1 — HERITAGE CONFERENCE ROOM — MORNING

[SFX: Water poured. Chairs settle.]

GRACE KELLAN: Before we argue, I want to state what Heritage is actually offering.

MARA: Please.

GRACE KELLAN: Funded independent counsel chosen by you. Archive certification at Heritage expense. Interim operational guarantees across Vale-controlled systems. A capped legal-cost reserve. Non-opposition to a second-birth registration if the evidence meets the court’s standard.

MARA: And the release.

GRACE KELLAN: Release of civil claims based solely on historical continuity-program identity use, except intentional physical harm and claims already adjudicated.

EVELYN: “Solely” is doing a lot of work.

GRACE KELLAN: It is supposed to.

MARA: Property waiver.

GRACE KELLAN: No claim to existing Vale trust assets merely because the late registration establishes a second child connected to the same maternal line.

MARA: Confidentiality.

GRACE KELLAN: Nonpublic operational sources stay protected unless required by court. Facts already admitted remain public.

MARA: So this is not “we give you a birth certificate if you stay quiet.”

GRACE KELLAN: No. That would be indefensible.

MARA: It is “we help remove the administrative barriers while narrowing the consequences of why those barriers exist.”

GRACE KELLAN: That is a fair description.

EVELYN: I dislike that I like her answer.

## SCENE 2 — SAME — LATER

MARA: What happens if I refuse?

GRACE KELLAN: Heritage still obeys court orders. We still have obligations not to destroy or conceal evidence. We may oppose remedies we believe create disproportionate downstream liability or record instability.

MARA: Will you oppose a second birth registration?

GRACE KELLAN: Not if the evidentiary chain is sufficient and the order protects unrelated records.

MARA: Then why am I buying your non-opposition?

GRACE KELLAN: You are not. You are buying speed, funded administration, and certainty about collateral claims.

MARA: With my claims.

GRACE KELLAN: With negotiated claims, yes.

MARA: That is the part I needed said plainly.

GRACE KELLAN: I am not offended by plain language.

MARA: Most institutions prefer euphemism.

GRACE KELLAN: Most institutions prefer signatures.

EVELYN: There she is.

GRACE KELLAN: I told you I would remain inconvenient.

MARA: I am not signing today.

GRACE KELLAN: I would be concerned if you did.

## SCENE 3 — CELESTE’S OFFICE — AFTERNOON

[SFX: Stapler. City traffic below.]

CELESTE: The offer is rational.

MARA: That is why I brought it.

CELESTE: The legal-cost reserve matters. The internal continuity guarantee matters. The waiver is broader than I would accept without mapping every property and identity claim it touches.

MARA: Can we separate administrative support from release?

CELESTE: We can ask. They may price it differently.

EVELYN: You sound like we are buying insurance.

CELESTE: In part, you are. But do not confuse the financial architecture with the personhood remedy.

MARA: That is the problem.

CELESTE: Which part?

MARA: They are bundling “we will help you exist cleanly in databases” with “you will limit what that existence can mean legally.”

CELESTE: Then unbundle it.

MARA: If they refuse?

CELESTE: You decide what uncertainty you can live with. Not what I think is morally elegant.

EVELYN: I like that answer.

CELESTE: It is expensive advice. Enjoy it.

MARA: Can you build an independent claims map before tomorrow?

CELESTE: Not a final one. A decision-safe one, yes.

MARA: Do that.

## SCENE 4 — SIDEWALK OUTSIDE CELESTE’S OFFICE — EVENING

[SFX: Pedestrian crossing. Phone vibrates.]

VOSS: Ms. Quinn.

MARA: How did you get this number?

VOSS: It has been in too many files for too long.

MARA: Bad answer.

VOSS: Then use the better one. I asked Shaw to forward a request for contact. He refused. I called the number in an old public filing.

MARA: What do you want?

VOSS: You are negotiating with Heritage as if the missing certificate was an unavoidable defect.

MARA: It began as a defect. Intent later is still open.

VOSS: Helena knew it could be repaired.

[Beat.]

MARA: When?

VOSS: Years before Rosegate.

MARA: What is your source?

VOSS: I was in the room for one of the reviews.

MARA: Your memory is testimony. What corroborates it?

VOSS: Threat packets. Victoria’s objections. A legal-repair memorandum. Bellweather implementation notes.

MARA: Do not summarize them to me. Identify repositories.

VOSS: I can.

MARA: Through counsel. Recorded. No private meeting.

VOSS: You still think procedure protects you.

MARA: No. I think it protects the next person from having to believe either of us.

VOSS: Tomorrow, then.

MARA: Counsel will send terms.

NARRATOR: Mara ended the call and reopened the Heritage proposal.

NARRATOR: She had thought the question was whether she could afford to reject a clean administrative solution.

NARRATOR: Voss had just made the price larger.

NARRATOR: If Helena had known correction was possible, then the missing birth record was no longer only a clerical wound.

END OF EPISODE

# EPISODE 104 — HELENA KNEW

## SCENE 1 — RECORDED COUNSEL INTERVIEW ROOM — MORNING

[SFX: Recorder tone. Chairs.]

COUNSEL: This interview is voluntary. No immunity, no settlement consideration, no agreement that testimony is accepted as fact.

VOSS: Understood.

MARA: Start with the hospital error. Not Helena.

VOSS: The second registration failed during an emergency transfer. The pediatric transfer occurred before the registrar completed the second packet.

MARA: How do you know?

VOSS: Later reconstruction. Hospital closeout, correction packet, transfer ledger.

MARA: Those records already support an initial omission. They do not prove intent.

VOSS: I agree.

MARA: When did Helena first learn the second child remained unregistered?

VOSS: When the protected placement review reached her office. Years later.

MARA: Source.

VOSS: Risk-review docket. Helena’s annotated copy may still exist in the Vale governance archive.

COUNSEL: We have requested it.

MARA: What did she do?

VOSS: Ordered a threat assessment before correction.

MARA: That is not the same as refusing correction.

VOSS: No.

EVELYN: How long did the assessment take?

VOSS: Three weeks.

MARA: And after?

VOSS: She approved continued concealment of the second subject count and deferred civil repair.

## SCENE 2 — DOCUMENT RECONCILIATION — LATER

[SFX: Scanner. Pages align.]

FORENSIC TECH: We have three independent artifacts relevant to Voss’s statement. First, the threat-review docket. Second, Victoria’s written objection. Third, a legal-repair memorandum prepared by outside counsel.

MARA: Read dates.

FORENSIC TECH: Threat review closes May twelve. Outside counsel memo May fourteen. Victoria objection May sixteen. Helena decision note May eighteen.

EVELYN: What does the legal memo say?

COUNSEL: High level only: a delayed second registration was legally possible but would require disclosure of a second surviving child and supporting custody history.

MARA: So correction was possible.

COUNSEL: The memo says a route existed. Jurisdiction-specific validity still requires specialist review. For story evidence, it disproves “nobody knew a repair mechanism existed.”

MARA: Helena’s note?

FORENSIC TECH: “Do not expose second subject during active threat period. Preserve current single-public-heir architecture. Revisit after stabilization.”

EVELYN: Single public heir.

MARA: That is more than safety language.

VOSS: Yes.

MARA: Did you write it?

VOSS: No.

FORENSIC TECH: Authorship metadata maps to Helena’s office credential and a verified assistant chain. We cannot prove which hand typed each word.

MARA: Enough to attribute the office decision, not a private thought.

FORENSIC TECH: Correct.

EVELYN: Victoria?

COUNSEL: She objected that indefinite deferral would turn protection into ownership and leave one child without lawful existence.

## SCENE 3 — RECORDED INTERVIEW — AFTERNOON

MARA: Now your part.

VOSS: Years later, Bellweather needed a stable operational identity for the unregistered subject.

MARA: Needed according to whom?

VOSS: My program.

MARA: Say the program.

VOSS: Continuity protection. We sourced low-collision dormant records that could survive ordinary institutional checks without exposing protected lineage.

EVELYN: Dead children.

VOSS: In this case, yes.

MARA: Did Helena order you to use Mara Elise Quinn?

VOSS: No.

MARA: Did she approve that specific source?

VOSS: I have no evidence she saw the Quinn file.

MARA: Then do not let anyone turn “Helena deferred correction” into “Helena selected my dead child shell.”

VOSS: Agreed.

COUNSEL: Who approved operational use?

VOSS: My office and Bellweather continuity intake.

MARA: Victoria objected.

VOSS: She did.

MARA: And you proceeded.

VOSS: Yes.

MARA: Why?

VOSS: Because the threat model still said convergence was dangerous and the legal repair had become politically expensive.

EVELYN: Politically expensive to whom?

VOSS: Vale governance. Trust structure. Public succession. Liability.

MARA: So the original omission was clerical. Helena later preserved it under a mixed safety-and-control rationale. You later operationalized a stolen shell under a program that benefited from the unresolved defect.

VOSS: That is fair.

MARA: It is testimony until the records close each link.

VOSS: Of course.

## SCENE 4 — HALLWAY — EVENING

EVELYN: You did not ask whether Helena loved us.

MARA: It would not answer the record question.

EVELYN: It might answer mine.

MARA: Then you should ask it when you want the answer from the person who can only give testimony about it.

EVELYN: She is dead.

MARA: I know.

EVELYN: Sometimes I hate your precision because it leaves grief exactly where it was.

MARA: Precision is bad at grief.

EVELYN: That sounded almost human.

MARA: Do not spread it around.

[SFX: Phone buzzes.]

SHAW: We located Bellweather approval logs. Voss’s operational program is there. Quinn source lineage is there. Helena is not in the specific source approval chain.

MARA: Good. Keep the responsibility separated.

SHAW: There is one more thing. The court clerk reviewed the second-birth petition framework.

MARA: And?

SHAW: If you establish Baby B continuity, late registration is possible in the story’s current legal model. But the default proposed name is not Mara Quinn.

MARA: What is it?

SHAW: The birth mother’s surname from the surviving maternity record.

NARRATOR: Mara had spent days proving that a stolen name had been attached to her before Noah ever met her.

NARRATOR: Now the law was offering to fix the missing birth by taking the name away.

END OF EPISODE

# EPISODE 105 — THE CLEAN ANSWER GETS WORSE

## SCENE 1 — EVIDENCE COMPILATION ROOM — MORNING

[SFX: Whiteboard marker. Pages pinned.]

NARRATOR: Mara had asked for one room, one wall, and no speeches. By ten, the wall held the closest thing they had ever had to a clean answer.

MARA: Start at seventeen, because that is the strongest body mapping.

FORENSIC TECH: E96: historical card labeled Mara Quinn maps to current Mara. Companion card labeled Evelyn maps to current Evelyn.

MARA: Earlier continuity.

FORENSIC TECH: Hawthorne withdrawal file, school health fragments, protected placement route, and Source Guardian subject number connect historical teenage Mara Quinn backward through juvenile protection.

SHAW: E92 established Baby B entered protected placement without permanent civil name.

MARA: Does the chain yet prove Baby B became historical Mara Quinn?

FORENSIC TECH: Strongly, but not conclusively on one document. The juvenile placement identifier evolves into the later protected subject number through a migration ledger. We have two independent archive custodians and one contemporaneous transition sheet.

MARA: Confidence?

FORENSIC TECH: High enough for a court argument. Not a magic link.

EVELYN: And me?

FORENSIC TECH: The surviving registered Infant A line maps through family records to the protected Evelyn subject and current Evelyn. Again cumulative, not one clue.

MARA: So the clean working conclusion is current Mara equals the unregistered second child and later historical Mara Quinn; current Evelyn equals the registered line.

COUNSEL: Working legal conclusion, subject to hearing.

MARA: Now the name.

## SCENE 2 — SAME — LATER

FORENSIC TECH: The civil shell used by historical Mara Quinn was sourced from deceased Mara Elise Quinn. Bellweather linked that source before Noah’s involvement. Historical Mara was later informed and chose to continue using Mara Quinn pending lawful separate identity.

MARA: After Rosegate, Noah used his sister’s vaccination card to keep the already-existing shell operational.

SHAW: Correct.

MARA: So solving “which sister was I” does not clean my current record.

COUNSEL: It makes the contamination clearer.

MARA: Say it plainly.

COUNSEL: The living person is strongly continuous. The current civil identity record is partly derived from a deceased child’s compromised source. A court can distinguish the person from the defective record, but agencies will need correction orders.

[Mara says nothing.]

EVELYN: Mara.

MARA: Continue.

COUNSEL: Late registration of the second birth can establish a lawful foundation. The name issue is separate.

MARA: Continue.

COUNSEL: Default registration practice in the current story model would attach the maternal surname from the birth record unless the court simultaneously recognizes an adult legal-name choice.

MARA: So the clean answer is: I was the second child. I became Mara Quinn. Mara Quinn was never lawfully mine at the foundation. My adult life is built on the repair of a stolen shell.

COUNSEL: That is too compressed.

MARA: It is also true enough to ruin my afternoon.

## SCENE 3 — STAIRWELL — CONTINUOUS

[SFX: Heavy door. Footsteps stop.]

NARRATOR: Mara left before anybody could call it a break.

EVELYN: I am not coming after you.

MARA: Thank you.

EVELYN: I am standing here because the coffee machine is downstairs.

MARA: Convenient.

[Silence.]

EVELYN: You do not have to solve the name in the next five minutes.

MARA: I know.

EVELYN: That sounded terrible.

MARA: Because I am not thinking about the name.

EVELYN: What are you thinking about?

MARA: That every time we find the accurate answer, it removes one excuse for why I should not be angry.

EVELYN: At who?

MARA: I do not know yet.

EVELYN: Good.

MARA: Do not use my word.

EVELYN: I mean it is good you do not know. You used to choose the most administratively useful person to blame.

MARA: That is unfair.

EVELYN: Yes.

MARA: Also accurate.

[Mara’s phone vibrates. She looks at it and turns it face down.]

EVELYN: Client?

MARA: Yes.

EVELYN: Are you calling back?

MARA: Not yet.

NARRATOR: Mara missed the call. Not accidentally. For the first time in weeks, she let the case cost her something without immediately converting the cost into a task.

## SCENE 4 — MARA’S APARTMENT — EVENING

[SFX: Voicemail playback. Kettle clicks off.]

CLIENT: Mara, we need your answer by five tomorrow on the held financing scope. Call me when you can.

[Mara deletes nothing.]

[SFX: Knock.]

ADRIAN: It is me. I can leave.

MARA: Come in for ten minutes.

ADRIAN: Ten.

[Door opens.]

ADRIAN: Evelyn told me the mapping is almost complete.

MARA: She should not have summarized it.

ADRIAN: She did not. She said, “Do not ask Mara what this means yet.”

MARA: That sounds like her.

ADRIAN: I brought food.

MARA: Of course you did.

ADRIAN: It is my least invasive skill.

[Beat.]

MARA: The answer is probably me. Baby B. Unregistered. Then Mara Quinn. The name came from your family’s protection machinery using a dead child’s file.

ADRIAN: I am sorry.

MARA: Do not apologize for the whole system. You will run out of language.

ADRIAN: What do you need?

MARA: For you not to tell me the name is “really mine” because I chose it.

ADRIAN: All right.

MARA: And not to tell me it is contaminated beyond use because of where it came from.

ADRIAN: All right.

MARA: I hate that those are the two speeches everyone wants to give.

ADRIAN: Then I will not give either one.

[Mara breathes.]

MARA: Thank you.

[SFX: Phone buzzes.]

MARA: Court clerk.

SHAW, PHONE: The judge will entertain a combined remedy: late registration plus separate adult-name recognition. But keeping Mara Quinn requires sworn provenance of the Quinn source. Noah’s sealed family file becomes necessary.

MARA: He controls access to some of it.

SHAW: Yes.

MARA: Then tomorrow we ask him. We do not trade forgiveness for evidence.

SHAW: Understood.

NARRATOR: Mara called the client back before midnight. She apologized for missing the first call and asked for twenty-four hours.

NARRATOR: The clean answer had made her legal life worse.

NARRATOR: It had also finally made the next choice hers.

END OF EPISODE
