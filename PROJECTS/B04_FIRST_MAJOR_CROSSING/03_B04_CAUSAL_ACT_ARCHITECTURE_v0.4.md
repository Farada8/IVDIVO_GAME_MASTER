## CAUSAL ACT ARCHITECTURE v0.4

Date: 2026-08-22
Status: **WORKING / ENGINE GATE GREEN / ACT ARCHITECTURE / NO PROSE**
Working engine: `TWO CASES / ONE CODE`
Working phenomenon pair: `FALSE OCCUPANCY / TWO KINDS OF PRESENCE`
Working hero: **Maya**
Adult co-lead: **Smith**

## PRIMARY STORY QUESTION

When two different realities trigger the same legitimate emergency classification, can Maya prove that responsible action means more than being locally right and willing to pay her own cost — while Smith proves that useful institutional history is not trustworthy merely because it once saved lives?

## READER WAITING QUESTION

**Why do two empty spaces both behave as occupied — and what happens if the station keeps treating them as the same thing?**

The question must evolve rather than repeat:
`What is in the empty room?`
-> `Why did the same code fire twice?`
-> `Why does the same response help one site and damage the other?`
-> `Which observable difference matters?`
-> `Did OES historically mix different cases together?`
-> `Can the station split response safely before it knows B's ontology?`
-> `What will Maya and Smith each give up to make the split usable?`

# ACT I — ONE CODE
## Function
Establish ordinary orbital life, Maya's concrete work want, both false-occupancy events, the legitimate unified response, Maya's externality blind spot, and the reason Smith/OES becomes relevant.

### Opening state
Approximately 10–14 weeks after B02 ending and after B03.
Maya works paid limited shifts as a supervised route-validation assistant. She is in the final evaluation window for a low-consequence independent endorsement.

The opening should be ordinary before strange:
- commuter/service route change;
- a resident annoyed about signage;
- Maya arguing with a staff confirmer about whether a correction is obvious;
- money/schedule/shift consequence;
- local friends' plans colliding with her evaluation slot;
- no franchise briefing.

### Inciting event A
A mixed-species residential/service module triggers a rare `unattributed occupancy / local actuation` code.
No registered resident is inside the affected room/zone, but:
- environmental demand indicates sustained occupancy-like load;
- local interfaces enter legitimate occupied-state behavior;
- credential logs show no ordinary actor;
- physical sweep finds no ordinary occupant.

Maya arrives because a routine traveler-facing route through the module needs a detour, not because she is an anomaly investigator.

Local incident command applies the current rare-code procedure: restrict access, preserve environment/evidence, stop nonessential movement, verify credentials and network command paths.

This is a reasonable response.

### First pressure
Maya notices the affected room's behavior is stable in relation to the resident environmental installation rather than to people entering/leaving.
She says so as an observation, not theory.
The commander records it but does not split the classification from one event.

### Inciting event B
Within hours, a separate service/maintenance zone triggers the same rare code.
Again, a space behaves occupied/active without an attributable person.

The station links A and B under one parent incident because:
- same rare multi-signal bundle;
- two sites inside one habitat;
- unknown propagation/correlation risk;
- insufficient evidence to assume coincidence.

This is also reasonable.

### Maya's first correct insight
She notices the physical sequence differs:
- A's behavior stays with one habitat/environmental installation;
- B's behavior changes location after a removable service component is transferred.

She argues for two provisional response branches.
Incident command refuses a full split yet but logs the distinction and assigns separate evidence tracks under one emergency parent.

### Wrong-strategy action / first price
A service corridor near Incident B becomes congested under the unified closure.
Maya has legitimate low-consequence authority to post/activate one of two pre-cleared traveler detours during her evaluation shift.

She chooses the fastest local detour because:
- it is permitted;
- it clears her assigned area;
- she discloses the change correctly;
- she accepts the direct schedule burden.

What she does not model:
the detour pushes normal service traffic into the approach used to deliver time-sensitive environmental support to the quarantined Incident-A household/module.

No catastrophe occurs, but:
- support delivery is delayed;
- a resident/environmental system must be held on backup longer;
- service/housing staff incur avoidable work;
- people outside Maya's route decision carry the cost.

Her supervisor pauses the endorsement assessment pending review.

Maya is furious because she did not lie, exceed her assigned action or hide the change.

The supervisor's answer is not moralistic:
**“Correct route. Incomplete dependency picture.”**

### OES trigger
Incident B's material-following sequence reaches a legacy comparison threshold in the current incident system. A bounded historical-consult request goes to OES because the behavior resembles an old `presence without attributable person / carrier-associated` class.

Smith receives the request while OES's B03 doctrine review is still active.
He does not arrive with command authority.

### Act I turn
Smith's first archive read appears to support the station's decision to keep both cases under one rare umbrella class.
Maya thinks this is exactly the wrong conclusion.

**Act-I question:** Is Maya seeing a real causal split, or merely two manifestations of one phenomenon before the adults have enough evidence?

# ACT II-A — TWO STORIES INSIDE ONE INCIDENT
## Function
Make all sides competent; deepen the investigation; use ordinary orbital consequences; reveal that each knowledge system sees a different subset of reality.

### Smith arrival
Smith enters through an evidence-consult role and is briefed by the orbital incident commander, not by Maya.

First Smith/Maya encounter should create asymmetry, not mentor chemistry:
- Smith wants exact transfer provenance for Incident B.
- Maya wants him to stop treating the shared incident code as evidence of shared cause.
- Smith says the code is not ontology; it is a response family.
- Maya asks why the response family is already changing two sites the same way.

Both have a point.

### Ordinary-life consequence line
While the technical investigation advances:
- one household cannot use its normal support environment;
- service routes are lengthened;
- school/work handoffs move;
- staff are paid overtime or lose other work windows;
- public boards use one incident label because that is the current legal/operational status;
- local gossip/social feeds invent a cleaner story than evidence supports.

Aoife may appear here as a bounded public-story/source-consent pressure, not as the solver.
Samir remains in sport/social life and may offer one ordinary categorization observation only if causally earned.
Ethan/Ollie remain on their own current work paths; no committee scene assembled for exposition.

### Incident A evidence ladder
1. The occupancy-like load persists with the environmental installation when residents leave.
2. It changes predictably when lawful support conditions change.
3. It does not follow ordinary credential/network command paths.
4. A limited Contact/Confederation advisory request identifies the general class as a distributed living support ecology used by some nonhuman households/environments.
5. This does NOT prove every actuation is intended, sentient or safe; it proves the system has been misclassified as ordinary environmental support rather than living infrastructure.

The Confederation analyst's correction is bounded:
**“You have a living system in the category you built for equipment.”**
Not: `this is the answer to both incidents`.

### Incident B evidence ladder
1. The false-occupancy signature stops in the original room after one removable component leaves.
2. It appears at the next staging point after that component arrives.
3. It persists with network command disabled and no metabolic/living-system signature equivalent to A.
4. A controlled hold proves the effect is component-associated at operational resolution.
5. OES archive contains old cases where isolating/removing a material carrier changed the event, but ontology remained unresolved.

### Smith's false confidence
Smith treats the OES carrier-associated cases as evidence that B belongs to a historically useful operational subtype inside the old umbrella class.
He acknowledges A is a modern known-class misclassification but still assumes the umbrella can survive as a response family with subtypes.

This is subtler than `OES is wrong`.

### Maya's pressure
Maya is now right about the causal split but wrong about what being right entitles her to.
She pushes her supervisor to restart the endorsement assessment.
The supervisor refuses until the service-dependency failure is closed separately from the anomaly insight.

**Being right about the case does not erase the work error.**

### First convergence test design
The incident commander authorizes a controlled, non-catastrophic comparison:
- Incident A: move/alter only a lawful environmental-support condition while keeping people/network constant.
- Incident B: move only the suspect component into a controlled unoccupied service cradle while preserving chain of custody.

Prediction:
If both are one causal family, their response to controlled relocation/state change should share a meaningful common pattern.
If they are different, A should track living-environment conditions while B should track material custody.

### Midpoint — THE CODE SPLITS
The test succeeds at exactly the level needed:
- A tracks the living ecology/support environment.
- B tracks the material component.

The station formally splits the parent incident into two provisional response classes.

Then the harder result lands.

Smith searches the historical OES outcome table by response behavior instead of old category name and finds divergent clusters that were known but compressed into one umbrella label.

A legacy classification-change note shows OES retained one category because it was operationally convenient under poor historical discrimination — not because one cause was proved.

The old archive is not just incomplete.
**Its category contaminated its own statistics.**

At the same time, Confederation analysis can classify A much better but has no explanation for B beyond `not this living class`.

### MIDPOINT CHANGE
Before midpoint:
`Which institution has the better explanation?`
After midpoint:
`How do we act when the categories themselves are part of the uncertainty?`

Maya gains evidentiary ground but not authority.
Smith loses category trust but gains a more honest problem.

# ACT II-B — THE COST OF SPLITTING THE TRUTH
## Function
Turn the intellectual split into practical conflict, institutional price and worsening ordinary consequences. Force Maya and Smith to pay before climax.

### Branch A operational problem
The living support ecology has been stressed by quarantine procedures designed for contamination/evidence preservation.
The household/environment cannot simply return to normal because:
- some local automation still treats the ecology as unauthorized occupancy;
- moving it requires controlled environmental support;
- resident rights, service safety and unfamiliar system behavior all matter.

The correct response is preservation/reclassification, not purge.

### Branch B operational problem
The material-bound component has entered a service logistics chain before the split was proven.
Because it is ordinary-looking removable hardware, custody records are good enough to trace but not designed as hazardous-material control.

The current component is located, but moving it to a controlled cradle requires service redundancy and a clean transfer window.

The response is custody/containment, not habitat-wide biological isolation.

### Public/institutional pressure
Once the cases split, a new problem appears:
people ask why one rare code produced two contradictory restrictions.

The orbital commander needs an auditable basis for the split, especially because one branch will reopen a household/environment while the other tightens material custody.

OES operational notes alone are insufficient because their historical category is now suspect.

### Smith institutional conflict
An OES reviewer/supervisor argues:
- release current useful response observations;
- do not release the historical classification/outcome ledger beyond what the case requires;
- the internal doctrine review can handle historical defects later.

This is not corruption. It is a defensible information-control position.

Smith understands why it exists.
B03 taught him the cost of stripping context from lessons.
B04 now forces a harder choice: context disclosure will weaken OES's own authority over the category.

He first tries a bounded summary.
The orbital commander rejects it as insufficient to justify divergent public restrictions.

### Maya relationship/social pressure
Maya's local peers continue ordinary life around the incident.
Lea, if used, asks for one specific route/service fact and refuses to turn the event into a referendum on Maya's character.

Maya loses social time and one paid/evaluation shift, but this is secondary.
The primary pressure remains the endorsement window.

### Maya discovers the shared-resource conflict
The two corrected response branches require the same limited service redundancy during overlapping windows:
- A needs protected environmental support capacity for safe stabilization/relocation.
- B needs a controlled service transfer/containment window to stop the suspect component moving through normal logistics.

There are two viable sequences.

**FAST LOCAL / MAYA-FAVORING SEQUENCE**
- clear B from Maya's evaluated route first;
- completes the last observed route-validation action inside her current endorsement window;
- restores her zone faster;
- consumes redundancy that forces A to remain longer on stressed backup and pushes service cost/risk onto people outside Maya's direct route.

**SLOW SHARED-SAFE SEQUENCE**
- stabilize A first while B remains immobilized in place under local guard/control;
- then transfer B when redundancy recovers;
- leaves Maya's zone restricted longer;
- causes her endorsement deadline to expire before the final evaluation action.

Both are technically defensible inside different scopes.

### Commander refuses false hero shortcut
The incident commander does NOT tell Maya what choice proves maturity.
The commander sets system-wide hard limits and delegates only the narrow traveler/service sequencing decision Maya's role actually owns.

Maya must choose inside real bounded authority.

### Smith pays before climax
Smith releases the OES classification/outcome ledger with provenance and its damaging historical rationale.

Result:
- local commander can formally document why OES's old umbrella class cannot govern both branches;
- OES classification family enters independent review;
- current useful observations remain usable but no longer self-authorizing;
- Smith loses the institutional comfort of a category he once trusted.

He does not become anti-OES. He makes OES more accountable at real cost.

### Pre-climax setback
A local change in B's service staging makes the fast sequence more attractive: delay now threatens a larger backlog/closure.
At A, backup remains stable but not indefinitely desirable.

Maya has a real reason to pick fast.
Nobody accuses her of selfishness.

Act II-B ends when the incident commander confirms:
**both sequences are legal within the hard safety envelope; the delegated local sequence choice is Maya's.**

# ACT III — TWO RESPONSES
## Function
Force the hero's physical choice, make Smith's disclosure matter operationally, close both incidents, pay ordinary cost before reward, and separate the characters after the crossover.

### Climax choice
Maya sees that the fast sequence would:
- fix the route she personally owns;
- satisfy her final current-cycle evaluation action;
- move B sooner;
- leave A's people/environment carrying the external cost.

She chooses **SLOW SHARED-SAFE**.

No speech about responsibility is required.
Her action is the route/service sequence she signs and then physically executes with the team.

### Physical climax actions
1. Maya holds the fast traveler/service detour closed even though it is the easiest route in her own zone.
2. She keeps the B component immobilized at its current controlled staging point instead of moving it through the shared support window.
3. She walks/validates the alternative service approach needed for A's protected support transfer, because this is precisely the domain she actually knows.
4. Adult/environmental specialists execute the living-ecology stabilization/relocation.
5. The incident commander retains habitat-wide command and resource authority.
6. Once A reaches stable support and redundancy recovers, B moves under chain-of-custody containment to the unoccupied cradle.
7. Maya validates only the traveler-facing handoff/route conditions assigned to her; she does not control the containment science.

### Immediate price before validation
The service-cycle timestamp passes.
Maya's endorsement window closes.
Her supervisor records:
`CURRENT CYCLE INCOMPLETE — REAPPLY NEXT CYCLE.`

No heroic exception.

### Incident A closure
- living support ecology remains viable;
- affected household/service environment receives a legitimate local classification path;
- automation/sensor semantics are patched or flagged for redesign;
- resident life resumes with inconvenience, not a perfect technology fix.

### Incident B closure
- suspect component is immobilized under controlled evidence custody;
- false-occupancy actuation no longer travels through ordinary service logistics;
- origin/mechanism remains UNKNOWN;
- local systems know what operational condition contains the current risk.

### Institutional closure
- parent one-code emergency closes;
- current cases are recorded under two different response classes;
- OES umbrella class is explicitly `NON-UNITARY / REVIEW REQUIRED` rather than erased from history;
- Confederation advisory contribution is recorded as bounded classification evidence for A only;
- no standing joint jurisdiction/team is created.

### Maya professional resolution
Her supervisor does not congratulate her for sacrificing the endorsement.
Instead:
- confirms the base role remains available after normal review;
- tells her next endorsement cycle date when known;
- requires the same evaluation standard next time.

Maya is annoyed.
That is healthy.

Her change is behavioral:
On a later ordinary shift, she finds a route correction that is obviously better locally. Before signing the routine change, she checks the downstream service dependency because someone outside her route will carry it.
Then she submits the correction without turning the act into a moral speech.

### Relationship resolution
Lea/local peers remain ordinary people with their own schedules.
The original five do not all have to be present for the final beat.
Maya may miss one gathering because of a shift and join later, reversing the old assumption that shared friendship requires synchronized life.

### Smith resolution
Smith leaves with:
- B03 interim context/provenance discipline intact;
- a new institutional lesson: response categories are hypotheses about useful action, not proof that cases belong to one physical family;
- OES still necessary, less comfortable and more auditable.

No permanent orbital assignment.
No mentor status over Maya.
No family exposition dump.

### Series hook — final pages only
After the current incident and Maya's ordinary-life resolution are complete, one bounded external comparison arrives through the existing Contact channel.

It matches **one** material-bound operational feature of Incident B closely enough to be evidence-grade correspondence.

It does NOT establish:
- shared origin;
- universal category;
- precursor identity;
- Root/Matrix answer;
- permanent joint task force.

The correct last saga-facing question is smaller:
**If another civilization once separated this kind of case from its own wrong category, what did it learn that Earth has not asked yet?**

Then stop.
