# SHILLELAGH RESEARCH FACTCHECK v1

## Safe anchors for visual storytelling
- Shillelagh is a south County Wicklow village associated with the historic Fitzwilliam/Coollattin estate.
- The village had a railway terminus on the Shillelagh branch; the line opened in 1865 and passenger/goods service at Shillelagh ended in the 1940s.
- Coollattin House is a major Georgian estate house near Shillelagh and a legitimate regional-history anchor.
- Tomnafinnoge Woods is a surviving remnant of the historic oak woods of south Wicklow and provides a strong landscape/ecology anchor.
- Aghowle Church is a medieval/12th-century Romanesque church and national monument in the wider Shillelagh barony/region.
- Rathgall hillfort is a major prehistoric site in south Wicklow and can be used as **regional deep-time heritage**; do not label it “the first settlement of Shillelagh” without a stronger direct source.
- River Derry belongs to the local landscape system and can serve as a visual connector.

## Important correction gate
Do **not** state as fact that Tomnafinnoge oak was the historical source of the famous “shillelagh” walking sticks unless a specific reliable source is supplied. Keep the stick as cultural/heritage symbolism only where sourced through the local heritage trail.

## Narrative rule
Every named person, date, building, industrial activity or migration claim that appears in final competition copy must have an evidence record. Decorative allegories may be invented, but must be labelled as allegorical rather than historical fact.
