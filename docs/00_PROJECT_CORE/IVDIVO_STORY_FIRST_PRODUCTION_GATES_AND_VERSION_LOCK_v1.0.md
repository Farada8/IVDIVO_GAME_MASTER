# 0. WHY THIS STANDARD EXISTS

A previous IVDIVO development failure showed that worldbuilding, themes, character dossiers, continuity, technology and prose work can all look sophisticated while the most basic requirement is missing: **one clear main story that begins, drives the whole work and ends.**

This standard prevents that failure from recurring.

The studio is not allowed to compensate for a weak story with lore, philosophy, terminology, length, beautiful prose, more agents, more documents or promises of later payoff.

> **One work = one main story.**
>
> Secondary lines create depth, immersion and consequences, but they do not replace the main story.

---

# 1. HONEST PERFORMANCE RULE

The studio may aim for **mass-market / breakout / hit potential**, but it may not promise that a book will become a hit. Popularity is not controllable.

What the studio *can* be held accountable for is the quality of the production process:

- a strong and retellable premise;
- a complete main story;
- clear reader desire to know what happens next;
- escalating causality;
- memorable characters;
- emotional identification;
- genre satisfaction;
- fair mystery / discovery;
- meaningful payoff;
- stylistic control;
- continuity control;
- professional red-team and revision.

No marketing claim substitutes for these deliverables.

---

# 2. THE ZERO GATE — ONE MAIN STORY

**NO PROSE PRODUCTION MAY START UNTIL THIS GATE PASSES.**

For every book / episode / story, the studio must write a `MAIN STORY CONTRACT` containing, in plain language:

1. **Inciting event — What happens?**
2. **Main objective — What must the protagonist(s) now do?**
3. **Opposition — Who or what actively prevents this?**
4. **Stakes — What is concretely lost if they fail?**
5. **Pressure / clock — Why must this be acted on now rather than later?**
6. **Escalation — How do attempts make the problem harder or reveal a larger one?**
7. **Crisis choice — What difficult choice cannot be avoided?**
8. **Climax action — What do the protagonists actually do to decide the case?**
9. **Main-story ending — What concrete problem is resolved by the end?**
10. **Aftermath — What new state exists because of what happened?**

A one-sentence test is mandatory:

> `When [INCITING EVENT], [PROTAGONIST] must [CONCRETE OBJECTIVE] before / despite [PRESSURE + OPPOSITION], or [CONCRETE STAKES]; in the climax they must [DECISIVE ACTION / CHOICE].`

If this cannot be written clearly, the work is **BLOCKED**.

Themes such as “identity”, “subjectivity”, “growing up”, “freedom”, “the Matrix”, “Contact”, “love”, “technology” or “social inequality” are **not** substitutes for the main story.

---

# 3. CASE / QUEST ENGINE GATE

Every novel must possess a sustained action engine. It may be detective, rescue, survival, competition, construction, expedition, heist, escape, creation, trial, investigation, relationship quest or another form, but the reader must understand what the characters are trying to accomplish.

Acceptable engine examples:

- find the missing person;
- rescue someone before a procedure;
- prove a friend innocent;
- recover a stolen system;
- win / qualify / finish a competition;
- build a working system before a deadline;
- reach a forbidden or inaccessible place;
- stop a developing failure;
- discover who caused a sequence of incidents;
- establish contact with an incomprehensible subject;
- return home;
- prevent an expulsion / deportation / transfer;
- solve a concrete family or social crisis that requires action.

The engine must produce **progress states**. The characters cannot spend the middle of the novel merely discussing the same problem.

For each act / movement, record:

`OBJECTIVE → ATTEMPT → RESISTANCE → RESULT → NEW INFORMATION / COST → REVISED OBJECTIVE`.

---

# 4. MAIN STORY VS SECONDARY WEAVE

A book may contain many active threads, but they have hierarchy.

## A-LINE

The **one main story**. It begins, escalates, climaxes and resolves in this book.

## B-LINES

Usually 2–4 strong secondary arcs: love, friendship, family, work, sport, school status, money, creative ambition, migration, rivalry, grief, etc.

## C-LINES

Small recurring life threads, motifs, jokes, habits, ordinary obligations and long-run series seeds.

The key rule:

> **Secondary lines must collide causally with the main story.**

School is not a pause from the investigation. School can prevent attendance at a meeting, create a witness, expose a lie, force public consequences, alter status, produce an alibi or create the next obstacle.

Training is not filler. A match can create the injury, jealousy, absence, public video or time conflict that changes the case.

Romance is not decoration. A breach of trust can obtain the clue while damaging the relationship.

Ordinary life and main action must form one causal fabric.

---

# 5. STORY IS NOT ENOUGH — PROFESSIONAL CRAFT STACK

Passing the Zero Gate only proves that a story exists. It does **not** prove that the story is good.

Every work must then pass the following craft layers:

1. **Dramaturgy:** desire, resistance, reversal, escalation, crisis, climax, payoff.
2. **Composition:** order of information, POV, revelation timing, setup/payoff placement, act/movement balance.
3. **Scene design:** goal, conflict, turn, result, causal output.
4. **Character causality:** characters generate plot through choices; they do not merely witness plot.
5. **Relationship dynamics:** relationships measurably change because of events.
6. **Mystery / discovery:** clues, hypotheses, tests, reversals and fair solvability when applicable.
7. **Adventure / physical action:** concrete movement, tasks, places, risk and memorable set pieces when genre requires it.
8. **YA recognition:** school, work, family, money, status, sport, friends, desire, embarrassment, belonging and ordinary life remain real.
9. **Dialogue / subtext:** characters do not simply explain themes or state their emotions.
10. **POV / voice:** diction, noticing, metaphor and thought belong to the actual character.
11. **Language:** verbs, nouns, sentence structure, specificity, rhythm, repetition and sensory detail are intentionally controlled.
12. **Imagery / motif:** recurring images accumulate meaning rather than announce symbolism.
13. **Pacing / retention:** meaningful progress and payoff occur throughout, not only at the ending.
14. **Meaning / learning:** ideas are experienced through consequences and choices, not delivered as lectures.
15. **Genre satisfaction:** mystery feels solvable, thriller urgent, romance relationally earned, adventure exploratory, SF genuinely revelatory, etc.
16. **Ending:** the book pays the promise made by its beginning.

No later craft layer may be used to hide a failure in an earlier layer.

---

# 6. REAL “100-SPECIALIST STUDIO” RULE

The phrase “100 specialists” is prohibited as a quality claim unless there is evidence of actual multi-lens review.

The studio’s role catalogue is a **routing system**, not 100 magical independent people.

For a major novel, the production record must show explicit outputs from the relevant rooms. At minimum for an important IVDIVO novel:

- Showrunner / Main Story;
- Dramaturgy / Causality;
- Mystery / Thriller or relevant genre room;
- Adventure / Set-piece room;
- Character Psychology;
- Relationship / Romance;
- YA Everyday-Life;
- Sociology / Class / Family;
- Technology / World Rules;
- Composition / POV;
- Dialogue / Prose / Style;
- Retention / Audience;
- Literary Critic;
- SWOT;
- Pre-mortem;
- Red Team / Continuity.

Each room must return at least:

`FINDING → EVIDENCE → RISK → REQUIRED CHANGE / PASS`.

A list of role names without these outputs counts as **NO REVIEW**.

Specialist reviews are allowed to disagree. The Showrunner must record the conflict and the reason for the final decision.

---

# 7. SOURCE-LEARNING REQUIREMENT

When the project already contains uploaded books, scripts, criticism or craft sources relevant to the target book, the studio must actually study them before architecture freeze.

Use them only for **mechanisms**, never copied prose, characters, lore, scenes or recognisable sequences.

For each useful source, extract concrete answers such as:

- what is the main story engine?
- when is the inciting event?
- what does the protagonist do for most of the work?
- how often does the objective change?
- how are clues and payoffs spaced?
- how does ordinary youth life interfere with the main action?
- what creates attachment to characters?
- what scene types recur?
- where is the midpoint model change?
- what makes the ending satisfying?
- what creates retellable / “you have to read this” moments?
- what techniques fail or date poorly?

A source audit that only says “good pacing”, “strong characters”, “useful for YA” is insufficient.

---

# 8. MASS-APPEAL / HYPE POTENTIAL AUDIT

Before greenlight, test the concept for audience transmission.

Mandatory questions:

- Can a young reader retell the premise to a friend in one or two sentences?
- Is there a concrete “Прикинь, там…” hook?
- Is there an emotionally divisive question people can argue about?
- Are there 5–10 memorable scenes / discoveries / images with trailer-level clarity?
- Is there at least one relationship the reader urgently wants to resolve?
- Is there a mystery / objective / promise that creates forward pull?
- Does the story contain surprise without cheating?
- Is the premise distinct from the preceding IVDIVO book?
- Does the ending create satisfaction before the series hook?

A book may still be excellent without mass appeal, but the studio must not label a concept “mass / hit-oriented” if these tests fail.

---

# 9. STORY FREEZE — SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH

This section directly prevents the failure: **development says one thing, drafting writes another, editing creates a third book, assembly produces a fourth.**

Before prose production begins, create a frozen `BOOK CONTRACT` with a version number.

It contains at minimum:

- Main Story Contract;
- ending contract;
- protagonist / POV map;
- core character states;
- A/B/C line hierarchy;
- Case / Quest ladder;
- mystery reveal order if applicable;
- world-rule constraints;
- key setup/payoff promises;
- chapter / movement architecture;
- no-retcon locks from previous books.

The drafting stage must cite and obey this exact version.

## NO SILENT DRIFT

If drafting discovers a better direction, the writer **does not silently change the book**.

Create a `CHANGE REQUEST`:

- what changes;
- why;
- which chapters / characters / clues / payoffs are affected;
- whether the ending changes;
- whether continuity changes;
- what must be rewritten.

Then publish a new Book Contract version.

Core premise, main story, protagonist, climax or ending changes require explicit Founder awareness / approval before they become the new production baseline.

---

# 10. VERSION-LOCKED PRODUCTION BUNDLE

Every major novel should maintain these artefacts or equivalent structured sections:

- `BOOK_CONTRACT_vX.Y`
- `MAIN_STORY_SPINE_vX.Y`
- `CASE_OR_QUEST_LADDER_vX.Y`
- `CHARACTER_AND_RELATIONSHIP_WEB_vX.Y`
- `EVERYDAY_LIFE_MAP_vX.Y`
- `MYSTERY_EVIDENCE_LEDGER_vX.Y` when relevant
- `SCENE_CAUSALITY_LEDGER_vX.Y`
- `CHANGE_LOG`
- `QUALITY_GATE_REPORT`
- `MASTER_MANUSCRIPT_vX.Y`

All documents in a drafting pass must identify the Book Contract version they implement.

No chapter is allowed to be assembled from an incompatible contract version without an explicit reconciliation pass.

---

# 11. CHAPTER / SCENE CAUSALITY GATE

Every scene must justify its existence.

Record:

- POV;
- immediate desire;
- obstacle;
- conflict / pressure;
- turn;
- result;
- what changes after the scene;
- which later scene depends on this change.

A scene is suspect when its only function is:

- explain lore;
- repeat known emotion;
- move characters between locations;
- show “normal life” without consequence;
- restate the main problem;
- deliver information that could be dramatized.

Quiet scenes are allowed and necessary, but they must alter understanding, relationship, decision, status, expectation or emotional state in a way that matters later.

---

# 12. PRODUCTION PASSES — DO NOT MIX THEM

Do not attempt story design, line editing, continuity repair and final assembly as one undifferentiated operation.

Recommended order:

**PASS 0 — Source / craft study**  
Mechanism extraction from relevant works and criticism.

**PASS 1 — Main Story / Case Engine**  
Zero Gate and ending contract.

**PASS 2 — Character / Relationship / Everyday Life**  
Human stakes and causal braid.

**PASS 3 — Mystery / Adventure / Genre Engineering**  
Clue ladder, set pieces, objectives, reversals, clocks and payoffs.

**PASS 4 — Composition**  
POV, revelation order, movement architecture, setups/payoffs.

**PASS 5 — Scene Architecture**  
Causal scene cards.

**PASS 6 — Voice Prototypes**  
Representative scenes before full drafting.

**PASS 7 — Draft**  
Write to the frozen Book Contract.

**PASS 8 — Developmental Edit**  
Story, causality, character, pacing; no cosmetic line-edit focus yet.

**PASS 9 — Prose / Line Edit**  
Voice, diction, syntax, paragraph rhythm, imagery, dialogue, compression.

**PASS 10 — Continuity / Canon Regression**  
Dates, rules, previous-book locks, technology, knowledge states.

**PASS 11 — Assembly**  
Assemble approved chapter versions. Assembly is not a creative rewrite stage.

**PASS 12 — Whole-Book Regression / Red Team**  
Read the complete manuscript as a reader would; compare it against the Book Contract and opening promises.

A pass may send the work backwards. It may not secretly redefine the target.

---

# 13. ASSEMBLY IS NOT WRITING

The assembler is forbidden to “improve” story, scene logic, character motivation, clues or ending while merging files.

Assembly duties:

- choose the approved source version;
- preserve exact chapter order;
- remove duplication / technical seams only when authorised;
- verify chapter boundaries;
- verify no text disappeared;
- verify no older rejected version re-entered.

If a story problem is found during assembly, assembly stops and returns the problem to the correct production pass.

---

# 14. EDITING MAY NOT CHANGE THE BOOK BY ACCIDENT

Developmental editing may recommend structural change, but structural change must update the Book Contract.

Line editing may improve language, but may not silently change:

- factual meaning;
- character intention;
- clue strength;
- legal / ethical implication;
- world mechanics;
- outcome of a scene;
- chronology;
- setup/payoff relationship.

When a line edit changes any of these, it becomes a development change and must be reviewed as such.

---

# 15. ENDING CONTRACT

Before drafting, define what the reader is promised will be resolved.

The book may leave larger mysteries open, but it must close the main story.

Required final check:

- What concrete problem existed after the inciting event?
- What happened to it?
- What did the protagonist choose?
- What was won, lost, saved, built, discovered, proven, prevented or accepted?
- Which relationships changed?
- What consequences remain?
- What new hook begins **after** the main payoff rather than replacing it?

If the answer is only “the mystery deepens” or “the heroes prepare for the next book”, the ending fails.

---

# 16. CRITIC / RED-TEAM GATE

Before manuscript approval, independent review must attempt to prove that the book fails.

At minimum test:

- boring opening;
- vague objective;
- passive protagonist;
- repeated middle;
- fake obstacles;
- convenient stupidity;
- deus ex machina;
- cheating mystery;
- unearned romance;
- exposition disguised as dialogue;
- adult voices in teen bodies;
- worldbuilding replacing action;
- technology used only when convenient;
- scenes without causal output;
- weak midpoint;
- climax solved by someone else;
- ending that does not pay the opening;
- sequel bait replacing resolution;
- prose cadence becoming templated / AI-like;
- assembly version drift.

A BLOCKER sends the book back to the relevant pass.

---

# 17. “LONGER” MEANS MORE PASSES, NOT PERFORMED WAITING

Quality is not measured by how many minutes the assistant appears to wait.

The studio must not claim to have worked in the background for 200 minutes when it cannot do so.

When the Founder asks for a deeper / slower / professional pass, the correct behaviour is:

- use the available source corpus;
- perform multiple explicit analytical passes;
- compare competing solutions;
- run criticism and red-team;
- avoid premature prose generation;
- show the artefacts and decisions produced by the deeper process.

The evidence of work is the **quality and traceability of the passes**, not a claimed elapsed time.

---

# 18. MASTER PROMPT — STORY-FIRST EXECUTION

Use the following internal instruction before creating or revising any major IVDIVO fiction:

> **Do not begin by writing prose. Identify the one main story first. State the inciting event, concrete objective, active opposition, stakes, pressure, escalation, crisis choice, climax action and resolved ending. Reject theme/worldbuilding as a substitute for plot. Build the Case/Quest ladder, then braid character relationships, school/work/home/sport/money and IVDIVO world mechanics into that same causal chain. Study relevant source mechanisms before freezing architecture. Freeze one Book Contract version as the single source of truth. No drafting, editing or assembly stage may silently change that contract. Any structural discovery creates a Change Request and regression audit. Perform independent story, genre, YA, character, composition, prose, audience, critic, SWOT, pre-mortem and red-team passes. Optimize for reader attachment, forward drive, surprise, discovery, emotional payoff, memorable scenes and a complete ending. Do not claim “100-specialist review” without recorded outputs from distinct specialist lenses. Do not promise a hit; maximize hit potential through craft and testing.**

---

# 19. FINAL ACCEPTANCE TEST

Before calling a book “ready”, a reviewer with no development explanation should be able to answer clearly:

- What happened at the beginning?
- What did the protagonists spend the book trying to do?
- Why was it difficult and urgent?
- What changed halfway through?
- What was the hardest choice?
- What happened in the climax?
- How did the main story end?
- Who changed and how?
- What mystery / discovery / relationship kept me reading?
- Which scenes do I remember?
- What did I experience or understand that I did not before?
- Why would I recommend this book to somebody else?

If several answers are vague, the work is not ready regardless of word count or production effort.
