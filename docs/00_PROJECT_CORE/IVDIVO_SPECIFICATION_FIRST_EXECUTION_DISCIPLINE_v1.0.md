# 0. FOUNDER DIRECTIVE

The central production requirement is not speed and not the number of roles, prompts, agents or documents.

> **First define in sufficient detail what the finished work must be in order to satisfy its artistic, narrative, emotional, genre, audience and franchise goals. Then execute that exact specification.**

The studio may develop hundreds or thousands of parameters when the work requires them. Complexity is acceptable. Drift is not.

The studio is forbidden to:

- improvise a different story during drafting;
- replace an approved dramatic engine with a different one because it is easier to write;
- change character motivation during line editing without reopening development;
- change the ending during assembly;
- invent new canon to patch a local scene unless the gap is explicitly opened for invention;
- silently reinterpret a Founder instruction into a weaker or more convenient version;
- treat a plausible first answer as the finished result merely because it can be generated quickly;
- declare quality because many specialist roles were named.

# 1. SPECIFICATION BEFORE EXECUTION

Before major prose production, create a **BOOK / STORY ACCEPTANCE SPECIFICATION**.

The specification is the single operational target for the work. It may be large. It must be explicit enough that another competent editor could compare the manuscript against it and determine whether the intended work was actually produced.

At minimum the specification covers the following layers.

## A. MAIN STORY

- one main story;
- inciting event;
- concrete objective;
- opposition;
- stakes;
- pressure / clock when relevant;
- escalation chain;
- midpoint model change;
- crisis choice;
- climax action;
- concrete ending;
- aftermath.

## B. GENRE ENGINE

- primary genre pleasure;
- secondary genre pleasures;
- mystery / investigation rules;
- adventure / quest engine;
- romance engine;
- thriller pressure;
- comedy / relief rhythm;
- discovery / wonder promises;
- genre-specific payoffs.

## C. CHARACTER SYSTEM

For every major character:

- desire;
- need;
- fear;
- wound / formative pressure when relevant;
- false strategy;
- strength;
- weakness;
- secret;
- social position;
- economic position;
- family relation;
- friendship relation;
- romantic relation;
- speech pattern;
- humour pattern;
- decision pattern under stress;
- arc start;
- irreversible decisions;
- arc end.

## D. RELATIONSHIP WEB

- start-state of each important relationship;
- points of attraction / friction;
- trust changes;
- betrayals / misunderstandings;
- repair attempts;
- dependency changes;
- final state;
- consequences for the main story.

## E. EVERYDAY-LIFE FABRIC

- school / university;
- work / jobs;
- money;
- transport;
- neighbourhood;
- gym / sport / field / court;
- clubs / arts / media / hobbies;
- food / cafés / shops / third places;
- family schedules;
- parties / dates / ordinary weekly events;
- social media / reputation;
- ordinary obligations that causally interfere with the main story.

## F. WORLD / TECHNOLOGY

- what exists;
- who has access;
- what is normal in this zone;
- what is rare;
- cost;
- limitations;
- ownership;
- legal status;
- infrastructure dependencies;
- security consequences;
- social consequences;
- continuity with previous books.

## G. COMPOSITION

- POV plan;
- revelation order;
- chapter / movement architecture;
- setup/payoff locations;
- information asymmetry between reader and characters;
- act / movement turns;
- quiet / intense rhythm;
- scene transitions;
- ending architecture.

## H. SCENE CRAFT

For every scene:

- scene purpose;
- POV desire;
- conflict;
- subtext;
- turn;
- result;
- causal output;
- later dependency;
- emotional colour;
- location function;
- what is shown rather than explained.

## I. PROSE / STYLE

- narrative distance;
- vocabulary range;
- POV-specific diction;
- sentence rhythm;
- paragraph rhythm;
- dialogue density;
- dialogue subtext;
- sensory detail;
- metaphor policy;
- imagery / motif system;
- humour register;
- prohibited tics;
- exposition limits;
- repetition limits;
- chapter-ending policy;
- line-level quality targets.

## J. AUDIENCE / POPULARITY POTENTIAL

Popularity cannot be guaranteed, but hit-potential can be engineered and tested.

The specification must define:

- one-sentence retellable hook;
- target emotional promise;
- target age / crossover audience;
- reasons to identify with the hero;
- reasons to argue about the book;
- 5–10 memorable / trailer-level moments;
- mystery or promise that creates forward pull;
- chapter-level retention strategy;
- novelty versus current IVDIVO books;
- shareable / discussable moral dilemma;
- satisfying payoff before series hook;
- risks of cliché / imitation / datedness.

## K. MEANING / EXPERIENCE

- what the reader discovers;
- what the reader feels rather than being told;
- what new human experience the story simulates;
- what question remains open;
- what is actually resolved;
- what educational or ethical insight emerges from consequences rather than lecture.

# 2. PARAMETER DEPTH IS NOT A PROBLEM

The studio is explicitly allowed to use:

- 100 parameters;
- 300 parameters;
- 1000 parameters;
- multiple ledgers;
- multiple specialist reviews;
- source studies;
- comparative matrices;
- SWOT;
- pre-mortem;
- red-team review;
- alternative-outline testing;
- scene simulations;
- prose prototypes;
- reader-path testing.

The question is not whether the preparation looks excessive.

The question is whether the preparation **reduces uncertainty about the finished work and prevents drift during execution**.

A thousand decorative parameters are useless. Fifty decisive parameters may be enough. The required depth is determined by the risk and ambition of the work.

---

# 3. NO OFF-SPEC IMPROVISATION

Once a specification is frozen, invention is constrained.

### Allowed invention

The writer may invent local details only inside areas that the specification deliberately leaves open, provided those details do not alter:

- the main story;
- character intention;
- major relationship trajectory;
- mystery logic;
- technology rules;
- chronology;
- climax;
- ending;
- thematic meaning;
- continuity locks.

### Not allowed

If a new idea changes any locked field, it is not a “small improvement”. It is a **CHANGE REQUEST** and must return to the correct development stage.

No silent substitution.

---

# 4. SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH

For each major work there must be one current authoritative production package.

Every draft, edit, scene rewrite and assembly pass must identify which package/version it implements.

If two documents disagree, production stops until the conflict is resolved.

The studio must never rely on “someone remembers how the book was supposed to work.”

The specification itself must contain the answer.

---

# 5. DEVELOPMENT, DRAFTING, EDITING AND ASSEMBLY HAVE DIFFERENT AUTHORITY

## Development

May design and redesign the story before freeze.

## Drafting

Executes the frozen specification. It may discover problems but may not silently redefine the target.

## Developmental editing

May propose structural changes, but those changes reopen the specification and create a new version.

## Line editing

May improve language, rhythm, diction, dialogue, clarity and compression. It may not change story facts, motives, clues, rules or outcomes without escalation.

## Continuity

Detects contradictions; it does not invent new lore to hide them.

## Assembly

Combines approved materials. It is not a creative rewriting stage.

## Final regression

Compares the finished manuscript against the frozen specification from first promise to final payoff.

---

# 6. REQUIRED QUALITY PASSES

For a major IVDIVO novel, the default is not “one brainstorm + one outline + draft”.

The studio should perform as many sequential passes as necessary, including at least:

1. source / craft study;
2. main-story alternatives;
3. premise / hook comparison;
4. genre-engine design;
5. character-system design;
6. relationship causality;
7. everyday-life integration;
8. mystery / clue / discovery engineering;
9. adventure / set-piece engineering;
10. social / economic / institutional logic;
11. world / technology logic;
12. composition alternatives;
13. ending alternatives;
14. SWOT;
15. pre-mortem;
16. literary-critic review;
17. YA reader-recognition review;
18. market / retellability review;
19. red-team attack;
20. freeze;
21. scene architecture;
22. voice prototypes;
23. draft;
24. developmental regression;
25. prose / line edit;
26. continuity regression;
27. assembly;
28. whole-book reader simulation;
29. final red-team;
30. acceptance against specification.

The studio is allowed to loop backwards whenever a gate fails.

---

# 7. TIME / DEPTH RULE

The studio must not optimise for the fastest possible answer when the user asked for production-quality work.

There is no requirement that a book architecture be produced in one short response or one reasoning pass.

The assistant cannot perform unsupervised background work for days or weeks. Therefore “take a week” is interpreted operationally as:

> **use as many explicit sequential analysis, source-study, comparison, red-team and revision passes as the available session/task requires before presenting the result as production-ready.**

Do not confuse speed with completion.

Do not present an early plausible concept as a finished architecture.

---

# 8. PROOF OF WORK

When a result is described as “studio-developed”, the record should show what was actually tested.

At minimum:

- alternatives considered;
- reasons for rejection;
- specialist findings;
- risks found;
- revisions made;
- unresolved uncertainties;
- current frozen version;
- final acceptance report.

Internal effort that leaves no effect on the artefact has no production value.

---

# 9. ACCEPTANCE, NOT INTENTION

The final question is not:

> “Did the team try to follow the plan?”

It is:

> **“Does the finished work actually satisfy the frozen acceptance specification?”**

For each required parameter, final regression returns:

`PASS / PARTIAL / FAIL / NOT APPLICABLE`.

Critical FAIL = work is not production-ready.

A high score in prose cannot compensate for a failed main story.

A strong mystery cannot compensate for characters the audience does not care about.

Excellent worldbuilding cannot compensate for missing payoff.

---

# 10. ANTI-DRIFT PRINCIPLE

The production system must preserve intent across the whole chain:

`FOUNDER INTENT → SPECIFICATION → STORY DESIGN → SCENE DESIGN → DRAFT → EDIT → ASSEMBLY → FINAL WORK`.

At every arrow, verify meaning did not mutate.

The most dangerous failure is not a visible typo. It is a manuscript that is polished, coherent and professionally written — but is no longer the book that was designed.

---

# 11. FOUNDER CORRECTION OVERRIDES CONVENIENCE

When the Founder identifies that production has drifted, simplified, substituted another problem, or delivered “something approximately similar”, the correct response is not to rationalise the existing output.

The studio must:

1. identify the exact mismatch;
2. update the specification if the Founder changed the target;
3. return to the earliest corrupted production stage;
4. redo dependent stages;
5. regression-test the new result.

The cost of rework does not justify preserving the wrong artefact.

---

# 12. CORE EXECUTION LAW

> **THINK BROADLY. SPECIFY PRECISELY. FREEZE THE TARGET. EXECUTE EXACTLY. TEST RUTHLESSLY. REVISE ONLY THROUGH CONTROLLED CHANGE.**

A large creative studio earns its value not by generating more ideas than the author, but by making sure the right idea survives intact from conception to the finished work.
