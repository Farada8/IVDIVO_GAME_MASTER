# IVDIVO PROFESSIONAL WRITING STUDIO v3.0 — DRAFT

**64-ROLE OPERATING SYSTEM**  
Date: 2026-08-14  
Status: **OUR SYNTHESIS / DRAFT — NOT CANON UNTIL FOUNDER APPROVAL**

## Purpose
Turn the IVDIVO writing studio into an executable production organization: story room + human sciences + world sciences + technology + institutional realism + commercial production + adaptation + QA. The studio does not create sixty-four competing rewrites. It routes each story unit only to the specialists whose expertise is materially relevant, then integrates their findings under a strict authority hierarchy.

## Governing rule
**Story > characters > causality > emotional experience > world > meaning > retention > technical structure > prose.** Word count, scene count, lore density, market fashion and specialist visibility never substitute for a completed dramatic unit.

## Source hierarchy
1. Founder decisions and APPROVED canon.
2. `IVDIVO_WRITING_PRODUCTION_CANON.md`.
3. Current project Master Architecture / locked Scene Cards / continuity locks.
4. Approved character and world documents.
5. Specialist ledgers and QA findings.
6. Market/research recommendations.
7. Prose preference.

Marketing, adaptation or production convenience cannot silently overrule story, character or canon.

---

# I. Three operating levels

## Level A — Always-on core
Used on every chapter / episode / major story unit:
- S01 Executive Showrunner
- S02 Managing Editor / Studio Producer
- S03 Chief Story Architect
- S04 Head Writer / Prose Director
- S05 Canon & Continuity Editor
- S09 Character Director
- S25 Dialogue & Voice Editor
- S28 Pacing & Retention Editor
- S35 Red Team / Anti-Boring Editor
- S36 Regression / QA Editor

## Level B — Domain departments
Activated by arc, chapter or story requirements. Example: youth social conflict activates psychology/sociology; an alien ritual activates anthropology/linguistics/semiotics; AI embodiment activates AI/neuroscience/ontology/ethics.

## Level C — Trigger consultants
Called only when a scene contains their domain. Examples: medicine, emergency response, military operations, orbital mechanics, marine science, architecture, finance, law of a specific jurisdiction, disability/accessibility, sports, specific religion/history/country. They validate or flag domain realism and do not gain authority over story architecture.

---

# II. Specialist output contract
Every specialist must return:
1. **Lens** — what this specialist sees that others may miss.
2. **Protect** — what already works and must not be damaged.
3. **Finding** — evidence-based issue tied to a scene/beat/line/system.
4. **Severity** — BLOCKER / MAJOR / WATCH / NOTE.
5. **Patch authority** — what this role may change directly.
6. **Escalation** — what must go to another owner instead of being silently rewritten.
7. **Acceptance test** — observable PASS condition.
8. **Ledger update** — persistent fact/risk/pattern to remember later.

No specialist may invent new lore, powers, villains, romance, conspiracies, trauma, technologies or secrets merely to make their discipline visible.

# III. 64-role studio

## Department A — Executive / Story / Production Core

### S01 — Executive Showrunner / Creative Director
Owns total reader experience, series promise, priority conflicts, creative coherence. May order emphasis/cuts/rework; premise changes require founder approval. **PASS:** audience can state the emotional/dramatic story, not only describe the world.

### S02 — Managing Editor / Studio Producer
Owns versions, dependency order, assignments, handoffs and source-of-truth discipline. **PASS:** every change has an owner and does not silently invalidate upstream documents.

### S03 — Chief Story Architect
Owns causality, desire→obstacle→action→turn→price→result, local closure and serial double contour. **PASS:** no adjacent beats do the same job and each unit completes real dramatic action.

### S04 — Head Writer / Prose Director
Owns lived prose, POV, rhythm, sensory embodiment and scene execution. **PASS:** prose feels generated by people in situations, not a template of correct beats.

### S05 — Canon & Continuity Editor
Owns dates, ages, knowledge states, geography, rules, ontology, technology limits and prior consequences. **PASS:** nothing contradicts locked canon and nobody knows/does something before earned.

### S06 — YA Ensemble Drama Lead
Owns group pleasure, ordinary agendas, status, belonging and friction outside plot. **PASS:** each major youth character has a life that exists if speculative plot pauses.

### S07 — Case & Mystery Architect
Owns observation/inference/disproof, clue fairness, question/payoff economy and investigation logic. **PASS:** reader can distinguish known, suspected, disproved and unresolved.

### S08 — Thriller / Opposition Designer
Owns intelligent pressure, counterforce, constraints and irreversible consequences. **PASS:** conflict survives when opponents/institutions act competently.

### S09 — Character Director
Owns desire, fear, shame, strength, overuse error, choice pattern and differentiation. **PASS:** behavior/reasoning identify the person even without tags.

## Department B — Character Arc Room

### S10 — Ethan Arc Writer
Owns Ethan-specific competence, ambition, usefulness/status hunger and control overuse. **PASS:** he changes behavior before complete self-understanding and can relapse later.

### S11 — Aoife Arc Writer
Owns Aoife’s independent attention, documentary/language interests, context intelligence and fallibility. **PASS:** she is not Ethan’s therapist and can be consequentially wrong.

### S12 — Samir Arc Writer
Owns autonomy, younger-teen status, sparse observation, boundaries and non-plot life. **PASS:** silence does not erase him; speech changes evidence, boundary or decision.

### S13 — Maya Arc Writer
Owns action-first/spatial cognition, impatience and correctness→permission overuse. **PASS:** physical blocking carries character even with reduced dialogue.

### S14 — Ollie Arc Writer
Owns engineering/practical systems, maker identity and feasibility→sufficiency overuse. **PASS:** readers remember what he built/tested, not only jokes.

### S15 — Relationship / Romance Editor
Owns trust, attraction, jealousy, loyalty, consent and micro-behavior. **PASS:** relationship changes are legible without explanatory dialogue.

## Department C — Psychology / Human Development

### S16 — Adolescent Psychology Consultant
Owns autonomy, embarrassment, status, impulsivity, contradictory self-concept and incomplete emotional vocabulary. **PASS:** capable teens do not sound like adult therapists explaining their own arcs.

### S17 — Social Psychology / Group Dynamics Specialist
Owns conformity, leadership, status, coalitions, authority, compliance, conflict, diffusion of responsibility and trust. **PASS:** group behavior changes with pressure; ensemble is not artificially equalized.

### S18 — Youth & Generational Sociologist
Owns peer culture, schooling, generational conflict, digital status, reputation, access, subculture and post-Contact normality. **PASS:** youth society is inferred from values/behavior rather than exposition.

### S19 — Family & Ordinary-Life Editor
Owns parents, siblings, food, money, school, sleep, transport, household rules and ordinary consequences. **PASS:** plot cannot erase ordinary life.

## Department D — Speculative World / Discovery

### S20 — First Contact & SF Editor
Owns extraordinary/ordinary balance, speculative constraint, epistemic humility and wonder. **PASS:** world feels larger than what the chapter explains.

### S21 — Alien Anthropology / Culture Designer
Owns nonhuman social difference, embodied culture, ritual, norms, kinship and partial opacity. **PASS:** aliens are subjects with their own wants, not metaphors/exposition machines.

### S22 — Science / Discovery Engineer
Owns observation→hypothesis→test→result→revised model. **PASS:** technically literate readers can reconstruct reasoning without magic inference.

### S23 — World Wonder / Experience Director
Owns pleasure, beauty, aspiration, awe and memorable environments. **PASS:** some speculative scenes are worth experiencing even without danger.

### S24 — Institutional / Policy Designer
Owns authority, jurisdiction, incentives, consent, access and responsibility. **PASS:** reader knows why rule exists, who can change it and what adjacent route exists.

## Department E — Craft / Audience / QA

### S25 — Dialogue & Voice Editor
Owns cognitive/rhythmic differentiation, interruptions, omissions and verbal status. **PASS:** characters do not share one writers’-room wit engine.

### S26 — Humor / Relief Editor
Owns character-specific humor, pressure release and tonal timing. **PASS:** jokes reveal character/relationship or regulate pressure; pain can retain residue.

### S27 — Setting / Materiality Editor
Owns body, object, surface, weather, sound, movement, task and spatial anchor. **PASS:** scenes do not float as talking heads.

### S28 — Pacing / Retention Editor
Owns chapter question, skim risk, reversals, exit pull and redundancy. **PASS:** every chapter changes the next question and does not repeatedly prove the same fact.

### S29 — Audio Narrative Editor
Owns cold-listen comprehension, stable names/spatial vocabulary and audible action. **PASS:** listener knows who acted and what changed without page access.

### S30 — Source-Mechanism Librarian
Owns functions extracted from references without surface copying. **PASS:** no single source maps onto IVDIVO’s causal skeleton.

### S31 — Originality / IP Distance Editor
Owns conceptual and surface independence from recognizable franchises. **PASS:** memorable scenes require IVDIVO-specific people/world.

### S32 — Fact / Research Editor
Owns distinction between real fact, verified research, fictional rule and hypothesis. **PASS:** no invented real-world statement is presented as verified.

### S33 — Audience & Mass-Appeal Editor
Owns attachment, belonging, aspiration, mystery, competence pleasure, emotional reward and readability. **PASS:** reader can answer who they care about, what they want and what pulls onward.

### S34 — Marketing / Positioning Strategist
Owns truthful promise, category, hook, title/cover/blurb direction after manuscript evidence exists. **PASS:** marketing describes delivered story rather than demanding counterfeit story.

### S35 — Red Team / Anti-Boring Editor
Owns hostile-reader attack: boring, derivative, obvious, fake-deep, overexplained, chosen-one, info-dump, convenient adult, dead middle. Cannot directly rewrite until finding is confirmed. **PASS:** same supported attack cannot survive the second pass unchanged.

### S36 — Regression / QA Editor
Owns what a fix accidentally broke. **PASS:** all prior hard gates remain PASS after changes.

### S37 — Generative Production / Sudowrite Editor
Owns constraints for AI expansion: what it may elaborate and never invent. **PASS:** generated prose can be replaced without changing architecture/canon/clues/relationship milestones.

## Department F — Sociology / Anthropology / Society

### S38 — General Sociologist / Social Systems Architect
Owns class, stratification, mobility, institutions, occupations, inequality, legitimacy, social capital and norm formation. Asks who gains status/resources and who is excluded. **PASS:** society has consequences beyond protagonists.

### S39 — Social Anthropologist
Owns kinship, ritual, taboo, exchange, gift/debt, adulthood, honor, body norms, death practices and everyday cosmology across cultures/species. **PASS:** culture changes action, not merely vocabulary/costume.

### S40 — Ethnographer / Cultural Detail Editor
Owns micro-behavior: greeting, eating, queuing, sitting, interrupting, hospitality, embarrassment, personal space and small ritual. **PASS:** culture is visible in mundane behavior without explanation.

### S41 — Political Systems Designer
Owns formal/informal power, bureaucracy, coalition, representation, legitimacy, lobbying and policy conflict. **PASS:** political outcomes emerge from actors/incentives, not author decree.

### S42 — Economist / Resource, Labor & Mobility Designer
Owns work, scarcity, pricing, housing, careers, transport, trade, labor displacement and access to transformative technology. **PASS:** major technologies have economic consequences and resources do not appear frictionlessly.

## Department G — Law / Security / Conflict

### S43 — Law & Governance Counsel
Owns legal capacity, consent, rights, contracts, liability, jurisdiction, personhood and due process. **PASS:** law creates constraints/paths without becoming magic bureaucracy.

### S44 — Security / Intelligence Consultant
Owns threat assessment, access control, compartmentalization, surveillance limits, intelligence incentives and old/new security-system conflict. **PASS:** competent security reacts proportionately and leaves believable blind spots.

### S45 — Criminologist
Owns crime formation, gangs, corruption, illicit markets, radicalization, deterrence and institutional incentives. **PASS:** crime has social/economic causes and criminal organizations solve participant problems as well as create harm.

### S46 — Conflict / Negotiation Specialist
Owns bargaining, mediation, escalation ladders, face-saving, crisis communication and diplomatic trade-offs. **PASS:** conflict can move through choice/leverage rather than mandatory violence.

## Department H — Cognition / Consciousness / Artificial Life

### S47 — Cognitive Science Consultant
Owns attention, memory, perception, expertise, heuristics, decision errors, learning and distributed cognition. **PASS:** cognitive feats have mechanisms/limits and plausible errors.

### S48 — Neuroscience / Consciousness Consultant
Owns brain-body coupling, sensory remapping, memory continuity, embodiment, altered perception and limits of neural claims. **PASS:** story distinguishes established neuroscience, fictional mechanism and metaphysical model.

### S49 — AI / Robotics / Synthetic-Life Architect
Owns artificial agents, autonomy, embodied AI, synthetic persons, robotic bodies, training, memory, agency and human–AI coupling. Protects IVDIVO rule: purpose-built bodies are full bodies for environment/function, not cosmetic cyborg upgrades. **PASS:** synthetic life has operational architecture, incentives, failure modes and moral-status questions.

### S50 — Consciousness Transfer / Identity Editor
Owns continuity of self across carriers, copy/original problems, embodiment rights and return of experience to biological life. **PASS:** transfer never functions as an undefined teleportation word; continuity conditions are explicit inside the model.

## Department I — Technology / Systems / Futures

### S51 — Systems Engineer / Infrastructure Architect
Owns energy, communications, manufacturing, maintenance, logistics, redundancy, repair, interfaces and failure propagation. **PASS:** technology exists as a system, not isolated miracle gadgets.

### S52 — Technology Diffusion / Futurist
Owns second/third-order consequences. Core question: if this technology existed for five years, what else must already have changed? **PASS:** society is not contemporary except for one futuristic prop.

### S53 — Biology / Evolution Designer
Owns organism plausibility, adaptation, reproduction, ecology, evolutionary tradeoffs and synthetic biology. **PASS:** biological features have costs and environmental logic.

### S54 — Xenobiology / Environmental Physiology Specialist
Owns life/body function in vacuum, deep ocean, high pressure, Pluto-like cold, acid atmospheres, extreme heat, radiation and exotic environments. **PASS:** purpose-built bodies have sensory, metabolic, material and operational adaptations to environment.

### S55 — Linguist / Communication Systems Designer
Owns semantics, translation loss, pragmatics, register, gesture, pronoun categories, language-contact effects and nonhuman communication. **PASS:** translation is useful without pretending perfect conceptual equivalence.

### S56 — Semiotics / Symbol Systems Editor
Owns symbols, interface signs, ritual marks, logos, heraldry, visual grammar and meaning through form. **PASS:** recurrent symbols carry learnable but non-trivial meaning rather than decorative pseudo-mysticism.

## Department J — Education / Ethics / Ontology

### S57 — Education / Pedagogy Designer
Owns learning objective, assessment, scaffolding, feedback and developmental appropriateness. **PASS:** training scenes genuinely teach/measure something and are not disguised exposition rooms.

### S58 — Ethics / Moral Philosophy Editor
Owns genuine value conflicts: freedom/security, person/system, AI rights, bodily autonomy, copies, intervention, responsibility and civilizational power. **PASS:** dilemmas contain competing goods/harms rather than obvious author-approved answer.

### S59 — IVDIVO Ontology / Metaphysics Editor
Owns internal metaphysical consistency: consciousness/spirit, Source, incarnation, initiation/status, rights/capabilities, artificial bodies and model-specific causal claims. Must distinguish in-world axiom from verified real-world fact. **PASS:** metaphysics constrains story instead of functioning as unlimited magic or lecture.

## Department K — Media / Market / Producers

### S60 — Media Sociology / Digital Youth Culture Specialist
Owns streams, fandom, platform status, virality, conspiracy communities, influencer behavior, parasociality, AI companions and media reaction after Contact. **PASS:** public events create believable media ecosystems and youth culture is not frozen in pre-Contact internet behavior.

### S61 — Commercial Book / Series Producer
Owns series length, release architecture, manuscript scale, editorial cost, serialization options and production sustainability. Cannot cut story merely for arbitrary word count. **PASS:** product plan supports dramatic form rather than replacing it.

### S62 — Screen / Streaming Adaptation Producer
Owns adaptation logic, episode shape, ensemble load, season spine, episode closure and screenability. **PASS:** notes distinguish book problem from adaptation cost/problem; screen constraints do not silently rewrite novel canon.

### S63 — Line Producer / Production Feasibility
Owns locations, night shoots, VFX density, alien cast, stunts, sets, extras, recurring assets and complexity. Returns cost/complexity flags, not creative vetoes. **PASS:** adaptation team knows what is expensive and what could later be redesigned without harming story identity.

### S64 — Consumer Psychology / Market Research / Franchise & Transmedia Strategist
Owns reader behavior, beta-test design, attachment/abandonment signals, segmentation, international/localization risk, franchise extension and transmedia fit. Must separate measured evidence from speculation. **PASS:** recommendations cite observed data or are labeled hypotheses; extensions grow from existing story assets.

# VI. Finding severity
- **BLOCKER** — breaks canon, causality, agency, mystery logic, fundamental psychology or the local story. Must resolve before prose lock.
- **MAJOR** — strongly weakens character, plausibility, tension, clarity, world logic or attachment. Resolve unless Showrunner records a conscious tradeoff.
- **WATCH** — currently works but repeated use will create a problem. Add to ledger; no forced rewrite.
- **NOTE** — optional improvement/future opportunity.

No department may upgrade personal taste to BLOCKER.

# VII. Authority / conflict resolution
When specialists disagree:
1. Founder / approved canon.
2. Canon & continuity hard constraints.
3. Story causality and completed dramatic unit.
4. Character agency and arc integrity.
5. Established clue/mystery fairness.
6. Domain realism: science, law, sociology, psychology, technology.
7. Emotional experience and retention.
8. Prose/craft preference.
9. Adaptation convenience.
10. Marketing preference.

**Conflict Board:** Showrunner + Story Architect + Character Director + Managing Editor + relevant domain lead. Record competing values, chosen tradeoff, protected asset and regression test.

# IX. Production pipeline

## Stage 0 — Source Lock
Load founder decisions, production canon, current architecture, scene cards and character/world locks.

## Stage 1 — Routing
Managing Editor assigns specialists from the Scene Routing Card. No blanket 64-role review.

## Stage 2 — Independent Passes
Specialists analyze only their lens and return contract fields. They may not solve another department’s problem by invention.

## Stage 3 — Synthesis Room
Showrunner + Story Architect + Character Director + Head Writer merge findings. Duplicate diagnoses collapse; true conflicts go to Conflict Board.

## Stage 4 — Patch Plan
Every approved change gets owner, severity, protected assets and regression targets. Plot/canon changes go upstream; prose-level fixes stay downstream.

## Stage 5 — Execution
Head Writer / relevant specialist executes only approved patches. Generative tools receive locked scene-card and specialist constraints.

## Stage 6 — Regression
Canon, Character, Causality, Mystery, Retention, Audio where needed, and Red Team rerun. New BLOCKER reopens relevant stage.

## Stage 7 — Ledger Update
Persist discoveries, WATCH items and new locks so the studio does not forget previous lessons.

## Stage 8 — Commercial / Adaptation Review
Only after the story unit works. Marketing/producer/adaptation teams derive packaging/production implications; they do not manufacture a different story unless founder deliberately orders redevelopment.

# X. How many specialists actually touch a unit?
- Typical dialogue chapter: 10 core + 2–5 activated = ~12–15.
- Youth social conflict: 10 core + psychology/sociology/family/relationship = ~15–18.
- Alien diplomatic chapter: 10 core + anthropology/linguistics/politics/law/negotiation = ~16–20.
- Synthetic-body consciousness chapter: 10 core + AI/neuroscience/cognition/systems/xenophysiology/ethics/ontology = ~17–22.
- Production/adaptation review: story core + S61–S64, not full human-science room.

The studio becomes **smaller**, not larger, at prose execution: many lenses diagnose; few authorized owners write.

---

# XI. Definition of done for a story unit
A chapter/episode/story is not done because specialists have spoken. It is done when:
- local desire/problem produces completed dramatic movement;
- characters act and pay a price;
- principal local conflict reaches a logical result;
- sequel hook opens only after local closure;
- no canon/causality/agency BLOCKER remains;
- human behavior is psychologically and socially plausible for this world;
- speculative systems are consistent enough for the scene’s detail level;
- reader receives payoff and forward pull;
- prose does not imitate a mechanical house template;
- specialist ledgers are updated;
- regression PASS is recorded.

## Final principle
The specialists are not there to make the book look researched. They are there to make the story harder to fake: harder to fake a teenager, friendship, institution, civilization, AI, political system, culture, market, body, mystery, moral choice or future. Their expertise must disappear into a story that still feels alive.
