# FOUNDER CORRECTION — ORBITAL CIVILIZATION ECONOMY / ROBOTS / TECHNOLOGY BOUNDARIES

Date: 2026-08-16
Status: **FOUNDER DIRECTIVE / CANON CORRECTION**
Authority: newest direct Founder instruction; overrides conflicting working drafts/options.

## 1. MONEY — NOT PART OF ORBITAL CITY LIFE

In the mature orbital cities currently being developed for the IVDIVO youth line, **money is no longer used as the ordinary organizing mechanism of life**.

Money has already been judged a low-level / low-efficiency motivational and economic-development mechanism compared with the civilization's later systems.

Therefore the following are **REJECTED for Orbital Youth unless a specific historical/enclave exception is later ordered by Founder**:

- wages as reason to work;
- rent as reason to keep housing;
- paid first job as economic survival milestone;
- rich-versus-poor conflict driven by purchasing power;
- credits functioning as disguised money;
- shops/markets as primary material distribution system;
- company-building primarily for profit;
- eviction because a teenager cannot pay;
- scarcity of ordinary manufactured goods as a normal social engine.

## 2. MATERIAL ECONOMY

The material base is highly automated.

**Robotic production can manufacture essentially any ordinary object, system or technology available to the civilization.**

Civilizational leadership / governance is responsible for maintaining the resource base, including extraction from:

- asteroids;
- planets;
- other available sources within the Solar System.

The Solar System contains all or almost all material resources required for ordinary orbital civilization.

### Consequence

Ordinary manufactured objects are not meaningful plot stakes merely because they are expensive.

The remaining high-value human stakes must come from things that cannot be solved simply by fabricating another object:

- relationship;
- trust;
- reputation;
- recognition;
- responsibility;
- belonging;
- access to a specific project/mission/event;
- unique lived experience;
- competition;
- creative authorship;
- consent;
- social influence;
- education / mastery;
- personal choice;
- limited time windows;
- unique places or historical objects;
- governance decisions;
- safety and coordination under real conditions.

These are **WORKING consequences**, not a hidden replacement currency. Do not turn them into points/credits without Founder instruction.

## 3. WORK / CONTRIBUTION

Humans may still study, train, research, teach, create, explore, organize, govern, care for others, compete, perform, investigate and participate in technical work.

But the primary motivation is **not survival income**.

A youth character may want a place in a laboratory, sport team, expedition, artistic collective, civic project, school, research direction or public role because it offers:

- meaning;
- mastery;
- identity;
- status/recognition;
- responsibility;
- belonging;
- adventure;
- influence;
- service;
- curiosity;
- personal ambition.

Do not reinsert capitalism by renaming salary as “credits.”

## 4. HOUSING

Housing exists as ordinary infrastructure of orbital life, but **rent is not the governing mechanism**.

Housing stories may concern:

- privacy;
- independence from parents;
- living with friends;
- preferred district / gravity / culture / proximity;
- family rules;
- incompatible lifestyles;
- mixed-species environmental needs;
- social belonging;
- temporary safety relocation;
- responsibility for shared space.

Do not make “cannot afford room” the default problem.

## 5. ROBOTS — SEPARATE CONSCIOUS CIVILIZATION

Robots are **not merely tools or owned AI servants**.

They constitute a separate civilization and possess their own consciousness.

Therefore:

- robotic persons have their own subjectivity;
- their role in production must not be written as slave labor by default;
- human ownership language must not be casually applied to conscious robotic persons;
- robot culture, goals, rights, disagreements and independent interests may exist;
- a robot character is not automatically a human mind inside a machine.

The existence of conscious robotic civilization is distinct from human consciousness-transfer technology.

## 6. NO HUMAN CONSCIOUSNESS / SOUL TRANSFER TECHNOLOGY IN ORBITAL CIVILIZATION

At the current Orbital Youth civilization level:

- there is **no operational technology for transferring human consciousness into another body**;
- there is **no technology for transferring / resettling the soul**;
- do not treat robotic bodies as upload vessels for humans;
- do not use “synthetic body = human consciousness moved into robot” as ordinary orbital technology;
- do not use subspace as a casual mechanism for consciousness relocation.

Any later IVDIVO embodiment line must be introduced only when the saga earns it and must not be back-projected into ordinary orbital civilization.

## 7. DIMENSIONAL / MULTI-WORLD KNOWLEDGE

Knowledge of dimensional worlds is **not general civilizational knowledge among orbital inhabitants**.

The mainstream orbital civilization does not live with a public, mature science/technology of dimensional worlds.

However, **separate schools, directions, traditions or specialist circles may possess or teach partial knowledge about these matters**.

Therefore world reveal must distinguish:

`MAINSTREAM ORBITAL KNOWLEDGE`
from
`SPECIALIST SCHOOL / DIRECTION KNOWLEDGE`
from
`LATER SAGA REALITY`.

Do not let a niche school's claims become public technological fact merely because the reader knows the larger saga may eventually validate some of them.

## 8. STORY CONSEQUENCES FOR ORBITAL YOUTH

Orbital adolescence must not be a 2020s Earth teen drama with futuristic decoration.

Because money/material scarcity no longer organizes daily life, youth conflict shifts toward:

- Why do anything when survival does not require a job?
- What is a meaningful contribution when robots can fabricate almost anything?
- What makes achievement yours when production is easy?
- What can still be prestigious when objects are abundant?
- How do you gain trust for a dangerous or important role?
- What does independence mean when housing and food do not depend on income?
- How do romance, jealousy, embarrassment and status behave when material wealth loses much of its signalling power?
- What does a young person choose to become when they are not forced into a profession for survival?
- How do humans coexist with a conscious robotic civilization that is not subordinate to them?
- Why are some specialist schools interested in dimensional/ontological questions that mainstream civilization does not yet understand?

These are story generators, not exposition topics. Every novel still requires one concrete main story with a closed local conflict.

## 9. IMMEDIATE REJECTIONS / REVISIONS

The following recent working concepts are invalid in their current form:

### `THE MISSING WATER`
REJECT CURRENT VERSION because its engine relies on paid placement, worker housing, rent/credits, economic displacement and resource scarcity logic inconsistent with this correction. A physical-system mystery may be reused only after rebuilding motivation and stakes from the correct orbital economy.

### `FIRST RENT`
REJECT as currently framed. Rent/lease scarcity is incompatible with the current orbital social model.

### `NIGHT SHIFT`
REVISE if reused: media/public responsibility may remain; paid-employment survival stakes must be removed.

### `SPIN CHANGE`
POTENTIALLY COMPATIBLE after removing housing-income/service-class assumptions. Sport, belonging, secrecy, friendship, changing station geometry and rescue/search stakes remain usable.

### `GRAVITY LEAGUE`
POTENTIALLY COMPATIBLE. Sport, recognition, team identity, body/safety rules and competition survive the economy correction.

## 10. PRODUCTION GATE — ORBITAL ECONOMY / TECHNOLOGY

Before any Orbital Youth scene is approved, test:

1. Does the scene accidentally use money, rent, wages or purchasing power as ordinary motivation?
2. Could a robot fabricate the material object being treated as scarce? If yes, why is it still narratively scarce?
3. Is the real stake social, temporal, relational, ethical, experiential, positional or safety-based?
4. Is a conscious robot being treated as a tool/owned product?
5. Is human consciousness transfer being smuggled in through synthetic-body language?
6. Is dimensional-world knowledge being presented as mainstream when it belongs only to specialist schools/directions?
7. Does the conflict still produce a concrete goal, resistance, choice, price and resolution?

If 1, 4, 5 or 6 fail: **STOP / REBUILD BEFORE PROSE**.
