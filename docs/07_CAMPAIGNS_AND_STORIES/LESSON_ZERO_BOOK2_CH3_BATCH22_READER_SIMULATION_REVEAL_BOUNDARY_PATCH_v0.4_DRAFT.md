# REPLACEMENT PASSAGE

Replace the block beginning with **“Samir had stopped looking at the Saturday trace.”** and ending immediately before **“Voss changed the simulation.”** with the following:

Samir had stopped looking at the Saturday trace.

He was looking at the transition diagram beneath it.

Ethan waited.

It took effort.

That was useful information about himself.

Samir enlarged one relation state. Then another.

The transition did not depend only on the current participant state. It depended on who had moved first.

If Mel corrected toward Taven, one next state became available. If Taven corrected toward Mel, the system assigned a different confidence update.

Same two people. Same final position. Different path through the model because actor order changed.

Ollie came closer.

“Is that normal?”

Voss answered before Samir did.

“In relational systems? It can be.”

Samir nodded slowly.

Ethan looked at him.

There was something in the nod he recognized from the old archive work: not certainty. Recognition trying not to become certainty too early.

“What are you seeing?” Ethan asked.

Samir kept his eyes on the wall.

“Actor order changes the next available relation state.”

“Yes,” Voss said.

“I have seen that kind of move before.”

Ethan felt the old names arrive anyway.

R-14. Insert 7C. Root Library.

He kept them to himself.

Voss turned toward Samir.

“In current civic routing?”

“No.”

“Where?”

Samir considered the question.

“Some older material I do not want to characterize from memory.”

That was not the answer Ethan wanted.

It was probably the right one.

Voss looked back at the transition diagram.

“Then do not characterize it from memory.”

Samir glanced at her.

“Can I see the published lineage for this component?”

“Yes.”

“How far back?”

“Twelve years in pieces. Four years as an integrated civic model. Earlier references vary by component.”

“Participant data?”

“Not for the lineage review.”

“My record?”

“I have not requested it.”

Samir nodded once.

“Good.”

Voss opened a reference panel but did not expand it yet.

“If you find a structural resemblance, I want the comparison done against the documented implementation, not against what either of us remembers.”

Samir looked at her for a second longer.

“That is what I want too.”

Ethan looked from the reference panel to the transition diagram.

He wanted to ask whether familiar meant related.

He wanted to ask whether related meant inherited.

He wanted to ask how many older systems had used the same move and whether anybody already knew why.

Those were three different questions.

He did not turn them into one.

“Okay,” he said. “Lineage first.”

Voss gave him a brief look.

Not approval exactly.

That helped.

# WHY THIS PATCH IS STRONGER

## 1. Reader participation survives

The reader receives a concrete anomaly and the fact of Samir's familiarity, but not the answer. This creates a provisional model rather than delivering a solved comparison.

## 2. Samir owns the epistemic boundary

He refuses to characterize the older material from memory. This is consistent with Book One's separation of observation, interpretation and record rights.

## 3. Ethan's growth is behavioral

He experiences the urge to collapse three questions into one conclusion and does not do it. The prose demonstrates growth without narrating a moral lesson.

## 4. Voss remains competent

She does not become evasive or sinister. She explicitly prefers documented comparison over memory, preserving the stronger non-villain institutional conflict.

## 5. Later reveal regains ownership

The explicit structural comparison can now occur in the chapter designed to own it, producing genuine recognition rather than repetition.
