# 0. Non-negotiable Book-2 tests

Every candidate must pass:

1. one concrete main problem;
2. one hero with a pre-existing ordinary want;
3. orbit creates the problem, not merely the scenery;
4. housing / work / money / transport / maintenance / relationships appear causally;
5. adults remain competent;
6. youth matter because of position, relationships, access or choices—not because adults inexplicably disappear;
7. local conflict fully resolves;
8. one small saga hook only after resolution;
9. no Smith takeover;
10. no full consciousness-transfer/metaphysical dump;
11. if moved to an Earth campus with renamed rooms, the plot breaks.

# OPTION A — THE MISSING WATER
## Hero candidate: OLLIE
## Genre: orbital social mystery / engineering adventure / coming-of-age

### HERO
Ollie, now in an orbital residency/apprenticeship period, wants his **first real paid technical placement** and enough income/credit to remain in orbit without treating the Youth Cohort as his only identity.

### WANT
Pass probation, keep the job, prove his ordinary maker/maintenance competence has value in a city full of Confederation technology.

### WHY NOW
His first month of orbital housing is conditional on work/training participation. He is finally living away from Earth routines rather than visiting a facility.

### INCITING INCIDENT
A closed-loop water audit finds a growing unexplained loss in the working/service residential ring where Ollie and several young workers live.

It is not yet a life-support emergency, but the deficit is large enough that station operations announces:

**find/isolate the loss within a fixed number of days or reduce occupancy / close several residential modules for safety.**

People may lose rooms, shifts and orbital placements.

### CONCRETE OBJECTIVE
Find where the missing water is going **before the service ring is partially evacuated**.

### OPPOSITION
Not a villain.

- the station is a closed physical system with many legitimate users;
- old/adapted human plumbing intersects newer Confederation environmental modules;
- undocumented modifications are common in cheap worker housing;
- residents hide rule violations because eviction/status consequences are real;
- corporate/municipal operators have legitimate safety thresholds;
- wealthy districts can absorb rationing costs more easily than service workers;
- Ollie's probation supervisor will not let an apprentice tear systems apart by intuition.

### WRONG STRATEGY
Ollie initially treats it as a pure engineering defect he can solve if people stop interfering.

He distrusts explanations involving social behavior because they feel less fixable.

### CAUSAL MIDDLE
1. metering proves the water exists until a specific transfer layer;
2. physical inspection reveals many tiny unauthorized branches, none large enough alone;
3. a local orbit-born teen refuses access to one residential module, creating suspicion;
4. Ollie discovers some “illegal” branches support ordinary human life: showers, food gardens, extra roommates, low-cost humidification, mixed-species visitors;
5. the loss is not one theft but **a city-level mismatch between officially modelled occupancy and actual lived occupancy**;
6. closing every unofficial branch would “solve” the numbers by making poorer residents leave.

### MIDPOINT
A real hardware leak is finally found—and it explains only part of the deficit.

Ollie's preferred technical answer is true and insufficient.

### PRICE
To continue investigating, he must report some unauthorized modifications, damaging trust with the very residents who helped him.

He also risks his placement by refusing a fast but socially destructive “close the ring, reconcile later” recommendation.

### CLIMAX CHOICE
A pressure/water-transfer event forces a near-term decision:

- isolate the whole affected housing branch, which is unquestionably safe but displaces many residents;
- or perform a staged manual redistribution using local tanks, service robots and residents' hidden branch map while crews isolate only the true leak and re-register actual occupancy.

Ollie cannot simply decide this alone. His contribution is the only workable local map + fallback sequence; authorized adults decide/approve the safety envelope.

He chooses to **make the hidden reality legible**, including violations that may cost friends something, rather than protect everyone through silence or solve the spreadsheet by eviction.

### RESOLUTION
The true leak is repaired.
The ring remains occupied under a temporary re-permitting/amnesty process.
Some illegal modifications are removed; some are legalized.
Ollie passes probation but loses the fantasy that engineering is separate from who gets to live where.

His housing/first-job status becomes real, not ceremonial.

### ORDINARY-LIFE BRAID
- rent/credits;
- first shift;
- roommates;
- shared bathrooms/water quotas;
- food gardens;
- dating/visitors constrained by environmental settings;
- rich ring vs service ring;
- embarrassment over cheap housing;
- party water use / showers / laundry becoming actual closed-loop economics;
- parents on Earth worrying about “unsafe worker housing.”

### ORBIT-ONLY TEST
Fails on Earth: water is not a near-total closed-loop civic inventory tied directly to occupancy, pressure sectors and habitation permission.

### SERIES HOOK
During repair, one pre-Contact-style Earth notation appears in an old transferred subsystem classification. No causal explanation. Save for later.

### RED TEAM
**Strengths:** maximum lived-city density; build/repair story; money/housing/work all causal; Ollie gets hero status instead of gadget role.  
**Risks:** can become plumbing procedural; must keep people, deadlines, physical spaces and relationship costs foregrounded.  
**Current score:** **9.1/10**.

# OPTION B — SPIN CHANGE
## Hero candidate: MAYA
## Genre: youth search thriller / orbital city adventure

### HERO
Maya wants to make an orbital gravity-sport team and prove she can build an independent life not defined by Youth Cohort responsibility.

### WANT
Win a place on the station's mixed-gravity club roster and stay in orbit through the next term.

### WHY NOW
The habitat is preparing a scheduled **spin-state transfer / maintenance rebalancing** that temporarily changes which rings are connected at compatible gravity and which routes close.

The same weekend hosts a youth sport/social festival.

### INCITING INCIDENT
After a party / unofficial after-event, an orbit-born teen from Maya's new social circle disappears into a service section shortly before the spin transfer begins.

The teen's device goes offline.
Adults immediately start the proper missing-person protocol—but the physical transition means sectors will progressively isolate.

### CONCRETE OBJECTIVE
Find the missing teen's actual route/location before the relevant service ring becomes inaccessible for the maintenance window.

### OPPOSITION
- rotating geometry changes in time;
- routes that exist now will not exist later;
- station search teams prioritize plausible safety zones;
- friends lie about the party because of disciplinary consequences;
- the missing teen had a reason to avoid official tracking;
- different gravity states change speed, injury risk and access;
- Maya's sport status gives her social access but creates conflict of interest.

### WRONG STRATEGY
Maya assumes spatial competence gives her the right to coordinate everyone and pushes friends harder than they consent to.

### MIDPOINT
Evidence shows the teen is not simply lost: they deliberately went into a low-rent/service area to meet someone / retrieve something tied to a personal housing/family problem.

Search becomes consent/privacy versus imminent physical risk.

### PRICE
Maya must expose some party lies and damage her chance at the sport team because she misses/abandons a key trial.

### CLIMAX CHOICE
At the final compatible-rotation window, Maya can follow the route she thinks the teen took or trust a local orbit-born friend's contradictory knowledge of how service workers actually move through the habitat.

She gives up control, follows the local route logic, and helps authorized rescue reach the teen before isolation.

### RESOLUTION
Teen found alive; specific local crisis closes.
Maya loses one clean sport opportunity but earns a different route back based on honesty/performance later.
Friend group changes permanently.

### ORDINARY-LIFE BRAID
sport, party, attraction, lying to parents, transit, station curfew, housing shame, service work, local vs Earthborn status.

### ORBIT-ONLY TEST
The search map physically changes because the city is rotating and reconfiguring gravity-compatible access.

### SERIES HOOK
The place the teen entered has an old unexplained classification inherited from pre-Contact hardware catalogues.

### RED TEAM
**Strengths:** strong page-turner; space is physical causality; youth social life naturally integrated.  
**Risks:** “missing friend” can overpower city-building promise; danger of teen amateur rescue cliché unless adults are visibly competent and youths contribute local/social information adults lack.  
**Current score:** **8.8/10**.

# OPTION C — FIRST RENT
## Hero candidate: ETHAN, but de-centered ensemble
## Genre: social/building drama with emergency climax

### HERO
Ethan wants the five to take a semi-independent orbital residency together because he sees it as proof that their post-Book-One future is real.

### WANT
Secure and keep a shared youth-residency module for the term.

### WHY NOW
A newly opened human-adapted habitat ring offers a limited number of low-cost youth/cooperative leases tied to maintenance/community hours.

### INCITING INCIDENT
Their allocated module fails environmental certification days before move-in. Contract rules give them a short remedy window before the unit returns to the waiting list.

### CONCRETE OBJECTIVE
Make the module legally habitable by the deadline without using an adult/professional exemption they do not have.

### OPPOSITION
- incompatible human/nonhuman environmental standards;
- procurement delays;
- money/credits;
- landlord/operator rules;
- roommates want different things;
- richer applicants can simply switch units;
- temporary Earth housing means losing shifts/classes if move-in fails.

### WRONG STRATEGY
Ethan treats the apartment like another group project and starts assigning everyone solutions.

### MIDPOINT
They can technically pass inspection by removing a feature one roommate/local partner actually needs. “Success” would make the home legally compliant but socially unlivable.

### CLIMAX
A localized environmental failure during the final inspection forces them to choose between preserving their lease and opening their module as a temporary refuge for adjacent residents, risking damage and automatic certification failure.

They choose people; later evidence shows their improvised cross-compatible setup is worth formalizing.

### RESOLUTION
They do not get the exact apartment on the original terms. They receive/earn a revised cooperative housing arrangement with real responsibilities and costs.

### ORDINARY-LIFE BRAID
roommates, rent, furniture, food, privacy, dating, noise, chores, parents, commute, class difference.

### ORBIT-ONLY TEST
Habitability is active environmental engineering; a home is part of life-support topology.

### SERIES HOOK
One component's legacy classification is anomalously old.

### RED TEAM
**Strengths:** radical commitment to ordinary orbital life; makes housing story-worthy; relationship engine strong.  
**Risks:** weak external propulsion unless emergency/inspection clock is excellent; Ethan risks repeating “coordinate team / learn not to control.”  
**Current score:** **8.0/10**.

# OPTION D — GRAVITY LEAGUE
## Hero candidate: MAYA or new orbit-born co-lead
## Genre: sports drama / social SF

### HERO
Maya wants admission to a mixed-gravity sport league that could become her first identity in orbit not inherited from Book One.

### INCITING INCIDENT
A serious but nonfatal injury during a qualifying match exposes a disagreement over whether the league's dynamic-gravity field is safe across human, synthetic and nonhuman bodies.

### CONCRETE OBJECTIVE
Keep the season alive by discovering what actually caused the injury and producing a safe competition format before the league is suspended.

### OPPOSITION
sponsors, coaches, insurers, body differences, status rivalry, athletes who hide symptoms, AI training models optimized on unequal data.

### WRONG STRATEGY
Maya thinks better rules and technical analysis will make everyone agree.

### MIDPOINT
The injury was not one defective field setting; athletes have been adapting bodies/training in unequal ways to compete, and some of the risk is socially chosen.

### CLIMAX
Maya must choose between competing in the event that would secure her place or test a safer format that invalidates her qualifying advantage.

### RESOLUTION
League survives with changed format/classification; Maya's sport identity changes.

### ORBIT-ONLY TEST
Sport depends on variable gravity and multi-body adaptation.

### RED TEAM
**Strengths:** youth pleasure, rivalry, body/world reveal, marketable set pieces.  
**Risks:** could feel like a side novel rather than Book 2's broad civilization launch; mystery hook may feel bolted on.  
**Current score:** **8.2/10**.

# OPTION E — NIGHT SHIFT
## Hero candidate: AOIFE
## Genre: workplace mystery / social thriller

### HERO
Aoife wants an independent paid media/communications job in the orbital city rather than being “the Youth Cohort recorder.”

### INCITING INCIDENT
During her first late shift at a mixed human/nonhuman venue/transit district, an environmental alert evacuates one section—but the official event record and what she personally recorded disagree about the order of warnings.

### OBJECTIVE
Find out which account is wrong before a formal blame decision costs a young service worker their job/residency.

### OPPOSITION
partial sensor coverage, translation latency, labor hierarchy, corporate liability, her employer wanting a clean public narrative, friendships with people in the story.

### WRONG STRATEGY
Aoife initially believes preserving more evidence automatically gets closer to truth.

### MIDPOINT
Both records are accurate from different timing frames; the problem lies in orbital synchronization/relay context plus human assumptions about who received which warning.

### CLIMAX
She can publish a clean culprit and save her position, or release a context-heavy reconstruction that implicates system design and her own employer while also admitting the worker made a separate mistake.

### RESOLUTION
Worker is not falsely blamed; system changes; Aoife loses the clean career launch but earns a tougher independent role.

### ORBIT-ONLY TEST
Distributed station timing, relays and environmental zones make “when did the warning happen?” physically frame-dependent at civic scale.

### RED TEAM
**Strengths:** excellent Aoife story; work/money/media; SF concept through ordinary employment.  
**Risks:** too close to her Book-One/old Lattice “truth/context” arc; repeats instead of expands unless radically reworked.  
**Current score:** **7.4/10**.

# OPTION F — THE UNREGISTERED ROOM
## Hero candidate: SAMIR
## Genre: housing mystery / identity drama

### HERO
Samir wants ordinary privacy in orbit—his own room, his own schedule, and one part of life not turned into a special case.

### INCITING INCIDENT
A housing transfer shows two legitimate occupants attached to one physical residence slot across incompatible registry layers: one human teen and one synthetic young person who has been living there through a legally recognized remote body.

### OBJECTIVE
Resolve who has the right to occupy the module before one is displaced from the station.

### OPPOSITION
conflicting legal definitions of presence/residency, family claims, scarce housing, social prejudice, incomplete Contact-era handover records.

### WRONG STRATEGY
Samir tries to keep himself out of the dispute and treat it as a records problem because he is exhausted by being interpreted through data.

### MIDPOINT
Both claims are valid under different jurisdictions; there is no clean “correct owner.”

### CLIMAX
A real habitat problem forces both claimants to decide who may use the physical room while one body/subject is temporarily elsewhere; Samir must participate publicly despite wanting privacy.

### RESOLUTION
A new shared/portable residency category is created from the specific dispute; one person still pays a cost.

### ORBIT-ONLY TEST
Physical life-support space is scarce while personhood/presence can be distributed across biological and synthetic embodiments.

### RED TEAM
**Strengths:** thematically sophisticated; housing/personhood truly futuristic.  
**Risks:** too early for embodiment complexity under Book-2 tech ceiling; could become legal seminar; risks repeating Samir data-rights story.  
**Current score:** **7.1/10**.
