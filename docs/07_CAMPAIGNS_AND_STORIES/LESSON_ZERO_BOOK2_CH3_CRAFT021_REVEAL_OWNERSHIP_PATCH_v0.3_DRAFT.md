## Replacement prose

Samir had stopped looking at the Saturday trace.

He was looking at the transition diagram beneath it.

Ethan waited.

It took effort.

That was useful information about himself.

Samir enlarged one relation state, then another.

The transition did not depend only on where the two participants ended up. It depended on who moved first.

If Mel corrected toward Taven, one next state became available. If Taven corrected toward Mel, the system assigned a different confidence update.

Same two people. Same final position. Different path through the model.

Ollie leaned closer.

“Because of sequence?”

“And actor order,” Samir said.

Voss nodded. “Yes.”

“Is that standard here?”

“In this class of relation update, yes.”

Samir looked at the diagram for another few seconds.

Aoife noticed before Ethan did.

“You know that face.”

Samir ignored her.

Maya said, “That is absolutely a face.”

“I am thinking.”

“That was the accusation.”

Ethan felt the question arrive.

He almost asked it in the form his brain preferred: *Is this R-14?*

That would have turned recognition into a conclusion before Samir had even said what he recognized.

So he asked, “What should we write down?”

Samir looked at him once, then back at the display.

“Actor order changes the next available relation state.”

Ethan wrote exactly that.

No second line.

Voss watched him finish.

“You have seen something that makes that interesting?”

Samir considered the question.

“I have seen enough that I want the technical lineage for this operator.”

“The development history?”

“Yes.”

“That is available at the component level. Some parts are older than the integrated system.”

“How old?” Ethan asked.

Voss looked at him.

He heard the speed in his own voice.

“Sorry. That can wait.”

Voss did not smile.

“Good correction.”

Ethan disliked how much he appreciated that.

Samir saved the operator identifier to his participant-controlled notes.

He did not open any old file.

He did not call it a match.

He did not call it random either.

That was all they actually knew.

Voss advanced the simulation.

The twenty-seven circles moved again.

This time Ethan watched the order.
