# 1. City function

The city is not a magical school and not a military academy pasted into space.

It is simultaneously:

- a real city;
- a training/education hub;
- a research and controlled-deployment zone;
- a mixed-species residential environment;
- a logistics node at the outer edge of normal Solar civilization;
- a safety buffer around technologies too experimental/restricted for ordinary Earth or orbital deployment;
- a place where cadets have jobs, bills/credits, roommates, transport problems, sport, attraction, boredom, status and disciplinary consequences.

If cadets only attend classes and missions, the world fails.

# 2. Technology ladder inside the city

The earlier future map used orange/red labels as speculative categories. In-world, the useful production translation is **access tier**, not color-coded exposition.

## TIER A — CITY-NORMAL

Advanced but ordinary to residents:
- high-density AI coordination;
- mixed-species environmental zoning;
- adaptive architecture;
- autonomous transit/logistics;
- synthetic service bodies;
- advanced regenerative medicine;
- telepresence;
- highly capable simulation/world models.

Cadets do not stop to explain these.

## TIER B — TRAINING-LICENSED

Accessible only under supervised qualification:
- specialized synthetic bodies for vacuum/ocean/extreme environments;
- high-bandwidth neural interfaces;
- temporary embodiment/remote-body control systems;
- restricted matter/field manipulation;
- experimental cognitive augmentation;
- extreme-environment biological adaptation;
- advanced autonomous scientific systems.

These technologies generate exams, status, injuries, insurance/liability, friendship, competition and career tracks.

## TIER C — RESEARCH / PROTOTYPE

Present in labs, test ranges or controlled environments:
- continuity-preserving embodiment experiments;
- distributed-person architectures;
- advanced synthetic-person substrate transitions;
- collective cognition protocols;
- programmable living systems;
- radical programmable matter;
- high-risk field/space-time systems compatible only with Confederation infrastructure.

Cadets may observe, assist or qualify for bounded interfaces. They do not own the technology or understand full Confederation ontology.

## TIER D — RESTRICTED / CIVILIZATION-LEVEL

Not ordinary cadet access:
- unrestricted strategic propulsion/navigation;
- civilization-scale mind/substrate technologies;
- full Confederation interstellar infrastructure;
- dangerous self-replicating systems;
- multidimensional systems that cannot safely operate under local Solar conditions;
- technology whose release would collapse human social/industrial adaptation faster than institutions can absorb it.

These remain horizon, not lore dump.

# 3. Core world-reveal rule

Observable phenomenon first.

The reader should learn a technology by seeing:

`CADT WANTS SOMETHING → TECHNOLOGY AFFECTS ACCESS / BODY / MONEY / STATUS / RELATIONSHIP → SOMETHING GOES WRONG OR COSTS SOMETHING → ONLY THEN TERMINOLOGY`.

Example:

Not:
> Transferable embodiment allows a consciousness to operate a synthetic body optimized for vacuum.

Better story exposure:
> A cadet fails an exterior practical because the body assigned to him maps wrist orientation differently from his biological proprioception. His rival passes in the same model. He has forty-eight hours before reassignment costs him the expedition slot.

The reader learns embodiment through loss and competition.

# 4. Cadet voice classes — not species stereotypes

Major cadets need individual voice sheets, but the city creates useful **experience asymmetries**.

### Earth-arrival cadet

Likely attention:
- price;
- access;
- unfamiliar environmental assumptions;
- prestige;
- fear of looking provincial;
- comparison to Earth infrastructure.

Do not make every Earth cadet awestruck.

### Orbit-born cadet

Likely attention:
- gravity transitions;
- station etiquette;
- maintenance competence;
- resentment when Earth arrivals romanticize routine hazards.

Do not make orbit-born teens automatic engineers.

### Confederation-species cadet

Likely attention:
- human assumptions that seem invisible to humans;
- translation/social equivalents;
- home-civilization status expectations.

Do not use them as exposition machines.

### Synthetic cadet / synthetic young person

Likely attention depends on biography, body history and social position.

Forbidden default:
- monotone;
- hyperliteral speech;
- emotionless analysis;
- “I am a robot” identity exposition.

Synthetic person = character first.

# 5. Instructor voice versus cadet voice

The Confederation training center should have a strong register gap.

### Instructors
- exact scope;
- qualification language;
- procedural safety;
- explicit uncertainty;
- controlled terminology;
- long historical perspective only when required.

### Cadets
- translate rules into consequences;
- negotiate shortcuts;
- misunderstand prestige;
- joke about equipment;
- compare instructors;
- care about who got selected;
- hide mistakes;
- flirt during impossible technology;
- complain about schedules;
- resent body/skill classifications;
- spend money/credits badly.

If a teen debate could be pasted into a standards committee without changes, rewrite it.

# 6. Technology must create new youth problems

Every radical technology admitted into Book 2 needs a **human problem bundle**:

`WHAT IT LETS YOU DO`
`WHO GETS ACCESS`
`WHAT QUALIFICATION COSTS`
`WHO PAYS`
`WHAT CAN FAIL`
`WHO IS LIABLE`
`WHAT NEW STATUS IT CREATES`
`WHAT NEW CHEATING EXISTS`
`WHAT NEW EMBARRASSMENT EXISTS`
`WHAT NEW ROMANTIC/FAMILY PROBLEM EXISTS`
`WHAT RIGHT TO REFUSE EXISTS`.

Examples:

### Specialized synthetic body
- coveted expedition body class;
- body fit/sensory mismatch;
- rental/deposit liability;
- body-mod cheating;
- sexual/romantic identity after embodiment;
- parents object to body transition;
- discrimination against biological-only cadets;
- cadet prefers borrowed body to biological body and does not want to return.

### BCI / cognitive augmentation
- study advantage;
- privacy;
- unauthorized memory aid;
- unequal access;
- emotional leakage;
- cheating rules;
- whether an instructor may inspect neural logs after an accident.

### Artificial/synthetic biology
- environmental adaptation;
- prohibited dorm organisms;
- food culture conflict;
- lab organism escapes into recycling ecology;
- cadet hides responsibility to protect a placement.

### Distributed/collective cognition
- team exam advantage;
- consent to merge;
- private thought leakage;
- post-link jealousy;
- one member refuses disconnection;
- responsibility for an action made by the temporary collective.

# 7. Book-2 voice promise

The prose promise should be:

**advanced civilization experienced by teenagers who still have ordinary lives.**

Not:

**teenagers explaining advanced civilization to the reader.**

Reader should turn pages for:
- who gets selected;
- who fails;
- who likes whom;
- who lied;
- who is expelled/transferred;
- who cannot afford something;
- who takes the dangerous slot;
- who breaks trust;
- who refuses an enhancement;
- who comes back from a body-training exercise changed;
- what happens to the friend group.

Technology intensifies those waits.
