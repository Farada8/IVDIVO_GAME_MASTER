# 3. ONE MAIN STORY ENFORCEMENT

The five lines are **not five subplots running beside Lattice**.

They are five ways the same public-system problem becomes human:

`LATTICE / RIVERGATE`
→ authority allocation (Ethan)
→ fallback resilience (Ollie)
→ information framing (Aoife)
→ inference/data rights (Samir)
→ responsibility/autonomy (Maya)
→ convergent evidence + fallback + authority in Ch31–33.

A scene may contain ordinary teen life, school/family/maker/media material, humor or romance texture, but if it belongs to one of these arcs it must eventually alter:

`SYSTEM DECISION | EVIDENCE | RELATIONSHIP | AUTHORITY | FALLBACK CAPACITY | PUBLIC INTERPRETATION | COST`.

# 5. VALIDATION OF CURRENT OPENING

## Chapter 1 — THE BADGE

**Protect.**

The laminated authority badge is an efficient object-level story engine. It does several jobs at once:

- gives Ethan what Book One denied him, but only in bounded form;
- immediately creates comic friction without undercutting the serious theme;
- places Ollie's ordinary maker world onstage before Rivergate prestige;
- shows Aoife already recording selectively;
- shows Ethan actively resisting the impulse to manage what is outside his authority.

The chapter answers the opening test: **something has been set in motion**. Ethan is no longer asking whether he is allowed inside; he must now decide what to do with a real, limited power.

## Chapter 2 — authority becomes no-exit accountability

**Protect.**

Voss's distinction between responsibility for a justified decision and being sole cause of all consequences is a strong complication. Samir's question about non-action closes the obvious escape route: doing nothing can also be reviewable.

This advances the main character problem rather than repeating Book One.

## Chapter 3 — shared event, differentiated lenses

**Protect core mechanism.**

One Lattice demonstration produces different forms of attention:

- Maya: framing/manipulation;
- Ollie: concrete cost;
- Aoife: policy authorship;
- Samir: model/person ontology;
- Ethan: restraint and authority learning.

This is the preferred ensemble model for later chapters: **one event → several character-specific pressures**, not five disconnected scene chains.

Five candidate continuation families were tested from the state at the end of Ch3:

All five are already embedded in the existing architecture.

### Production verdict

**Do not add a sixth novelty fork merely to make the middle busier.**

The job of prose development is to make these consequences feel discovered through action, not preassigned from an outline.

# 7. DIALOGUE-MODE APPLICATION

Book Two contains institutional and technical material that can easily produce long procedural exchanges.

Use direct dialogue only when exact words perform one of these actions:

- assign/refuse authority;
- define consent or rights;
- challenge evidence;
- alter relationship leverage;
- expose model assumptions;
- force a decision;
- create a consequential misunderstanding;
- produce character-specific humor under pressure.

Routine briefings, repeated scheduling, ordinary access logistics and already-established policy should generally move through summary/indirect dialogue unless the exact phrasing creates new conflict.

This is a specific anti-bloat and anti-`ping-pong paragraph` control.
