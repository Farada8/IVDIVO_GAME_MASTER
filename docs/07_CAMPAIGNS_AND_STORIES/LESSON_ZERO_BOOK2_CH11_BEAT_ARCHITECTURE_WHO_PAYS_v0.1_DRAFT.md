## Story job

After Ethan has co-signed limited expansion, ordinary successful use must reveal a new problem that aggregate metrics hide: the same kinds of cooperative/flexible people may repeatedly absorb small inconvenience.

The chapter does **not** prove predicted accommodation. It changes the audit question from route-level efficiency to person-level burden distribution.

## Owner

Maya.

## Prior consequence

Ethan authorized continued limited use because Lattice had real measurable benefit and no established hold-threshold violation.

## Immediate objective

Maya is not initially “investigating fairness.” She is doing ordinary observer/audit work and wants the high-load period to run without people being treated like pieces of equipment.

## Human case — working specification

Use one recurring young/public user as the visible anchor. **Do not promote name/details to canon until prose/Founder approval.**

Working ordinary-life facts:
- they are trying to reach a scheduled group/activity/service inside Rivergate;
- another young person/friend is already waiting at the destination;
- being late is mildly embarrassing, not catastrophic;
- they have previously accepted small routing adjustments without complaint;
- they genuinely do not mind the first accommodation today;
- by the third small adjustment, the social/emotional meaning changes even though each request remains individually reasonable.

This is important: **same person, different consent across moments**.

## Scene form

Lived field observation → repeated small accommodations → Maya tests whether she is seeing a pattern → request for burden-by-person analysis.

No screen-first opening.

## Beat chain

### Beat 1 — ordinary success
Rivergate is busy but functioning. Lattice routes people smoothly. Maya sees several tiny acts of cooperation that appear normal and even considerate.

Reader state: system still looks useful.

### Beat 2 — first accommodation
Visible anchor user receives a secondary-corridor instruction / yield request. They accept easily because the cost is trivial.

Maya does **not** object.

### Beat 3 — unrelated human business
The user messages/checks where their friend/group is, or otherwise reveals that they have somewhere ordinary to be. Keep this brief. It establishes a life outside the metric.

### Beat 4 — second accommodation
A later access cycle asks the same user to wait/yield again. Still defensible: another user has a tighter constraint.

Maya notices recurrence, but there are obvious innocent explanations.

### Beat 5 — Maya's first wrong move
Maya is tempted to intervene case-by-case or complain about this specific route. A staff/Voss-side explanation correctly shows why this individual allocation is reasonable.

This prevents Maya from being instantly right.

### Beat 6 — third small cost
The same user is shifted again. No dramatic injury. The person says some version of “fine” while body/social behavior says the cost is no longer neutral: checking time, apologizing to someone, changing pace, visible irritation, etc.

Do not make “fine” proof of hidden resentment. Maya sees ambiguity.

### Beat 7 — comparison instinct
Instead of arguing morality, Maya asks a different operational question: not “why this route?” but “how many changes has this person absorbed today / across comparable runs?”

This is the bull's-eye.

### Beat 8 — resistance
Initial dashboard/reporting is organized by route/event/aggregate performance, not burden per person. Staff can answer how many route changes happened but not immediately who repeatedly paid them.

No conspiracy. It simply was not the primary metric.

### Beat 9 — team contributions
- Aoife can help identify sequence/context of observed adjustments without claiming motive.
- Samir warns against turning anecdote into mechanism.
- Ollie asks what variables need matching before comparison is meaningful.
- Ethan recognizes that his Ch10 authorization makes this his responsibility too, but he does not seize Maya's observation.

Keep contributions unequal and brief. Maya owns the chapter.

### Beat 10 — matched-case request
Maya requests burden grouped by person/profile across comparable cases while controlling as much as possible for route geometry, load, accessibility, staffing and declared preference.

Voss/operations accepts this as a legitimate audit question, not an attack.

### Beat 11 — outcome
They do not yet have proof. They have changed what the system must measure.

The visible user gets where they were going, late enough for the social cost to be real but not melodramatic.

### Beat 12 — exit pressure
The first person-level aggregation begins to show that recurrence may not be random / may cluster, but sample and alternatives remain live.

End on a changed operational question, not a thematic statement.

## Information ceiling

Reader may conclude:
- aggregate success can conceal unequal repeated inconvenience;
- Maya has identified a testable distribution question.

Reader may NOT conclude:
- Lattice intentionally exploits helpful people;
- predicted accommodation is the cause;
- the system is malicious;
- shutdown is justified.

## Character movement

Maya begins by wanting to protect a person from one unreasonable-looking allocation.
She ends by realizing the stronger move is to **measure the pattern rather than overrule an individual decision she does not yet understand**.

This sets up her Ch13 danger: measurement/protection can later tempt her toward control of Nico.

## Relationship movement

Ethan should not tell Maya the theme. His useful action is to recognize that her new audit request is within the conditions he co-signed and help make it reviewable without claiming authorship.

Potential micro-friction: Maya may initially think Ethan is proceduralizing a human problem; Ethan may be genuinely trying to make the observation actionable. Neither is fully wrong.

## Voice constraints

Maya notices:
- bodies changing direction;
- who steps back;
- who apologizes;
- who keeps making room;
- who acts as if inconvenience is not worth complaining about.

Her narration/dialogue should not begin with “fairness,” “optimization externality,” “consent” or a polished thesis.

## Anti-preachiness

Do not use production slogans in prose:
- “cooperation became infrastructure”;
- “the helpful people pay”;
- “prediction is not consent.”

Let recurrence + changed measurement create the idea.

## Youth grounding

Use only causal ordinary-life detail:
- someone is waiting for the affected user;
- a message/time check;
- mild embarrassment/lateness;
- food/drink/bag/phone/ordinary movement if it affects the scene.

No decorative “teen montage.”

## Scene acceptance test

WHO WANTS WHAT? Maya wants the field period to treat people properly and initially wants to fix a visible case.  
WHY NOW? Expansion has produced enough real use for recurrence to become visible.  
WHAT STOPS HER? Each allocation has a legitimate local explanation; she lacks proof of a pattern.  
WHAT CHANGES? The audit starts measuring burden by person, creating the evidentiary path toward Ch14.

## Red Team before prose

PASS if:
- visible user feels like a person with an objective;
- first accommodation is genuinely acceptable to them;
- Maya is not instantly right;
- Voss/staff explanation is reasonable;
- no causal mechanism is proved;
- chapter changes the next investigation action.

FAIL if:
- user gives thesis speech;
- dashboard announces bias;
- Voss dismisses human cost;
- Maya wins by moral certainty;
- Ethan solves the scene;
- chapter ends with narrator explaining the lesson.
