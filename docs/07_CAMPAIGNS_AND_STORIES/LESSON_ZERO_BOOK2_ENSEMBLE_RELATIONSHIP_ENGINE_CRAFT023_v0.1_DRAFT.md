# LESSON ZERO BOOK TWO — ENSEMBLE RELATIONSHIP ENGINE
## CRAFT-023 application

Version: v0.1
Date: 2026-08-16
Status: DRAFT / ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT TOOL / NOT CANON UNTIL FOUNDER APPROVAL

## Purpose
Prevent Book Two from becoming five individual subplots plus Lattice. Each major recurring relationship needs a durable conflict line that evolves through consequences and collides with the A-story.

Rule:
`RELATIONSHIP DIFFERENCE → CASE ACTIVATION → CHOICE/COST → NEW RELATIONSHIP STATE → DIFFERENCE SURVIVES IN EVOLVED FORM`.

---

# Core pairwise engines

## Ethan ↔ Maya
**Durable difference:** Ethan trusts explicit scope/process; Maya distrusts language that turns responsibility into control.

Book One state: Maya learned negotiated autonomy; Ethan learned competence ≠ authority.

Book Two activation: Maya becomes responsible for Nico while Ethan actually holds bounded authority.

Progression target:
1. early banter around badges/authority;
2. Maya resists being framed as “responsible for” Nico;
3. Ethan initially sees scope language as protective clarity;
4. Nico chooses a legal but noncompliant route;
5. Maya discovers responsibility without command authority;
6. later Ethan must use real authority without treating Maya/Nico as objects of policy;
7. new stability: both accept that responsibility and authority can be asymmetrical and negotiated.

Do not reset into “Maya hates all rules / Ethan loves all rules.”

## Ethan ↔ Samir
**Durable difference:** Ethan wants to convert uncertainty into actionable structure quickly; Samir protects the distinction between observation, interpretation and ownership of meaning.

Book Two activation: R-14/Lattice comparison and Samir's data rights.

Progression target:
- Ethan asks fewer questions and waits more;
- Samir still refuses raw-data access when public-safety arguments are plausible;
- Ethan must defend Samir's right to refuse even when the refusal slows the investigation;
- Samir later chooses a derived comparison on his own terms;
- new stability: Ethan becomes a better collaborator without becoming passive; Samir can share without surrendering ownership.

## Ethan ↔ Aoife
**Durable difference:** Ethan optimizes toward action/decision; Aoife optimizes toward framing, context and what information will do once released.

Book Two activation: misleading true footage and public transparency.

Progression target:
- early teasing around access/recording remains light;
- Aoife obtains true footage whose release would create a false narrative;
- Ethan initially wants an actionable communication answer;
- Aoife rejects both “publish because true” and “hide because harmful” as insufficient;
- later public-review sequence forces them to separate evidence from framing;
- new stability: they can disagree about action without assuming the other is morally careless.

## Ethan ↔ Ollie
**Durable difference:** Ethan sees systems and coordination; Ollie trusts bounded mechanisms, failure modes and physical fallback.

Book Two activation: breadcrumb rovers versus predictive infrastructure.

Progression target:
- opening establishes Ollie's own maker-team authority, not Ethan's;
- Rivergate initially calls simple machines crude;
- Ethan is attracted to Lattice's sophistication;
- Ollie's fallback proves useful under degraded conditions;
- conflict becomes not “technology good/bad” but elegance vs inspectability / central prediction vs local robustness;
- new stability: Ethan respects low-tech redundancy; Ollie accepts that simple systems also impose tradeoffs.

## Maya ↔ Nico
**Durable difference:** Maya values autonomy but now carries duty toward someone less experienced.

This is a deliberate inversion of her Book One position.

Progression target:
`I hate being controlled → I hate sounding controlling → I still have to act when another person's risk becomes mine to carry`.

No easy solution where Maya simply lets Nico do anything.

## Aoife ↔ Voss
**Durable difference:** both value accurate representation, but Voss treats bounded models as operational necessities while Aoife is sensitive to how accurate fragments can mislead outside context.

Use this to make Aoife's subplot causally relevant to Lattice public legitimacy.

## Samir ↔ Voss
**Durable difference:** Voss wants more useful comparison data; Samir insists that usefulness does not erase purpose limitation or participant ownership.

Voss must remain intellectually and ethically strong enough that Samir's refusal has real cost.

## Ethan ↔ Aaron
**Durable difference:** Book One status comparison evolves into Book Two accountability comparison.

Aaron is not a rival. He is a near-future mirror:
- he has the adult authority Ethan wanted;
- he can explain its burden without becoming a mentor who always knows the answer;
- Ethan should sometimes disagree with him.

# Ensemble-level conflict routing

For each major Book Two chapter, mark:

`GLOBAL PRESSURE`
`LOCAL RELATIONSHIP PRESSURE`
`INNER PRESSURE`
`WHICH PAIR CHANGES STATE`
`WHAT DIFFERENCE SURVIVES`
`HOW THIS COLLIDES WITH LATTICE A-STORY`.

A chapter with only Lattice mechanics must justify why no relationship state changes.
A chapter with only banter must justify why it is not detachable.

# Opening validation — Chapters 1–3

## Ch1 THE BADGE
PASS.

Humor functions as character reactivation rather than random joke distribution. The stop-button incident immediately converts the comic badge premise into real responsibility. That tonal turn is essential: the book promises warmth without trivializing authority.

## Ch2 YOUR CALL
PASS.

Aaron/Ethan evolves rather than repeats the Book One hierarchy. Aoife/Ethan access banter also reactivates their difference without replaying their old documentary argument.

## Ch3 THE PEOPLE IN THE MODEL
PASS STRONG FOR ENSEMBLE FILTER DIFFERENTIATION.

Same stimulus, different first questions:
- Maya: manipulative framing;
- Ollie: concrete cost;
- Aoife: authorship/accountability;
- Samir: ontology/boundary of model;
- Ethan: restraint from answering first.

Protect this pattern in later group scenes.

# Development risks to monitor

1. Ethan becomes the relational hub for every subplot while the other four rarely conflict directly.
2. Maya/Nico becomes a simple lesson in obedience instead of responsibility-without-command.
3. Voss becomes a straw technocrat, making the ethical problem too easy.
4. Ollie's simple-machine line becomes “old tech beats AI” instead of a tradeoff between robustness and optimization.
5. Aoife's media subplot remains commentary rather than changing deployment/public legitimacy.
6. Samir's privacy line becomes absolute secrecy; it must create legitimate cost and require bounded alternatives.
7. Banter density remains identical in crisis/aftermath scenes.

# Acceptance test for future chapters

A chapter passes the ensemble engine when at least one of the following is true:
- a pair's relationship state changes;
- a previous relationship consequence changes today's decision;
- a character chooses against their habitual filter because prior growth now matters;
- a conflict between two characters changes the Lattice case;
- a Lattice consequence changes how two characters trust, interpret or negotiate with one another.

If none is true, check for detachable subplot or technical exposition.
