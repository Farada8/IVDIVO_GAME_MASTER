## Suspense decision

Book Two should not conceal that:
- Lattice genuinely works;
- it has agency/fairness risks;
- stopping it has real safety costs;
- Ethan has a narrow hold.

Knowing these facts increases suspense because the reader can anticipate the impossible tradeoff. The climax should answer `WHAT WILL ETHAN CHOOSE UNDER COST?`, not merely `WHAT SECRET WAS LATTICE HIDING?`

## POV audit rule for Ethan chapters

For every explanatory paragraph ask:
1. What does Ethan physically notice first?
2. What does he infer from it?
3. What does he want to do because of that inference?
4. What remains outside his knowledge?
5. Does the wording sound like Ethan-filtered narration or like a generic systems essay?

If a paragraph is technically limited-third but reads like a detached policy paper, revise the discourse layer without changing the story event.

## Macro skeleton — PROTECT

`authority given → system works → Ethan authorizes → burden by person → inferred accommodation → provenance strengthens → system saves someone → structural burden confirmed → patch → limited approval → local patch failure → decision window → controlled evidence + fallback → narrow hold → visible cost → rebuild under terms`.

This is the Book Two causal skeleton. Subplots must attach to it through consequences, not merely alternate with it.
