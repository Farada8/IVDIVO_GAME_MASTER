## Replace the Chapter-3 block beginning with Samir studying the transition diagram and ending before Voss expands the simulation with the following:

Samir had stopped looking at the Saturday trace.

He was looking at the transition diagram beneath it.

Ethan waited.

It took effort.

That was useful information about himself.

Samir enlarged one relation state. Then another.

The transition did not depend only on the current participant state. It depended on who had moved first.

If Mel corrected toward Taven, one next state became available. If Taven corrected toward Mel, the system assigned a different confidence update.

Same two people. Same final position. Different path through the model because actor order changed.

Ollie moved closer to the display.

“Is that normal?”

Voss looked at the transition rather than at him.

“In a relational system? It can be.”

“That is a very engineer answer.”

“It is an engineer question.”

Samir opened a blank note on his tablet.

He copied the time stamp.

Then the state labels.

Then, after a moment, he added one line Ethan could not read from where he stood.

Ethan wanted to ask what it was.

The question reached the back of his teeth.

He left it there.

Samir noticed.

That was worse.

Or better.

Ethan was still learning which.

Voss said, “The order sensitivity is not itself unusual. What matters is what variables the system lets that sequence change.”

Samir nodded.

“So we need more than one trace.”

“Yes.”

“And the implementation history.”

“If you want it.”

“I want the published lineage.”

Voss's attention sharpened slightly.

“Why?”

Samir looked at the wall.

“Because I want to know which part I am looking at before I decide what it reminds me of.”

Nobody answered for a second.

Ethan liked the sentence immediately and distrusted himself for that too.

Voss said, “Good. The non-participant-data references are available. Some components go back through older academic methods, some are newer, and the integrated civic model is about four years old.”

“No private training records?” Samir asked.

“Not for this review.”

“Then yes.”

Voss marked the request.

No one opened an old diagram.

No one named R-14.

The pattern on the wall stayed exactly what it was: one behavior in one current system, interesting enough to record and not yet interesting enough to explain.

Voss changed the simulation.

Twenty-seven circles became eighty-four.

The line network thickened.

Then Rivergate's map appeared underneath it.

Public hall.

Transit platform.

Refuge rooms.

Food court.

Translation nodes.

Family services.

Every relation became movement.

A human child followed a guardian. A volunteer changed route because a stranger requested help. A Nharu visitor waited because another body blocked a turn. A translation relay moved because two people who did not know each other happened to need the same language resource thirty seconds apart.

Lattice adjusted all of it.

Not perfectly.

Fast.

Ethan felt the old attraction to a system that could see the whole board.

He knew enough now to distrust the feeling without pretending it had disappeared.
