# 1. Corrections

## REJECTED DEFAULT: MONEY AS CADET STAKE

Do not use:
- wages;
- rent;
- “cannot afford training”;
- paid placement as survival milestone;
- credits as disguised money;
- purchasing power as primary youth status engine.

Replace with stakes such as:
- trust;
- qualification;
- permission;
- one-time access;
- mission selection;
- team membership;
- responsibility;
- reputation;
- mastery;
- recognition;
- consent;
- relationship cost;
- safety authority;
- mentor confidence;
- limited research slot;
- time window;
- competing obligations.

## REJECTED DEFAULT: OPERATIONAL HUMAN CONSCIOUSNESS TRANSFER

Do not write ordinary orbital cadets transferring human consciousness/soul into specialized bodies.

The current orbital civilization does NOT possess this operational technology.

Therefore prior example:
`cadet certification in vacuum body via consciousness transfer`
is NON-CANON and must not be used as a Book-2 story engine.

Future saga embodiment material remains later / specialist / undiscovered unless Founder changes the boundary.

## REJECTED DEFAULT: DIMENSIONAL KNOWLEDGE AS MATURE PUBLIC SCIENCE

Rare schools may study consciousness, yoga, contemplative practice, subtle perception, dimensional hypotheses and related fields.
But:
- practitioner numbers are low;
- traditions may disagree;
- reproducibility is limited;
- mainstream orbital science may know correlates without knowing ontology;
- advanced external technology does not automatically solve practitioner scarcity.

# 2. What remains valid from Pass 15

## VALID: CHARACTER FILTER
Advanced technology must be perceived through a specific person, not described as a catalogue.

## VALID: EMBODIMENT AFFECTS POV — WITH CURRENT-CANON LIMIT
Physical embodiment, gravity, suit, medical adaptation, robotic personhood, sensory augmentation and ordinary biological differences may alter POV.

But do not use mind/soul-transfer into synthetic bodies as ordinary technology.

## VALID: INSTRUCTOR / CADET REGISTER DIFFERENCE
Experts and young trainees should not speak with identical conceptual language.

## VALID: SYNTHETIC / ROBOT PERSONHOOD
A conscious robot is a subject with agency, not an object or human-owned tool.

# 3. Kress motivation application

Nancy Kress provides a useful corrective because motivation must be culturally legible.

Working formula:

`CULTURE + PERSONAL HISTORY + VALUES → PRESENT WANT → FRUSTRATION → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC RESPONSE → CONSEQUENCE`.

In Orbital Youth, a scene is suspect if its motive only makes sense in a money-scarcity civilization.

# 4. Post-money youth frustration matrix

## MATERIAL WANT — usually weak
“I need an expensive device.”
Why weak: ordinary fabrication may solve it.

## ACCESS WANT — strong
“I want to be allowed onto the expedition.”
Cannot be fabricated because trust/qualification is relational and institutional.

## RECOGNITION WANT — strong
“I want this achievement to be mine, not something the system did for me.”
Cannot be fabricated.

## BELONGING WANT — strong
“I want this team to choose me.”
Cannot be fabricated.

## AUTONOMY WANT — strong
“I want my parents / mentors / institution to stop deciding my future.”
Cannot be fabricated.

## MASTERY WANT — strong
“I can ask robots to build it, but I want to understand and do it myself.”
Cannot be fabricated instantly.

## RARE-PRACTICE WANT — strong
“I want to enter a small consciousness-practice school whose results are disputed.”
Cannot be solved by ordinary automation because long-term trained practitioners are scarce.

## ROBOT-HUMAN RELATION WANT — strong
“I need cooperation from a conscious robot person/culture that does not owe humans obedience.”
Cannot be fabricated as ownership.

## TIME-WINDOW WANT — strong
“This event / alignment / expedition / competition happens once.”
Time itself creates scarcity.

# 5. Emotional causality gates for Book 2

Every major youth scene should answer:
1. What does the POV character want NOW?
2. Why does this want make sense in post-money orbital culture?
3. What blocks/complicates it?
4. How does THIS character react differently from the others?
5. What emotion follows because a valued outcome changed?
6. What action or relationship changes next?

Do not accept:
`worldbuilding fact → emotional label → witty line → next worldbuilding fact`.

Prefer:
`want → friction → specific response → consequence → revised want`.

---

# 6. Specialist consciousness-school story rule

A consciousness-practice school can generate a strong YA story only if it remains a thin frontier rather than a solved magic system.

Required questions:
- Why does this youth choose years of difficult practice when mainstream life offers easier prestigious paths?
- What can the school actually demonstrate?
- What remains interpretation?
- How many trained practitioners exist?
- What does mainstream science accept vs dispute?
- What failures / false positives occur?
- What social cost comes from joining a marginal field?
- What does the character want beyond “learn powers”?

Possible conflict:
A youth wants recognition from a prestigious technical program but also becomes fascinated by a low-status contemplative school whose observations cannot be reproduced without years of practice. Both wants are legitimate. Choosing either costs something real.

# 7. Production lock

Before approving future Orbital Youth prose:

STOP and rebuild if any of the following appears without explicit Founder exception:
- rent/wages/credits as ordinary orbital motivation;
- ordinary material object scarcity without non-fabricable reason;
- human consciousness/soul transfer as operational city technology;
- dimensional worlds as mature mainstream science;
- conscious robots treated as owned infrastructure;
- “advanced technology means every knowledge domain is equally advanced.”

Craft knowledge remains; invalid world assumptions do not.
