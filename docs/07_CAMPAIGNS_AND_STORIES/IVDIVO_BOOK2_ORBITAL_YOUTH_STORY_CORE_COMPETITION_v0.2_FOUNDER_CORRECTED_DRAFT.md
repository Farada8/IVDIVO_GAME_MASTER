## Correction basis

v0.1 used invalid Earth-era assumptions: wages, rent, credits, worker poverty, paid-placement survival and material scarcity.

Founder correction establishes:
- orbital cities are post-money;
- money is a superseded low-level motivation/economic mechanism;
- robotic production can make essentially any available object/technology;
- leadership maintains abundant Solar-System resource extraction;
- robots are a separate conscious civilization;
- no human consciousness/soul transfer technology in ordinary orbital civilization;
- dimensional-world knowledge is not mainstream; only specialist schools/directions hold partial knowledge.

Therefore the Book 2 engine must come from **social choice, responsibility, trust, identity, competition, relationships, unique experience, time/location and real physical orbital conditions**, not buying power.

# OPTION A — SPIN CHANGE v2
## Hero: Maya or a new orbit-born co-lead
## Genre: youth search thriller / friendship / orbital-city adventure

### HERO
A young person wants a place in a mixed-gravity sport/social circle because it gives identity, belonging and recognition in a city where nobody needs a wage to survive.

### WANT
Become part of the team/community on their own terms rather than remain “Earth newcomer / Youth Cohort kid / somebody's child.”

### WHY NOW
A city-wide youth festival and sports weekend coincides with a scheduled habitat spin-state reconfiguration. Some connections between rings will progressively change or close while rotation/gravity states are altered.

### INCITING INCIDENT
After a late social event, a teenager from the new friend group disappears from ordinary contact shortly before the transition begins.

The city launches competent missing-person procedures. The youth group nevertheless has social information adults do not yet possess because several friends are hiding what really happened at the event.

### CONCRETE OBJECTIVE
Get the missing person's true last-route information into the official search **before the relevant ring becomes physically isolated by the scheduled transition**.

The youths are not amateur substitutes for rescue professionals; their story task is to uncover the social truth that determines where the professionals must look.

### OPPOSITION
- changing physical topology and gravity states;
- embarrassment, jealousy and attraction around the previous night's event;
- friends protecting one another with half-truths;
- privacy norms;
- a conscious robot youth from a separate civilization was involved in the last known interaction, creating human/robot misunderstanding without making either side villainous;
- Maya's sport trial happens during the same closing window.

### WRONG STRATEGY
Maya treats spatial competence as authority: she pushes people to surrender private information because “there is no time.”

### MIDPOINT
The missing teen was not abducted and was not randomly lost. They deliberately avoided normal tracking after a humiliating/social conflict and used a route known mainly through a cross-civilization youth friendship.

This makes the search narrower but morally harder: privacy was real, and physical danger is now also real.

### PRICE
Maya must:
- abandon/miss the event that could have secured her desired team place;
- admit her own role in the social conflict;
- stop treating the robot participant as a data source and negotiate for what can legitimately be shared.

### CLIMAX CHOICE
The final safe access interval opens. Two route hypotheses remain.

Maya can force an unauthorized privacy breach to get a cleaner answer or accept an uncertainty-preserving route supplied voluntarily by the robot friend and stake the search on trust plus her physical reading of the changing habitat.

She chooses the bounded, legitimate path and gives the official team the route case they can actually act on.

### CLIMAX ACTION
Authorized search/rescue reaches the teen before ring isolation. Maya contributes the decisive social/spatial reconstruction, not heroic physical substitution for professionals.

### RESOLUTION
- missing teen found;
- the lie chain around the party/event is exposed;
- friend group permanently changes;
- human/robot youth privacy protocol/social expectation changes locally;
- Maya's sport future is altered, not “rewarded for heroism” automatically;
- Book 2 local crisis closes.

### ORBITAL LIFE BRAID
- mixed-gravity sport;
- festival/party;
- Earthborn vs Orbitborn embarrassment;
- living independently from parents without wage/rent stakes;
- transit etiquette;
- privacy;
- human/robot friendship;
- changing gravity;
- youth status and belonging.

### ECONOMY TEST
PASS: no money, wages or consumer scarcity required.

### ROBOT TEST
PASS if the robot youth remains a sovereign person with reasons/limits, not a useful device.

### DIMENSION TEST
PASS: no transfer/dimensional technology required.

### RED TEAM
Strength: page-turner + ordinary youth + city physics + robot-civilization relationship.
Risk: missing-person thriller can dominate the larger “how do young people live here?” promise. Must spend enough early pages making the city desirable and socially inhabited before disappearance.

Current working score: **9.0/10**.

# OPTION B — GRAVITY LEAGUE v2
## Hero: preferably new orbit-born protagonist, with Maya as crossover/pressure character
## Genre: sports drama / social SF / mixed-civilization coming-of-age

### HERO
An orbit-born human teenager wants recognition on a prestigious mixed-gravity league team. Material goods are trivial; athletic mastery, public performance and team identity are not.

### WANT
Earn a real starting place and prove their style matters in a league increasingly shared with robotic and nonhuman competitors.

### WHY NOW
The season introduces a new mixed-body competition format intended to make human, robot and nonhuman teams compete in one event rather than separate divisions.

### INCITING INCIDENT
A serious but nonfatal accident during qualification shows the new format is not producing the same risk across body types.

The league suspends the final event until the cause and a defensible competition rule can be established.

### CONCRETE OBJECTIVE
Help determine **what rule/design made the event unfairly dangerous** and produce a testable safe format before the championship window closes.

### OPPOSITION
- no villain: every faction has evidence;
- “same rule for everyone” is simple but may not be fair;
- different body architectures tolerate gravity transitions differently;
- robot athletes are conscious participants, not equipment;
- athletes hide training practices or vulnerabilities because status matters;
- coaches want clarity;
- medical/safety staff demand evidence;
- the hero benefits from the existing rule.

### WRONG STRATEGY
Hero assumes fairness means identical conditions and treats technical equalization as sufficient.

### MIDPOINT
The accident is not one hardware defect. The competition combines bodies whose adaptation curves and recovery constraints are genuinely different. No single “neutral” setting exists.

### PRICE
The safest/fairer classification or transition rule removes the hero's current competitive advantage and may cost the starting place they wanted.

### CLIMAX CHOICE
At the final validation event, hero can exploit a still-legal advantage and probably secure the roster position, or publicly support the new format and participate in a retest that resets the qualification order.

### RESOLUTION
- cause understood;
- league returns under a changed, reviewable format;
- no species/civilization is declared naturally superior;
- hero's status changes through an earned choice;
- human/robot relationships become more concrete and less abstract.

### ORBITAL LIFE BRAID
sport, attraction, rivalry, team politics, parents, body confidence, local celebrity, training, public events, mixed-civilization friendship.

### ECONOMY TEST
PASS: competition and recognition are non-monetary.

### ROBOT TEST
PASS only if robot athletes own their participation and bodily-risk decisions.

### DIMENSION TEST
PASS.

### RED TEAM
Strength: very strong post-money motivation demonstration — people strive intensely even when survival is guaranteed.
Risk: may under-display housing/governance/education unless braided through ensemble life.

Current working score: **9.1/10**.

# OPTION C — THE UNNECESSARY PROJECT
## Hero: new Earth newcomer or orbit-born creator
## Genre: creation drama / friendship / human–robot cultural conflict

### HERO
A teenager desperately wants authorship and recognition in a civilization where a robotic production system can fabricate anything they can physically design.

### WANT
Lead a youth-created public environment for an upcoming human–robot cultural festival and be known for something genuinely theirs.

### WHY NOW
A newly opened orbital civic ring will host its first major youth festival. A mixed human/robot team is given one public-space project because both civilizations want the opening to represent actual cooperation rather than diplomatic display.

### INCITING INCIDENT
The first design fails—not technically. It is physically perfect and socially wrong. Robot participants reject the assumptions built into how humans expect a public gathering to work.

The opening committee gives the youth team a fixed deadline: produce a jointly usable environment or cancel the shared project and use standard neutral infrastructure.

### CONCRETE OBJECTIVE
Create and validate one public space that both human and robot youth actually choose to use **before festival opening**.

### OPPOSITION
- unlimited fabrication means technical iteration is easy, so bad ideas cannot hide behind manufacturing limits;
- humans and robots disagree about privacy, attention, proximity, identity display or collective/individual presence;
- hero wants personal authorship;
- robot co-designer refuses to be treated as the optimizing machine that “makes the human idea work”;
- friendships and romance complicate team loyalties;
- every redesign publicly exposes whose assumptions were wrong.

### WRONG STRATEGY
Hero tries to beat robotic competence by producing a more spectacular design and hoarding authorship.

### MIDPOINT
Robot partner states the core problem in action, not lecture: **“We can fabricate every version you propose. You still have not decided what relation you want this place to create.”**

The project is not an object problem. It is a social-design problem.

### PRICE
The working solution requires abandoning the hero's signature centerpiece and credit hierarchy.

### CLIMAX
At the full live test, conflicting crowd behaviors create a real safety/coordination failure. The hero can preserve the original “human lead” design or authorize a joint redesign that erases sole authorship but works.

### RESOLUTION
The shared environment opens and becomes genuinely used. The hero loses exclusive authorship and gains something harder: a real creative identity defined by choices/relationships rather than ownership of an object.

### ORBITAL LIFE BRAID
art, public design, friendship, attraction, reputation, school/project life, human/robot culture, festival, architecture, zero/variable gravity.

### ECONOMY TEST
PASS: abundance is not a background fact; it creates the story's central question of purpose/authorship.

### ROBOT TEST
STRONG if robot co-designer has independent culture, wants and refusal power.

### DIMENSION TEST
PASS.

### RED TEAM
Strength: uniquely post-scarcity; cannot be translated back into wage-era Earth without losing its meaning.
Risk: weaker thriller propulsion than A/B. Needs concrete live-test failures and relationship pressure to avoid becoming a design seminar.

Current working score: **8.8/10**.
