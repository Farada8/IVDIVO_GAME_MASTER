# ORBITAL YOUTH — BATCH 36 APPLICATION GATE
## Believability · Viewpoint · Humor · Post-money Everyday Life

Date: 2026-08-16
Status: WORKING / CURRENT-FOUNDER-CORRECTION COMPATIBLE

## Canon firewall
This application follows the newer orbital-civilization corrections:
- no ordinary money/rent/wage/credit economy as the default organizing system;
- conscious robots are a separate civilization/persons, not owned tools;
- no ordinary operational human consciousness/soul-transfer technology in current Orbital Youth;
- dimensional/subtle-world knowledge is specialist, uneven and bottlenecked by the small number of long-term practitioners.

## 1. Story-first gate
Before worldbuilding prose, every candidate book must answer:
`THE BOOK ENDS WHEN ________`.

The blank must describe completion of a concrete main problem, not completion of a tour of orbital society.

## 2. Ordinary-life extraction gate
Before adding a spectacular technology to generate interest, mine one ordinary post-money behavior with causal relevance.

Examples of useful pressure domains:
- access to a prestigious expedition/team/project;
- mentor trust;
- peer belonging and exclusion;
- public reputation after a consequential decision;
- family expectations around contribution;
- privacy in sensor-rich environments;
- human/robot etiquette and disagreement;
- limited training windows or unique experiences;
- specialist-practice status without treating specialist claims as mainstream science.

None of these is a disguised currency.

## 3. Character independence gate
For every named major/supporting human or robotic person:
- what do they want in this book?
- what do they want outside the protagonist's goal?
- who matters to them?
- what can they refuse?
- what would they still be doing if the protagonist vanished from this chapter?

A character failing this test is likely an exposition device.

## 4. Robot-person test
If a conscious robot can be replaced by a sophisticated appliance without changing relationships, consent, conflict or outcome, the character is not yet written as a person.

Require at least one scene consequence produced by the robot's independent judgment, preference, refusal, loyalty or disagreement.

## 5. Specialist-school test
A practitioner/instructor must not function as an ontology vending machine.

Show:
`TRAINING LINEAGE → YEARS/PRACTICE LOAD → WHAT IS REPRODUCIBLE → WHAT IS INTERPRETATION → COMPETING SCHOOL/DOUBT → PERSONAL MOTIVE`.

The small practitioner base itself is a world constraint and potential story pressure.

## 6. Believability gate for extraordinary behavior
The city may treat advanced external technology as ordinary. That does not excuse extraordinary character behavior.

For any climax-critical action ask:
- was this capacity/value/relationship visible before?
- why now?
- why this person?
- what previous pattern is being continued or broken?
- what cost follows?

## 7. Humor gate
Youth humor remains important, but:
- no universal witty-author voice;
- humor belongs to the character's perspective;
- do not place a joke automatically after injury, coercion, betrayal, consent violation or grave moral choice;
- allow aftermath/residue when reader investment depends on seriousness.

## 8. POV ownership gate
A scene's POV belongs to the person whose mind provides the best combination of:
`DECISION OWNERSHIP + INFORMATION LIMIT + EMOTIONAL PRICE + READER EFFECT`.

Do not switch merely to reveal convenient lore.

For limited third:
- one viewpoint per scene unless deliberate omniscient architecture is chosen;
- switch only at a clear break;
- immediately establish the new owner/time/place;
- deep/light/cinematic distance may vary with function.

## 9. Mystery fairness gate
If the viewpoint character knows a decisive fact, do not hide it from the reader solely to preserve a later reveal.

Allowed mystery comes from:
- information the viewpoint genuinely lacks;
- competing interpretations;
- unreliable but legible sources;
- partial evidence;
- consent/access limits;
- another character's private knowledge.

## 10. Post-money motive test for youth characters
For each major young character, define:
`WANT → WHY IT MATTERS TO THIS PERSON → WHO CAN RESIST/REFUSE → WHAT UNIQUE THING IS LIMITED → WHAT COST IS NON-MONETARY → HOW STRATEGY CHANGES`.

Bad replacement for wages: points/credits/status tokens treated as money.
Good limited resources: trust, time, slots, relationships, consent, responsibility, unique mission opportunity, reputation, experience, attention, access requiring qualification.

## 11. Book-One protection
No Card-derived craft idea by itself reopens LESSON ZERO Book One development. Patch only if a current defect is demonstrated against the locked manuscript or by external reader evidence.

## 12. Approval checklist for a future chapter
A chapter does not pass merely because its technology/worldbuilding is interesting. It must pass all relevant questions:
1. What changed because of the previous chapter?
2. Who wants what now?
3. What resists them?
4. Why can fabrication/automation not trivially solve the actual stake?
5. Is every named robot treated as a subject if conscious?
6. Does POV have ownership?
7. Is any withheld information fair?
8. Does humor preserve required seriousness?
9. What state changes by the end?
10. How does that state cause the next intention?
