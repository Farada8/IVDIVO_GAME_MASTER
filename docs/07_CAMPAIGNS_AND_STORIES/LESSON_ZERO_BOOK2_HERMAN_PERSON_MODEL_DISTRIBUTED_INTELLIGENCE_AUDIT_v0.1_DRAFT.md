# LESSON ZERO BOOK TWO — HERMAN PERSON-MODEL / DISTRIBUTED-INTELLIGENCE AUDIT

**Version:** v0.1 DRAFT  
**Date:** 2026-08-16

## Core verdict

Book Two's Lattice conflict is structurally stronger when phrased not as **AI/system versus humans**, but as a boundary error between three different things:

`PREDICTED BEHAVIOR`
`PERSON'S REASON / WILLINGNESS`
`INSTITUTIONAL RIGHT TO USE THAT PREDICTION`.

Lattice may predict behavior correctly and still allocate burden illegitimately. Preserve this ambiguity; do not reveal the system as secretly malicious or useless.

## Concrete manuscript requirement — three accommodation cases

Before the climax, dramatize at least three people who outwardly perform the same behavior (yield / wait / assist) for different reasons:

### Case A — genuine preference
A participant willingly yields because helping the companion/group is actually what they want.

### Case B — mistaken obligation
A participant yields because they interpret the recommendation/UI as effectively mandatory.

### Case C — relational pressure
A participant yields because refusal would impose translation, mobility or social cost on someone they care about.

Lattice may classify all three as high-probability accommodation. The reader then sees why behavioral prediction does not settle person-level meaning.

## Where to place these

- Ch11 `WHO PAYS` — establish repeated burden by person.
- Ch14 `PROBABLE YES` — expose the accommodation variable and explicitly separate probability from consent.
- Ch21 `RELIABLE PEOPLE` — demonstrate longitudinal cost and the heterogeneous reasons hidden inside the same model category.

Do not add three new subplots; embed the cases inside existing Rivergate users/volunteers/families.

## Ensemble intelligence map

The climax should remain distributed:

| Function | Owner |
|---|---|
| evidence that patch remains structurally risky | Samir |
| contextual record of what happened | Aoife |
| lived autonomy / participant account | Maya + Nico |
| fallback capability | Ollie |
| strongest keep-it-live counterargument | Aaron |
| scope interpretation / institutional record | Rowe / Voss |
| final bounded decision | Ethan |

**Do not let Ethan acquire the others' jobs in the last act.** His arc becomes stronger if authority means deciding with incomplete, distributed knowledge rather than becoming the smartest person in the room.

## POV specialization rule

When drafting or revising a scene, identify the owner of the first meaningful perception:

- Ethan → authority, threshold, procedure, time, responsibility.
- Maya → actual choice versus coerced/paternalistic choice.
- Aoife → framing, sequence, omitted context, audience inference.
- Samir → evidence category, model boundary, provenance, interpretation rights.
- Ollie → physical constraint, failure mode, maintenance, local fallback.

If the same paragraph could move between their POVs unchanged, rewrite the attention logic before polishing voice.

## Dialogue rule

In major debates, dialogue must change social position, not merely deliver arguments.

Example for Ch14:
- Voss does not just define predicted accommodation; by defending its use she positions herself as responsible for aggregate public safety.
- Samir does not merely object; by refusing the equivalence between prediction and permission he protects the boundary between model and person.
- Ethan's job is not to pronounce who is correct; his reaction should alter what test/action happens next.

## Storyworld rule

Keep Rivergate legible through concrete user interaction. Every new technical abstraction should be paired, quickly, with one of:

- a route changing;
- a person waiting;
- a translation node being reallocated;
- refuge capacity changing;
- a companion relation being inferred;
- a fallback device doing physical work;
- a visible cost of system on/off state.

No long explanatory passage earns space merely because the system is conceptually interesting.

## Acceptance test for the central problem

By Ch21 the reader should be able to answer all five without a lecture:

1. What does Lattice predict?
2. Why is the prediction useful?
3. Why can the prediction be accurate?
4. Why does accuracy still fail to prove consent/justification?
5. Why would simply switching Lattice off also harm people?

If any answer is missing, add **dramatized evidence**, not abstract explanation.
