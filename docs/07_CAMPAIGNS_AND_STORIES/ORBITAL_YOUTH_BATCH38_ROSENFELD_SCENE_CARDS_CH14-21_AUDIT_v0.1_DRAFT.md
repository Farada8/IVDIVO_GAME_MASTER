# 2. CHAPTER 14 — ALMOST LOCAL

## Scene 43 — The Adult Message

**KEEP.**

Strong scene-specific intention: Ethan wants an ordinary morning and receives a real personal option that reactivates an old desire.

Subtext in finished prose should be carried by:
- how quickly he opens the message;
- what wording he rereads;
- whether he hides/minimizes the screen when someone passes;
- which part of the offer attracts him;
- what he does **not** tell the group.

Do **not** write the scene-card line `Book One’s denied door is finally opening` as narration. That is development-editor language, not prose.

## Scene 44 — A Schedule That Doesn't Exist

**KEEP / HIGH VALUE.**

This is excellent subtext material because literal topic = scheduling, real topic = group drift / Ethan leaving / whose commitments count.

Dialogue gate:
- Ethan should not say `I’m afraid we’re growing apart`;
- Aoife should not deliver a thesis about his control problem;
- use the tactical dispute over what is `movable`.

Best underlying pattern:

`ETHAN: make five people fit`  
`OTHERS: protect commitments that now exist outside Ethan's planning frame`.

## Scene 45 — Five Minutes

**KEEP / PROTECT AS QUIET SCENE.**

Do not inflate into conflict.

Its function is emotional causality: Ethan experiences separation as ordinary life rather than as a fight.

Potential subtext carrier: unfinished food / people checking different departure cues / shared conversation that keeps being truncated by legitimate obligations.

No narrator sentence explaining `the group was dissolving`. Let departures demonstrate it.

# 4. CHAPTER 16 — THE INFERENCE

This chapter is the strongest direct application of Rosenfeld subtext + suspense.

## Scene 49 — Approved

**KEEP.**

Do not make Aoife visibly nervous about the exact failure that will happen. She has followed the explicit process.

The scene earns later damage only if current action feels responsible and competent.

## Scene 50 — What the Background Says

**KEEP / SLOW THE REALIZATION.**

Rosenfeld’s foreground/background mechanism fits almost exactly:
- foreground = approved content;
- background = timestamp / marker / interface tone / maintenance context;
- third party reads the background as evidence.

Recommended suspense shape:

`ordinary repost → one oddly specific comment → Aoife checks frame → realizes why the inference is possible → sees second account repeat it → source message arrives`.

Do not start with instant viral catastrophe if a slower inferential recognition will produce more anxiety.

## Scene 51 — Too Late

**KEEP.**

Dialogue with source should not become ethics lecture.

Literal argument may concern:
- approval;
- what was removed;
- whether removal was enough;
- copies already circulating.

Subtext:
- `I trusted your process`;
- `you are trying to protect your innocence`;
- `the harm exists even if the rule was followed`.

Aoife stopping her own defense is stronger than a polished apology speech.

# 5. CHAPTER 17 — PLANET PEOPLE

**MAJOR WATCH: DEBATE DIALOGUE.**

The chapter has correct thematic pressure but could become a panel discussion about intent vs impact.

## Scene 52 — Not About You

Require local tactics, not thesis turns.

Maya wants to protect Aoife and the cohort’s social standing. Locals want recognition of a real injury and may also want Maya to stop centering Earth visitors.

Ground the argument in a youth social environment:
- someone leaves a table;
- food/order is interrupted;
- a local refuses to share footage / invite / route;
- a friend who would normally joke with Maya does not;
- attention from surrounding peers changes what speakers are willing to say.

Lea’s line about another delay should interrupt the media argument rather than conclude it philosophically.

## Scene 53 — Another Month

**KEEP / SHOW BEFORE EXPLAINING.**

Temporary household arrangement should be revealed through concrete lived friction:
- incompatible sleep schedule;
- sibling study space;
- commute timing;
- personal objects split across locations;
- family members meeting only in transit windows.

Avoid poverty coding and money/rent explanations.

Lea’s position `delay also makes decisions for people` is strong, but prose should earn it through what Maya has just seen.

## Scene 54 — The Person Who Covers

**KEEP.**

Excellent subtext/relationship-debt scene.

The exact issue is not abstract privacy. It is that Maya’s freedom depended on someone else assuming social risk.

Do not over-explain the lesson. The peer’s local grievance is enough.

# 8. CHAPTER 20 — OSCILLATION

**HIGH-VALUE SUSPENSE SEQUENCE.**

Rosenfeld Chapter 16 strongly supports slowing this down.

## Scene 61 — Move-In

Start with ordinary successful habitation. Let real-life movement create the unmodeled condition incidentally.

Do not telegraph failure with excessive ominous details.

## Scene 62 — Early

Best suspense mechanism:

`human/body warning → plausible adaptation explanation → repeated warning → model predicts recovery → service action reproduces equivalent transition → instruments still lag`.

Use close sensory specificity and measured time.

Short sentences only where action truly accelerates.

## Scene 63 — The Building Answers Late

Once fixed sensors confirm the failure, pace may accelerate.

Before that confirmation, slower reader attention is stronger because Ollie and adults are trying to decide which interpretation is real.

Adults remain competent; suspense comes from delayed evidence hierarchy, not stupidity.

## Scene 64 — Out

This is aftermath, not another emergency scene.

`It was in the people` is a strong line if earned, but do not follow it with narrator explanation of the theme.

Allow silence / practical evacuation detail / revoked access to carry the emotional impact.
