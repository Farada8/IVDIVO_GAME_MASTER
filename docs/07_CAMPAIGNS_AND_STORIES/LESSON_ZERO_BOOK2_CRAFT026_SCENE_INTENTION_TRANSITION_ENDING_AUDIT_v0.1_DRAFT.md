# 1. Book-level continuity lock

Book Two correctly does **not reset Ethan**.

Book One ended with Ethan accepting a real youth route while still wanting the adult professional path. Book Two therefore begins with the harder condition: he now has bounded legitimate authority and must decide how to use it.

Current Book Two question remains:

**Now that I am inside, what am I actually responsible for?**

The active plot engine is not “prove Ethan belongs.” It is:

`AUTHORITY GIVEN → DECISION UNDER UNCERTAINTY → COST → ACCOUNTABILITY`.

# 2. Chapter 1 — THE BADGE

## Prior consequence

Youth Cohort now exists as a legitimate bounded institution; Ethan has learned that competence and authority are not identical.

## Immediate intention

Use his **limited stop authority** correctly during an ordinary public demonstration without converting it into management authority.

## Character-specific pressure

The authority is exactly the kind of thing Ethan wanted for an entire book. Therefore restraint is more difficult now, not less.

## Complication structure

- public demonstration clock;
- mixed-species routing;
- Ollie's unfinished calibration;
- adaptive route layer;
- Ethan can stop something but cannot manage everybody;
- the system's behavior opens a larger question rather than simply failing.

## Strong protected devices

### `It was laminated.`
KEEP.

This is a deliberate short-paragraph impact beat. It compresses the irony of Ethan's lifelong status hunger into a mundane physical object. It is **not** the type of accidental fragmentation targeted by the old cadence repair.

### Character-specific humor
KEEP unless beta evidence says otherwise.

- Maya attacks institutional/control language.
- Ollie speaks in engineering/tool deadpan.
- Samir uses sparse literal deadpan.
- Ethan converts emotion into procedure/status logic.
- Aoife frames sound/recording/presence.

### Physical ordinary-life arena
KEEP.

The school-gym / maker-expo context is valuable because Book Two begins with Contact as normal public life rather than lore exposition.

## Ending job

Chapter 1 should end having done two things:

1. prove Ethan's new authority is real but narrow;
2. create a consequential bridge toward Rivergate/Lattice.

Preferred ending operator: **ZOOM-OUT / PRESSURE TRANSFER**.

Do not end by explaining “Ethan had learned restraint.” Let behavior carry that.

# 3. Chapter 2 — YOUR CALL

## Prior consequence

Chapter 1 exposes a real predictive-routing problem and establishes that Ethan's authority is meaningful only inside defined scope.

## Immediate intention

Understand Rivergate/Lattice well enough to perform the Youth Cohort audit role without treating access as ownership.

## Strong opening mechanism

The service entrance works because setting immediately carries character pressure:

- Ethan likes infrastructure;
- `STAFF ONLY` means status/access to him;
- Aoife recognizes the pattern;
- Rowe scopes access before Ethan asks;
- Rivergate becomes a social/technical system rather than a decorative SF building.

## Complications to protect

- credentialed access is real but limited;
- participant models are not automatically visible;
- raw logs are role-gated;
- Aoife's recorder has declared-use boundaries;
- Lattice can be useful while still raising agency questions;
- professional adults are not fools or villains.

## Transition economy

Compress unless causally loaded:

- train/car travel to Rivergate;
- routine security sequence;
- repeated credential handling;
- walking between rooms;
- generic onboarding explanation.

Expand only if the process creates:

`ACCESS CONFLICT | CONSENT ISSUE | CHARACTER CHOICE | NEW EVIDENCE | SOCIAL STATUS PRESSURE`.

## Ending job

Preferred ending operator: **ZOOM-OUT + QUESTION UPGRADE**.

The reader should leave with a more difficult question than “does Lattice work?”

Better:

**If it works by predicting relations between people, what exactly is it treating as available information—and whose burden becomes invisible?**

Do not answer this fully yet.

# 4. Chapter 3 — reveal-boundary lock

## Prior consequence

Lattice's behavior is not random; inferred relations and actor-order effects are becoming observable.

## Immediate intention

Test/document what the system actually does and determine what evidence is needed before making a historical/provenance claim.

## Required complication

Samir can recognize a familiar *shape* before he can responsibly claim a match.

This produces a stronger epistemic scene than immediate exposition.

## Chapter 3 allowed evidence ceiling

Allowed:

- actor order changes next relational state;
- Samir experiences familiarity;
- the group can describe the behavior neutrally;
- Samir asks for documented technical lineage / provenance material;
- Voss can offer ordinary engineering explanations;
- Ethan wants to jump ahead and consciously does not force the answer.

Not yet allowed as a proved reveal:

- controlled R-14 side-by-side comparison;
- declaration that old human models share the same structure;
- provenance-origin discussion treated as established;
- alien/common-source inference.

The explicit historical comparison belongs to its later owner chapter, where controlled evidence and counterevidence can be placed together.

## Ending job

Preferred ending operator: **ZOOM-IN → BOUNDED QUESTION**.

A good end-state is not:

> We found where Lattice came from.

It is:

> Samir knows enough to know the resemblance is worth testing—and not enough to say what it means.

That creates an honest reason for the next investigation step.

# 6. Anti-chop application

The principal lesson from this pass is that chopped prose is often caused upstream.

## Do not repair by bulk paragraph merging

Instead classify every short unit:

- intentional impact;
- speaker turn;
- focus change;
- temporal/spatial transition;
- revelation;
- rhythmic breath;
- accidental micro-dramatization.

Only the last category is automatically a repair target.

## Common upstream cause

Routine process written as full present-time action:

`opens door → looks → reacts → walks → checks credential → thinks → another line`.

If the process has no consequence, compress it.

# 7. Scene-ending regression rule

For Chapters 1–3 and future Book Two drafting:

At every chapter end record:

`WHAT CHANGED?`
`WHO NOW WANTS WHAT?`
`WHAT QUESTION GOT HARDER?`
`WHAT MUST THE NEXT SCENE DO BECAUSE OF THIS?`

If the answer is only “the theme was restated,” the ending fails.
