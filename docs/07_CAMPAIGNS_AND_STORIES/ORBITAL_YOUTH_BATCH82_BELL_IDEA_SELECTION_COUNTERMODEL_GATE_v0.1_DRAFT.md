# ORBITAL YOUTH — BATCH 82 APPLICATION GATE
## Bell Idea Selection · Countermodel Fairness · Post-Money Stakes

Status: DRAFT / ADDITIVE GATE
Date: 2026-08-18

This document does not rebuild accepted Orbital Youth architecture. It controls future premise generation, alternate engines, scene alternatives and repair variants.

## 1. IDEA TOURNAMENT

When a new Orbital Youth problem is needed, generate materially different candidates before selecting one.

A candidate is materially different when it changes one or more of:
- decision owner;
- want;
- source/evidence;
- relationship under pressure;
- world rule;
- tactic;
- cost bearer;
- changed state.

A different orbital ring, gadget or spectacular hazard without causal change is not a new engine.

## 2. POST-MONEY STAKES FIRST

Before using any Earth-2020s economic pressure, attempt:

- trust of a specific mentor / peer / robot;
- consent;
- privacy;
- responsibility;
- one unique mission or training place;
- mastery / qualification;
- reputation after a public choice;
- belonging;
- relationship;
- access to a specific person or time window;
- lived specialist experience;
- safety;
- evidence integrity.

Do not translate money into `credits`, `points`, `allocation tokens` or similar disguised currency.

## 3. COMPETENT COUNTERMODEL

For any central disagreement, write the strongest credible case for both sides before scene drafting.

Examples:

### Autonomy ↔ coordinated safety
Both autonomy and coordination must have real demonstrated value and real failure modes.

### Predictive efficiency ↔ consent
A predictive system may genuinely improve outcomes while still overstepping person-level consent or burden allocation.

### Rare practitioner knowledge ↔ empirical caution
A specialist school may have reproducible practice effects and valuable observations without possessing a verified ontology of consciousness or dimensional worlds.

### Human plan ↔ conscious robot plan
The robot must have independent reasons, relationships, rights and consequences. It is not the `other side` merely to sharpen a human theme.

## 4. RESEARCH-TO-STORY

For every impressive technology/social rule/research discovery:

`WHAT IS IT? → WHO CARES? → WHY NOW? → WHAT OPTION CHANGES? → WHAT DOES SOMEONE DO? → WHAT CHANGES AFTERWARD?`

If nothing changes except reader knowledge, keep the material in the world database or compress it.

## 5. ENDING-FIRST FIREWALL

Generating a climax or ending image before the book is allowed.

It becomes binding only after:
- causal backward support;
- character truth;
- local proof ceiling;
- canon consistency;
- personhood / consent;
- ONE MAIN STORY fit.

Discovery can invalidate the planned ending.

## 6. CONSCIOUS ROBOT ACCEPTANCE TEST

A named conscious robot in a premise must answer:

- What does it want that is not reducible to helping a human?
- Why does it remain involved?
- What can it refuse?
- Whom does it care about besides the focal human?
- What social or physical conditions shape its options?
- What does its decision cost or change for itself?

Failure means the robot is currently a device, not a person.

## 7. SPECIALIST CONSCIOUSNESS SCHOOL TEST

No story engine may jump from a school's language to world ontology.

Required ladder:

`PRACTICE LINEAGE → YEARS OF TRAINING → OBSERVATION → REPRODUCIBLE EFFECT → SCHOOL INTERPRETATION → COMPETING INTERPRETATION → EVIDENCE LIMIT → POSSIBLE ONTOLOGICAL CLAIM`

The scarcity of long-trained practitioners remains a bottleneck even in a technologically advanced orbital civilization.

## 8. IDEA BANK

Good ideas that compete with the selected causal engine are not forced into the current book.

`STRONG BUT NONESSENTIAL → BANK FOR LATER BOOK / SIDE STORY / DIFFERENT PROJECT`.

This is mandatory protection against saga bloat.
