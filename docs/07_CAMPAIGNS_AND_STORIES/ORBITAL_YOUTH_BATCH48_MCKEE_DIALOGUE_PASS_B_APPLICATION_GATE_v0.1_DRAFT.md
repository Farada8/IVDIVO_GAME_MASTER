## HIGH-VALUE PASS-B TESTS

### 1. Group scenes
Reject equal-airtime position panels.
For each group exchange identify:
- local owner;
- immediate want;
- who controls evidence/access/time;
- who can interrupt;
- who risks embarrassment/status/trust;
- what reaction changes the next tactic.

A scene with five distinct opinions but no tactical interaction is still a panel.

### 2. Ch18–20 corridor
Because Ch18 group-voice work is already accepted, preserve it unless continuity validation reveals a concrete regression.
For Ch19 surgical trim, remove only exchanges that repeat an already-completed beat or restate a conclusion already inferable from action/context. Do not flatten character-specific disagreement in the name of economy.
For Ch20, preserve technical/relational ambiguity until evidence actually changes status; spoken interpretation is not system truth.

### 3. Ollie
His dialogue should reveal maker attention through what he names, notices, asks and refuses to assume—not through generic engineer slang. After Ch20, changes in his speech should arise from changed responsibility/evidence discipline, not a sudden mature-author voice.

### 4. Ethan
Do not give him policy speeches to demonstrate growth. Stronger proof is a precise correction, refusal to overclaim, or a bounded procedural question that changes what others can do.

### 5. Aoife
Her voice should distinguish raw record, interpretation, publishable claim, privacy boundary and uncertainty. Do not let recording/reporter vocabulary become a costume. Exact phrasing matters when publication, consent or source trust changes.

### 6. Maya
Autonomy/consent conflict works best through asymmetric dialogue: she may refuse framing, redirect a question, or force a concrete choice. Avoid authorial speeches explaining autonomy after behavior already demonstrated it.

### 7. Samir
Preserve sparse, observational speech where appropriate. Do not compensate for fewer lines by giving him unnaturally perfect aphorisms. His sport/social identity must remain visible outside analytic function.

### 8. Iri and conscious robots
Named conscious nonhuman/robotic persons require independent conversational goals and the possibility of refusal, misunderstanding, humor, culture or competing obligation. If their dialogue only supplies data or helps Ollie, rewrite the scene function before polishing voice.

## RHYTHM / PROSE FIREWALL
McKee's performance-oriented brevity is not a novel rule.

Allowed and protected:
- short impact lines;
- cumulative conversational sentences;
- longer reflective syntax;
- silence;
- indirect dialogue;
- summary.

Selection rule: **reader effect first**. Do not optimize short-line percentage.

## DIALOGUE SURGICAL-TRIM CHECK
Before cutting a line in Pass B, classify it:
1. NEW TACTIC / NEW EVIDENCE / NEW RELATIONSHIP STATE → protect.
2. CHARACTER-SPECIFIC TEXTURE WITH FUTURE PAYOFF → usually protect.
3. SAME BEAT IN NEW WORDS → trim/merge candidate.
4. PROCEDURAL TALK WHOSE EXACT WORDING DOES NOT MATTER → summary/indirect candidate.
5. EXPLANATION OF SUBTEXT ALREADY CLEAR → cut candidate.
6. SOURCE-STATUS CLARIFICATION → protect if it prevents inference inflation.
