## 2. Rivergate is not scenery

Current physical design already earns narrative value:
- service entrance vs public facade;
- credentialed access;
- variable-height handrails;
- environmental refuge rooms;
- translation nodes;
- route-width changes;
- mixed-family routing;
- operations balcony and audit suite.

These details communicate that post-Contact civic life is becoming infrastructure rather than spectacle.

### KEEP

The Chapter 2 contrast between the polished public side and the service side should stay. Ethan preferring gray doors, floor tape and imperfect infrastructure is character through setting, not decorative description.

### EXPAND THROUGH ACTION, NOT LORE

Rivergate must also generate social pressure. Add these pressures only when they collide with existing scenes:

1. **Youth reviewer legitimacy** — some adult staff treat the five as genuine auditors; others unconsciously simplify explanations or wait for Rowe/Aaron to validate their questions.
2. **Public-image pressure** — staff behavior changes when tours, local media or public comment are present.
3. **Family trust** — a parent or mixed-family group can accept slower/manual routing because they understand the trade-off; another can prefer Lattice precisely because ordinary human systems have failed them.
4. **Peer/status pressure** — Youth Cohort access can be admired, mocked or treated as institutional branding by ordinary teens without creating a school subplot.
5. **Species/social norms** — 'helpful' behavior may mean different things across groups, making predicted accommodation socially loaded rather than abstract.

Rule: each addition must change a decision, interpretation, relationship or measurable cost. No tourism paragraphs.

## 3. Chapter 2 — YOUR CALL

### Core scene function

`Ethan enters an institution where he finally has legitimate access → learns the hold is narrow → experiences authority as scope rather than prestige`.

### Setting function

Doors, credentials, restricted logs and the operations balcony physically stage the difference between:
- access;
- curiosity;
- permission;
- operational authority.

### POV double vision

Current Ethan material is strong because the prose does not lecture him. His attraction to STAFF ONLY doors and access he has not been offered exposes the old appetite while his behavior is already more restrained.

### PROTECT

Do not add explanatory narration such as:
`Ethan knew he still confused access with authority.`

The reader already has evidence.

## 4. Chapter 3 — THE PEOPLE IN THE MODEL

### Core scene function

This chapter should answer:
**What does Lattice treat as a person/group/relation, and what happens when some relations are inferred?**

It should NOT yet prove historical lineage.

### Strong line of pressure

Voss's demonstration is effective because the system actually works. The movement from `CONFLICT RESOLVED` to `CONFLICT REDISTRIBUTED` makes the moral problem visible without authorial explanation.

### Reveal boundary

Allowed in Ch3:
- inferred relations exist;
- actor order matters;
- Samir experiences familiarity;
- he asks for lineage/documentation;
- Ethan notices but does not force a theory.

Reserved for Ch7:
- controlled side-by-side comparison with R-14 / old relational-state family;
- articulated similarities and differences;
- bounded statement that resemblance is real but lineage is unproven.

If Ch3 includes the old diagram and provenance discussion, Ch7 loses its job.

## 5. Obstacle differentiation across movements

Book Two should not repeat Book One's sequence of 'Ethan overreaches, gets corrected.' Each new obstacle must make authority harder in a different way.

### Required progression

**Ch1–5: SCOPE**
Can Ethan distinguish having a control from needing to use it?

**Ch6–10: SUCCESS**
Can he authorize something because the evidence is genuinely good, knowing approval creates ownership of later consequences?

**Ch11–15: DISTRIBUTION**
Can he see that aggregate success hides repeated local burden?

**Ch16–20: PROVENANCE + BENEFIT**
Can he resist turning suspicious origin into a reason to ignore current performance?

**Ch21–25: RIGHTS / EVIDENCE**
Can he act when Samir legitimately withholds raw data and Maya/Nico expose conflicts of responsibility?

**Ch26–30: PATCH / CLOCK**
Can he decide under constrained time when the apparent fix has failed but fallback also has cost?

**Ch31–33: BOUNDED HOLD**
Can he use exactly the authority he has rather than waiting for certainty or grabbing more power?

This is obstacle differentiation, not escalation by spectacle.

## 7. Scene vs summary routing for Book Two

### FULL SCENE REQUIRED
Use full scene for:
- first bounded use/non-use of the hold;
- Ch6 major success;
- Ch10 co-sign authorization;
- Ch12 footage/context conflict;
- Ch13 Maya/Nico autonomy conflict;
- Ch15 public review where Ethan corrects his own side;
- Ch20 Lattice genuinely saving someone;
- opening-weekend decision window;
- Ch31–33 evidence + HOLD + immediate cost.

### SUMMARY / HALF-SCENE PREFERRED
Compress:
- routine audit briefings after rules are established;
- repeated access procedures;
- multiple similar log reviews;
- transportation between Rivergate sessions unless relationship movement occurs;
- repeated public comments that do not change the argument;
- technical retesting whose result can be stated cleanly before the next live decision.

## 8. Dialogue routing

Direct dialogue earns space when exact wording changes:
- authority scope;
- consent;
- interpretation of evidence;
- relationship;
- public framing;
- decision.

Indirect/summarized dialogue is preferred for repeated procedure and technical housekeeping.

This is also an anti-'obрубки' rule: fewer unnecessary micro-exchanges means fewer artificial one-line reaction paragraphs.

## 9. Social-milieu inserts to seed later (not new subplots)

### Seed A — decorative youth suspicion
A staff member initially directs the answer to Rowe after Ethan asks a real audit question. Rowe does not rescue him; Voss answers Ethan directly. This makes status visible through behavior.

### Seed B — ordinary peer perception
One short message/social exchange from school or maker peers can reveal that outsiders think Youth Cohort means privileged Contact access, while Ethan experiences mostly constraints and paperwork.

### Seed C — family routing trust
At least one family should make a rational choice that favors Lattice because previous manual routing created real risk. This protects the system's human case.

### Seed D — 'helpfulness' as social expectation
A recurring user burdened by predicted accommodation should not merely be 'nice'. Their cooperation can be shaped by role, culture, work duty or family expectation. This prevents the model flaw from becoming cartoon psychology.

## Verdict

Architecture remains strong. CRAFT-025 does **not** justify adding a new major plot. It does justify strengthening Rivergate as a social/action system, protecting good-faith opposition, differentiating Ethan's obstacles, and policing the Chapter 3/Chapter 7 reveal boundary.
