# ORBITAL YOUTH — BATCH 118 APPLICATION GATE
## Public/Private Identity · Institution · Audience · Youth Agency

Date: 2026-08-19
Status: ADDITIVE PRODUCTION CONTROL

## Verdict

Use Collins to strengthen youth/institution/audience causality. **Do not import Panem scarcity, Games brutality, sponsorship economy or dystopian default assumptions.**

## 1. Ordinary life before institutional pressure

Before major institutional conflict, establish at least two ordinary-life anchors that reveal:
- what the young person wants;
- what competence they already possess;
- which relationship is nonfungible;
- what the orbital city treats as normal;
- what the protagonist incorrectly assumes is normal everywhere.

`ORDINARY LIFE → SPECIFIC VALUE → LATER PRESSURE HAS MEASURABLE COST`.

## 2. Post-money stakes

Do not translate Hunger Games sponsor/resource mechanics into salary, rent, credits or purchasing status.

Preferred scarce/nonfungible stakes:
- trust;
- mission role;
- specific training window;
- time of a mentor or loved person;
- consent;
- reputation;
- belonging;
- privacy;
- responsibility;
- unique experience;
- location/time compatibility;
- relationship continuity.

## 3. Public/private identity

If a cadet, youth cohort member or young specialist becomes publicly visible:

`PRIVATE WANT → PUBLIC ROLE → PERFORMANCE → AUDIENCE MODEL → INSTITUTIONAL / SOCIAL CONSEQUENCE → PRIVATE COST`.

A public persona must change something causal: access, trust, responsibility, relationship, scrutiny or future options.

## 4. Audience is not currency

Public reputation is allowed as a social consequence, but it must not become a hidden monetary points system.

Ask:
- who watches?
- why do they care?
- what decision can their model influence?
- what can they NOT legitimately decide?

Popularity never proves competence, morality or personhood.

## 5. Institution fairness audit

For a universal orbital rule:

`WHO CAN REFUSE? → WHO HAS MORE PRESSURE TO ACCEPT? → WHO REPEATEDLY BEARS COST? → WHO BENEFITS? → WHO CAN APPEAL?`

A formally equal training or allocation rule may still create unequal burdens.

## 6. Success→responsibility youth engine

Protect:

`DOMAIN COMPETENCE → REAL SUCCESS → MORE VISIBILITY / TRUST / ACCESS → MORE RESPONSIBILITY → HARDER CHOICE`.

Do not manufacture incompetence after the protagonist has already earned competence.

## 7. Intelligent mistake gate

`OBSERVATION → REASONABLE LIMITED MODEL → CHOICE → NEW EVIDENCE → MODEL UPDATE`.

Youth error should often arise from incomplete knowledge or social experience rather than stupidity.

## 8. Relationship under observation

A friendship or romance may have two simultaneous states:
- what the participants privately understand;
- what the public/institution believes.

`PUBLIC PERFORMANCE ≠ PRIVATE AGREEMENT`.

If performance starts producing real feeling, preserve ambiguity and consent. Do not retroactively rewrite earlier strategy as secret truth.

## 9. Conscious robot/nonhuman public role

A conscious robot may also have a public identity distinct from private goals.

Required:
`OWN WANT → OWN MODEL → OWN RELATIONSHIPS → PUBLIC ROLE/PERFORMANCE → CONSENT/REFUSAL → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE`.

Human audience approval or fear does not determine personhood.

## 10. Forced binary gate

If an instructor/institution says the protagonist must choose A or B:

`WHO DEFINED THE BINARY? → IS IT ACTUALLY EXHAUSTIVE? → WHO IS THE LEGITIMATE DECISION OWNER? → WHAT SYSTEM REQUIREMENT CREATES LEVERAGE? → CAN A THIRD OPTION BE BUILT?`

A strong climax may come from refusing the offered frame rather than winning within it.

## 11. Public record / edited story gate

If an incident is publicly summarized:

`RAW EVENT → RECORDERS → EDITOR / SELECTOR → OMISSIONS → PUBLIC NARRATIVE → CONSEQUENCE`.

Preserve raw/source evidence separately from the story told about it.

## 12. Aftermath

Shared crisis does not reset relationships.

Required after major public/institutional conflict:
- what trust changed?
- what was performed that now needs clarification?
- what private feeling remains uncertain?
- what public label cannot be easily removed?
- what responsibility has increased?

## 13. Scene acceptance

A public/institutional scene survives if it changes at least one:
- audience model;
- institutional rule/response;
- protagonist public identity;
- private relationship state;
- access/authority;
- consent/boundary;
- evidence status;
- future option space.

Otherwise compress/merge.

## 14. House-style firewall

Do not imitate Collins' first-person-present thriller cadence as IVDIVO default.

`SUSPENSE ≠ SHORT-SENTENCE DENSITY`.

Use sentence and paragraph length according to reader effect, POV and scene duration.
