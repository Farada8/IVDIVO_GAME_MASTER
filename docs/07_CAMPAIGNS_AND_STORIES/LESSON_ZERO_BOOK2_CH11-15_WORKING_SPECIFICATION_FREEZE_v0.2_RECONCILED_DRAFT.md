# 4. CH14 — PROBABLE YES

## Frozen claim under test

A Lattice variable/function behaves as a measure of **predicted accommodation**: the likelihood that a person will yield, wait, assist or change route.

The question is not whether Lattice reads consent. It does not.

The question is whether probable cooperation is operationally treated as an allocatable resource.

## Test form

Use permitted diagnostics/replay/shadow comparison.

The team must distinguish the accommodation variable from:
- route geometry;
- accessibility/safety priority;
- crowd state;
- service availability;
- declared preference;
- correlated movement history.

A strong test holds the major operational conditions stable enough to show that changing the predicted-accommodation relation can change which otherwise-comparable user receives the inconvenience.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:
- predicted accommodation is an operationally relevant input under defined conditions;
- burden concentration is not explained solely by route geometry/load;
- the team now has evidence-grade reason to investigate governance consequences.

Not allowed:
- malicious intent;
- universal behavior;
- R-14 lineage;
- proof that every use is illegitimate;
- proof that the later patch cannot work.

## Voss protection

Voss can reasonably argue that predicting flexible responses is a rational way to minimize conflict in a public system.

The conflict becomes:

`ENGINEERING BENEFIT` versus `NORMATIVE RIGHT TO SPEND SOMEONE'S LIKELY COOPERATION`.

Samir's credibility comes from stating the narrowest claim the evidence supports.
