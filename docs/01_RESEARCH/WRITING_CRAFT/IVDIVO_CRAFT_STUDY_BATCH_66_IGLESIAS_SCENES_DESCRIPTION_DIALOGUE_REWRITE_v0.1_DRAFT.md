# IVDIVO CRAFT STUDY — BATCH 66
## Iglesias: Scenes · Description · Dialogue · Subtext · Rewrite

Date: 2026-08-17
Status: SOURCE-GROUNDED / OPERATIONALIZED
Source: Karl Iglesias, *Writing for Emotional Impact* (2005), strict Ch8–11 completion.

## 1. CORE SYNTHESIS
The second half of Iglesias is strongest when treated as a reader-effect toolbox, not a screenplay formatting constitution.

Transferable core:
`MEANINGFUL STORY STATE → SCENE MODE → CHARACTER/PERSON TACTIC → READER EFFECT → MODULATION → REVISION`.

Medium firewall:
screenplay layout, white-space, dialogue-length and page-reader habits do not automatically govern novels.

## 2. SCENE GATES
### B66-G01 — NO MANDATORY CONFLICT
A scene can be accepted without overt conflict if it changes action, knowledge, relationship, trust, responsibility, access, emotion or reader model.

### B66-G02 — SCENE MINI-STORY WITHOUT MINI-FORMULA
Track prior state, owner, want, action/tactic, response and changed state. Do not require every scene to reproduce the whole novel in miniature.

### B66-G03 — READER EMOTIONAL PALETTE
Track dominant + secondary + contrasting reader effects. Permanent one-note intensity is a failure mode.

### B66-G04 — SCENE POLARITY DECOMPOSITION
Do not demand a binary positive/negative flip. Accept qualitative state changes: certainty→uncertainty, distance→trust, access→responsibility, hypothesis→contested evidence, safety→moral risk.

### B66-G05 — SETUP/PAYOFF FUNCTION
A setup is structural only if later use changes action, inference, relationship or outcome. Pure echo may be stylistic rather than causal.

## 3. DESCRIPTION / PROSE GATES
### B66-G06 — MEDIUM FIREWALL
Do not import screenplay vertical-writing/page-density rules into prose.

### B66-G07 — SPECIFICITY BEFORE INTENSIFIER
Prefer precise object/action/behavior over generic emotional adjectives and intensifiers.

### B66-G08 — DESCRIPTION DOUBLE-PAY
A conspicuous detail should preferably do at least two jobs: orientation, POV, character, physical possibility, relationship, causality, question, rhythm, or theme.

### B66-G09 — MOTION IS MULTIVARIABLE
Perceived motion can arise from action progression, verb choice, duration, paragraphing, attention and expectation. Short syntax is only one optional tool.

### B66-G10 — SYMBOLISM RESTRAINT
Metaphor, motif, color and weather are optional. If meaning is already clear through action, repeated symbolic reinforcement may become authorial restatement.

## 4. DIALOGUE GATES
### B66-G11 — DIALOGUE AS TACTIC
`WANT → VERBAL/NONVERBAL TACTIC → REACTION → TACTIC CHANGE → CHANGED STATE`.

### B66-G12 — RELATION-SPECIFIC VOICE
Character voice must shift naturally by relationship, authority, intimacy, risk and agenda. Catchphrases/slang alone do not create a voice.

### B66-G13 — DIRECT/INDIRECT ROUTING
Direct speech earns page space when exact wording changes evidence, consent, power, trust, relationship, decision or emotion. Routine/repeated/logistical conversation is a candidate for summary/indirect dialogue.

### B66-G14 — EXPOSITION PURPOSE
`WHO NEEDS INFORMATION? → WHY NOW? → WHO CONTROLS IT? → WHAT CHANGES AFTER IT IS LEARNED?`
If nothing changes, compress or move it.

### B66-G15 — NO EXPOSITION CAMOUFLAGE MONOCULTURE
Conflict can carry exposition, but it is not mandatory camouflage. Curiosity, competence, intimacy, urgency, shared work, ritual, comedy, discovery and consequence can also make information dramatically active.

### B66-G16 — SUBTEXT COST
Use subtext when directness creates a believable cost. Do not force everyone to speak indirectly simply because `subtext = quality`.

### B66-G17 — DIRECT SPEECH PERMISSION
Direct language can be strongest when it is emotionally safe, when a long-withheld truth has been earned, or when simplicity itself is the dramatic event.

### B66-G18 — CONTEXT-AS-SUBTEXT
Before adding clever dialogue, test whether action, silence, physical spacing, prior history or reader knowledge already carries the meaning.

### B66-G19 — VOICE ≠ EARTH SLANG
For Orbital Youth, social assumptions, attention, rhythm, agenda and relation matter before slang. No contemporary Earth-teen voice pasted onto a future society.

### B66-G20 — CONSCIOUS ROBOT VOICE PERSONHOOD
No universal `AI voice`. A conscious robot needs independent agenda, relation-specific tactics, preferences, refusal rights and speech shaped by its own life.

## 5. REWRITE / LINE EDIT GATES
### B66-G21 — PASS-SPECIFIC REVISION
Plan separate passes for structure/causality, scenes, POV, dialogue mode, paragraphing, syntax and wording. Do not random-walk through the manuscript changing everything at once.

### B66-G22 — READER-EFFECT VALIDATION AFTER STRUCTURE
First verify the causal/scene function. Then ask what the reader actually experiences. Do not optimize prose for excitement while the scene itself is wrong.

### B66-G23 — ANTI-CHOP PIPELINE
For `обрубки`:
`ONE MAIN STORY → SCENE NECESSITY → SHOW/TELL → POV → DIALOGUE MODE → PARAGRAPH INTENT → SENTENCE RHYTHM → WORDING`.
Short-sentence density is not a quality metric.

## 6. FAILURE MODES
- conflict inserted into every scene;
- each scene forced to flip positive↔negative;
- screenplay white-space imported into novel paragraphing;
- `active verbs` interpreted as permanent high-energy diction;
- all teen dialogue fragmented to simulate realism;
- all characters given signature phrases;
- exposition chopped among speakers without causal purpose;
- exposition disguised with arbitrary conflict;
- subtext inserted where direct trust would be more truthful;
- clever dialogue that does not change state;
- symbols/images repeated after meaning is already clear;
- line editing before structural diagnosis.

## 7. IVDIVO APPLICATION
### LESSON ZERO
No reopen by theory alone. If a reader-effect defect is found in the current locked text, diagnose in this order: causal/scene defect → POV/mode defect → paragraph/rhythm defect → wording.

### ORBITAL YOUTH
Ordinary post-money life can carry strong scenes through responsibility, belonging, consent, love, rivalry, mastery, reputation, privacy, time and unique opportunity. Do not restore money because `stakes` need to feel familiar.

### MYSTERY
Curiosity cannot justify POV cheating. Exact evidence status outranks the desire for surprise.

### CONSCIOUSNESS SCHOOLS
Dialogue by practitioners should expose epistemic status naturally: observation, reproducible effect, school interpretation, competing interpretation, unknown ontology. Do not let eloquent dialogue silently canonize one school's metaphysics.

## 8. FINAL BATCH RULE
`READER EFFECT IS A VALIDATION LAYER, NOT A LICENSE TO MANIPULATE CAUSALITY, PERSONHOOD OR PROSE RHYTHM.`
