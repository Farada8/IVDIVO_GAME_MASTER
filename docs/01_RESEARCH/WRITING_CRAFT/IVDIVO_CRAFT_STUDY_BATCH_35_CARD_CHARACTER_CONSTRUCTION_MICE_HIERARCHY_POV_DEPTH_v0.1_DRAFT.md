## 1. The useful meta-model: character work has three different failure levels

Card's broad architecture separates three jobs that Writers' Room often accidentally collapses:

### A. INVENTION
Who is this person as a causal human being?
- action patterns;
- motives;
- past;
- reputation;
- social network;
- habits;
- abilities;
- body;
- tastes/preferences;
- contradictions.

### B. CONSTRUCTION
How much of that person does this particular story need, and what causal role do they play?
- dominant story factor;
- major/minor/walk-on attention level;
- emotional-stakes function;
- relationship function;
- transformation requirement.

### C. PERFORMANCE
How does the reader experience that person?
- narrator;
- viewpoint;
- first/third person;
- reliability;
- distance;
- penetration/intimacy;
- narrative voice/attitude.

### IVDIVO diagnostic law
`CHARACTER DEFECT → classify INVENTION / CONSTRUCTION / PERFORMANCE before editing.`

Examples:
- weak motive = INVENTION;
- five characters competing for equal page weight = CONSTRUCTION;
- all five sounding like one author = PERFORMANCE;
- scene tells us a character changed but behavior never changed = INVENTION + CONSTRUCTION, not line prose.

## 2. ACTION → MOTIVE → PATTERN

Card repeatedly treats action as the reader's strongest first evidence of character. Motive then changes how the action is interpreted.

IVDIVO transformation:
`OBSERVABLE CHOICE → READER INFERENCE → MOTIVE EVIDENCE → REVISED MODEL OF PERSON`.

### Failure mode
`TRAIT LABEL → EXAMPLE` is weaker than `CHOICE UNDER PRESSURE → CONSEQUENCE`.

### Production gate KG-01 — ACTION BEFORE LABEL
Do not approve a paragraph that explains a major trait already demonstrated by a costly action unless the explanation adds a new contradiction, motive or limitation.

## 3. THE THIRD-DEGREE / CLICHÉ-SHELF TEST

Card's repeated questioning method is highly compatible with IVDIVO's Red Team:
- Why?
- What caused this?
- For what purpose?
- What results from it?
- What else could explain it?

The first answer is often stock material.

### Production gate KG-02 — THIRD ANSWER MINIMUM
For any high-value character premise, generate at least three materially different causal explanations before locking one.

Examples to reject as first-answer defaults:
- ambitious cadet because `he wants to prove himself`;
- rebellious girl because `parents control her`;
- quiet genius because `childhood trauma`;
- robot dissenter because `humans treat robots badly`;
- consciousness-school practitioner because `special gift`.

A first answer can survive, but only after competing alternatives fail.

## 4. MICE AS DOMINANCE DIAGNOSTIC — NOT TEMPLATE

Card distinguishes four story emphases:
- Milieu;
- Idea;
- Character;
- Event.

IVDIVO should not adopt this as rigid genre ontology. The useful question is narrower:

**Which factor's completion tells the reader the book is actually over?**

### Orbital Youth implication
A book whose true ending condition is `we have toured enough orbital civilization` is a **milieu book**.
A book whose ending condition is `we finally understand how post-money society works` is an **idea book**.
Neither is automatically wrong, but both conflict with the current IVDIVO production demand for one complete youth story unless deliberately chosen.

### Recommended Book-Two dominance
`EVENT PRIMARY + CHARACTER CO-PRIMARY + MILIEU STRONG SECONDARY + IDEA SUBORDINATE`.

Meaning:
- a concrete problem begins and ends;
- a protagonist/ensemble strategy changes because of it;
- orbital civilization supplies unique causal conditions;
- post-money/robotic/consciousness-world knowledge is discovered only as required by the story.

### Production gate KG-03 — ENDING CONDITION TEST
Before prose, finish this sentence:
`The novel is over when ______.`
If the answer is mainly `the reader has learned enough about the world`, rebuild unless a deliberate milieu novel was ordered.

## 5. CHARACTER HIERARCHY = ATTENTION CONTRACT

Card's walk-on / minor / major continuum is valuable for ensemble control.

### WALK-ON
Makes the social world believable; no promise of return.

### MINOR
Can be memorable and causally useful, but does not earn a full independent arc by default.

### MAJOR
Recurrent desire/action alters the ongoing story; reader expects consequence and resolution.

### Production gate KG-04 — DEPTH MUST MATCH PROMISE
For every named recurring Book-Two character mark:
`MAJOR / SUPPORTING-MAJOR / MINOR / WALK-ON`.
Do not give a minor character a five-page wound/backstory/POV package unless the story is deliberately promoting them.

### Failure mode
**Ensemble fog:** every cadet receives equal witty dialogue, equal trauma, equal introduction detail, equal subplot and equal competence, so no one owns the book.

## 6. MEMORABLE MINOR ≠ SECRETLY IMPORTANT

A minor character may be memorable through a specific eccentricity, obsession, rhythm or behavior. But this must not create false mystery promise.

### Production gate KG-05 — FLASH WITHOUT FALSE PROMISE
If a minor receives a vivid entrance, verify that the vividness serves tone/social ecology/contrast rather than accidentally promising future plot significance.

## 7. EMOTIONAL STAKES WITHOUT CHEAP MATERIAL SCARCITY

Card's tools include suffering, sacrifice, jeopardy, sexual tension and wider-world resonance. For IVDIVO they are not a checklist and must be filtered through the current orbital canon.

In post-money Orbital Youth, stakes can rise through:
- exclusion from a unique expedition or qualification window;
- loss of trust;
- public failure;
- damaged relationship;
- responsibility for another person's safety;
- irreversible consent violation;
- status inside a team/school/project;
- loss of a rare lived experience;
- moral compromise;
- time window that cannot be fabricated again.

### Production gate KG-06 — NO STAKES INFLATION BY OLD ECONOMY
Do not manufacture urgency with rent, wages, credits or expensive objects when the actual canon has removed ordinary money/material scarcity as the default engine.

## 8. SYMPATHY ≠ MORAL CORRECTNESS

One of Card's strongest mechanisms is allowing the reader to care about both sides of a serious conflict.

### IVDIVO use
A strong opposition character should often be:
- competent;
- acting from a legitimate value;
- personally understandable;
- wrong only in a specific inference, priority or jurisdiction—not secretly malicious.

### Production gate KG-07 — SYMPATHETIC OPPOSITION
Before assigning a villain role, ask whether the book becomes stronger if both desired outcomes are legitimate but incompatible.

High-value Orbital examples:
- cadet autonomy vs instructor duty;
- human youth ambition vs robotic-person consent;
- specialist consciousness school vs mainstream reproducibility standards;
- individual privacy vs real group-safety need;
- orbital local law vs planetary family/legal claim.

## 9. ORDINARY + EXTRAORDINARY

Card's `hero/commonman` discussion points to a useful reader effect: familiarity earns belief; unusual capacity/choice/problem earns sustained attention.

### Production gate KG-08 — DUAL ANCHOR
Every major youth protagonist needs:
1. one ordinary-life anchor readers can recognize;
2. one story-worthy asymmetry that makes following this person necessary.

Not necessarily a power. It may be:
- unusual responsibility;
- rare access;
- a difficult relationship;
- exceptional persistence;
- a specific skill;
- a position at the exact junction where the problem occurs.

## 10. TRANSFORMATION MUST BE CAUSED

The more central the character and the larger the change, the more causal work must support it.

### Production chain
`OLD PATTERN → PRESSURE → FAILED/INSUFFICIENT STRATEGY → NEW INFORMATION/RELATIONSHIP COST → CHOICE → NEW BEHAVIOR UNDER A SIMILAR TEST`.

### Production gate KG-09 — CHANGE PROOF
A character arc is not complete until changed behavior is visible in a situation that resembles the earlier test enough for comparison.

### Production gate KG-10 — WITHHELD-CAUSE SIGNAL
If a character behaves radically out of pattern and the cause is intentionally hidden, another element of the text must mark that discrepancy as an intentional question rather than an author mistake.

## 11. VOICE IS RELATIONAL, NOT A SINGLE CHARACTER FONT

Card usefully observes that real people shift vocabulary, syntax, formality and attitude by relationship/context.

### Character voice matrix
For each major youth, sample at least:
- closest friend;
- parent/family;
- instructor/authority;
- stranger;
- romantic/attraction context;
- robot person/nonhuman peer;
- formal review/testimony.

### Production gate KG-11 — RELATIONSHIP REGISTER
If a character sounds identical in all seven contexts, the voice is probably an author style with a character name attached.

### Production gate KG-12 — NO SLANG PATCH
Do not fix indistinguishable youth voices by assigning different slang. Change what they notice, what they avoid saying, sentence strategy, humor engine, tolerance for uncertainty and relationship register.

## 12. POV DEPTH AS TEMPERATURE

Card's cinematic / light / deep penetration continuum is operationally useful if detached from prescriptive labels.

### DEEP
Best for:
- high personal cost;
- identity threat;
- shame;
- attraction;
- private contradiction;
- body/sensory transformation;
- decision under uncertainty.

Risk: claustrophobia, over-explanation, exhausting constant intensity.

### LIGHT/MIDDLE
Best default for:
- active scenes with enough interior filtering to keep character ownership;
- social ensemble scenes;
- technical activity where interpretation matters.

### DISTANT/CINEMATIC
Useful temporarily for:
- physical clarity;
- letting behavior speak;
- social observation;
- contrast after deep intimacy.

Risk: prose starts behaving like a poor screenplay and loses fiction's comparative advantage: direct access to interiority.

### Production gate KG-13 — POV TEMPERATURE CHOICE
For every major scene mark why the current distance is necessary. Do not let the manuscript remain permanently `hot` or permanently `camera-only` by habit.

## 13. DIALOGUE EXPOSITION FAILURE

Card demonstrates that dialogue becomes melodramatic when characters explicitly tell each other facts, histories and feelings that exist mainly for reader orientation.

### Production gate KG-14 — SHARED-HISTORY TEST
If both characters already know a fact, direct dialogue may repeat it only when the repetition itself is a tactic: accusation, denial, power move, joke, reminder, boundary or reinterpretation.
Otherwise use:
- POV inference;
- behavior;
- summary;
- a changed object/environment;
- implication.

## 14. Cross-source synthesis

### Card + Kress
Shared ground: behavior needs motive; character change must be prepared; POV distance affects reader experience.
Card adds stronger **attention hierarchy** and story-factor appropriateness.

### Card + Bell Voice
Shared ground: voice includes attitude, diction and sentence behavior rather than slang alone.
Card adds **relationship-specific register switching**.

### Card + Rosenfeld Intimate Character
Shared ground: proximity is controlled access to mind/sensation, not merely first vs third person.
Rosenfeld is stronger for embodied sensory intimacy; Card is useful for relative hot/cool modulation.

### Card + Brody
Shared ground: characters must be shaped by the kind of story and developmental pressure.
Card adds the warning that a story may legitimately require different amounts of characterization; Brody is stronger on YA-specific developmental stakes.

## 15. Reader-effect conclusions for IVDIVO

1. The reader should infer character before the narrator diagnoses character.
2. The world becomes immersive when a character's motive selects which parts of it matter.
3. Ensemble richness comes from differentiated causal jobs, not equal page allocation.
4. Post-money settings require **stronger motive design**, not weaker stakes.
5. A conscious robot must enter hierarchy as a person/actor with motives and relationships, not as movable milieu unless the specific robotic entity is genuinely non-personal infrastructure.
6. Specialist consciousness schools become interesting when rare practitioner motives, training cost in time/discipline, competing interpretations and institutional reproducibility limits are dramatized through people.
