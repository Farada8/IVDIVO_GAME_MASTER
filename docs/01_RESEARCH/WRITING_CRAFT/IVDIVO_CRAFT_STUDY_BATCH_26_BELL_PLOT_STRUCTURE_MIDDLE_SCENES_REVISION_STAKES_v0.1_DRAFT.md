## 1. Source structure map

The book progresses from definition and macro structure into idea generation, beginnings, middles, endings, scenes, complex plots, character arc, plotting systems, revision, plot patterns, common problems and practical tools. This makes its professional role primarily **plot architecture + scene diagnosis + revision troubleshooting**, not prose style, narratology or empirical reader psychology.

## 2. Mechanism A — structure as ordered pressure, not sacred geometry

Bell's useful transferable proposition is that story material becomes stronger when events are ordered so that pressure, choice and consequence accumulate rather than merely occur. The IVDIVO use is diagnostic, not doctrinal:

`DISTURBANCE → GOAL/PROBLEM → ESCALATING CONSTRAINTS → STRATEGY CHANGE → CRISIS CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE → ENDING PAYOFF`.

### Failure mode

Turning approximate structural landmarks into compulsory percentages can cause the manuscript to serve a template instead of its own causal logic. IVDIVO therefore treats Bell's structural model as a **stress test**, never as a canon of exact page numbers.

## 3. Mechanism B — three-O scene diagnostic

Bell's revision method can be abstracted as:

`OBJECTIVE → OBSTACLE → OUTCOME`.

A scene needs a local want/intention owned by a character; something must complicate or resist that intention; by the end, the state must be different enough to affect what happens next.

### Important modification after cross-source comparison

Bell often recommends an unrealized or negative outcome for tension. IVDIVO does **not** make that universal. Rosenfeld's scene model usefully allows blockage, complication, partial success or support; Hodgins and Le Guin also caution against reducing all narrative motion to combat.

Therefore the production rule becomes:

`OBJECTIVE → PRESSURE/COMPLICATION/SUPPORT → RESULT → CHANGED NEXT INTENTION`.

The result may be:
- failure;
- partial success;
- costly success;
- success that reveals a worse problem;
- success that strengthens opposition;
- restraint that prevents an error.

### Failure mode

If every scene ends in defeat, the novel becomes a mechanical failure machine. If every scene ends in success, pressure evaporates. Variation matters more than a fixed ratio.

## 4. Mechanism C — scene bull's-eye

Bell's practical revision language asks the writer to know what the scene is aiming toward: the exchange, discovery, choice, action, reversal or realization that makes this particular scene worth dramatizing.

IVDIVO abstraction:

`SCENE BULL'S-EYE = the smallest moment after which the scene's dramatic state cannot honestly be described exactly as before`.

It can be quiet. A bull's-eye is **not synonymous with cliffhanger**.

Examples:
- a character with authority chooses not to use it;
- a relationship receives a new explicit term;
- a clue changes category from observation to supported inference;
- a public claim becomes impossible to publish without context;
- a fallback option becomes feasible.

### Failure mode

Forcing an explosion, revelation or punchline into every scene produces melodramatic uniformity and destroys contemplative pacing.

## 5. Mechanism D — middle-variable rotation

A weak middle often repeats the same obstacle at higher volume. A stronger middle changes **which variable is under pressure**.

IVDIVO Middle Pressure Matrix fields:

`SEQUENCE | PRIMARY VARIABLE | CHARACTER OWNER | CURRENT BELIEF | TEST | OUTCOME | NEW COST | NEXT VARIABLE`.

For a systemic YA/SF novel, useful variables can include:
- authority;
- measurable usefulness;
- fairness/burden;
- privacy;
- truth/context;
- autonomy;
- fallback feasibility;
- provenance uncertainty;
- public legitimacy.

### Failure mode

`same argument + new meeting + slightly worse data` is not escalation. It is repetition.

## 6. Mechanism E — plot tests character

Bell's character/plot linkage aligns with Weiland, Storr, McKee and earlier IVDIVO synthesis: external pressure is most valuable when it forces the character to choose in a way that exposes the operating model underneath.

`PRESSURE → INTERPRETATION → CHOICE → ACTION/RESTRAINT → CONSEQUENCE → MODEL PRESSURE`.

A character arc that exists only in explanatory thoughts is not fully integrated. Conversely, a plot that could happen identically with any protagonist is under-coupled.

## 7. Mechanism F — stakes without artificial inflation

Bell's broader suspense framework often speaks in terms of physical, professional or psychological death. IVDIVO retains the useful insight — the reader should understand what can be meaningfully lost — but rejects compulsory literal-death escalation.

For YA / civic SF, high stakes can be:
- loss of legitimate authority;
- damage to a public record;
- loss of trust;
- consent/agency violation;
- privacy loss;
- public misrepresentation;
- repeated burden on vulnerable or reliable participants;
- removal of a safety benefit;
- institutional loss of legitimacy;
- relationship rupture.

### Failure mode

Adding a bomb, killer or apocalypse merely to make a moral/technical story feel 'big enough' can cheapen the actual dilemma.

## 8. Mechanism G — weak-scene ranking before line editing

A useful revision move is to rank the weakest scenes, then diagnose their objective, pressure and outcome before polishing sentences.

IVDIVO procedure:

1. Select the 10 weakest scenes/chapters by reader-energy and causal necessity.
2. For each, state `OWNER / OBJECTIVE / PRESSURE / BULL'S-EYE / OUTCOME / NEXT INTENTION`.
3. If no meaningful objective or outcome exists, test CUT / MERGE / SUMMARY / REBUILD.
4. Only after structural repair run prose/cadence/line-edit passes.

This protects the project from spending hours beautifying a scene that should not exist.

## 9. Cross-source comparison

### Bell ↔ Rosenfeld
Agreement: scenes need intention, pressure and changed state. Modification: Rosenfeld offers a broader result vocabulary than compulsory negative outcome.

### Bell ↔ Swain
Agreement: scene causality and forward pressure. Swain gives a finer stimulus/reaction and scene/sequel bridge; Bell gives simpler revision diagnostics.

### Bell ↔ McKee
Agreement: a scene must matter through change, gap, choice or value shift. McKee is stronger on value systems; Bell is more operational for rapid weak-scene triage.

### Bell ↔ Chatman
Potential tension: a non-kernel passage can still be valuable as satellite material. Therefore 'not plot-critical' does not automatically mean 'cut'. Character, tone, motif, social reality and foreshadowing can justify a satellite.

### Bell ↔ Hodgins / Le Guin
Correction against dogma: conflict is not the only model of narrative energy. Process, relation, recognition, growth, social texture and rhythm may matter without conventional antagonism.

## 10. IVDIVO production gates — KQ to LB

### Gate KQ — Scene Objective Exists
A dramatized scene must contain an identifiable immediate intention, need, question or task owned by someone in the scene.

### Gate KR — Obstacle Type Changes
Across a sequence, pressure should rotate among interpersonal, internal, physical, institutional, informational, ethical and temporal constraints rather than repeat one obstacle.

### Gate KS — Outcome Changes State
A scene must change action options, knowledge status, relationship, cost, authority, evidence, emotion or future intention.

### Gate KT — Bull's-Eye Has Function
A scene has a specific focal turn worth dramatizing. A bull's-eye need not be loud.

### Gate KU — No Mandatory Negative Outcome
Do not force every scene to end in failure. Use failure, partial success, costly success, restraint, support and success-that-complicates.

### Gate KV — Middle Variable Rotation
The middle must change what kind of problem the protagonist is solving, not merely intensify the same conversation.

### Gate KW — Stakes Specificity
State exactly what can be lost by this character, relationship or institution now.

### Gate KX — No Artificial Death Inflation
Do not introduce lethal stakes solely to make a nonlethal human problem seem important.

### Gate KY — Character Choice Reveals Model
Major plot pressure must produce a choice that reveals or changes the character's operating model.

### Gate KZ — Weak-Scene Ranking Before Line Edit
Developmental triage precedes cadence polishing.

### Gate LA — Structure Is Diagnostic, Not Template
No percentage/beat model may override better character causality or the Story Contract.

### Gate LB — Movement Transition Changes Strategy
A movement/act transition must alter plan, authority, evidence threshold, alliance, cost or governing question.
