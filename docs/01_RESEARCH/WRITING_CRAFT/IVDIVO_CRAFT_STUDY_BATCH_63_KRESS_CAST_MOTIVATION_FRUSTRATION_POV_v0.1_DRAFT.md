## 1. HIGH-VALUE SYNTHESIS

### Character engine
`CULTURE + HISTORY + CURRENT WANT → FRICTION → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC RESPONSE → TACTIC → COUNTERPRESSURE → TACTIC CHANGE / REFUSAL → CONSEQUENCE`.

### POV engine
`WHO EXPERIENCES → WHAT THEY CAN KNOW → HOW CLOSE NARRATION STANDS → WHAT VERSION OF THE STORY THE READER RECEIVES`.

## 2. PRODUCTION GATES

### B63-G01 — CAST ATTENTION BUDGET
Personhood is equal; narrative weight is not. Assign explicit book-level hierarchy.

### B63-G02 — INTRODUCTION THROUGH ATTENTION
Character introduction should reveal observer and observed simultaneously where possible.

### B63-G03 — ACTION BEFORE LABEL
Replace personality labels with behavior under meaningful pressure whenever practical.

### B63-G04 — CULTURAL MOTIVATION GROUNDWORK
The stranger a motivation is within that society, the more causal context it needs.

### B63-G05 — CHANGER/STAYER DECISION
Do not assume every YA lead must transform. Decide whether the book tests change, resistance or costly stability.

### B63-G06 — CHANGE MUST BE EARNED
If the character changes, later relevant behavior must demonstrate the new strategy without an explanatory lesson paragraph.

### B63-G07 — FRUSTRATION FINGERPRINT
Major characters must not all respond to blocked desire with the same sarcasm, anger, competence or withdrawal.

### B63-G08 — SECONDARY EMOTION
Frustration/love/fear may carry a second emotion that changes tactic and prevents emotional monoculture.

### B63-G09 — BREAKING POINT PRELOAD
No major emotional rupture without accumulated pressure plus prior self-control.

### B63-G10 — EMOTIONAL DIALOGUE SHAPING
Compress realistic repetition; preserve only the amount/style that is character-true and reader-useful.

### B63-G11 — UNDERSTATEMENT OPTION
A smaller-than-expected response may carry more force when contextual contrast supports it.

### B63-G12 — HUMOUR EXPECTATION CONTRACT
Reversal depends on shared audience expectation; do not explain a functioning joke afterward.

### B63-G13 — POV VERSION TEST
Prototype at least one major scene through an alternate POV before locking a complex multi-character book.

### B63-G14 — POV MINIMUM
Every POV pays a switching cost. Keep only minds that own necessary action, information or meaning.

### B63-G15 — FIRST-PERSON FAIRNESS
Do not conceal decisive information the narrator actually knows merely to protect suspense.

### B63-G16 — POV DISTANCE CONTINUITY
Close/middle/distant third is a continuum. Avoid unmotivated deep→encyclopedic→deep jumps.

### B63-G17 — NO ROTATION FILLER
A scene is not justified because it is a POV character's turn.

### B63-G18 — CLIMAX POV OWNERSHIP
Final decisive POV belongs to the consciousness whose choice/price/interpretation best completes the local book engine.

### B63-G19 — OMNISCIENT COMMITMENT
Authorial presence must be deliberate and established; random head-hopping is not omniscience.

### B63-G20 — FOUR-PERSONA WORKFLOW
`CHARACTER → WRITER → READER → CRITIC`.
Do not run all revision gates during generation.

## 3. IVDIVO-SPECIFIC CORRECTIONS

### Post-money youth
Do not import Kress's jobs/money examples literally. Replace with nonfungible stakes: trust, concrete responsibility, belonging, consent, time, relationship, unique role/mission, mastery and reputation.

### Conscious robots
Kress's historical assumption that nonhuman POV must be mostly human is not adopted as a law. IVDIVO requires reader legibility **plus** real nonhuman embodiment/attention where canon supports it, while preserving full personhood and refusal rights.

### Consciousness schools
A character's belief or spiritual interpretation is never promoted automatically to world fact. Use POV-access and evidence provenance.

### YA psychology
Developmental pressure does not imply compulsory positive transformation or healing.
