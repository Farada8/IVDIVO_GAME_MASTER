# 1. Highest-value transferable mechanisms

## 1.1 Developmental engine is not a genre
YA can carry SF, mystery, romance, thriller, institutional drama, adventure, sport or social fiction. What makes the story meaningfully YA is that the external conflict changes specifically youth-relevant conditions:

`AUTONOMY / ADULT AUTHORITY / PEER BELONGING / FIRST RESPONSIBILITY / IDENTITY / INTIMACY / FUTURE SELF / STATUS / CONSENT`.

Production test:
> If the protagonist could be forty years old with no material change to the social/developmental stakes, the YA layer is probably decorative.

## 1.2 Active commitment after disturbance
Brody's Catalyst/Debate/Break-Into-2 sequence is useful when stripped of percentage doctrine:

`DISTURBANCE → PROCESSING → MEANINGFUL CHOICE OR ACCEPTED RESPONSIBILITY → TRACKABLE OBJECTIVE`.

A story can begin with a quiet disturbance, a correct decision, a discovery or a relational event. The event need not be huge or irreversible.

## 1.3 Trackable A-story objective
Brody is strongest when warning against an Act 2 that becomes a pile of premise scenes. The protagonist/team needs an objective that can accumulate causal attempts.

IVDIVO formulation:

`ONE MAIN STORY → OBJECTIVE → ATTEMPT → RESISTANCE/NEW INFORMATION → CHANGED STATE → NEXT ATTEMPT`.

## 1.4 Premise payoff
The distinctive experience promised by the novel must actually occur on the page.

For Orbital Youth this means:
- orbital transit/space/robotic society/training/education/social structures must alter choices and relationships;
- the book cannot be an Earth teen plot with renamed corridors;
- equally, worldbuilding cannot become tourism detached from the A-story.

## 1.5 Midpoint = model change
Brody's false-victory/false-defeat doctrine is too restrictive, but her underlying observation is useful: the middle should alter the story's operational state.

Use:
`GOAL / METHOD / MODEL / RELATIONSHIP / EVIDENCE STATUS / CONSTRAINTS` changes materially.

Do not require:
- false victory/defeat;
- ticking clock;
- public spectacle;
- secret;
- death;
- romance escalation.

## 1.6 Aftermath as causality
A reflective scene is not dead space if it changes:
- interpretation;
- commitment;
- relationship;
- threshold for action;
- next strategy.

This retains useful Dark-Night functionality while rejecting a mandatory breakdown/wallowing beat.

# 2. Relationship correction: from `B Story` to personhood

Brody repeatedly treats the B-story person as an embodiment/teacher of theme. That is useful as a diagnostic for relational pressure but dangerous as a production rule.

## IVDIVO replacement

`INDEPENDENT PERSON A + INDEPENDENT PERSON B → DIFFERENT WANTS/VALUES/BOUNDARIES → SHARED TASK/ATTRACTION/CONFLICT → MUTUAL INFLUENCE → CHANGED RELATIONSHIP AND STRATEGY`.

Requirements:
- each named relationship character has an independent want;
- each can refuse;
- each has costs that exist even when protagonist is absent;
- disagreement is not automatically a misunderstanding;
- the relationship does not exist to speak the author's thesis.

### Conscious robot gate
For a conscious robotic person this is non-negotiable.
If the robot can be replaced by an intelligent terminal without changing consent, conflict, relationship or decision, the robot has not been written as a person.

# 3. Buddy Love — useful and rejected layers

Brody's ingredients:
`incomplete hero + counterpart + complication`.

Useful:
- relationship as A-story must have a real relational objective/change, not merely decorative attraction;
- counterpart should reveal dimensions of the other person that ordinary relationships do not;
- complication should be causal and relationship-specific;
- the relationship may change both people even if they do not end together.

Rejected:
- one person makes another whole;
- every relationship needs a wedge;
- the wedge may be manufactured from a secret/misunderstanding;
- equal POV/transformation is mandatory for both partners.

IVDIVO model:
`TWO COMPLETE AGENTS → UNIQUE MUTUAL PRESSURE → RELATIONAL CHOICE → PRICE → NEW TERMS OF RELATIONSHIP`.

# 4. Institutionalized — major relevance to Orbital Youth

Brody's `group / choice / sacrifice` lens exposes a useful YA pressure: belonging to systems while developing autonomy.

## Transferable institutional questions
- What real benefit does the institution provide?
- Why do reasonable people join/stay?
- What are the rules and who can change them?
- What access/responsibility comes with membership?
- What is voluntary, inherited, assumed or coerced?
- What happens to dissent?
- What does exit cost?
- Can a member negotiate/reform rather than simply submit or flee?

## IVDIVO expansion beyond Brody's trinary
Brody tends toward:
`JOIN / ESCAPE / BURN IT DOWN`.

IVDIVO must also allow:
- reform;
- negotiated membership;
- partial membership;
- bounded refusal;
- plural institutions;
- internal appeal;
- changing rules;
- institution learning from failure;
- loyalty without villainy.

### Anti-caricature gate
An institution becomes dramatically stronger when it is competent and beneficial in some domains while still having contested norms, blind spots or procedural failures.

`INSTITUTIONAL OPPOSITION ≠ EVIL INSTITUTION`.

# 5. New failure modes

1. **B-STORY-AS-TUTOR** — side character exists to teach theme.
2. **COMPLETION ROMANCE** — hero needs another person to become a whole person.
3. **RELATIONSHIP-WEDGE MANUFACTURING** — arbitrary secret/misunderstanding merely keeps pair apart.
4. **EQUAL-POV FOG** — equal saga importance becomes equal book page time.
5. **TRACK WITHOUT CONNECTION** — multi-POV stories become unrelated mini-novels.
6. **INSTITUTIONAL BINARY** — only join/escape/destroy are imaginable.
7. **COMPANY-MAN CARICATURE** — loyalty is treated as proof of moral corruption.
8. **NAIF INFO-DUMP TOUR** — newcomer exists to receive explanations.
9. **WRONG-WAY MONOCULTURE** — competent youth forced into stupid choices to manufacture growth.
10. **ACCEPTANCE-AS-ONLY-GROWTH** — every developmental arc ends in a therapeutic acceptance lesson.
11. **CATALYST SIZE FETISH** — story can only start with a huge external event.
12. **STAKES-INFLATION TOOLBOX** — death, secret, clock, spectacle added to satisfy escalation doctrine.
13. **TEN-GENRE TOTALISM** — taxonomy mistaken for complete theory of story.
14. **GENRE-INGREDIENT CHECKBOXING** — conventions inserted because a model says they belong.

# 6. Cross-source synthesis — provisional

## Brody × Truby
Brody provides useful YA developmental pressure and reader-promise diagnostics. Truby is stronger on organic causality. Default to Truby-style causality when a beat label conflicts with character action.

## Brody × Rosenfeld
Rosenfeld supplies the more precise scene engine:
`prior consequence → intention → complication/support → result → next intention`.
Use Brody at book-level diagnosis, Rosenfeld at scene-level causality.

## Brody × Card
Card's character hierarchy corrects Brody's tendency to define multiple heroes through roughly equal attention/transformation.
`SAGA EQUALITY ≠ BOOK-LEVEL EQUAL POV`.

## Brody × Kress
Kress's culture/motivation/frustration fingerprint prevents generic YA psychology. A young person can act competently and still need to grow.

## Brody × Herman/Zunshine
For institutions/AI/person-model stories, prediction, institutional category or narrator interpretation must not replace a person's reasons/consent/source status.
