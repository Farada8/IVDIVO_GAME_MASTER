## 4. PROVISIONAL CLAIMS — CH1–10 ONLY
### L40-C01 — An opening is a reader threshold, not a mandatory action beat
The beginning must establish enough voice/world/question/character promise to make the reader cross from actual world into the fictional one. Lodge demonstrates radically different viable openings: character summary, enigmatic confession, landscape, dialogue, self-introduction, philosophical reflection, jeopardy, frame-story, metafiction.

### L40-C02 — Voice and information load are part of opening friction
At the beginning the reader is still learning syntax, names, relationships, time/place and tonal rules. Opening design therefore includes cognitive onboarding, not only “hook intensity.”

### L40-C03 — Authorial presence is a rhetorical variable
Intrusive narration can weaken immersive realism but can also supply thematic/encyclopedic perspective, irony or comic self-consciousness. “No authorial intrusion” is not a universal law.

### L40-C04 — Suspense is question + delayed answer, and delay can slow the prose
Hardy sustains acute physical suspense by restricting POV and spending time on detailed perception/reflection. Suspense therefore does not imply short syntax or constant acceleration.

### L40-C05 — Narrative mode can transform the same underlying uncertainty
“What happens?” may become “How did it happen?” or “What does it mean?” depending on what information is withheld or supplied. Suspense and mystery are neighboring information structures, not fixed genres only.

### L40-C06 — Youth voice is designed, not transcribed speech
Salinger’s skaz creates an illusion of spontaneous teenage speech through repetition, colloquial syntax, hyperbole, errors, limited vocabulary and rhythmic control. Literal transcription of real speech would be unreadable.

### L40-C07 — Voice earns length through implication and rhythm
A limited or apparently impoverished vocabulary can carry a whole novel when it produces humor, implied emotion, social judgment, subtext and distinctive rhythm. Slang alone is insufficient.

### L40-C08 — Addressee changes voice
Epistolary narration is shaped by the anticipated response of a specific recipient. Even one-sided discourse becomes relational and revealing because the narrator imagines objections/reactions.

### L40-C09 — POV is an ethical/emotional allocation of access
The same event produces a different novel depending on whose experience is privileged. Point of view affects reader sympathy, judgment, information and intensity.

### L40-C10 — POV consistency is a reader contract
A sudden unmotivated shift into another mind does more than add information: it changes subject-status and reader expectations about access. Switching requires a visible aesthetic/function principle.

### L40-C11 — Character language and narrative language need not be identical
Henry James demonstrates a naive/limited perceiver rendered through mature authorial language. Close access does not require total lexical imitation of the POV character.

### L40-C12 — Mystery can remain unresolved when the story function is not puzzle-solving
Kipling uses mystery effects to lead toward tragedy/irrecoverability rather than a detective solution. Closure requirements depend on the book’s actual contract.

### L40-C13 — A name is part of character/world rhetoric
In fiction names are rarely neutral; connotation, social plausibility and symbolic pressure interact. But over-signifying names can become schematic or absurd.

### L40-C14 — Free indirect style can create intimacy without eliminating narrator agency
It merges third-person grammar with character vocabulary/perception while preserving room for tonal distance and authorial shaping.

### L40-C15 — Interior monologue requires variation
Pure stream rendering risks slowness and trivial overload. Joyce maintains effect through shifts among interior monologue, free indirect style and external narration and by making associative patterns character-specific.

## 5. PROVISIONAL IVDIVO MECHANISMS
### M-L40-01 — OPENING FUNCTION MATRIX
For the first pages choose the dominant function(s):
`VOICE / CHARACTER CONTRADICTION / STORY QUESTION / RELATIONSHIP / WORLD RULE IN ACTION / JEOPARDY / FRAME / MYSTERY`.

Do not require all at maximum intensity.

### M-L40-02 — READER ONBOARDING BUDGET
`NEW NAMES + NEW RELATIONSHIPS + NEW WORLD RULES + NEW TERMS + NEW POV RULES` must fit the reader’s early working model. If everything is novel at once, reduce one axis rather than adding explanatory paragraphs.

### M-L40-03 — SUSPENSE TEMPO SEPARATION
`QUESTION URGENCY ≠ SENTENCE SPEED`.
A high-stakes unresolved question can support slowed perception, thought, sensory detail or long syntax if the delay intensifies reader attention.

### M-L40-04 — YA SKAZ AS CONTROLLED ILLUSION
`CHARACTER ATTITUDE → RECURRENT LEXICAL HABITS → SYNTAX RANGE → HYPERBOLE/UNDERSTATEMENT → WHAT IS NOT SAID → RHYTHM`.

Do not equate “teen voice” with fashionable slang.

### M-L40-05 — ADDRESSEE-SHAPED VOICE
For diary/message/testimony/interview scenes ask:
`WHO IS BEING ADDRESSED? → WHAT RESPONSE IS EXPECTED? → WHAT DOES SPEAKER WANT THEM TO BELIEVE? → WHAT GETS OMITTED/EXAGGERATED?`

### M-L40-06 — POV ACCESS CONTRACT
Before a scene specify:
`WHO PERCEIVES? → WHAT CAN THEY KNOW? → WHAT LANGUAGE FILTERS IT? → WHAT IS READER ALLOWED TO KNOW BEYOND THEM? → WHY?`

### M-L40-07 — MODE VARIATION FOR INTERIORITY
Use deliberate modulation among:
`EXTERNAL NARRATION ↔ FREE INDIRECT ↔ DIRECT THOUGHT / INTERIOR MONOLOGUE`.
The mode changes distance and pace; do not leave every paragraph at maximum interior depth.

### M-L40-08 — MYSTERY CONTRACT TEST
Ask whether the uncertainty promises:
- a solvable answer;
- a bounded operational answer while ontology remains open;
- an intentionally irrecoverable human truth;
- retrospective reinterpretation rather than puzzle solution.

Then pay the correct contract.

## 6. PROVISIONAL FAILURE MODES
1. **Hook Monoculture** — every book forced to open with jeopardy/action.
2. **Opening Encyclopedia** — reader receives names, history, systems and terminology before a human model stabilizes.
3. **Intrusion Purism** — any narrator commentary treated as automatically bad.
4. **Suspense = Chop** — tension mechanically rendered as short sentences/fragments.
5. **Teen Slang Costume** — youth voice reduced to topical slang.
6. **Universal Wit** — every teenager shares the same clever authorial cadence.
7. **Transcript Fallacy** — “realistic dialogue/voice” confused with literal speech transcription.
8. **POV Teleportation** — convenient unmarked access to another mind.
9. **Deep-POV Monoculture** — all prose forced into maximum character lexicon and interiority.
10. **Mystery Contract Mismatch** — book promises puzzle closure and substitutes ambiguity, or promises existential uncertainty and over-explains it.
11. **Name Overcoding** — names become obvious symbolic labels instead of plausible social facts.
12. **Interior Monologue Saturation** — constant direct thought slows action and erases narrative texture.

## 7. EARLY CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON
### Edgerton / Brody vs Lodge opening
Edgerton/Brody are useful for disturbance and YA tracking, but Lodge demonstrates a much wider viable opening repertoire. IVDIVO should diagnose whether an opening creates a compelling reader threshold rather than force an inciting-action form.

### Bell / McKee vs Lodge suspense
Bell and McKee emphasize questions, pressure and delay; Lodge’s Hardy analysis is a strong anti-chop counterexample: suspense can be intensified by slowed thought and expanded temporal experience.

### Card / LaPlante vs Lodge POV
Strong convergence on POV as access and reader alignment. Lodge adds evidence that POV character vocabulary and narration need not collapse into one voice; distance can be artistically productive.

### Zunshine vs Lodge mystery
Zunshine gives provenance/evidence-status machinery. Lodge distinguishes the reader contract: not every mystery exists to be solved. Together: first determine whether the book promises solution, bounded operation, or irrecoverability; then design evidence accordingly.

## 8. EARLY IVDIVO GATES
**L40-G01 Opening Contract** — opening earns attention by a specific reader function, not mandatory disaster.

**L40-G02 Early Load** — do not introduce more world/social terms than the scene can causally use.

**L40-G03 Suspense Syntax Freedom** — sentence length follows reader effect, not genre label.

**L40-G04 Youth Voice Ownership** — lexical/rhythmic habits belong to one character and arise from attitude/social experience.

**L40-G05 No Slang Substitute** — slang cannot stand in for motivation, relationship or worldview.

**L40-G06 Addressee Test** — messages/testimonies must change according to recipient.

**L40-G07 POV Access Contract** — no convenient mind access without established rule/function.

**L40-G08 Distance Modulation** — deliberate external/FIS/interior shifts are allowed; accidental whiplash is not.

**L40-G09 Mystery Contract** — define what category of answer this book owes before constructing reveal ladder.

**L40-G10 Interior Mode Rotation** — direct thought is one tool, not default prose texture.

## 9. EARLY IVDIVO APPLICATION
### Orbital Youth
The `Teenage Skaz` chapter supports a crucial distinction: a young voice should be a crafted rhythm and attitude, not copied contemporary slang. Post-money orbital teenagers should differ by what they notice, exaggerate, refuse to say, joke about and assume is normal — especially where their “normal” (robots as persons, abundant material goods, orbital infrastructure) differs from current Earth teenagers.
