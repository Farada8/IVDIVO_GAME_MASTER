## 4. PROVISIONAL CLAIMS — PART I ONLY
### Z38-C01 — Readers routinely infer mental states from behavior
Fiction relies on readers constructing possible wants, beliefs, feelings and intentions behind observable behavior, often with very little explicit explanation.

### Z38-C02 — Mental-state attribution is fallible
The usefulness of inferring mental states does not make an inference true. A behavior can support multiple motives; this gap is narratively productive.

### Z38-C03 — Fiction can strategically undertell mental states
A writer can provide physical/linguistic cues and require the reader to supply a plausible emotional/intentional model. The amount of supplied interpretation is a design variable.

### Z38-C04 — Reader participation can be calibrated
Too much explicit mental explanation reduces interpretive work; too little can destroy coherence. Strong prose calibrates the amount of inference required.

### Z38-C05 — Different narrative forms distribute mind-information differently
Drama, close prose, distant prose and experimental narration give readers different combinations of direct mental access, behavior and ambiguity.

### Z38-C06 — Complexity is not inherently anti-reader
A cognitively demanding text may create value for readers who enjoy tracking layered or ambiguous mental states. Difficulty itself can become part of the reader experience.

### Z38-C07 — Literary experimentation should not be reduced to a simple progress/history story
Techniques that seem modern or anti-natural can have historical precedents. Genre/form judgments need literary-historical context.

### Z38-C08 — Cognitive explanation should coexist with literary-historical explanation
A cognitive model is not a replacement for character, genre, style, history or culture. Zunshine explicitly argues for interdisciplinary compatibility rather than totalizing reduction.

### Z38-C09 — “Character as possible person” is a reader effect, not literal ontology
Readers can know that a character is fictional while still using person-modeling machinery to reason about that character. The resulting richness should not be confused with an independently existing off-page person.

### Z38-C10 — Reader enjoyment may involve controlled uncertainty about minds
Part of fiction’s appeal may come from temporarily receiving more or differently structured access to minds than ordinary life permits while still confronting gaps, misreadings and hidden motives.

## 5. PROVISIONAL IVDIVO MECHANISMS
### M-Z38-01 — BEHAVIOR → MULTIPLE MOTIVE MODELS
`OBSERVABLE ACTION → MODEL A / MODEL B / MODEL C → NEW EVIDENCE → MODEL UPDATE`.

Do not attach one definitive motive too early merely because the writer knows the backstory.

### M-Z38-02 — MENTAL-STATE UNDERTELLING
`ACTION / WORDING / GESTURE → ENOUGH CONTEXT → READER INFERENCE`, with explicit interior explanation added only when it creates additional value.

### M-Z38-03 — INFERENCE LOAD CALIBRATION
For an important scene ask:
`WHAT MUST READER KNOW? → WHAT CAN READER INFER? → WHAT SHOULD REMAIN UNCERTAIN? → WHAT EVIDENCE WILL LATER UPDATE IT?`

### M-Z38-04 — ACCESS-DESIGN MATRIX
`DIRECT INTERIOR ACCESS / OBSERVABLE BEHAVIOR / OTHER-PERSON REPORT / DOCUMENT / SYSTEM INFERENCE / UNKNOWN`.
Use the mix deliberately rather than defaulting to explanatory close POV.

### M-Z38-05 — DIFFICULTY-AS-FUNCTION
Complexity is justified when it produces a specific reader effect: ambiguity, social inference, ethical uncertainty, layered minds or retrospective re-modeling. Complexity that merely withholds basic orientation is not automatically sophisticated.

## 6. PROVISIONAL FAILURE MODES
1. **Mind Explanation Saturation** — every gesture is immediately translated into motive by narration.
2. **Single-Motive Certainty** — complex behavior is collapsed into one author-certified psychological cause.
3. **Inference Starvation** — reader receives too little evidence to construct any stable model.
4. **False Cognitive Authority** — historical cognitive claims are treated as current science.
5. **Character Ontology Confusion** — inferred off-page richness is mistaken for actual written character development.
6. **Complexity Prestige** — obscurity is treated as literary value without reader-function payoff.
7. **Culture-Blind Cognitivism** — cognitive tendencies are invoked while genre, history, institution and culture are ignored.
8. **Direct-Access Monoculture** — close interior narration is treated as the only way to create intimacy.

## 7. EARLY CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON
### With LaPlante
Strong convergence: observation is not interpretation; POV belief is not world fact. LaPlante is stronger on practical reliability/POV routing; Zunshine supplies a deeper vocabulary for the reader’s construction and tracking of mind-models.

### With Card / McKee Character
Card/McKee emphasize character evidence through choice and action. Zunshine explains why behavior can support provisional mental models without requiring narration to name traits or motives.

### With McKee Dialogue / Baxter (future strict)
Dialogue can function as evidence of intention while remaining incomplete or misleading. Exact wording matters because readers model what a person wants another person to believe.

## 8. EARLY IVDIVO GATES
**Z38-G01 Behavior Before Motive Label** — render observable choice/gesture first when reader inference can do useful work.

**Z38-G02 Multiple Motive Models** — before canonizing a surprising behavior, test at least two plausible character interpretations.

**Z38-G03 Reader Inference Budget** — decide what the reader infers rather than explaining every mind-state.

**Z38-G04 No System-Mind Telepathy** — an AI/robotic prediction of behavior is not automatically knowledge of motive.

**Z38-G05 Conscious Robot Personhood** — treat conscious robots as minds with opaque/partial access, not transparent diagnostic devices.

**Z38-G06 Historical Science Firewall** — do not use Zunshine’s 2006 cognitive/clinical claims as current technical canon.

**Z38-G07 Complexity Must Pay** — layered mind-tracking earns complexity only if it changes reader model, choice, ethics or suspense.
