## 4. PROVISIONAL CLAIMS — INTRODUCTION + CH1–2 ONLY

### C39-C01 — SF is better treated as a historical discussion/mode than as one fixed plot genre
The Introduction argues that SF borrows plot structures from mystery, romance, horror, adventure and other genres; much of its distinctiveness lies in how the world, language, ideas and consequences are read.

### C39-C02 — Sense of wonder is not enough; consequence converts spectacle into story
Early SF often treated invention/new place as sufficient payoff. The genre's maturation involved asking what follows from the novum rather than ending at amazement or destroying the invention to avoid consequences.

### C39-C03 — Conceptual breakthrough is a distinctive reader payoff
A strand of SF behaves like a scientific/conceptual mystery: accumulated observations or explanations change the reader's model until a new conceptual frame becomes available.

### C39-C04 — SF reading requires active translation
Terms, metaphors, ordinary words and inherited genre shorthand may behave differently in a transformed world. Readers infer missing rules from context and genre competence.

### C39-C05 — Future people should not exist to explain their normality to the reader
The Introduction notes that competent inhabitants of a transformed future normally accept their own environment. World information is more convincing when embedded in action, assumptions and partial clues rather than tutorial dialogue.

### C39-C06 — Genre is cumulative memory
SF texts inherit devices, metaphors, assumptions and counter-arguments from prior SF. A later work can communicate quickly by activating that accumulated reading competence.

### C39-C07 — World can function as causal protagonist without replacing human character
SF can foreground ecology, planet, institution or conceptual world-system because its conditions shape how occupants live. This does not justify flat human/nonhuman agents; the world earns prominence through causal force.

### C39-C08 — Narrative form evolves alongside the problem of making speculation credible
Stableford's history repeatedly links speculative ambition to the search for frames/facilitating devices capable of making inaccessible places, futures and transformations narratable and sufficiently plausible.

### C39-C09 — Plausibility is produced partly by narrative labour, not only by scientific correctness
Poe/Wells/Verne histories show writers developing devices, voices and frames that help readers accept speculation. This is a craft observation, not permission to violate current IVDIVO technical canon.

### C39-C10 — SF repeatedly hybridizes with existing popular genres
Western, adventure, mystery, romance, satire, utopia, disaster and other forms supplied plot engines into which speculative conditions were introduced.

### C39-C11 — Market/editor/community structures shape formal conventions
The magazine era was not merely a list of authors: editors, letter columns, fandom, payment, distribution and format influenced what kinds of stories could be written, repeated, criticized and evolved.

### C39-C12 — Infodump can function as clue, but only when information changes the reader's model
Attebery observes that early SF explanatory blocks sometimes functioned like evidence in a scientific mystery leading toward conceptual breakthrough. IVDIVO adaptation: explanation earns space when it changes hypothesis, action, stakes or world model—not because SF 'needs exposition'.

### C39-C13 — Escalation by bigger technology is historically easy and artistically limited
Early space opera repeatedly increased powers, machines and villains. This can produce widening vistas, but without new human/social/conceptual pressure it becomes magnitude inflation.

### C39-C14 — Competence narratives carry ideology
The Campbell-era engineer/rational hero pattern demonstrates that technical competence and 'rational' solutions may quietly embed assumptions about whose costs count and whose emotions are dismissed.

### C39-C15 — Technical success does not settle ethical or social truth
Stories such as the Campbell-shaped competence tradition reveal why IVDIVO must distinguish `WORKS TECHNICALLY` from `IS JUST / CONSENSUAL / HUMANLY ACCEPTABLE`.

### C39-C16 — SF matures when distinctive voice and social/emotional complexity survive conceptual ambition
The history from pulp formula to Bester/Cordwainer Smith/Galaxy demonstrates that concept, style, character, satire and social thought are not mutually exclusive.

## 5. PROVISIONAL IVDIVO MECHANISMS

### M-C39-01 — NOVUM → CONSEQUENCE ENGINE
`CONSEQUENTIAL DIFFERENCE → LOCAL USE/BENEFIT → SECOND-ORDER EFFECT → HUMAN/NONHUMAN RESPONSE → REVISED PRACTICE`.

Do not stop at gadget wonder.

### M-C39-02 — CONCEPTUAL BREAKTHROUGH LADDER
`OBSERVATION → PARTIAL MODEL → NEW FACT → CONTRADICTION → REVISED MODEL → ACTION`.

Information without model/action change is lore, not a major reveal.

### M-C39-03 — FUTURE-NORMALITY EMBEDDING
`NORMAL ACTION FOR CHARACTER → UNFAMILIAR DETAIL FOR READER → CONTEXTUAL INFERENCE → LATER CONFIRMATION ONLY IF NEEDED`.

### M-C39-04 — GENRE HYBRID CORE
Name the actual local plot engine separately from SF mode:
`MYSTERY / ROMANCE / RESCUE / INSTITUTIONAL / ADVENTURE / COMING-OF-AGE / PROCEDURAL / OTHER` + `SPECULATIVE CONDITION`.

This protects ONE MAIN STORY from becoming 'a tour of the future'.

### M-C39-05 — TECHNICAL / NORMATIVE SPLIT
`SYSTEM WORKS?` and `SYSTEM SHOULD GOVERN PEOPLE THIS WAY?` are different questions.

### M-C39-06 — READER SHORTHAND WITH CANON FIREWALL
Inherited SF vocabulary can compress explanation only if the term has the same meaning in IVDIVO. Familiar genre shorthand must not silently import another universe's ontology.

### M-C39-07 — EDITORIAL ECOLOGY LESSON
Production systems shape prose. A Writers' Room can increase quality only if its gates create variation and correction rather than forcing every book into the same formula.

## 6. PROVISIONAL FAILURE MODES
1. **Wonder Without Consequence** — technology/place exists mainly to amaze.
2. **Explosion Reset** — disruptive invention is destroyed so long-term consequences need not be written.
3. **Future Tutorial People** — inhabitants explain normal life to one another for the reader.
4. **Legacy Shortcut Leakage** — familiar SF term imports unapproved ontology.
5. **Infodump Immunity** — exposition excused merely because SF often contains explanation.
6. **Magnitude Escalation** — bigger machine/enemy substitutes for new pressure.
7. **Competence = Moral Authority** — best technician is assumed to have best ethical answer.
8. **Emotion as Irrational Noise** — costs to vulnerable people discounted because a system is efficient.
9. **Formula Ecosystem** — production process rewards repetition until all stories converge.
10. **SF as Plot Engine** — 'future technology' mistaken for the book's actual causal story.

## 8. EARLY IVDIVO APPLICATION
### Orbital Youth
Do not use SF as a license for continuous wonder. Orbital normality becomes story when one difference changes friendship, autonomy, trust, movement, consent, responsibility or belonging. Material abundance does not erase non-fungible human stakes.

### Conscious robots
Avoid inheriting the old SF machine/servant default. The genre history is useful precisely because it shows how conventions encode assumptions. Current Founder canon—robots as a separate conscious civilization—outranks legacy robot tropes.

### Consciousness / dimensional schools
Historical traditions of reincarnation/body transfer in SF are evidence of genre genealogy only. They are **not** evidence that current orbital civilization possesses human consciousness-transfer technology. Current canon firewall remains binding.

### Mystery / SMITH
Use conceptual breakthrough only when each fact changes the operational model. Bounded case resolution can coexist with unresolved ontology.
