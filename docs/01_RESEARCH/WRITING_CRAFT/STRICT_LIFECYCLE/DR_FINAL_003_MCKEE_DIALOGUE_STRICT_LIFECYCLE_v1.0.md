## 3. CORE CLAIMS — ACCEPT / MODIFY / REJECT

### ACCEPT
1. **Dialogue is action.** A speaker uses words to produce an effect, not merely to transfer information.
2. **Exact wording matters selectively.** Direct quotation earns space when wording changes power, evidence, consent, relationship, decision, comedy, or emotional state.
3. **Subtext emerges from motive + situation + tactic.** It is not decoration added after dialogue.
4. **Character-specific dialogue grows from character-specific knowledge, attitudes, relationships, culture and strategy.**
5. **Repeated beats are not progression.** Rephrasing the same tactic in new words is still repetition.
6. **Dialogue flaws are often story/scene flaws.** Repair desire, opposition, beat progression and scene function before polishing wording.
7. **Silence, physical action and paralanguage can perform verbal work.**
8. **Indirect/narratized dialogue is legitimate prose craft.** It can compress routine/process and foreground interpretation.
9. **Conversation may be asymmetric, indirect, reflexive or minimal and still be dramatically active.** Conflict need not equal shouting.
10. **Minimal language can carry maximal meaning when context/subtext is strong.**

### MODIFY
1. McKee often frames every scene around a positive/negative value turn. IVDIVO uses the broader `CHANGED STATE` test: action, knowledge, relationship, evidence status, consent, access, obligation, emotional state or plan may change without a binary value flip.
2. His claim that escalating conflict should progressively demand greater risk is useful as a trend, not a law. Quiet scenes can carry decisive relational or epistemic cost.
3. His preference for short, direct, active speech is medium- and character-dependent. Longer reflective syntax may be exactly right in prose.
4. `Said / unsaid / unsayable` is a powerful diagnostic but does not require a hidden trauma or unconscious desire for every character.
5. His line-cue advice (core word near the end) is valuable for spoken performance, but novel sentences require rhythmic variety, not permanent periodic construction.

### REJECT AS UNIVERSAL LAW
1. Every important line must be suspense-shaped.
2. Every scene must contain overt conflict.
3. Every scene must turn through a binary value charge.
4. Emotion must generally shorten sentences; intellect must generally lengthen them.
5. Comic characters must be psychologically shallower as a production requirement.
6. Long speeches are inherently inferior.
7. Screen/stage cueing rules automatically govern prose.

## 4. TRANSFERABLE MECHANISMS

### DIA-M01 — VERBAL ACTION CHAIN
`WANT → LISTENER/OBSTACLE MODEL → TACTIC → WORDING/BEHAVIOR → REACTION → TACTIC CHANGE → STATE CHANGE`.

### DIA-M02 — EXACT-WORDS ROUTER
Use **DIRECT** when exact wording changes the story. Use **INDIRECT/SUMMARY** for repetition, procedure, logistics, already-known exchanges, or when interpretation matters more than wording.

### DIA-M03 — SUBTEXT TRIANGLE
`WHAT I WANT + WHAT I CANNOT/WILL NOT SAY + WHAT I DO SAY/DO`.
Subtext is inferable tension between these layers.

### DIA-M04 — BEAT PROGRESSION
A beat is action/reaction. If characters repeat the same tactic, the scene is not progressing even if the wording changes.

### DIA-M05 — TACTIC ROTATION
When the first approach fails or partly works, a living scene changes method: persuade → joke → challenge → retreat → disclose → bargain, etc. Do not rotate tactics mechanically; let reaction cause the change.

### DIA-M06 — THIRD-THING / TRIALOGUE
Characters can act on each other through a third subject, object, task, institution, place or cultural reference. This is especially useful for romance, distrust and ideological conflict without on-the-nose declaration.

### DIA-M07 — CHARACTER-SPECIFIC LEXICON
`KNOWLEDGE/ATTENTION → nouns/verbs` and `ATTITUDE/RELATION/POWER → modifiers/modalities/syntax`.
Use as diagnosis, not formula.

### DIA-M08 — PAUSE / SILENCE ECONOMY
A pause before a consequential turn can increase anticipation; a pause after can create absorption. Repetition destroys its power. Silence must change interpretation, pressure, or relationship.

### DIA-M09 — MINIMAL-CONFLICT INTIMACY
Quiet scenes can progress through confession, withholding, mirroring, jokes, pauses, indirect offers and acceptance/rejection. Loud conflict is not required.

### DIA-M10 — ASYMMETRIC CONFLICT
One character may pursue while the other evades, delays, redirects or quietly resists. This is highly useful for parent/teen, mentor/cadet, institution/youth and privacy scenes.

### DIA-M11 — REFLEXIVE DIALOGUE
Inner speech may stage self-vs-self conflict, but it must affect choice/model/behavior; otherwise it becomes repetitive rumination.

### DIA-M12 — EXPOSITION AS TACTICAL MATERIAL
Facts enter scenes because someone uses, contests, hides, misreads, requests or weaponizes them now.

### DIA-M13 — PARAPHRASING TRAP
If dialogue feels false, do not begin by endlessly rephrasing lines. First check scene purpose, desire, opposition, relationship, evidence and tactic.

### DIA-M14 — LINE MEANING TIMING
Place the key idea where it produces the intended reader effect. In prose, vary suspense/cumulative/balanced structures; do not use one design as a house style.

### DIA-M15 — DIMINISHING RETURNS
Repeated emotional tone, joke structure, tactic, pause, image or syntax loses force. Preserve contrast.

## 5. FAILURE MODES
1. **Position-panel dialogue** — five characters take turns stating viewpoints.
2. **Beat repetition** — same action/reaction repeated with synonyms.
3. **On-the-nose aftermath** — action already showed the relationship change; narrator/dialogue explains it again.
4. **Paraphrasing repair** — polishing words while scene motive/design is broken.
5. **Universal wit** — every character has the same authorial cleverness.
6. **Slang-as-voice** — differentiation reduced to dialect, profanity or catchphrases.
7. **Exposition neutrality** — characters neutrally explain facts they all know.
8. **False mystery by withholding** — a POV knows something but author conceals it only to manipulate suspense.
9. **Silence inflation** — every important exchange receives a pause/ellipsis.
10. **Short-speech monoculture** — all dialogue chopped into minimalist fragments.
11. **Third-thing opacity** — metaphor/subject becomes so indirect the reader cannot infer the actual relation.
12. **Subconscious overfitting** — inventing hidden wounds/desires because a model expects them.
13. **Scene-turn dogma** — forcing every quiet scene into a dramatic reversal.
14. **Performance-rule contamination** — screen actor cue rules applied mechanically to novel prose.
15. **Dialogue solves structure** — trying to rescue episodic chapters with sharper banter.

## 6. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

### McKee *Story*
Strong convergence: action/reaction, change, opposition, scene design. *Dialogue* is more useful at beat/tactic/subtext level. IVDIVO keeps the broader changed-state model from Rosenfeld/Truby rather than strict binary value turns.

### Rosenfeld — *Make a Scene*
Convergence: intention, complication, result, next intention. Rosenfeld is more permissive about contemplative/recovery scenes; this corrects McKee's tendency toward constant conflict/turn pressure.

### Card — *Characters & Viewpoint*
Card strengthens relationship-specific self and POV limits. McKee strengthens outward verbal action. Combine as: `WHO THIS CHARACTER IS WITH THIS PERSON → WHAT THEY WANT NOW → WHAT THEY CAN SAY → HOW THEY SAY IT`.

### Truby
Convergence: desire/action/opposition and scene weave. Truby better protects whole-book causal architecture; McKee is better for live verbal tactics.

### Bell — *Conflict & Suspense*
Bell reinforces competing will and local uncertainty; McKee provides more precise dialogue beat analysis. Neither author justifies permanent high-intensity dialogue.

### Zunshine / source-monitoring layer
McKee's revelation/exposition mechanics must remain subordinate to source tracking: a character's assertion is not a fact simply because it is spoken persuasively.

## 7. IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION — PRODUCTION GATES

**DIA-G10 DIALOGUE-PURPOSE GATE** — before line edit, state each major speaker's immediate want and relation to listener.

**DIA-G11 TACTIC-CHANGE GATE** — repeated exchanges must change tactic, evidence, leverage, emotion or relation; synonyms do not count.

**DIA-G12 EXACT-WORDS GATE** — dramatize exact speech only when exact wording matters.

**DIA-G13 PANEL-TO-SCENE GATE** — if five people are presenting positions, assign unequal goals, standing, interruption rights, evidence control, fear, time pressure or relationships.

**DIA-G14 SUBTEXT-DO-NOT-EXPLAIN** — if reader can infer inner action from context/behavior, do not append interpretation.

**DIA-G15 QUIET-SCENE VALIDITY** — quiet dialogue is valid if trust, intimacy, knowledge, consent, obligation or plan changes.

**DIA-G16 CHARACTER REGISTER MATRIX** — same character should sound differently with friend / parent / instructor / romantic interest / public authority / conscious robot while remaining recognizably the same person.

**DIA-G17 ROBOT PERSONHOOD SPEECH** — named conscious robots must express their own goals, norms, relationships and right to refuse. No generic assistant register.

**DIA-G18 POST-MONEY FIREWALL** — orbital dialogue must not reintroduce rent/wage/credits/purchasing status as ordinary motivation.

**DIA-G19 SPECIALIST-SCHOOL EVIDENCE REGISTER** — practitioners distinguish observation, practice result, school interpretation, lineage claim and uncertainty; no single world-bible voice.

**DIA-G20 LINE-DESIGN VARIETY** — suspense, cumulative, balanced, fragments and long reflective sentences are selected by effect, not percentage/KPI.

**DIA-G21 SILENCE EARNS SPACE** — pause/silence must perform an action or change interpretation; decorative ellipses fail.

**DIA-G22 STRUCTURE-BEFORE-PARAPHRASE** — scene defect → repair scene first; line defect → line edit.

**DIA-G23 THIRD-THING RELATIONSHIP TEST** — indirect subject/metaphor earns space only if it advances relationship/action and remains inferable.

**DIA-G24 INTERNAL-DIALOGUE ACTION** — interior debate must alter model, choice or behavior; repeated self-commentary is compressed.

**DIA-G25 SOURCE-STATUS SPEECH** — spoken theory remains attributed to the speaker until independently verified.

## 9. SYNTHESIS
Best final IVDIVO formula:

`CHARACTER-SPECIFIC WANT → RELATIONSHIP/CONSTRAINT → VERBAL OR NONVERBAL TACTIC → REACTION → TACTIC/MODEL CHANGE → CHANGED STATE`.

Dialogue is not successful because it is witty, naturalistic, short or full of subtext. It succeeds when character-specific expression performs causal work while leaving the reader enough inferential space to participate.
