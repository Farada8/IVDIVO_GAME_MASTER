## 3. PROVISIONAL CLAIM EXTRACTION — READ MATERIAL ONLY

### L19-C01 — Generative mode and evaluative mode are different cognitive jobs
Early generation benefits from suspension of premature censorship; craft/revision becomes more useful once material exists.

### L19-C02 — Strong creative writing combines surprise with conviction
Freshness without plausibility becomes arbitrary; plausibility without surprise becomes received language or cliché.

### L19-C03 — Strong prose resists paraphrase
If a summary preserves nearly all of a passage's effect, the writing may be under-rendered. Details, voice, rhythm, perspective and implication should add irreducible value.

### L19-C04 — Sentimentality is an unearned stock response, not merely sweetness
Cynicism, grimness, uplift, tragedy, heroism and victimhood can all become sentimental when the response is prepackaged rather than earned by specific context.

### L19-C05 — Attention is a source of voice
What a narrator/POV character notices, ignores, compares and remembers differentiates voice more reliably than ornamental diction or slang.

### L19-C06 — Write into what remains unknown inside familiar material
A known topic can remain artistically alive if the writer locates what they genuinely do not understand about it rather than transmitting a settled lesson.

### L19-C07 — Particular before generalization
Starting from a thesis and choosing details to prove it creates propaganda/travelogue effects. Starting from specific particulars allows a more complex general pattern to emerge.

### L19-C08 — Detail needs hierarchy
Concrete specificity is not a license for catalogs. Select details that render world, POV, relation or implication; excess specificity can crowd the reader out.

### L19-C09 — Metaphor must earn its cognitive cost
First establish what the thing is. Comparison is valuable when it clarifies/complicates perception; strained cleverness and metaphor pile-up slow the prose and advertise the writer.

### L19-C10 — Symbols are strongest when accumulated, not pasted on
A recurring image/object may gather symbolic force through story context. Conscious symbolic insertion cannot substitute for character, causality and scene work.

### L19-C11 — Form is discovered and diagnosed, not imposed automatically
Conflict-crisis-resolution, epiphany, change and other conventional models can diagnose some stories after generation; they are not universal creation laws.

### L19-C12 — Character change is optional; consequential opportunity is not
A character may change, fail to change, misunderstand or decline an opportunity. What matters is that a significant state/opportunity has passed and the story earns its closure.

### L19-C13 — Show and tell are complementary tools
Scene gives eyewitness immediacy; narration gives compression, thought, history, interpretation, time control and voice. `Show, don't tell` is not a universal production rule.

### L19-C14 — The right unit is functional proximity, not maximum dramatization
Important exact words/actions may deserve scene. Routine/process/background can be summarized. Sometimes narration is more elegant and powerful than dramatization.

### L19-C15 — POV has independent axes of knowledge access and narrative distance
First/third person does not determine intimacy or information by itself. A narrator may be limited but distant, or omniscient but close.

### L19-C16 — POV grammar is not a universal repair
Changing `I` to `he/she` or vice versa does not solve a defect caused by wrong access, wrong distance, unstable attitude or illegal knowledge.

### L19-C17 — Reliability is calibrated, not binary
A narrator can be accurate about observable action and unreliable about interpretation, memory, self-knowledge or causation. Readers need evidence with which to calibrate the gap.

### L19-C18 — Distorted POV needs a recoverable reality channel
When focal perception is unreliable, the story must still supply observable traces, independent sources or consequences from which a reader can reconstruct a more stable model.

### L19-C19 — Speculative strangeness is not automatically POV distortion
An apparent impossibility may be an established world rule, a focal-character error, an information gap, or an author/canon error. The text must distinguish these possibilities through evidence.

### L19-C20 — Dialogue is edited action, not transcription
Good dialogue selects wording, rhythm, silence and gesture that reveal intention, personality or relationship and move the piece; raw realism is not the target.

### L19-C21 — Voice is relationship-specific
The same person speaks, thinks and behaves differently with a friend, parent, teacher, lover, authority, stranger or conscious robot because the relationship changes desire, risk and power.

### L19-C22 — Silence, gesture and environment belong to dialogue
Nonverbal response and surrounding physical action may carry as much relational information as spoken lines.

### L19-C23 — Placeholder dialogue can be generatively useful
A first pass may map information and agenda plainly, then revision can convert it into character-specific wording, indirection, subtext, rhythm and gesture.

### L19-C24 — Plot causality may be soft rather than mechanical
A scene can matter by increasing emotional, relational, social or epistemic pressure even when it does not directly trigger the next external event.

### L19-C25 — Render how before reducing behavior to why
Specific behavior unfolding under specific circumstances usually produces richer motivation than a single explanatory backstory or diagnostic label.

### L19-C26 — Overt antagonist/conflict is not mandatory
Desire, situation, pressure, relationship, opportunity, misperception and emerging consequences can generate plot without a conventional villain.

### L19-C27 — Plot order is information design
Chronological sequence and narrative sequence are not identical; reorder only when the information timing changes reader understanding or effect.

### L19-C28 — Genre-obligatory scenes carry cliché risk
A scene that feels mandatory because “this kind of story always has one” should be challenged with alternative embodiments specific to this world and cast.

### L19-C29 — Roundness is surprising-yet-convincing behavior, not biography quantity
A character gains dimensionality when new behavior revises our model while remaining retrospectively believable. Supporting characters need only the depth required by the story.

### L19-C30 — Character is revealed through relation-specific behavior
What a person notices, says, withholds, wants and tolerates changes in different relationships; these differences are stronger character evidence than trait labels.

### L19-C31 — Internal events can be plot events
Memory, imagination, fantasy, interpretation and projection may alter later behavior and therefore belong to causality when they change a choice or strategy.

### L19-C32 — Openings promise an experience, not a mandatory action beat
A useful opening establishes a reason to continue, tone/world/character/situation and a contract of attention. It may begin with action, narration, imbalance, partial knowledge or another suitable mode.

## 4. PROVISIONAL MECHANISMS

### M-L19-01 — GENERATE / DIAGNOSE SPLIT
`GENERATE WITHOUT PREMATURE POLICING → IDENTIFY LIVE MATERIAL → DIAGNOSE STRUCTURE → REVISE → LINE EDIT`.

### M-L19-02 — SURPRISING-YET-CONVINCING TEST
`FRESH? → CHARACTER/WORLD-TRUE? → CAUSALLY PREPARED? → READER CAN RECONSTRUCT WHY?`

### M-L19-03 — UNKNOWN-INSIDE-KNOWN
`KNOWN MATERIAL → WHAT IS STILL GENUINELY UNCERTAIN? → OBSERVABLE PARTICULARS → ALTERNATIVE MODELS → TEST/CHOICE`.

### M-L19-04 — PARTICULAR-TO-GENERAL
`SPECIFIC PERSON / OBJECT / ACTION / PLACE → PATTERN → POSSIBLE MEANING` rather than `THEME → ILLUSTRATIVE DETAIL`.

### M-L19-05 — DETAIL DOUBLE-PAY
A selected detail should ideally do at least two jobs among world, POV, character, relation, tension, causality, foreshadowing or rhythm.

### M-L19-06 — SHOW/TELL ROUTER
Use DIRECT SCENE when exact action/wording changes state. Use NARRATION/SUMMARY when compression, thought, context or time movement is the stronger reader experience.

### M-L19-07 — POV ACCESS MATRIX
`WHAT CAN POV OBSERVE? → WHAT CAN POV KNOW? → WHAT DOES POV INFER? → WHAT DOES TEXT INDEPENDENTLY CONFIRM? → WHAT DISTANCE IS USED?`

### M-L19-08 — RELIABILITY CALIBRATION
`POV CLAIM → OBSERVABLE TRACE → INDEPENDENT SOURCE / CONSEQUENCE → READER MODEL UPDATE`.

### M-L19-09 — ONTOLOGY VS POV TEST
`STRANGE EVENT → ESTABLISHED WORLD RULE? / POV DISTORTION? / INFORMATION GAP? / AUTHOR-CANON ERROR? → EVIDENCE`.

### M-L19-10 — DIALOGUE ACTION ROUTER
`WANT → VERBAL/NONVERBAL TACTIC → RESPONSE → TACTIC CHANGE → RELATIONSHIP/DECISION/INFORMATION STATE CHANGE`.

### M-L19-11 — PLACEHOLDER → LIVING DIALOGUE
`NECESSARY INFORMATION / AGENDA → PLAIN FIRST PASS → CHARACTER FILTER → INDIRECTION / RHYTHM / GESTURE / SUBTEXT / ENVIRONMENT → TRIM`.

### M-L19-12 — SOFT CAUSALITY
`EVENT → EMOTIONAL / RELATIONAL / EPISTEMIC PRESSURE → COPING OR TACTIC → SUCCESS/FAILURE → NEXT CHOICE`.

### M-L19-13 — RENDER-HOW
`ACTION PATTERN → SPECIFIC CONTEXT → CONSEQUENCE → READER INFERS LAYERED WHY` rather than `BACKSTORY LABEL → BEHAVIOR EXPLAINED`.

### M-L19-14 — SCENES-A-FAIRE TEST
When a scene feels required by genre, generate multiple alternative embodiments and choose the version most specific to the characters, world and causal engine.

### M-L19-15 — RELATIONAL CHARACTER MATRIX
For each major character, compare behavior with at least three relationship contexts: `WHAT DO THEY WANT HERE? → WHAT DO THEY RISK? → WHAT TACTIC/VOICE CHANGES? → WHAT REMAINS CHARACTER-CONTINUOUS?`

### M-L19-16 — OPENING CONTRACT
`READER QUESTION / ATTENTION → TONE → PHYSICAL/SOCIAL WORLD → CHARACTER/SITUATION → PROMISE OF STORY MODE`, without requiring action-first or a fixed percentage.

## 5. PROVISIONAL FAILURE MODES
1. Premature Editor — revision intelligence kills generative discovery before material exists.
2. Writerly Voice Costume — ornate diction substitutes for perception.
3. Sentiment Button — stock emotional event is expected to generate feeling automatically.
4. Grim Sentimentality — darkness/cynicism treated as inherently profound.
5. Thesis-First Detail Selection — world evidence is cherry-picked to prove a predetermined message.
6. Sensory Checklist — one detail for every sense without hierarchy.
7. Detail Crowding — specificity excludes reader participation and forward motion.
8. Metaphor Pile-Up — comparison replaces clear rendering.
9. Symbol Retrofit — author pastes symbolic meaning onto structurally weak material.
10. Epiphany Factory — every character receives a packaged realization.
11. Mandatory Change Arc — refusal/failure to change is treated as invalid fiction.
12. Theory-before-Material — beat/arc formulas determine what must happen before the story earns it.
13. Show-Don't-Tell Dogma — narration is deleted regardless of function.
14. Scene Inflation — routine/logistics are dramatized because scene is assumed superior.
15. Knowledge/Distance Confusion — changing person when the actual POV defect is access or distance.
16. POV Telepathy Leak — focal character suddenly knows inaccessible information.
17. Ontology-by-Unreliable-Narrator Excuse — canon contradiction is waved away as POV distortion without evidence.
18. Reliability Fog — everything may be false and the reader has no stable evidence channel.
19. Universal Voice — person sounds identical with parent, friend, instructor, lover, stranger and robot.
20. Dialogue Fact Conveyor — direct speech carries facts that stronger narration could compress.
21. Talking Heads — dialogue floats without gesture, silence, physical world or consequence.
22. Mandatory Subtext — every line is forced to secretly mean something else.
23. Phonetic Accent Costume — character voice is reduced to misspelling/accent gimmick.
24. Pop-Psych Causality — one trauma/backstory explanation falsely solves complex behavior.
25. Conflict Inflation — writer adds fights, villains or crises because a quiet story feels insufficiently dramatic.
26. Mechanical Consequence Chain — meaningful emotional/relationship causality is deleted because every scene must directly trigger the next external event.
27. Scene-à-Faire Compliance — familiar genre scene is included because convention says it belongs.
28. Biography Inflation — more backstory is mistaken for more character depth.
29. Opening Action Monoculture — action-first is treated as inherently superior to narration or partial knowledge.
30. Opening Contract Mismatch — opening promises a tone/genre/story mode that the book promptly abandons.

## 6. PROVISIONAL CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

### With Truby / McKee / Brody
LaPlante is a necessary anti-formula counterweight. Truby/McKee sharpen causal diagnosis; Brody identifies YA developmental pressure. LaPlante prevents causal tools from becoming compulsory beat templates and protects quieter/internal forms of causality.

### With Rosenfeld
Strong convergence on scene/narration routing and revision-stage discipline. Rosenfeld is stronger on scene engineering; LaPlante is stronger on discovery, ambiguity, POV calibration and resisting false closure.

### With Card / McKee Character
Convergence: character is specific behavior/perception, not label. LaPlante strengthens this with relationship-specific behavior and surprising-yet-convincing roundness while explicitly allowing minor characters to remain functionally light.

### With McKee Dialogue
Convergence on dialogue as action, relationship-specific tactics and direct/indirect routing. LaPlante more strongly protects narration and explicitly treats placeholder dialogue as a legitimate generative stage before voice/subtext refinement.

### With Bell Conflict & Suspense
Bell is stronger on pressure and local uncertainty; LaPlante provides a useful correction against mandatory overt conflict and supports soft emotional/epistemic causality.

## 7. PROVISIONAL IVDIVO PRODUCTION GATES

**L19-G01 Generate Before Policing** — do not run every production gate while generating a first living version of a scene.
**L19-G02 No Sentiment Buttons** — death, injury, child danger, robot innocence, parental rejection, first love and sacrifice require specific causal context before emotional payoff.
**L19-G03 Grimness Is Not Depth** — dystopian or tragic tone requires the same evidentiary specificity as optimism.
**L19-G04 Character Attention Fingerprint** — each major POV notices a different subset of the same environment for character-specific reasons.
**L19-G05 Unknown-Inside-Known** — mystery/metaphysical material retains genuine questions and testable distinctions rather than author-certified answers.
**L19-G06 Particular Before World Thesis** — orbital society is revealed through lived choices, not paragraphs selected to prove it is post-money, advanced or ethical.
**L19-G07 Detail Function** — unusual technology/object receives textual attention in proportion to its story/POV function.
**L19-G08 No Metaphor Repair** — clarify perception/action before decorating it.
**L19-G09 No Symbol Repair** — symbolism cannot rescue weak causal architecture.
**L19-G10 Change Is Not Mandatory** — arc may be change, refusal, partial change, mislearning or confirmation, provided consequences are real.
**L19-G11 Theory Is Diagnostic** — craft school maps the story; the story does not exist to validate the school.
**L19-G12 Show/Tell by Reader Effect** — choose scene/narration by what the reader must experience.
**L19-G13 No Logistics Scene by Default** — transport, setup, repair process and repeated procedure are compressed unless exact action changes state.
**L19-G14 Discovery Does Not Override Canon** — exploratory drafting does not outrank Founder directives and approved canon.
**L19-G15 Knowledge/Distance Separation** — diagnose POV person, knowledge access and distance separately.
**L19-G16 POV Access Legality** — every significant fact in close POV must have a lawful route into that consciousness.
**L19-G17 Independent Reality Channel** — unreliable focalization requires observable evidence or consequences that let the reader calibrate reality.
**L19-G18 Speculative Ontology Contract** — distinguish world fact, character belief, school interpretation and unresolved anomaly.
**L19-G19 Relationship-Specific Dialogue** — character voice/tactic changes with relationship while preserving core identity.
**L19-G20 Dialogue Must Change State or Be Compressed** — exact wording earns direct scene by changing decision, evidence, consent, trust, power, relation or emotion.
**L19-G21 Gesture/Silence/World Inside Dialogue** — important conversations remain embodied and situated.
**L19-G22 Placeholder Dialogue Allowed** — generation may use serviceable dialogue; revision must convert it into character-specific action.
**L19-G23 No Mandatory Subtext** — use subtext where agenda/relationship produces it, not as decoration on every line.
**L19-G24 Soft Causality Accepted** — emotional, relational, epistemic and status consequences count as causal movement.
**L19-G25 Render How Before Why** — show the pattern of behavior before reducing it to a single explanatory cause.
**L19-G26 No Mandatory Antagonist** — opposition may be person, institution, environment, relationship, uncertainty, self-model or incompatible legitimate goals.
**L19-G27 Plot Order vs Chronology** — reorder time only when the information sequence changes reader effect or character action.
**L19-G28 Scenes-à-Faire Alternative Test** — generate alternatives before accepting a genre-obligatory scene.
**L19-G29 Round Character Evidence** — surprise must revise the reader model while remaining retrospectively convincing.
**L19-G30 Character Hierarchy** — supporting/walk-on characters do not require equal biography or POV weight.
**L19-G31 Relationship Matrix** — major characters must exhibit different tactics/voice under different relational conditions.
**L19-G32 Opening Contract Before Formula** — opening must create continued attention and establish its story mode; no action-first requirement.

## 8. IVDIVO APPLICATION — CURRENT

### LESSON ZERO BOOK ONE
No concrete current defect found that justifies reopening the development lock. LaPlante strengthens the existing rule: new theory is not manuscript evidence.

### ORBITAL YOUTH / POST-MONEY CIVILIZATION
Reveal post-money life through particulars: trust, limited time, unique assignments, relationships, reputation, consent, responsibility and access to nonfungible experiences. Do not replace money with disguised credits. POV access should keep the city large: a teenager knows their routes, institutions and relationships, not the total ontology/economy by authorial telepathy.

### CONSCIOUS ROBOTS
Robot personhood is expressed through independent wants, refusal, relationship-specific speech/tactics, silence, interpretation and consequences. Do not create a universal “robot voice” or use a conscious robot as a neutral exposition terminal.

### SPECIALIST CONSCIOUSNESS / YOGA / METAPHYSICAL SCHOOLS
Maintain an epistemic ladder:
`PRACTITIONER OBSERVATION → REPRODUCIBLE PRACTICE RESULT → SCHOOL INTERPRETATION → COMPETING INTERPRETATION → ONTOLOGICAL CLAIM`.
A focal character's belief or a school's doctrine is not automatically civilization-wide fact.

### MYSTERY / SOURCE MONITORING
Use:
`POV BELIEF ≠ OBSERVED TRACE ≠ INDEPENDENT CONFIRMATION ≠ WORLD FACT`.
A reveal becomes plot when it changes a model, decision or strategy; unreliable POV cannot be used to excuse missing fair-play evidence.

### PROSE / “ОБРУБКИ”
Before changing sentence length, diagnose:
`SCENE NECESSITY → SHOW/TELL ROUTING → POV DISTANCE → DIALOGUE DIRECT/INDIRECT ROUTING → PARAGRAPH INTENT → SENTENCE RHYTHM`.
Choppiness may come from frame-by-frame dramatization, talking-head dialogue or POV-distance whiplash rather than short syntax itself.

### ONE MAIN STORY
LaPlante's soft causality does not weaken ONE MAIN STORY. A scene may advance the common causal engine through action, emotional pressure, relationship change, information/model change or decision readiness; what is forbidden is a scene whose state can be removed without changing anything downstream.
