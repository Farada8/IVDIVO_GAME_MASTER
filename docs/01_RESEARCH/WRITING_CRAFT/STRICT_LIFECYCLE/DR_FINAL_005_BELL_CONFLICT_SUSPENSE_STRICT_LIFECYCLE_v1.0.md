# 4. CLAIM EXTRACTION

## C1 — Conflict requires meaningful incompatibility
At least one side has conscious will; the opposing force may be another person, group, institution, environment or internalized demand.

## C2 — Reader concern precedes effective suspense
A microobstacle or threat matters only after the reader understands why the outcome matters to a character.

## C3 — Strong opposition can be legitimate
The most useful opponent need not be evil. A competent, ethical person with incompatible obligations can create stronger drama than a weak villain.

## C4 — Opposition needs its own model of rightness
The opposing person/system should possess reasons, resources, tactics and blind spots rather than exist to delay the hero.

## C5 — A bonding agent/crucible explains why parties cannot simply leave
Duty, location, relationship, shared project, governance, safety or a unique time window can hold incompatible agendas together.

## C6 — Cast orchestration creates renewable conflict
Meaningful differences in values, methods, status and history create more durable scene pressure than random quarrels.

## C7 — Scene suspense grows from an unresolved local objective
A character wants an outcome, meets resistance/uncertainty, and the answer is delayed until a changed state.

## C8 — Reaction is part of causality, not dead air
Emotion → interpretation/analysis → decision can control pace and convert consequences into new action.

## C9 — Allies still need independent agendas
Friends/teammates become flat if they only exchange information and agree.

## C10 — Subplots earn space by invading the main story
A subplot should change decisions, emotion, resources, relationships or the A-story trajectory rather than run as an isolated second novel.

## C11 — Inner conflict requires two genuinely weighted alternatives
A trivial counterargument does not create meaningful inner conflict.

## C12 — Competing emotions can reveal character more powerfully than one named emotion
Love/anger, fear/curiosity, loyalty/resentment, relief/shame can coexist and drive behavior.

## C13 — Dialogue is action
What each speaker wants, avoids, reframes or withholds matters more than informational completeness.

## C14 — Theme is stronger as distributed argument than author lecture
Different characters should embody defensible positions and pay consequences for them.

## C15 — Suspense is delayed resolution under emotional investment
Questions can operate at book, scene, paragraph, dialogue and relationship scales.

## C16 — Mystery and suspense are distinct but combinable
Mystery privileges interpretation of evidence; suspense privileges uncertain future consequence. Strong stories may braid both.

## C17 — Tension can be stretched by slowing fictional time
Interior thought, sensory detail, dialogue, description and micro-actions can lengthen an intense moment when each added beat increases emotional processing or uncertainty.

## C18 — Short syntax is one pacing tool, not the definition of action
Bell's own examples repeatedly slow intense action with long interior/sensory passages; therefore action can be syntactically expansive when reader engagement remains high.

## C19 — Setting can oppose without becoming a villain
Physical geometry, social norms, access rules, visibility, crowding, weather or environmental constraints can change tactics and risk.

## C20 — A microobstacle works only when its consequence can matter
Random annoyance without causal leverage becomes filler.

## C21 — A ticking clock is a compression mechanism, not a universal requirement
Time limits work when grounded in a real deadline rather than added merely to simulate urgency.

## C22 — Twists require retrospective justification
Surprise is cheap; a strong twist changes the model while remaining compatible with earlier evidence.

## C23 — Remove capacity to force strategy change
Loss of access, ally, route, evidence, permission, tool or social standing can create suspense without physical violence.

## C24 — Revision should diagnose where reader pressure leaks
Flat dialogue, pure exposition, static ally scenes, redundant reaction, weak scene openings/endings and disconnected subplots are high-value targets.

# 5. MECHANISM EXTRACTION

## M-B01 — LEGITIMATE OPPOSITION
`HERO GOAL -> OPPONENT GOAL -> BOTH REASONABLE -> INCOMPATIBLE OUTCOMES -> TACTIC / COUNTERTACTIC -> COST`

Use especially for institutions, engineers, parents, instructors, conscious robots and governance.

## M-B02 — BONDING AGENT
Ask: `WHY CAN'T EITHER SIDE SIMPLY WALK AWAY?`

Valid answers for IVDIVO:
- responsibility for the same habitat/project/person;
- safety duty;
- consent/relationship obligation;
- shared mission;
- governance jurisdiction;
- one-time astronomical/operational window;
- family or friendship;
- common dependence on the same system.

Do not use wages/rent/debt as default orbital bonding agents.

## M-B03 — SCENE PRESSURE LOOP
`LOCAL WANT -> RESISTANCE / UNCERTAINTY / SUPPORT WITH COST -> RESULT -> EMOTION -> INTERPRETATION -> DECISION -> NEXT ACTION`

This replaces Bell's over-narrow success/failure emphasis.

## M-B04 — ALLY FRICTION
`SHARED HIGHER GOAL + DIFFERENT METHOD / VALUE / RISK TOLERANCE = RENEWABLE CONFLICT`

The argument must change action, not exist merely to avoid a quiet conversation.

## M-B05 — SUSPENSE QUESTION HIERARCHY
Track:
- MACRO: what unresolved book-level outcome matters?
- SEQUENCE: what medium-term objective remains uncertain?
- SCENE: what answer/change is pending now?
- PARAGRAPH/BEAT: what tiny piece of information/action is delayed?

Do not let low-level suspense contradict or distract from the A-story.

## M-B06 — CONTROLLED DELAY
`QUESTION -> RELEVANT INTERVENING BEAT -> HEIGHTENED MODEL / FEAR -> ANSWER OR NEW QUESTION`

Every delaying beat must add information, emotion, constraint, relationship change or uncertainty.

## M-B07 — MICROOBSTACLE LEVERAGE
`SMALL OBSTACLE -> LARGE POSSIBLE CONSEQUENCE`

Examples: access lock, delayed reply, misunderstood route, unavailable expert, privacy boundary, incompatible environmental setting, robot refusal, social interruption.

## M-B08 — CAPACITY REMOVAL
Temporarily remove one capability the hero relies on:
- access;
- authority;
- data;
- ally;
- communication;
- route;
- trusted model;
- time;
- privacy;
- social permission.

Then require a new strategy rather than merely waiting for restoration.

## M-B09 — SUBPLOT INVASION
For every subplot, specify at least one arrow into A-story:
- EMOTIONAL: changes judgment, trust, courage or restraint;
- OPERATIONAL: changes access, evidence, time, alliance or choice.

No arrow = candidate for compression/removal.

## M-B10 — DIALOGUE TACTICS
For each speaker record:
`WANT / FEAR / WHAT THEY KNOW / WHAT THEY WON'T SAY / TACTIC / LEVERAGE`.

Possible tactics: direct ask, sidestep, silence, question-back, reframing, humor, procedural language, threat, concession, precise correction, refusal.

## M-B11 — THEME AS COMPETING OPERATING MODELS
Instead of speeches:
`POSITION A -> ACTION -> CONSEQUENCE`
versus
`POSITION B -> ACTION -> CONSEQUENCE`.

Reader experiences argument through outcomes.

## M-B12 — TENSION-STRETCH TEST
At a high-intensity beat, test expansion through:
- sensory selection;
- interior contradiction;
- micro-action;
- delayed reply;
- environmental detail;
- relationship cue.

Keep only beats that increase reader processing/uncertainty. This is explicitly **not** a command to shorten all action prose.

## M-B13 — TWIST JUSTIFICATION LEDGER
For each major twist:
`SURPRISE -> EARLIER SUPPORT -> EARLIER MISINTERPRETATION -> COUNTEREVIDENCE -> REVEAL -> NEW ACTION`.

If justification requires a post-hoc lecture, redesign.

## M-B14 — POSITIVE EXPERIENCE WITH TENSION
Pleasure, friendship, romance, sport, celebration and competence may remain positive. Tension can come from anticipation, incompatible wants, embarrassment, uncertainty or future consequence without converting every joyful scene into danger.

# 6. FAILURE MODES IF BELL IS APPLIED LITERALLY

## F1 — `Death must be on the line` as universal law
Bell expands death to physical/professional/psychological, which makes the category nearly unfalsifiable. For IVDIVO YA this invites artificial inflation.

Replacement:
`MEANINGFUL IRREVERSIBLE / COSTLY LOSS` may include trust, relationship, role, autonomy, mission access, public responsibility, unique experience, identity commitment, safety or future option.

No need to call all meaningful loss `death`.

## F2 — Every scene needs overt conflict
If interpreted narrowly, this produces constant arguing and destroys tenderness, awe, humor and recovery.

Replacement:
Every scene needs **meaningful pressure / desire / uncertainty / changed state**. Conflict may be subtle, internal, relational or absent for stretches if the scene performs another causal function and changes what follows.

## F3 — Most scenes should end in setback
Overuse creates mechanical misery and makes success unbelievable.

Replacement:
Outcomes may be success, partial success, costly success, refusal, discovery, restraint, support or setback—provided they change the next intention.

## F4 — Happy scenes are inherently weak
A post-money orbital city specifically needs desirable ordinary life. Pure positivity can be structurally useful when it builds attachment, relationship, contrast or a future choice.

Do not sabotage every good experience for fear of low tension.

## F5 — `Guy with a gun` / random disruption
Useful brainstorming device, dangerous as causal design. Random intrusions can create second-story contamination and coincidence-driven plotting.

Gate: disruption must connect backward or acquire a credible causal function that changes the A-story.

## F6 — Numeric doorway / subplot / scene-placement rules
Treat one-fifth, subplot-count formulas and similar numbers as heuristics, not gates.

## F7 — One-scene-one-POV as universal law
Strong default for close limited narration, not a universal prohibition on omniscient or formally controlled multi-perspective prose.

## F8 — Anti-second-person / anti-omniscient claims
Market/style preference, not timeless craft fact.

## F9 — Semicolon/adverb/adjective prohibitions
Revision prompts, not grammar law. Voice, clarity, rhythm and reader effect decide.

## F10 — Short sentences equal suspense
Bell's own strongest examples contradict a simplistic version of this claim. Long sentences/paragraphs can heighten suspense by slowing time and intensifying interior experience.

## F11 — Every subplot must generate conflict
Subplots may also create intimacy, belonging, delight, contrast, information or moral context. The stronger universal requirement is causal **interaction** with the main story.

## F12 — Ticking clock everywhere
Artificial deadlines feel manufactured. Use only when the world actually creates one.

## F13 — Theme as early counterargument formula
Useful pattern, not mandatory. Theme can emerge through multiple positions and changing action without a neat `anti-theme statement` in Act One.

## F14 — Historical market assertions
Agent/editor and publishing assumptions from 2011 require current verification before use in market decisions.

# 7. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

## Bell ↔ Truby
Convergence:
- desire/objective plus resistance drives causality;
- opponent must have power and logic;
- cast design is relational;
- revelations should alter action.

Difference:
- Bell foregrounds page-turning pressure and stakes;
- Truby gives the broader moral/character web and organic story system.

Synthesis:
Use Truby to define **why the story changes the person/system**; Bell to pressure-test whether scenes produce live uncertainty and tactical resistance.

## Bell ↔ Rosenfeld
Convergence:
- scene intention/objective;
- consequences create next movement;
- tension belongs inside scenes, not only plot points.

Correction:
Rosenfeld's result spectrum is broader and better for IVDIVO than Bell's setback-heavy bias.

## Bell ↔ Swain / Bickham
Convergence:
- stimulus-response;
- action/reaction;
- scene objective/obstacle/outcome;
- sequel/reaction converts failure into decision.

Caution:
Do not make micro-causality so explicit that prose becomes mechanical.

## Bell ↔ Card
Convergence:
- reader concern must precede danger;
- character-specific attitude/voice;
- relationship networks produce tension;
- POV controls reader information.

Card counterweight:
Character depth is not reducible to maximized trouble.

## Bell ↔ McKee
Convergence:
- conflict is meaningful opposition;
- strongest antagonism is not necessarily villainy;
- escalation should increase pressure;
- climax must pay prior conflict.

McKee counterweight:
Value change and choice can be more useful than Bell's generalized `death` vocabulary.

## Bell ↔ Brody / YA
Convergence:
- external pressure must matter to developmental identity;
- peers/allies need distinct agendas.

YA correction:
Do not adultify stakes. Losing first love, trust, team belonging, autonomy or one singular opportunity can be huge without being called psychological death.

## Bell ↔ Le Guin / Clark / Prose
Bell's sentence-length heuristics are only one local tool. Le Guin/Clark/Prose better support rhythmic variability and functional sentence/paragraph choice. Suspense prose must not become globally chopped.

## Bell ↔ Zunshine / Herman
Bell's suspense withholding must be source-aware. A withheld answer must respect what the POV character knows and preserve source-monitoring; prediction of behavior is not reason/consent.

# 8. IVDIVO PRODUCTION GATES — BS SERIES

## BS01 — MEANINGFUL PRESSURE, NOT MANDATORY DEATH
Name the concrete loss the character fears. If `psychological death` is only a dramatic synonym for embarrassment, rewrite the stake plainly.

## BS02 — OPPONENT RIGHTNESS
Write the best argument for the opposing side before drafting the confrontation.

## BS03 — BONDING AGENT
Why can't the parties simply disengage? If the answer is wages/rent/credits in mature orbital life, fail and rebuild.

## BS04 — ALLY AGENDA
Every major ally gets a distinct immediate want/method. Agreement on the mission is not agreement on tactics.

## BS05 — SCENE QUESTION
What exactly is unresolved when the scene begins, and what changed by the end?

## BS06 — REACTION EARNS SPACE
Reflection must convert consequence into emotion/model/decision. Repeated reflection without strategy change is drag.

## BS07 — SUBPLOT INVASION
Every subplot must alter A-story action/choice/relationship at least once. Otherwise compress or separate into another book.

## BS08 — DIALOGUE LEVERAGE
No dialogue participant exists only to feed exposition. Each has an agenda, constraint or reason to withhold/reframe.

## BS09 — SUSPENSE DELAY PAYS
Every delayed answer must add pressure, information, character or atmosphere relevant to the pending question.

## BS10 — MICROOBSTACLE CAUSALITY
Small obstacle must threaten a meaningful larger consequence; random annoyance is not suspense.

## BS11 — CLOCK LEGITIMACY
Deadline must arise from physical, social, institutional or relational reality—not author panic about pace.

## BS12 — TWIST RETROSPECTIVE FIT
After reveal, earlier evidence must become more intelligible, not less.

## BS13 — CAPACITY REMOVAL CHANGES STRATEGY
If access/tool/ally is removed, character must adapt. Waiting for restoration is not a story beat.

## BS14 — TENSION RHYTHM
Do not keep maximum intensity permanently. Build, release, regroup, then escalate.

## BS15 — SENTENCE RHYTHM NOT KPI
Short sentences/fragments are contrast tools. Never optimize suspense by minimizing average sentence length.

## BS16 — POSITIVE LIFE PROTECTION
Friendship, sport, romance, beauty, play and competence may remain genuinely pleasurable. Their job is not automatically to become disaster.

## BS17 — CONSCIOUS ROBOT OPPOSITION
Robot persons may disagree for their own reasons. Do not use them as convenient system obstacles or obedient tools.

## BS18 — POST-MONEY STAKE FIREWALL
Orbital youth stakes derive from trust, responsibility, mastery, belonging, unique opportunity, consent, safety, reputation, relationship and time—not disguised purchasing power.

## BS19 — SPECIALIST-KNOWLEDGE BOUNDARY
Mainstream orbital characters cannot use niche dimensional/soul-school claims as established engineering fact.

## BS20 — NO SECOND-STORY ESCALATION
Before adding a new danger/attacker/mystery to raise suspense, prove existing A-story pressures cannot generate the needed complication.

# 10. MANUSCRIPT / DEVELOPMENT VALIDATION — ORBITAL YOUTH BOOK TWO

Current viable working A-story: Ollie participates in habitat commissioning, produces a defensible technical interpretation, helps enable a reasonable partial opening, then must rebuild the evidence model after real occupancy exposes a coupled transition/failure.

Bell materially strengthens this design in five places.

## 10.1 Strong opponent without villain
Senior engineer / commissioning authority should be competent and ethically defensible. Their insistence on reproducible evidence has prevented real failures. Ollie must not be right because adults are stupid.

## 10.2 Bonding agent
Ollie and the senior team cannot simply walk away because they share responsibility for the same inhabited environment and the same people. This is stronger than job/salary dependence and compatible with post-money canon.

## 10.3 Suspense question
The book-level question is not `Will the ring explode?`

Better working question:
`Can Ollie learn when technically clean evidence is insufficient before his judgment harms people again—and can he act responsibly when the corrected evidence is still incomplete?`

External habitat safety and internal responsibility remain coupled.

## 10.4 Microobstacles without fake thriller escalation
Use small causal obstacles:
- sensor cannot reproduce resident report;
- privacy boundary limits access to body/environment data;
- conscious robot colleague refuses an unsafe/ethically questionable shortcut;
- route closure separates witness and test location;
- one compatible test window is about to end;
- conflicting but honest resident accounts.

Avoid assassins, sabotage and secret villains unless independently earned by the A-story.

## 10.5 Reaction beats
After midpoint failure, do not jump directly into another technical task. Give Ollie one consequential reaction sequence:
`emotion -> competing interpretation -> relationship cost -> decision to change evidence practice`.

But do not repeat guilt processing chapter after chapter.

# 11. FINAL SYNTHESIS

The durable Bell principle for IVDIVO is not `trouble every second` and not `death must always be on the line`.

It is:

`CARE -> MEANINGFUL UNCERTAINTY -> INTELLIGENT RESISTANCE -> CHOICE -> CONSEQUENCE -> NEW QUESTION`.

Conflict works best when both sides possess agency and reasons. Suspense works best when the reader understands what is unresolved, why it matters, and why the answer cannot yet be obtained.

For Orbital Youth this means the removal of money does **not** remove stakes. It forces better stakes: trust, responsibility, belonging, consent, autonomy, unique opportunity, safety, mastery, reputation, relationship and time.

Bell is especially valuable as a **pressure diagnostic**. He is less reliable when his metaphors (`death`, mandatory setback, universal cliff-hanger, numeric placement, punctuation bans) are treated as literal laws.

Strict lifecycle decision: **SYNTHESIZED / CALLABLE**.
