## 3. CLAIM EXTRACTION

### K-C01 — Cast members need unequal narrative weight
Protagonist, antagonist, secondary, minor and background characters perform different book-level functions. Equal personhood does not imply equal page-time or equal POV allocation.

### K-C02 — First impressions are functional
An introduction should make the character legible enough to orient attention, imply identity and create reader interest. Description is strongest when filtered through someone who notices for a reason.

### K-C03 — Character is demonstrated more reliably by choices than labels
Authorial labels such as brave, selfish or kind are weak evidence. Specific behavior under meaningful pressure provides stronger characterization.

### K-C04 — Character traits are contextual rather than mechanically consistent
People can act differently across situations without becoming incoherent. Contradiction can be believable when linked to values, relationship, power, mood or stakes.

### K-C05 — Motivation requires proportional groundwork
The more unusual a motivation is relative to the character's culture and circumstances, the more contextual/backstory support it requires. Familiar motives may need less explicit explanation.

### K-C06 — Backstory exists to explain present pressure
A writer may know more past than the reader needs. Include history when it changes current motivation, interpretation, relationship or choice.

### K-C07 — Conflicting values create meaningful choice
Characters become legible when two things they genuinely value cannot both be preserved unchanged.

### K-C08 — Character change is optional
A character can be a changer or a stayer. If change occurs, it must be a plausible consequence of accumulated story events. If a character stays, that stability must still produce consequences and pressure on others/the world.

### K-C09 — Validation matters more than declared transformation
If a story claims enduring change, later behavior should demonstrate the change under relevant pressure. But a mandatory end-of-book 'proof scene' is not treated as universal law.

### K-C10 — Genre expectations are constraints, not personality templates
Genre can create reader expectations about competence, role or plot participation, but offbeat occupation/genre role is not characterization by itself.

### K-C11 — Humour uses expectation and authorial attitude
Exaggeration, ridicule and reversal can create humour. Explaining the joke weakens it. Tone expresses the author's attitude toward material through aggregate choices.

### K-C12 — Humour has audience-context requirements
A reversal only works if the audience has the expectation being reversed. Unshared cultural context can destroy the intended effect.

### K-C13 — Emotional dialogue is character-specific and shaped
Real emotional speech is often repetitive/incoherent. Fiction compresses, selects, underplays or emphasizes while preserving the character's temperament and relationship.

### K-C14 — Understatement can increase emotional force
Silence or a smaller-than-expected response can reveal overwhelming emotion when contrasted with prior behavior and context.

### K-C15 — Emotional expression is relation-specific
To whom a character speaks emotionally, when, and with what degree of control is characterization.

### K-C16 — Breaking point requires prior pressure + prior control
A sudden outburst without visible accumulation is melodrama. A breaking point becomes meaningful when the reader has seen both the pressure and the person's previous strategy of control.

### K-C17 — Metaphor/symbol can carry emotion but must fit POV/world
Figurative devices import cultural associations. Extended metaphors work best when congruent with character experience and setting. Story should still function if a reader misses symbolic meaning.

### K-C18 — Emotion scenes remain character scenes
Love, sex, fights and deaths should be individualized by personality, relationship, mixed emotions and existing story pressure, rather than by generic genre staging.

### K-C19 — Frustration is a high-value characterization pressure
What matters is not merely that a character is blocked but how they react, what secondary emotion mixes in and whether they can modify their strategy.

### K-C20 — Frustration should eventually produce action
Thoughts and dialogue can clarify the state, but prolonged rumination without changed action risks stalling the story.

### K-C21 — POV determines meaning as well as access
Different POV choices can create genuinely different novels from the same events because they change what is witnessed, felt, interpreted and emphasized.

### K-C22 — Protagonist and POV need not be the same
A witness POV may preserve mystery, create interpretive distance or foreground a different thematic question. The gain must exceed the loss of immediacy.

### K-C23 — Use the fewest POVs that can tell the necessary story
Every new POV has cognitive switching cost. Additional viewpoints are justified when they provide necessary action, knowledge, interpretation or emotional price unavailable otherwise.

### K-C24 — First person is an information contract
A first-person narrator cannot naturally know what they do not know. If they know a decisive clue, withholding it solely to preserve suspense is generally cheating unless the narration explicitly justifies the omission.

### K-C25 — First-person voice is attention + attitude + diction
Its advantage is not the pronoun but how the character notices, evaluates and phrases the world.

### K-C26 — Unreliable narration must be evidentially legible
The text needs contradictions/anomalies that allow the reader eventually to distinguish narrator belief from the story's stronger evidence.

### K-C27 — Third-person distance is a continuum
Close/middle/distant third are relative positions. Abrupt oscillation produces POV-distance whiplash; transitions can be smoothed by paragraph/scene boundaries or intermediate distance.

### K-C28 — Close third does not require filter phrases
Once focalization is stable, "he thought/he wondered" can create unnecessary distance. But filters are legitimate when the act of perception/thought itself matters or a more formal distance is wanted.

### K-C29 — Multiple POV needs architectural discipline
POV cast should be introduced early enough, recur with meaningful actions, and not be expanded merely to deliver missing information. Mechanical rotation that forces filler is a failure.

### K-C30 — Climactic POV affects final meaning
The consciousness through which the reader experiences the decisive event strongly influences the emotional/thematic interpretation of the book.

### K-C31 — Omniscient narration is a deliberate authorial mode
True omniscience needs an intentional authorial presence and should be signaled early. Random head-hopping plus occasional exposition is not the same thing.

### K-C32 — Generation and criticism are separate cognitive modes
Kress's four-persona process—character → writer → reader → critic—supports a crucial production principle: do not demand that generation simultaneously perform every revision gate.

### K-C33 — Reader response precedes critic prescription
First ask what a reader experiences/understands. Only then diagnose and repair. A critic's proposed solution is not automatically authoritative.

## 4. MECHANISM EXTRACTION / PRODUCTION GATES

### K-M01 — CAST ATTENTION BUDGET
Assign book-level roles:
`PRIMARY MAJOR / SECONDARY MAJOR / SUPPORTING MAJOR / MINOR / BACKGROUND`.
Personhood remains independent of role.

### K-M02 — INTRODUCTION FILTER
`WHO NOTICES → WHAT THEY NOTICE → WHY IT MATTERS TO THEM → WHAT READER INFERS`.

### K-M03 — ACTION > LABEL
Whenever prose states a personality label, ask whether a costly/meaningful choice could provide the evidence instead.

### K-M04 — MOTIVATION GROUNDWORK
`CULTURAL NORMALITY + PERSONAL HISTORY + CURRENT WANT`.
If the want is unusual for that culture, increase causal groundwork—not generic trauma.

### K-M05 — CONFLICTING-GOODS CHOICE
Prefer `GOOD A vs GOOD B` or `VALUE A vs VALUE B` to compulsory flaw exposure when appropriate.

### K-M06 — CHANGER/STAYER ROUTER
Before designing an arc, choose whether the story tests change, resistance to change or the cost/value of remaining stable.

### K-M07 — CHANGE PROOF
`PRESSURE → CHOICE → NEW STRATEGY → LATER RELEVANT BEHAVIOR`.
Do not append an explanatory "he had changed" paragraph.

### K-M08 — FRUSTRATION FINGERPRINT
`WANT → BLOCK → PRIMARY RESPONSE → SECONDARY EMOTION → TACTIC → COUNTERPRESSURE → TACTIC MODIFICATION`.
Different major characters should not share the same default response.

### K-M09 — BREAKING-POINT LEDGER
Track accumulated pressure and prior self-control before permitting a major emotional break.

### K-M10 — EMOTIONAL DIALOGUE SHAPING
Compress real repetition; preserve character-specific incoherence/understatement only where it creates reader effect.

### K-M11 — RELATION-SPECIFIC EXPRESSION
For a given character, compare emotional expression with:
friend / parent / authority / rival / lover / public audience / conscious robot.

### K-M12 — HUMOUR EXPECTATION TEST
`AUDIENCE EXPECTATION → REVERSAL → CHARACTER/TONE CONSEQUENCE`.
If the audience lacks the expectation, the joke cannot rely on reversal alone.

### K-M13 — NO JOKE EXPLANATION
After a functioning humorous beat, do not explain why it was funny.

### K-M14 — MIXED-EMOTION SCENE
For romance, conflict, grief or fear, identify at least one secondary concern/emotion when psychologically justified. Avoid emotional monoculture.

### K-M15 — SYMBOL PLOT-INTEGRATION TEST
Symbolism may be decorative resonance or causal architecture; know which. Never require conscious decoding for basic plot coherence.

### K-M16 — POV VERSION TEST
Before locking POV for a major book, prototype at least one key scene through an alternative consciousness and compare gains/losses.

### K-M17 — POV ACCESS MATRIX
`OBSERVED / KNOWN / REMEMBERED / INFERRED / REPORTED / UNKNOWN / IMPOSSIBLE-TO-KNOW`.

### K-M18 — POV MINIMUM
Every POV must own enough necessary action/interpretation to justify reader switching cost.

### K-M19 — FIRST-PERSON FAIRNESS
Known decisive evidence cannot disappear from narration solely to manufacture suspense.

### K-M20 — POV-DISTANCE CONTINUITY
Map scene distance as close/middle/distant. Abrupt deep→authorial→deep shifts require a deliberate transition/function.

### K-M21 — MULTI-POV EARLY CONTRACT
If multiple POV is fundamental, teach the reader that architecture early enough to prevent a late new POV from looking like a repair patch.

### K-M22 — NO ROTATION FILLER
Never invent a scene merely because it is "this character's turn" in a POV pattern.

### K-M23 — CLIMAX POV OWNERSHIP
Give decisive POV to the consciousness whose choice/price/interpretation best completes the book's local engine—not automatically the franchise lead.

### K-M24 — OMNISCIENT COMMITMENT
Authorial commentary + free mind access must be deliberate, early and sustained. Otherwise stay with disciplined third person.

### K-M25 — FOUR-PERSONA WORKFLOW
`BECOME CHARACTER → GENERATE AS WRITER → READ AS READER → CRITIQUE/REVISE`.
Do not collapse all four into one drafting moment.

### K-M26 — READER-FIRST DIAGNOSIS
`READER EFFECT → ROOT DEFECT → MULTIPLE FIXES → AUTHOR CHOICE → RETEST`.

## 5. FAILURE MODES

2. Character-by-job stereotype.
3. Action-free personality labels.
4. Trauma/backstory used to mechanically explain every choice.
5. Motivation culturally implausible without groundwork.
6. Compulsory positive change arc.
7. Validation-scene lesson download.
8. Universal wit / universal sarcasm under frustration.
9. Humour dependent on unshared reference.
10. Explaining the joke.
11. Emotional dialogue transcriptionism.
12. Profanity/slang as substitute for voice.
13. Constant maximum emotion.
14. Sudden unprepared breaking point.
15. Pure-emotion scenes with no mixed motives/concerns.
16. Metaphor/symbol carrying story that literal plot cannot support.
17. Weather/pathos automation.
18. Generic love/fight/death scenes.
19. Thoughts substituting for consequential action.
20. POV chosen by habit rather than story version.
21. Too many POVs.
22. POV introduced late only to deliver information.
23. POV rotation filler.
24. First-person clue cheating.

## 6. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

### With Card / Corbett / McKee Character
Strong convergence on action under pressure, relation-specific self and unequal cast function. Kress adds especially useful frustration-response diagnostics and POV-distance control.

### With LaPlante
Strong convergence on separating generation from criticism and on contextual rather than absolute rules. LaPlante is stronger on organic form/show-tell; Kress is stronger on cast/POV mechanics.

### With Rosenfeld / McKee Dialogue
Converges on shaped dialogue and reader proximity. Kress's emotional-expression material should be used as character-specific modulation, not as a requirement to verbalize emotion.

### With Zunshine
Kress's unreliable narrator / first-person fairness pairs well with Zunshine's provenance system. Kress is craft-oriented; Zunshine offers stronger theory for separating belief from fact.

### With Brody YA
Kress's changer/stayer distinction is an important corrective to compulsory YA transformation. Developmental change is possible but not mandatory.

### With Lodge / W001
Converges on voice as more than slang and on POV as a reader-effect choice. W001 provides broader cross-genre rhythm/mode routing; Kress gives finer third-person distance architecture.

### With IVDIVO post-money / robot canon
Kress's period-specific occupation/money examples are not transplanted. Motivation mechanisms are abstracted from their economic examples.

## 7. IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION

### Orbital Youth motivation
Replace money/rent/credits examples with truly nonfungible stakes:
- trust of a specific person;
- role on a real mission/team;
- consent and relationship access;
- reputation after a public decision;
- mastery/certification where the opportunity itself is scarce because human time/experience is finite;
- responsibility;
- belonging;
- unique experience/window.

Do not convert these into a hidden points economy.

### Conscious robots
Kress's "nonhuman POV must be partly human-readable" is historically useful but too anthropocentric as a law. IVDIVO rule:
`NONHUMAN PERSONHOOD → legible interface for reader + genuinely different embodiment/attention/values where canon supports it`.
A robot cannot be reduced to a human with metallic sensory garnish, nor to a servant/tool.

### Specialist consciousness schools
Character belief is not ontology. Apply K-M17 plus current epistemic chain:
`OBSERVATION → REPRODUCIBLE PRACTICE EFFECT → SCHOOL INTERPRETATION → COMPETING INTERPRETATION → POSSIBLE WORLD FACT`.

### YA arcs
Allow changer, partial changer and stayer. Adolescence supplies developmental pressure, not a compulsory therapeutic conversion.

### Ensemble
Saga equality does not equal book-level equal POV. Assign attention hierarchy per novel. Each named robot/nonhuman retains independent life even when not POV.

### Dialogue / emotion
Understatement, silence and relation-specific expression are preferred over naming feelings. Exact words must change scene state or be compressed.

### POV
Use close third where intimacy is valuable, but do not force maximum depth continuously. New POVs require necessary action/information/meaning and recurrence sufficient to justify switching cost.

### Prose cadence
POV-distance whiplash can masquerade as "GPT prose": close thought → authorial encyclopedia → close thought. Diagnose distance before line editing.

## 9. SYNTHESIS
Kress's strongest IVDIVO contribution is a linked character/POV diagnostic:

`CULTURE + HISTORY + CURRENT WANT → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC RESPONSE TO FRICTION → CHOICE/TACTIC → CONSEQUENCE`

combined with

`WHO EXPERIENCES → WHAT THEY CAN KNOW → HOW CLOSE WE STAND TO THEIR MIND → WHAT VERSION OF THE STORY THE READER RECEIVES`.

The book is valuable when stripped of dated market/social/scientific assumptions and used as an operational craft system rather than psychology doctrine.
