## 4. PROVISIONAL CLAIMS FROM READ MATERIAL

### C15-C01 — Character is forged through interaction
Character becomes legible through what a person does with other people, circumstances and resistance, not through isolated trait lists.

### C15-C02 — Creation and discovery alternate
Deliberate design supplies raw material; drafting, incubation and revision can reveal behavior not obvious from the initial concept. Neither pure planning nor pure “the character told me” is sufficient.

### C15-C03 — Character source is not character destiny
Story role, dream/image, artwork, real person or composite may start a characterization, but remaining faithful to the initial impression produces elaboration rather than development.

### C15-C04 — Story-derived characters risk becoming plot puppets
A person created only because the plot needs a detective, cadet, mentor, robot ally or victim lacks independent wants and life beyond the story function.

### C15-C05 — Real people are incomplete evidence
Writers do not possess complete access to other people’s inner lives. Information richness must not be confused with full psychological knowledge.

### C15-C06 — Ordinary people are sufficient material
Character depth does not require exotic biography. Precision, empathy, contradiction and relationship history can make ordinary lives narratively rich.

### C15-C07 — Composite coherence requires causal history
Contrasting traits cannot simply be fused. The writer must understand what experiences/social treatment could plausibly produce the combination.

### C15-C08 — Research bridges lived experience to unfamiliar lives
Personal emotional analogy is useful only after respecting material differences in power, social relations, daily life and context.

### C15-C09 — Character work should use scenes, not only biography
Emotionally significant behavior becomes more useful when imagined as action under circumstance rather than static background information.

### C15-C10 — Five cornerstones are diagnostic, not mandatory formula
Corbett foregrounds desire, frustration/plan, contradiction, vulnerability and secret. He explicitly warns against treating these as a grab-bag formula. IVDIVO therefore treats them as prompts, not compulsory slots.

## 5. PROVISIONAL IVDIVO MECHANISMS

### M15-01 — CHARACTER SOURCE / INDEPENDENCE TEST
For any major/supporting character ask:
`What originated this character? What do they want that is not merely “help/oppose protagonist”? What life continues when protagonist exits the room?`

### M15-02 — CREATION ↔ DISCOVERY LOOP
`DESIGN HYPOTHESIS → WRITE PRESSURED SCENE → OBSERVE UNEXPECTED BUT PLAUSIBLE BEHAVIOR → UPDATE MODEL → TEST AGAIN`.

### M15-03 — STORY-FUNCTION FIREWALL
`BOOK FUNCTION ≠ STORYWORLD PERSONHOOD`.
A mentor, antagonist, witness, love interest or conscious robot must remain a person beyond its plot label.

### M15-04 — RESEARCH / EMPATHY BRIDGE
`MATERIAL RESEARCH → POWER/SOCIAL CONTEXT → DAILY-LIFE SPECIFICS → LIMITED PERSONAL ANALOGY → IMAGINATIVE SCENE`.
Personal experience cannot erase cultural or material difference.

### M15-05 — COMPOSITE CAUSALITY
When combining apparently contradictory traits, specify the contexts/relationships in which each trait developed and appears. Contradiction without causal context is decoration.

### M15-06 — SCENE-BASED CHARACTER BIOGRAPHY
Prefer a small set of remembered/imagined scenes that changed expectations, relationships or coping behavior over encyclopedic biography.

### M15-07 — FIVE-CORNERSTONE DIAGNOSTIC
If a character feels inert, inspect:
`DESIRE / RESPONSE TO FRUSTRATION / VULNERABILITY / SECRET-INFORMATION BOUNDARY / CONTRADICTION`.
Do not add whichever category is missing automatically; ask which missing pressure would actually change behavior in this story.

## 6. EARLY FAILURE MODES
1. **Plot Puppet** — character exists only to perform story function.
2. **Trait Collage** — interesting adjectives combined without causal life.
3. **Initial-Impression Prison** — first image/archetype defines every later action.
4. **Biography Without Behavior** — pages of past with no present tactical effect.
5. **Personalization Overreach** — author’s emotional analogy replaces research into a different life/world.
6. **Complete-Knowledge Fantasy** — narrator/author treats a character as fully solved.
7. **Mandatory Trauma Retrofit** — wound added because craft theory expects one.
8. **Mandatory Secret Retrofit** — secret manufactured to simulate depth.
9. **Contradiction Checklist** — arbitrary opposite traits mistaken for complexity.
10. **Psychoanalysis as Canon** — historical craft psychology imported as scientific fact.

## 7. EARLY CROSS-SOURCE POSITION
- **Corbett + McKee Character:** both emphasize behavior under pressure; Corbett is more useful for character-source discovery and scene-based intuitive development, McKee for modus operandi/cast design.
- **Corbett + Card:** both resist thin role-based characterization; Card is stronger on reader contract/POV hierarchy, Corbett on source material and intuitive scene discovery.

- **Corbett + Truby:** Truby’s character web supplies structural opposition; Corbett helps prevent web roles from becoming functions without private life.

## 8. EARLY IVDIVO APPLICATION
### Orbital Youth
Post-money conflict increases the importance of specific human desires and relationship stakes. Do not replace money with abstract “status points”; give each youth a concrete nonfungible want and a characteristic method of pursuing it.

### Conscious robots
A conscious robot is especially vulnerable to Plot Puppet failure. Require independent desire, off-axis relationships, refusals and culturally specific normality before using it as mentor/ally/opponent.

### Consciousness schools
Do not infer a practitioner’s personality or ontology from a school label. Research lineage, daily practice, social position and specific behavior; school doctrine remains an in-world interpretation unless independently established.
