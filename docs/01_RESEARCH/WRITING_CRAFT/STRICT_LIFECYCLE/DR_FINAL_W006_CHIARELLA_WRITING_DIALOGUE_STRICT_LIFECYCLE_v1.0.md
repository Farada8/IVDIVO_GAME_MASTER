# 1. FULL READ / STRUCTURE MAP
Continuous strict read completed from opening through end matter. Functional architecture:
1. listen to real speech and learn selective hearing;
2. analyze tension/direction/speed/distance in dialogue;
3. build character through diction, syntax, rhythm and selective dialect;
4. compress overloaded dialogue and use rhythm/scene rather than explanation;
5. use silence, gesture, place and narrator modulation as parts of dialogue;
6. listen to/read performed dialogue as a craft laboratory without copying surface effects;
7. use overheard/generated dialogue as a discovery engine for new scenes/stories;
8. control tags, punctuation, adverbs, participles and formatting;
9. final whole-story reading lab tests dialogue across an entire narrative rather than as isolated quotations.

The book repeatedly argues against hard universal rules; its categories are best treated as diagnostic/generative lenses.

# 3. CLAIM EXTRACTION
## W006-C01 — Dialogue is shaped speech, not transcription
Fiction selects from real speech. Raw filler, repetition and social noise are source material, not automatically finished dialogue.

## W006-C02 — Listening is a primary craft input
Diction, syntax, pauses, evasion, repetition, rhythm and social context should be observed before a writer invents generic speech.

## W006-C03 — Pressure has multiple vectors
Conversation can carry tension through direction, speed, relational distance, avoidance, incompatible attention or intimacy without an overt argument.

## W006-C04 — Dialogue categories overlap
Directed, interpolated, misdirected and modulated dialogue are not separate species. A strong exchange may move among them.

## W006-C05 — Character voice is larger than accent
Voice arises from word choice, syntax, rhythm, relation, context, attitude, gesture and selective silence. Accent spelling alone is a weak and often distorting shortcut.

## W006-C06 — Dialect must preserve dignity and intelligibility
Phonetic caricature can make the reader attend to spelling instead of the person. Diction/syntax/context often carry social language more effectively than apostrophe-heavy mimicry.

## W006-C07 — All, but not everything
A reader should receive enough selected speech to infer the person, but not every repeated catchphrase, filler word, curse or conversational loop.

## W006-C08 — Dialogue can be overloaded with story jobs
When one conversation is forced to restate exposition, perform conflict, supply metaphor, introduce a twist, deliver an epiphany and resolve the scene, it often becomes wooden because characters are serving the author rather than each other.

## W006-C09 — Silence is an active dialogue move
A non-answer can change distance, power, meaning and expectation. Narration, memory, place or gesture can occupy the space where speech is withheld.

## W006-C10 — Setting participates in dialogue
Ambient sound, movement, objects, interruptions and incidental gesture can alter rhythm and meaning without requiring explanatory speech.

## W006-C11 — The narrator can be turned up or down
Some exchanges need narrative interpolation; others gain force when the narrator becomes nearly invisible and the voices carry the scene. Neither mode is superior by default.

## W006-C12 — Dialogue can generate story during discovery
Overheard fragments, reframed settings and unexpected character turns can discover material the writer did not plan. This belongs primarily to generation, not to final structural validation.

## W006-C13 — Tags are rhythm/control devices, not emotion substitutes
`Said` is often transparent. Placement can shape pause and emphasis. Verb-thesaurus tags, adverbs and explanatory participles frequently signal that the spoken line or scene is not doing enough work.

## W006-C14 — Dialogue must be judged across the whole narrative
A line can sound brilliant in isolation and still be wrong for the surrounding story. Repetition, irony, voice establishment and payoff are longitudinal effects.

# 4. MECHANISM EXTRACTION
## W006-M01 — LISTEN → SELECT → SHAPE
`HEARD LANGUAGE → DISTINCTIVE SIGNAL → CHARACTER/RELATION FUNCTION → SELECTIVE USE → READER EFFECT`.

## W006-M02 — DIALOGUE PRESSURE VECTOR
`DIRECTION + SPEED + RELATIONAL DISTANCE + CURRENT CONTEXT → TACTIC → RESPONSE → NEW VECTOR`.

## W006-M03 — RELATION-SPECIFIC VOICE STACK
For each major character track:
`WORLDVIEW → ATTENTION → DICTION → SYNTAX → RHYTHM → DIRECTNESS/EVASION → GESTURE/SILENCE → HOW THIS CHANGES BY RELATION`.

## W006-M04 — ALL-BUT-NOT-EVERYTHING COMPRESSION
`RAW/INITIAL EXCHANGE → identify unique character signal → remove social filler/repeated function → preserve decisive wording/turn → test rhythm and inference`.

## W006-M05 — DIALOGUE LOAD BUDGET
Before writing/keeping a long conversation assign:
- PRIMARY JOB: what changes because exact words are spoken;
- SECONDARY JOB(S): character/relationship/world/subtext/evidence;
- FORBIDDEN OVERLOAD: unrelated exposition, surprise, theme statement and resolution stuffed into the same exchange merely for efficiency.

## W006-M06 — EXPOSITION NECESSITY TEST
`WHO NEEDS THIS INFORMATION? → WHY NOW? → WHAT WOULD THIS PERSON NATURALLY OMIT/RESIST? → WHY MUST IT BE SPOKEN RATHER THAN SUMMARIZED? → WHAT CHANGES AFTER IT IS SAID?`

## W006-M07 — SILENCE ROUTER
When a character does not speak, determine whether silence is:
`refusal / protection / uncertainty / status response / intimacy / contempt / overload / strategic delay`.
Then let gesture, POV, place or consequence carry the meaning. Do not use silence as authorial escape from a difficult scene.

## W006-M08 — PLACE-REACTION ROUTER
`AMBIENT DETAIL / OBJECT / INTERRUPTION → character-specific notice or gesture → pause/reframe → dialogue resumes differently`.
The environment becomes causal texture, not decorative stage direction.

## W006-M09 — MODULATION AGAINST CHOP
`SPEECH → meaningful narration/place/memory/gesture → changed interpretation → speech`.
Use to avoid monotonous `quote → microreaction → quote → microreaction` prose when continuous rapid exchange is not the intended effect.

## W006-M10 — DIALECT/DISTINCTIVE SPEECH GATE
Prefer `diction + syntax + rhythm + culturally grounded idiom` before phonetic spelling. If surface accent becomes the primary reader task, reduce it. Never infer a person's psychology from class/race/accent shorthand.

## W006-M11 — TAG PLACEMENT AS RHYTHM
Place a neutral tag at a natural pause when identification is needed. Move/delete it when speaker identity is already clear. Do not replace a weak line with a theatrical speech verb.

## W006-M12 — DISCOVERY / REVISION SEPARATION
Generation may begin from a word, overheard fragment, arbitrary detail or unexpected line. Revision then asks whether the discovered material belongs to ONE MAIN STORY and changes state. Discovery freedom does not waive causal validation.

# 5. FAILURE MODES
- TRANSCRIPT REALISM — retaining filler because people really talk that way;
- DIALECT COSPLAY — accent spelling used as substitute for cultural/character knowledge;
- CATCHPHRASE FLATTENING — one verbal tic repeated until it replaces personality;
- CURSE-AS-CHARACTER — profanity frequency treated as depth/voice;
- WRITER-FIRST EXPOSITION — characters reciting facts for the reader;
- DIALOGUE LOAD COLLAPSE — one conversation forced to carry setup, theme, twist, climax and resolution;
- ENERGY-INJECTION TWIST — pregnancy/death/attack/secret added because a conversation feels flat;
- REDUNDANT CONFLICT — the same disagreement restated instead of tactics changing;
- GESTURE CHOREOGRAPHY — constant cups, looks, shrugs and nods with no relational function;
- SILENCE AS ESCAPE — characters stop speaking because the writer cannot solve the exchange;
- NARRATOR OVERFILL — every line immediately explained by interior reaction;
- NARRATOR STARVATION — refusing useful place/memory/POV modulation because dialogue is assumed to be purer;
- SAID-THESAURUS — tags calling attention to writer cleverness;
- ADVERB EMOTION LABEL — `angrily`, `sadly`, etc. doing work the line/context should carry;
- PARTICIPLE PUPPETRY — repetitive `saying X while doing Y` used to manufacture motion;
- PUNCTUATION PERFORMANCE — exclamation marks/italics used instead of actual pressure;
- WALL-OF-DIALOGUE MONOCULTURE — all scenes rendered as naked back-and-forth;
- UNIVERSAL TEEN VOICE — contemporary Earth slang substituted for Orbital Youth worldview;
- UNIVERSAL ROBOT VOICE — all conscious robots given one synthetic speech style;
- ISOLATED-LINE OPTIMIZATION — brilliant quotations that damage scene/story causality.

# 7. IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION
## Production order for dialogue problems
`SCENE NECESSITY → EXACT-WORD NECESSITY → CHARACTER WANT → RELATION → PRESSURE VECTOR → TACTICS → MODULATION/SILENCE/SETTING → COMPRESSION → TAGS/PUNCTUATION → LINE EDIT`.

Never fix structural emptiness by decorating dialogue.

## Orbital Youth dialogue fingerprints
Do not treat existing sample rhythms as catchphrase generators. They are boundaries, not templates.

### Ollie
Low verbal performance; concrete feasibility language; notices tools/interfaces/failure modes. Under emotional pressure he should not simply become a TED speaker. His silence and delayed directness can carry more than repeated technical catchphrases.

### Aoife
Listening/context/reframing are strengths, but she can over-interpret. Her dialogue should sometimes misread a person despite correctly noticing language. Camera/recording presence should change how others speak.

### Samir
Silence is active and specific. Do not automatically fill his quiet with author explanation. When he becomes direct, the contrast should matter.

### Maya
Shorter, verb-forward argumentative turns are compatible with her character, but sentence brevity must not become a percentage target. Place/physical action can carry part of her exchanges.

### Ethan
Managerial language should shift by relationship; with a friend he cannot sound identical to an institutional briefing. His over-management is strongest when visible in what he tries to organize verbally, not when narrator labels it.

## Conscious robots
`PERSONHOOD → INDEPENDENT WANT → RELATION-SPECIFIC TACTIC → RIGHT TO REFUSE/NOT ANSWER → DISTINCT SILENCE/DIRECTNESS PATTERN`.
No universal robot diction. If replacing a robot with a smart terminal leaves the dialogue function unchanged, personhood is not yet written.

## Post-money youth
Youth authenticity must come from what characters value and assume: time, belonging, mission places, trust, intimacy, consent, reputation, responsibility and unique experience. Do not re-import money/rent/credits through contemporary work/career dialogue.

## Rare consciousness schools
Different speakers must mark epistemic status in their language. `I observed`, `our school interprets`, `we can reproduce`, `we suspect`, and `the world is` are not interchangeable speech acts.

# 9. FINAL SYNTHESIS
Chiarella's strongest transferable principle is not a dialogue formula but disciplined selection:

`LISTEN WIDELY → IDENTIFY CHARACTER/RELATION SIGNAL → SHAPE PRESSURE → LET SPEECH, SILENCE, PLACE AND NARRATION SHARE THE WORK → COMPRESS → TEST AGAINST THE WHOLE STORY`.

Dialogue should sound alive because characters appear to own their words, while remaining rigorously controlled by story function and reader effect.
