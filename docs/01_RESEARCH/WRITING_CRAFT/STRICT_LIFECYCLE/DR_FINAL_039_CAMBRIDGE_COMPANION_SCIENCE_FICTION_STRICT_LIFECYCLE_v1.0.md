## 3. CONSOLIDATED CLAIM EXTRACTION

### C39-01 — SF is a mode/history of conversations, not one plot engine
SF repeatedly borrows mystery, romance, rescue, adventure, procedural, satire, war, institutional and coming-of-age engines. A speculative condition does not replace a causal story.

### C39-02 — Novum earns narrative weight through consequences
Wonder alone is a legitimate local reader reward, but a book-level engine requires second-order effects on behaviour, institutions, relationships, values or knowledge.

### C39-03 — SF reading is active translation
Readers infer changed rules from objects, customs, language, relationships and what characters treat as normal. This makes embedded world-reveal structurally preferable to tutorial dialogue.

### C39-04 — Conceptual breakthrough is a distinct SF payoff
Observation and explanation can function like clues when they force the reader and characters to revise a model. Information that changes no model or action is primarily lore.

### C39-05 — Genre memory is cumulative and non-linear
Old and new conventions coexist, recombine and remain available simultaneously. Historical progression is not a simple ladder of artistic improvement.

### C39-06 — Authorial intention does not control reader effect
Dune's intended anti-superhero logic and its frequent reception as heroic wish fulfilment are a useful warning: production must test what the text actually rewards, not what the author says it means.

### C39-07 — Formal innovation can reveal worldview, but innovation can become mannerism
New Wave history demonstrates the value of subjectivity, linguistic experiment and epistemic instability, while also showing how experimentation can harden into empty style.

### C39-08 — Future reality can overtake SF iconography
Once a once-exotic device becomes mundane, fiction must find its human/social meaning rather than rely on novelty alone.

### C39-09 — Post-scarcity changes stakes rather than abolishing story
Historical post-scarcity SF demonstrates that material scarcity is not a mandatory engine. Conflict can relocate to autonomy, attention, purpose, access, power, belonging, relationships, time, responsibility and non-fungible opportunities.

### C39-10 — Spectacle and story are separable variables
Film/television history shows spectacle can be a deliberate attraction, but increased visual scale does not guarantee narrative or character innovation.

### C39-11 — Representation is not the same as agency
A text can appear socially progressive while preserving conservative power/functions. Who acts, refuses, decides, pays and changes the causal state matters more than surface inclusion.

### C39-12 — Editorial ecology shapes the genre
Editors, magazines, markets, anthologies and critical institutions do not merely select fiction; they encourage recurring forms and construct canons. A Writers' Room can therefore create either productive variation or formula monoculture.

### C39-13 — Cognition effect is not scientific validation
A story can produce the rhetoric and felt logic of rational inquiry while its technical premise is false. Narrative plausibility and current scientific plausibility must be tested separately.

### C39-14 — Technical efficacy and normative legitimacy are independent questions
A system may work very well and still distribute burdens unjustly, undermine consent, centralize power or treat persons instrumentally.

### C39-15 — Scientific/technical competence can carry hidden ideology
The engineer-hero tradition can quietly assume that efficiency, rationality or technical mastery confers moral authority. IVDIVO must preserve competent countermodels and affected-person agency.

### C39-16 — Synthetic-person tropes historically encode servitude
The robot icon often begins as a worker/servant and is devalued by artificial origin. This is precisely what current IVDIVO robot canon must not inherit automatically.

### C39-17 — Personhood is not determined by substrate
The volume repeatedly shows SF using robots, cyborgs, clones, engineered beings and aliens to test who is allowed to count as a person. For IVDIVO, named conscious robots require independent goals, refusal and relationships.

### C39-18 — Otherness is historically contingent
Aliens have served as enemies, immigrants, minorities, diplomatic opponents and mirrors of human categories. Their story function is culturally produced, not inherent.

### C39-19 — An alien/world/ecology can carry genuine causal weight
A world becomes more than backdrop when its physical/ecological rules actively constrain decisions and create consequences.

### C39-20 — Hard SF is a contested practice, not a single attitude
Scientific relationship can be central without reproducing one masculine, militarist or libertarian voice. Factual currency ages; method and intellectual honesty are more durable craft concerns.

### C39-21 — Hard-SF rhetoric can become hardware fetishism
When the social/scientific reasoning drops out, a 'hard' attitude can persist as cold voice, military hardware and claims of realism. This is a failure mode, not proof of seriousness.

### C39-22 — Science is historically and politically situated
The hard-SF chapter shows scientific topics, publishing and political agendas interacting. 'Apolitical science' in fiction can still carry political assumptions.

### C39-23 — Space opera survives by recombination
The subgenre repeatedly renews itself by absorbing romance, satire, politics, diverse intelligent life, relationship depth and other engines. Scale alone is not renewal.

### C39-24 — Pure entertainment is a legitimate reader reward
A story does not need to disguise pleasure as philosophical importance. But entertainment still requires character, causality, variation and earned payoff.

### C39-25 — Alternate history is consequence design
Its core mechanism is divergence plus logically/socially legible consequences. The strongest versions often return from 'great men and wars' to ordinary people whose lives instantiate the change.

### C39-26 — Historical alteration must be reader-legible
Counterfactual significance depends on the reader understanding what changed; obscure differences require more narrative labour than culturally famous divergences.

### C39-27 — Utopia becomes dramatically stronger when it is critical, dynamic and plural
Classic perfect-system lectures tend toward static infodump. Modern critical utopias gain force by exposing tradeoffs, internal disagreement, incompleteness and continuing human agency.

### C39-28 — Post-money/post-scarcity is historically available SF territory
The utopia chapter explicitly discusses no-money societies, automation and post-scarcity. For IVDIVO this supports genre plausibility only; Founder canon defines the actual orbital system.

### C39-29 — Utopia is not the absence of conflict
Removing material want can expose conflicts of purpose, autonomy, authority, privacy, status, disagreement, goals and institutional design.

### C39-30 — Political life requires forums, not merely 'good administration'
A technically efficient society can become oppressive if disagreement has no legitimate route of expression. Institutions need visible processes for conflict, appeal and revision.

### C39-31 — Politics is process, compromise and coalition as well as ideology
SF often prefers clean technical logic; political stories gain realism when institutions contain bargaining, waiting, partial victories and legitimate disagreement.

### C39-32 — A working system cannot justify itself solely by 'it works'
The Starship Troopers discussion is a useful warning against authorial fiat: functioning output is not sufficient evidence for justice or legitimacy.

### C39-33 — Decentralization does not erase conflict
Technological self-sufficiency can reduce some dependencies while producing new coordination, legitimacy and community problems.

### C39-34 — Future gender/sexuality cannot be projected by merely relabelling present categories
Queer/feminist chapters stress the historical contingency of identities, language and social roles. Future societies require transformed assumptions, not present-day labels pasted onto advanced technology.

### C39-35 — Power and consent must survive speculative embodiment
Biological, reproductive, virtual or engineered changes matter narratively through agency, dependence and power asymmetry. Historical body-transfer stories remain genre evidence only, not IVDIVO technology.

### C39-36 — Cultural absence is not neutrality
The race chapter shows that omitting race/culture can still encode a dominant viewpoint. 'Everyone is just human now' does not automatically explain how historical/cultural difference was transformed.

### C39-37 — Alien-as-minority metaphor can reproduce the very hierarchy it critiques
If nonhuman peoples exist only as mirrors for human moral learning, their independent personhood/world remains unbuilt.

### C39-38 — Culture-specific everyday life is stronger than abstract diversity claims
Ordinary practices, relations, work, language and memory make difference causally real.

### C39-39 — Religion/spirituality in SF has often been flattened into primitive superstition or set dressing
A future culture needs internal diversity, history, practice and competing interpretations rather than one religion/planet or one doctrine/species.

### C39-40 — Ritual, faith, ontology and material explanation are distinct
A ritual may encode an actual biological/material process, symbolize faith, organize power, or be misunderstood. The story must not collapse these epistemic layers without evidence.

### C39-41 — Transcendence/uploading is a historical SF discourse, not an IVDIVO fact
The volume documents many upload, body-change and transcendence stories. These are mechanism/history references only. Current IVDIVO orbital canon explicitly lacks operational human consciousness-transfer technology.

### C39-42 — Specialist belief should be represented from inside as well as outside
Religion chapter's strongest counterexamples resist caricature by showing believers' internal logic. Likewise IVDIVO consciousness schools need practitioner motives, lineage, reproducibility and competing interpretations.

## 4. CONSOLIDATED MECHANISM EXTRACTION

### M39-01 — NOVUM → CONSEQUENCE
`SPECULATIVE DIFFERENCE → LOCAL USE/BENEFIT → SECOND-ORDER EFFECT → RESPONSE → REVISED PRACTICE`.

### M39-02 — CONCEPTUAL BREAKTHROUGH
`OBSERVATION → MODEL → NEW FACT → CONTRADICTION → REVISED MODEL → ACTION`.

### M39-03 — FUTURE NORMALITY
`NORMAL ACTION FOR CHARACTER → UNFAMILIAR DETAIL FOR READER → CONTEXTUAL INFERENCE → CONFIRMATION ONLY IF NEEDED`.

### M39-04 — SF MODE + LOCAL ENGINE
`MYSTERY / RESCUE / ROMANCE / INSTITUTIONAL / ADVENTURE / COMING-OF-AGE / PROCEDURAL / OTHER` + `SPECULATIVE CONDITION`.

### M39-05 — TECHNICAL / NORMATIVE SPLIT
`DOES IT WORK?` → separate from → `WHO BENEFITS? WHO PAYS? WHO DECIDES? WHO MAY REFUSE?`

### M39-06 — READER-EFFECT OVERRIDE
`AUTHORIAL INTENT → TEXTUAL REWARD STRUCTURE → ACTUAL READER INFERENCE`.
If the second and third contradict the first, revise the text rather than defend intention.

### M39-07 — ICON MEANING TEST
`ICON/TECHNOLOGY → inherited genre meaning → IVDIVO-specific meaning → causal use`.
No iconic object enters merely because it signals 'science fiction'.

### M39-08 — POST-SCARCITY STAKE ROUTER
Replace default money/rent scarcity with real non-fungible pressure:
`TIME / TRUST / ROLE / ACCESS / ATTENTION / RESPONSIBILITY / CONSENT / RELATIONSHIP / REPUTATION / UNIQUE EXPERIENCE / COMPATIBLE PLACE`.
Do not turn these into hidden credits.

### M39-09 — SYNTHETIC PERSONHOOD
`OWN WANT → OWN RELATIONSHIPS → RIGHT TO REFUSE → INDEPENDENT COST → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC TACTIC → CONSEQUENCE`.

### M39-10 — WORLD CAUSALITY
`WORLD RULE → OPTION/CONSTRAINT → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE`.

### M39-11 — CRITICAL UTOPIA
`SYSTEM BENEFIT → PERSON WHO BENEFITS → PERSON/CASE THAT EXPOSES LIMIT → LEGITIMATE FORUM/COUNTERMODEL → REVISION OR ACCEPTED COST`.

### M39-12 — POLITICAL PROCESS
`INTEREST A + INTEREST B → FORUM → LEVERAGE/COMPROMISE → PARTIAL RESULT → CONSEQUENCE`.

### M39-13 — DIVERGENCE / CONSEQUENCE
`CHANGE → FIRST-ORDER EFFECT → INSTITUTIONAL/CULTURAL ADAPTATION → ORDINARY-LIFE EFFECT → NEW CHOICE`.

### M39-14 — EMBODIMENT POWER TEST
`BODY/REPRODUCTION/INTERFACE CHANGE → WHO CONTROLS IT → WHO DEPENDS ON WHOM → CONSENT → SOCIAL ROLE → CHOICE`.

### M39-15 — CULTURAL SPECIFICITY
`HISTORY/LINEAGE → EVERYDAY PRACTICE → LANGUAGE/CATEGORY → RELATIONSHIP → INSTITUTIONAL EFFECT`.

### M39-16 — EPISTEMIC SPIRITUALITY
`OBSERVATION → REPRODUCIBLE PRACTICE EFFECT → SCHOOL INTERPRETATION → COMPETING INTERPRETATION → ONTOLOGICAL CLAIM`.
Never skip levels.

### M39-17 — EDITORIAL ECOLOGY TEST
For every Writers' Room gate ask:
`Does this gate diagnose a failure and preserve alternatives, or force every book toward one school?`

### M39-18 — SCALE / INTIMACY BRAID
`LARGE SYSTEMIC CHANGE ↔ ONE SPECIFIC RELATIONSHIP/DECISION`.
Each side should alter the other.

## 6. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

### Cambridge + Truby / McKee Story
Truby/McKee remain stronger for causal book architecture. Cambridge prevents their architecture from mistaking speculative worldbuilding or genre convention for causal story. Use `SF MODE + LOCAL ENGINE` before beat design.

### Cambridge + Lodge
Strong convergence on formal plurality and anti-formula discipline. Lodge operates at prose/mode scale; Cambridge shows the same plurality historically across the genre.

### Cambridge + LaPlante
LaPlante's discovery/revision separation prevents genre rules from strangling invention. Cambridge's history supplies evidence that new SF forms repeatedly arose by violating inherited expectations.

### Cambridge + Zunshine
Conceptual breakthrough becomes stronger when paired with provenance: `OBSERVATION → SOURCE → MODEL → CONTRADICTION → REVISED MODEL → ACTION`.

### Cambridge + Card / McKee Character
Cambridge exposes role/icon stereotypes that can flatten people. Card/McKee supply stronger person/cast differentiation. Conscious robots and aliens therefore require independent MO/relationships, not iconic function.

### Cambridge + Brody
Brody supplies YA developmental pressure; Cambridge warns that historical YA/social conventions must transform with the future society. Do not import present-day money/status scripts unchanged into orbital adolescence.

### Cambridge + current science
The Companion can identify a trope's history and ideological baggage. It cannot establish whether that technology or claim is currently plausible. Technical validation remains separate.

## 7. IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION

### ONE MAIN STORY
Every SF book must name:
1. the local engine;
2. the speculative condition;
3. the causal connection between them.
If removing the technology leaves the same story unchanged, the speculative condition is decorative. If removing the human/nonhuman engine leaves only a tour of inventions, the book has no main story.

### Orbital Youth
- Post-money life remains binding canon.
- Do not replace money with points/credits/status-currency unless canon explicitly establishes such a system.
- Material abundance shifts pressure to non-fungible stakes.
- Ordinary orbital life should reveal post-scarcity through assumptions and behaviour.
- A transport, gravity, habitat or AI detail earns prose when it changes action, relationship, access, responsibility or reader model.
- Large orbital wonder should periodically narrow into a specific adolescent decision.

### Conscious robot civilisation
- Historical robot-as-worker icon is a **negative reference**.
- A conscious robot must have independent relations, interests, obligations, disagreement and right of refusal.
- `REPLACE WITH SMART TERMINAL` test: if nothing important changes, personhood is not on the page.
- Do not assume human moral/linguistic categories map exactly onto robot civilization.

### Consciousness / dimensional schools
- Uploading, body-swapping, reincarnation and transcendence in historical SF are not technical evidence.
- Current orbital civilization still lacks operational human consciousness-transfer technology.
- Few long-term practitioners remain a plausible knowledge bottleneck.
- School claims remain school claims until independently validated.
- Different lineages should disagree in methods/interpretation rather than collapse into one omniscient esoteric system.

### SMITH / mystery
Use bounded conceptual breakthrough:
`TRACE → SOURCE → MODEL → COUNTERMODEL → TEST → OPERATIONAL DECISION`.
The case may resolve while ultimate ontology remains UNKNOWN.

### Politics / institutions
A future high-tech society still needs visible routes for disagreement, appeal, compromise and revision. A perfect administrative algorithm is not a substitute for legitimate political process where collective interests conflict.

### Prose / line editing
Cambridge does not justify infodumps or short-sentence SF style. Route by function:
`STORY NEED → WORLD/INFORMATION FUNCTION → POV → MODE → PARAGRAPH → SENTENCE`.

### Reader effect
Never defend a problematic payoff with 'the theme was supposed to be X'. Audit what outcomes, competence, relationships and rewards the text actually celebrates.

## 8. MANUSCRIPT / DEVELOPMENT VALIDATION

### LESSON ZERO Book One
No new defect strong enough to reopen the development lock was established. Cambridge is therefore an additive diagnostic source only.

### Orbital Youth / future books
Current canon corrections are strengthened, not weakened:
- post-money orbital civilization;
- no routine human consciousness transfer;
- conscious robots as separate civilization;
- uneven consciousness/dimensional knowledge because trained practitioner population is small.

Cambridge specifically validates why these differences must alter **story conventions**, not merely worldbuilding nouns.

### Development test to add before prose
For each major speculative element answer:
1. What historical SF convention is the reader likely to infer?
2. Is that convention true in IVDIVO?
3. What character action shows the difference?
4. What second-order consequence follows?
5. Does the element change ONE MAIN STORY?

## 9. FINAL SYNTHESIS
The strongest lesson of the full volume is not a recipe for writing science fiction. It is the opposite: **SF is a changing conversation whose icons, politics, technologies and forms repeatedly mutate as society changes.**

Therefore the IVDIVO production law is:

`CURRENT CANON / SOCIETY → HUMAN & NONHUMAN NORMALITY → SPECIFIC DIFFERENCE → CAUSAL CONSEQUENCE → READER MODEL CHANGE → CHOICE`.

Do not write twentieth-/early-twenty-first-century Earth stories with orbital nouns. If money has disappeared, motivation architecture must change. If robots are conscious citizens, relationship architecture must change. If consciousness science is bottlenecked by rare practice, epistemology must change. If the technology is normal to characters, exposition must change.

**SF MODE IS NOT THE STORY. THE SPECULATIVE WORLD MUST CHANGE THE CAUSAL STORY.**
