## 3. SOURCE PASSPORT

**Primary utility:** causal story architecture, value/state progression, opposition, turning points, scene analysis, information distribution, climax/ending design, revision method.

**Strongest domains for IVDIVO:** structural diagnosis before prose; scene action/reaction; setup/payoff; subtext; opposition/countermodels; exposition routing; mystery/suspense/irony information modes; progressive complication by kind; backward ending design.

**Medium-specific bias:** film/screenwriting. McKee's numerical act heuristics, dialogue-length prescriptions, anti-narration instincts, screenplay formatting and market history must not be imported into novels/audio fiction as universal law.

## 4. FINAL CLAIM / MECHANISM SET

### MCK-01 — STRUCTURE BEFORE COSMETICS
`CAUSALITY / MOTIVE / INFORMATION / CHOICE → SCENE → POV → PROSE`.
Polished dialogue cannot repair a false turn, weak motive or redundant scene.

### MCK-02 — WORLD AS POSSIBILITY SPACE
Setting earns weight when its laws alter credible actions, relationships, access, consequences or interpretation.

### MCK-03 — SPECIFIC LIMITATION CREATES INVENTION
A deeply known subset of a vast world is often more generative than shallow total coverage.

### MCK-04 — RESEARCH ↔ INVENTION LOOP
`STORY QUESTION → RESEARCH / MEMORY / IMAGINATION → NEW OPTION → STORY CHOICE → NEW QUESTION`.
Research that changes nothing is reference accumulation, not development.

### MCK-05 — MULTIPLE SOLUTIONS BEFORE COMMITMENT
For major beats, generate alternatives and select for causal truth, character truth, world truth and freshness.

### MCK-06 — GENRE AS PROMISE FIELD
Genre conventions are reader expectations and constraints, not mandatory beat sheets.

### MCK-07 — CONVENTION ≠ CLICHÉ
Convention may be necessary; cliché is stale execution.

### MCK-08 — GENRE MUST EVOLVE WITH SOCIETY
Future YA/SF cannot inherit rent, wage, consumer-status or institutional assumptions merely because contemporary genre often uses them.

### MCK-09 — CHARACTER THROUGH CONSEQUENTIAL CHOICE
Character becomes legible when alternatives matter and a choice produces consequences.

### MCK-10 — STRUCTURE ↔ CHARACTER CO-DESIGN
Changing deep decision logic without changing event design is cosmetic character work.

### MCK-11 — IDEA / COUNTER-IDEA
Theme strengthens when an opposing model has competence, evidence, benefits and human advocates.

### MCK-12 — MEANING BY CONSEQUENCE
If theme disappears when speeches are removed, repair action.

### MCK-13 — EXPECTATION / RESULT GAP
`INTENTION → SUBJECTIVELY REASONABLE ACTION → WORLD RESPONSE → GAP → MODEL UPDATE → STRATEGY CHANGE`.
A gap that changes no model or behavior is decorative surprise.

### MCK-14 — NO GAP MONOCULTURE
Expected success can change trust, responsibility, intimacy, evidence or dependency. Not every beat must disappoint.

### MCK-15 — THREE PRESSURE LEVELS
Inner/body, intimate relationship, and extra-personal/system/environment pressures can rotate to avoid repetitive opposition.

### MCK-16 — REACTION SPECIFICITY
Familiar events become character-specific through interpretation, reaction and next choice.

### MCK-17 — INCITING FUNCTION, NOT SIZE
The inciting event creates a durable imbalance/responsibility/pursuit; it can be physically quiet.

### MCK-18 — MOMENT RIPE
Start the main engine as soon as enough context exists for it to matter; no exposition runway beyond functional need.

### MCK-19 — AUTHENTICITY THROUGH INTERNAL CONSISTENCY
SF credibility comes from coherent rules + behavioral truth + selective detail, not maximal explanation.

### MCK-20 — POLARIZED CAST
Major people need meaningfully different responses grounded in values/history/relationships, not artificial contrarianism.

### MCK-21 — MACRO PROGRESSION WITHOUT ACT DOGMA
A long story needs consequential progression and major state changes; it does not require McKee's fixed three-act percentages.

### MCK-22 — RELATIVE REVERSAL BUDGET
A major turn is major only relative to smaller changes. Constant major reversals cause escalation inflation and reader numbness.

### MCK-23 — SUBPLOT INTEGRATION
A subplot should complicate, delay, counterpoint, reveal or causally alter the central engine. Parallel coexistence is not enough.

### MCK-24 — SCENE AS IMMEDIATE ACTION SYSTEM
`SCENE OWNER → IMMEDIATE WANT → TACTIC → REACTION → TACTIC CHANGE → TURN / CHANGED STATE`.
Use as diagnosis, not prose template.

### MCK-25 — SETUP / PAYOFF CONTRACT
Later payoffs may recontextualize earlier material, but fair support must exist and later discovery should alter the reader/character model.

### MCK-26 — VALUE / STATE CHANGE
Scene change may be physical, relational, informational, ethical, emotional, positional, access-based or safety-based; do not reduce everything to positive/negative labels.

### MCK-27 — TEXT / SUBTEXT SEPARATION
What characters say and what they are doing through speech are different layers. Direct statements of underlying struggle are often weaker than tactical speech.

### MCK-28 — BEAT AS TACTIC SHIFT
A beat changes when behavior/tactic changes in response to resistance. Gerund-label analysis is useful in revision, not as visible formula.

### MCK-29 — RHYTHM / TEMPO / CONTRAST
Reader effect needs modulation. A climactic or suspense scene can slow down and become more detailed if the story has earned the pause.

### MCK-30 — PROGRESSION BY KIND, NOT ONLY VOLUME
Pressure can widen socially, deepen personally, alter meaning or change ethical structure. Bigger danger is only one form of progression.

### MCK-31 — TRANSITION HINGE
A scene transition is strongest when scenes share or oppose a character, image, action, word, idea, sound or causal consequence. Routine logistics should usually compress.

### MCK-32 — CRISIS AS BOUNDED CONSEQUENTIAL CHOICE
Useful kernel of McKee's dilemma: culmination should demand a decision with a real tradeoff. It need not always be two irreconcilable goods/evils.

### MCK-33 — CLIMAX FUNCTION, NOT LOUDNESS
Climax is the decisive causal/value resolution of the local story. The loudest event may occur earlier.

### MCK-34 — MEANING PRODUCES EMOTION
Prefer action whose implications make the emotional/thematic meaning legible without post-event explanation.

### MCK-35 — BACKWARD ENDING DESIGN
A strong ending can be designed backward so earlier conditions and clues make the result retrospectively supported without making it predictable.

### MCK-36 — RESOLUTION / AFTERMATH
After climax, close remaining local threads, show important spread of effects, and allow emotional deceleration before an optional saga hook.

### MCK-37 — QUALITATIVE ANTAGONISM
Do not merely repeat the same obstacle louder. Change the kind of problem, dependency, ethical conflict or system consequence.

### MCK-38 — DRAMATIZED EXPOSITION
`INFORMATION → WHO NEEDS IT NOW → WHY NOW → WHAT ACTION/RELATION/MODEL IT CHANGES`.
Characters should not tell each other facts they both already know merely for the reader.

### MCK-39 — INFORMATION MODES
Mystery / suspense / dramatic irony are differences in who knows what when. They can be mixed deliberately.

### MCK-40 — FALSE MYSTERY
Do not withhold information the POV or story contract implies the reader should have solely to manufacture surprise.

### MCK-41 — SURPRISE MUST RECONFIGURE
A useful surprise creates retrospective support and a changed model, not only a jolt.

### MCK-42 — COINCIDENCE FIREWALL
Coincidence may initiate/complicate; it should not solve the climax.

### MCK-43 — BIG EVENT ↔ MOTIVATION PROPORTIONALITY
Spectacle becomes melodramatic when causal/motivational preparation is too thin for the event.

### MCK-44 — NO READER-FORGETFULNESS PATCH
Do not conceal causal holes by hoping readers fail to notice.

### MCK-45 — POV AS INFORMATION / EMPATHY DESIGN
Switch POV when the new mind materially changes knowledge, irony, emotional cost or decision; not for equal airtime.

### MCK-46 — CHARACTERIZATION ≠ FULL PERSON
Observable traits are not enough. But McKee's "true character" doctrine is a useful pressure test, not proof every person hides an opposite self.

### MCK-47 — SUPPORTING PERSONHOOD FIREWALL
Reject McKee's protagonist-centric "solar system" as universal. In IVDIVO, supporting humans and conscious robots retain independent wants, relations and refusal rights.

### MCK-48 — DIALOGUE AS ACTION/REACTION
Dialogue should sound conversational while doing story work through tactics and subtext. Screen-specific brevity is not a prose law.

### MCK-49 — LONG SPEECH REACTION TEST
A long speech can work if the speaker is being changed by silent/visible reactions and the scene state evolves; otherwise it is often exposition or essay.

### MCK-50 — PROSE/AUDIO MEDIUM FIREWALL
McKee's short-sentence, visual-first and anti-narration advice belongs to film. Novels and audio may use narration, summary, long syntax and interiority when they improve reader effect.

### MCK-51 — DESCRIPTION AS SELECTION
Description should direct attention to consequential/expressive detail rather than inventory.

### MCK-52 — IMAGE SYSTEM AS DEVELOPING MOTIF
Repeated imagery is useful when varied and transformed. Obvious labeled symbolism weakens effect.

### MCK-53 — INSIDE-OUT DEVELOPMENT
`STORY DESIGN → SCENE/SEQUENCE → SUBTEXT → DIALOGUE / LINE`.
Do not fall in love with polished dialogue before proving the story works.

### MCK-54 — FAVORITE-SCENE HOSTAGE FAILURE
A loved scene must still be cut/rebuilt if it forces the story to bend around it.

### MCK-55 — STRUCTURAL PROTOTYPE BEFORE EXPENSIVE PROSE
A step-outline/scene map can expose a dead story more cheaply than 100 pages of polished draft. For IVDIVO, this is a diagnostic option, not mandatory cards/treatment ritual.

### MCK-56 — REWRITE UPSTREAM
When a scene fails for causal reasons, repair setups and payoffs, not just dialogue/behavior inside the scene.

## 5. FINAL FAILURE MODES / FIREWALLS

1. **CLASSICAL-FORM UNIVERSALISM** — three acts/classical form are McKee's school, not canon.
2. **PERCENTAGE LAW** — 25% and screenplay timings are heuristics.
3. **MARKET HISTORY AS LAW** — 1990s Hollywood advice is not current universal market truth.
4. **GENRE TAXONOMY REIFICATION** — category lists are tools, not ontology.
5. **DEEP-CHARACTER MYSTIFICATION** — complexity does not require a hidden opposite self.
6. **ARC UNIVERSALISM** — not every major person needs a profound transformation per book.
7. **RISK = VALUE TOTALIZATION** — trust, consent, relationship, role, responsibility, time and unique experience can carry major stakes.
8. **GAP MONOCULTURE** — not every action must fail or surprise.
9. **DARKNESS INFLATION** — "negation of the negation" must not force cruelty/trauma escalation.
10. **CLIMAX MAXIMALISM** — decisive ≠ loudest.
11. **DILEMMA UNIVERSALISM** — meaningful choice need not always be lesser evil vs irreconcilable good.
12. **CUT-EVERYTHING-NOT-CLIMAX** — novels/YA need social ecology, recovery, texture and independent lives as long as material changes reader/story state.
13. **SHOW-DON'T-TELL DOGMA** — prose summary/narration are legitimate; dramatize what requires exact presence.
14. **SCREEN DIALOGUE BREVITY AS NOVEL LAW** — reject.
15. **PROTAGONIST DIMENSIONALITY MONOPOLY** — reject for ensemble/personhood.
16. **SUPPORTING-CHARACTER SERVITUDE** — reject; especially invalid for conscious robots.
17. **SYMBOL SYSTEM OVERDESIGN** — visible authorial labeling turns motif into homework.
18. **PITCH-REACTION ABSOLUTISM** — a ten-minute pitch is a useful prototype, not proof of novel quality.
19. **90% MEDIOCRITY NUMEROLOGY** — create alternatives, but do not fetishize a percentage.
20. **DIALOGUE-FIRST TABOO AS ABSOLUTE** — sometimes exploratory dialogue discovers character; the gate is whether structural validation follows.
21. **DATED SOCIAL TAXONOMIES** — historical examples involving gender/sexuality/status are not current authority.
22. **WORK = WAGES REGRESSION** — orbital contribution cannot be re-capitalized through renamed credits.
23. **CONSCIOUSNESS-TRANSFER LEAK** — current Orbital Youth does not have ordinary operational human soul/consciousness transfer.
24. **MAINSTREAM DIMENSIONAL-SCIENCE LEAK** — specialist schools are not equivalent to mature public science.

## 6. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

- **McKee × Truby:** converge on causal architecture; Truby is stronger on action/information/revelation chains, McKee on scene turns, audience expectation and antagonism.
- **McKee × Rosenfeld:** Rosenfeld corrects McKee's pressure bias by protecting contemplation, support, partial success and varied scene outcomes.
- **McKee × Brody:** both useful for reader promises; both need protection against beat/percentage lock.
- **McKee × Card:** Card better protects independent personhood, multiple relationship selves and POV; McKee is stronger on pressure/choice and structure-character interlock.
- **McKee × Bell:** strong convergence on conflict/suspense, but Bell/McKee both risk stakes inflation; IVDIVO retains meaningful uncertainty and intelligent resistance, not mandatory death/darkness.
- **McKee × Zunshine:** McKee's mystery/suspense/irony becomes more precise when coupled to source-monitoring: fact ≠ character claim ≠ inference.
- **McKee × Herman/Oatley:** character action gains precision through goals/reasons/person-level models; predicted behavior must not be mistaken for consent or reason.
- **McKee × Le Guin/Prose/Clark:** structural priority does not justify monotone "cinematic" prose; sentence/paragraph rhythm follows reader effect.
- **McKee × Hodgins/Rosenfeld:** screen scene-minimalism must be widened for the novel's social setting, recovery, voice and lived texture.

## 7. IVDIVO PRODUCTION GATES

**MCK-G01 STRUCTURE BEFORE POLISH** — structural defect blocks line polish as "solution".

**MCK-G02 ONE MAIN STORY / SUBPLOT RETURN** — every substantial side line must alter central causality, theme under pressure, relationship state, evidence, or culminating choice.

**MCK-G03 WORLD RULE → CHOICE** — worldbuilding earns page weight through changed options/consequences.

**MCK-G04 POST-MONEY GENRE UPDATE** — no rent/wages/credits as default youth motivation.

**MCK-G05 CONSCIOUS ROBOT PERSONHOOD** — a named conscious robot must have independent wants, relations and refusal rights.

**MCK-G06 SPECIALIST-KNOWLEDGE BOUNDARY** — niche consciousness/dimensional schools remain niche; claims require lineage/practice/reproducibility/disagreement.

**MCK-G07 SCENE OWNER / INTENTION** — before dialogue polishing, identify whose immediate intention drives the scene.

**MCK-G08 TACTIC CHANGE** — repeated talk with no tactic/state change is compression or rewrite candidate.

**MCK-G09 INFORMATION STATE** — track what changed in facts, hypotheses, source confidence and interpretation.

**MCK-G10 FALSE-MYSTERY BAN** — never hide known POV information solely for twist.

**MCK-G11 SETUP/PAYOFF FAIRNESS** — later recontextualization must have retrospective support.

**MCK-G12 NO COINCIDENCE CLIMAX** — chance cannot solve the local core conflict.

**MCK-G13 QUALITATIVE PRESSURE** — escalate by changing kind/structure of problem, not merely danger volume.

**MCK-G14 COUNTERMODEL COMPETENCE** — opponents/institutions need real benefit/evidence and legitimate aims.

**MCK-G15 EARN THE PAUSE** — a decisive scene may slow down; syntax/paragraph length follows effect, not "suspense = short."

**MCK-G16 CLIMAX = DECISIVE LOCAL CHOICE** — loudest event may be earlier.

**MCK-G17 AFTERMATH BEFORE HOOK** — close local story, register cost/state change, then optional saga hook.

**MCK-G18 DIALOGUE EXACT-WORDS TEST** — direct dialogue earns space if wording changes power, consent, trust, evidence, relation, decision or emotion.

**MCK-G19 SUMMARY ROUTING** — routine procedure/logistics may be summarized without violating "show don't tell."

**MCK-G20 MOTIF DEVELOPMENT** — repeated image/object/phrase must evolve in meaning.

**MCK-G21 FAVORITE SCENE IS NOT CANON** — accepted scene can be cut if upstream causality proves it harmful.

**MCK-G22 UPSTREAM REWRITE** — fix setup/motive/choice before "better wording."

**MCK-G23 MULTI-POV VALUE TEST** — switch only for material information/irony/emotional/decision gain.

**MCK-G24 NO PROTAGONIST KNOWLEDGE MONOPOLY** — ensemble intelligence can be distributed.

**MCK-G25 BIG EVENT PREPARATION** — event scale must be causally and emotionally earned.

## 8. MANUSCRIPT / SAGA VALIDATION

### LESSON ZERO Book One
No McKee finding independently justifies breaking the existing development lock. New craft knowledge is not itself evidence of a manuscript defect. Reopen only for a concrete current defect or external reader/editor evidence.

### Orbital Youth / future IVDIVO
The strongest McKee contribution is not a three-act retrofit. It is:
`ONE CAUSAL ENGINE → DIFFERENT CHARACTER TACTICS → INFORMATION / RELATION / WORLD STATE CHANGES → QUALITATIVELY DIFFERENT PRESSURE → BOUNDED DECISIVE CHOICE → LOCAL CLOSURE`.

Current world firewalls remain:
- no ordinary money/rent/wages/credits in mature orbital cities;
- conscious robots are a separate civilization/persons, not owned AI tools;
- no ordinary operational human consciousness/soul transfer technology at the current Orbital Youth level;
- dimensional/ontological knowledge is uneven and specialist because trained practitioners are scarce.

## 9. FINAL SYNTHESIS

McKee is strongest as a **diagnostic system for causal turns, opposition, information control and scene action**, and weakest when film-industry heuristics are universalized into human/narrative law.

IVDIVO retains:
- structure before cosmetics;
- consequential choice;
- scene tactics;
- setup/payoff;
- intelligent countermodels;
- information-mode control;
- qualitative antagonism;
- decisive-but-not-necessarily-loud climax;
- upstream rewriting.

IVDIVO rejects:
- fixed percentages/act dogma;
- compulsory darkness/dilemmas;
- screen dialogue brevity as prose law;
- supporting-character servitude;
- anti-narration universalism;
- outdated social/market assumptions.

**Final production compression:**
`CAUSE → INTENTION → TACTIC → RESPONSE → INFORMATION/RELATION/STATE CHANGE → MODEL UPDATE → NEXT CHOICE`.

That mechanism is now callable in Writers' Room without importing McKee's screenwriting dogma.
