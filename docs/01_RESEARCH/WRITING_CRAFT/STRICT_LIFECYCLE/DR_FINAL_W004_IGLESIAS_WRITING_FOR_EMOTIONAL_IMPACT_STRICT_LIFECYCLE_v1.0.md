## SOURCE PASSPORT / FIREWALL
This is a 2005 screenwriting craft book rooted in Hollywood script-reader conditions of its period. It is useful for reader-effect design, scene tactics, dialogue, exposition and revision diagnostics. It is not current market authority, scientific psychology, or a universal theory of the novel.

Do not automatically transfer screenplay-specific prescriptions into IVDIVO prose, especially page-format/white-space advice, camera-conscious compression, mandatory short dialogue, screenplay parentheticals, vertical writing, screenplay page-count assumptions, fixed act percentages, or gatekeeper-first-page rhetoric.

## SYNTHESIZED CLAIMS

### W004-C01 — Structural correctness and reader effect are separate diagnostics
A story may be causally coherent yet emotionally flat. Track both what changes in the story and what changes in reader expectation, feeling, curiosity or understanding.

### W004-C02 — Character emotion is not reader emotion
A crying character does not make the reader sad by itself. Reader effect is produced by context, attachment, expectation, evidence, contrast and consequence.

### W004-C03 — Concept is a promise of experience
A premise is useful when it clearly promises a distinctive experience and the causal engine can actually deliver it.

### W004-C04 — Theme should be tested, not announced
Theme becomes credible through competing models, choices and consequences. A thematic speech is not proof.

### W004-C05 — Character connection has multiple routes
Empathy is only one route. Fascination, mystery, competence, relationships and moral/tactical uncertainty can also sustain reader investment.

### W004-C06 — Instant-empathy devices are high-risk
Victimization, injury, illness, rejection, children, animals, humiliation and sacrifice can become sentimental manipulation when inserted primarily to purchase sympathy.

### W004-C07 — Anticipation is future-modeling
Goals, plans, promises, warnings, appointments, known future events and dramatic irony make readers model what may happen next. Physical danger is not required.

### W004-C08 — Surprise must remain fair after the reveal
A reveal should revise the reader's model while preserving the truth of earlier evidence. Author cheating destroys trust.

### W004-C09 — Emotional intensity requires modulation
Pressure works through contrast with success, humor, intimacy, ordinary life, release and quiet processing. Permanent maximum intensity flattens effect.

### W004-C10 — A scene has both story function and reader-effect function
Purpose, location, time, participants, prior state, wants, actions, resistance, stakes, information and outcome may all matter, but not every scene requires every variable at maximum intensity.

### W004-C11 — Not every scene requires conflict
Iglesias explicitly acknowledges scenes without conflict. A scene can earn its place through revelation, intimacy, competence, discovery, emotional processing, setup/payoff, relationship change or other meaningful state change.

### W004-C12 — Scene polarity is a diagnostic, not a mandatory binary flip
A scene should usually leave some relevant state different, but the change may be action, knowledge, relationship, trust, responsibility, access, emotion or expectation rather than a simplistic positive/negative reversal.

### W004-C13 — Setup/payoff is broader than objects
A setup may be a skill, habit, relationship, rule, environment constraint, phrase, fact, tool, social expectation or piece of knowledge. A structural payoff changes action, inference, relationship or outcome.

### W004-C14 — Description must be routed by medium
Screenplay advice about vertical layout, white space, avoiding camera directions and line economy is medium-specific. Transferable prose principles are specificity, active perception, meaningful sensory selection, rhythm, compression and character/world filtering.

### W004-C15 — Specificity is stronger than generic intensity
Concrete nouns, precise actions and character-filtered details usually outperform generic emotional adjectives or intensifiers.

### W004-C16 — Motion on the page is not synonymous with short syntax
Verb choice, action progression, paragraphing, duration, focus and reader expectation all affect perceived movement. Novel suspense/action does not require screenplay-like fragments.

### W004-C17 — Visual symbolism is optional and must be earned
Metaphor, motif, color, weather and symbolic objects can deepen meaning, but repeated symbolic insistence becomes authorial overstatement.

### W004-C18 — Dialogue is action when wording functions as a tactic
Strong dialogue usually has a purpose for the speaker and changes the other participant's response, information, trust, consent, relationship, emotion or decision.

### W004-C19 — Character voices differ through more than slang
Rhythm, vocabulary, sophistication, omissions, agenda, emotional tempo, preferred imagery, social relation and conversational tactic matter more than catchphrases alone.

### W004-C20 — Direct and indirect dialogue are both legitimate
Routine, repeated or low-value speech can be compressed. Exact words deserve direct dramatization when wording itself matters.

### W004-C21 — Exposition needs dramatic purpose, not camouflage by force
Information is strongest when someone needs it, resists it, acts on it, misreads it or changes strategy because of it. Conflict is one route, not the only route.

### W004-C22 — Subtext is useful when directness has a real cost
Indirect speech works when characters have something to protect, test, negotiate or avoid. Subtext inserted everywhere becomes artificial evasiveness.

### W004-C23 — Direct speech can be the strongest choice
Iglesias explicitly allows on-the-nose dialogue when emotional safety makes directness natural, when long-delayed truth has been earned, or when simplicity itself is the dramatic point.

### W004-C24 — Scene context can carry meaning without ornamental dialogue
The same literal line can mean different things because of prior events, relationship history, physical action, silence and what the reader knows.

### W004-C25 — Revision should be pass-specific
The source's final chapter supports diagnosing what is broken, then making targeted passes for character, structure, causal plot, dialogue and polish rather than treating rewriting as random sentence tweaking.

## OPERATIONAL MECHANISMS / PRODUCTION GATES

### W004-M01 — READER-EFFECT DOUBLE ENTRY
For a major scene record:
`STORY STATE CHANGE` + `READER EFFECT / EXPECTATION CHANGE`.
Neither substitutes for the other.

### W004-M02 — CHARACTER / READER EMOTION FIREWALL
`CHARACTER FEELS X != READER FEELS X`.
Ask what causes the reader response rather than naming the desired emotion in prose.

### W004-M03 — EXPERIENCE PROMISE
`PROMISED EXPERIENCE → CAUSAL ENGINE → REQUIRED PAYOFFS → PROMISE VIOLATIONS`.

### W004-M04 — UNIQUE / LEGIBLE SPLIT
`DISTINCTIVE WORLD / CONFLICT / RELATIONSHIP + LEGIBLE PERSONAL STAKE`.
For Orbital Youth, legibility must not restore wages, rent, credits or AI-servant defaults.

### W004-M05 — THEME CONSEQUENCE TEST
`THEMATIC QUESTION → COMPETING MODELS → CHOICES → CONSEQUENCES → PROVISIONAL ANSWER`.

### W004-M06 — CHARACTER CONNECTION ROUTER
Choose deliberately among `EMPATHY / FASCINATION / MYSTERY / COMPETENCE / RELATIONSHIP / MORAL-TACTICAL UNCERTAINTY`.

### W004-M07 — EMPATHY MANIPULATION AUDIT
If trauma, injury, illness, rejection, child/animal imagery, sacrifice or humiliation exists mainly to buy reader care, require an independent causal function or remove it.

### W004-M08 — FUTURE-MODEL ANTICIPATION
`KNOWN GOAL / PLAN / PROMISE / WARNING → READER MODELS FUTURE → CURRENT SCENE GAINS HOPE / WORRY / CURIOSITY → PAYOFF OR REVISION`.

### W004-M09 — FAIR SURPRISE
`PRIOR TRUE INFORMATION → MODEL A → NEW EVIDENCE → MODEL B → RETROSPECTIVE SUPPORT`.

### W004-M10 — SCENE ACCEPTANCE
A scene must earn its space by changing at least one meaningful state: `ACTION / INFORMATION / RELATIONSHIP / TRUST / RESPONSIBILITY / ACCESS / EMOTION / READER MODEL`.
Conflict is optional; changed state is not.

### W004-M11 — SCENE EMOTIONAL PALETTE
Do not require a single emotion. Track dominant, secondary and contrasting effects, then test whether they arise from the scene rather than labels.

### W004-M12 — SETUP / PAYOFF LEDGER
Track `OBJECT / SKILL / RULE / RELATIONSHIP / HABIT / INFORMATION / ENVIRONMENT / LANGUAGE` setups and whether later use changes something meaningful.

### W004-M13 — DESCRIPTION DOUBLE-PAY
A conspicuous detail should ideally do at least two jobs among `ORIENTATION / POV / CHARACTER / PHYSICAL POSSIBILITY / RELATIONSHIP / CAUSALITY / QUESTION / RHYTHM / THEME`.

### W004-M14 — DESCRIPTION MEDIUM FIREWALL
Do not port screenplay vertical-writing or page-density rules into novels. For prose use `clarity → specificity → POV filtering → paragraph intent → rhythm`.

### W004-M15 — DIALOGUE TACTIC CHAIN
`WANT → VERBAL/NONVERBAL TACTIC → REACTION → TACTIC CHANGE → CHANGED STATE`.

### W004-M16 — DIALOGUE MODE ROUTER
Use DIRECT when exact wording changes evidence, consent, power, trust, relationship, decision or emotion. Use INDIRECT/SUMMARY for routine, repetition, logistics and less consequential wording.

### W004-M17 — VOICE RELATIONSHIP MATRIX
For each major person, compare speech/behavior with `FRIEND / FAMILY / INSTRUCTOR / RIVAL / INTIMATE / PUBLIC / CONSCIOUS ROBOT`. Distinction should arise from relation and agenda, not decorative slang.

### W004-M18 — EXPOSITION ACTION GATE
`WHO NEEDS THIS INFORMATION? → WHY NOW? → WHO CONTROLS IT? → WHAT CHANGES AFTER IT IS LEARNED?`
If nothing changes, compress or relocate.

### W004-M19 — SUBTEXT COST GATE
Use indirectness when direct speech creates a plausible social/emotional/strategic cost. Otherwise allow clear direct speech.

### W004-M20 — CONTEXT-AS-SUBTEXT
Before adding clever lines, test whether prior action, physical behavior, silence or known relationship history can carry the meaning more cleanly.

### W004-M21 — REVISION ROUTER
`STRUCTURAL / CAUSAL → SCENE → POV → DIALOGUE MODE → PARAGRAPH → SENTENCE → WORD`.
Do not use line polish as a substitute for structural repair.

### W004-M22 — READER-EFFECT VALIDATION
After structural passes, mark where a reader actually feels `CURIOSITY / ANTICIPATION / TENSION / RELIEF / HUMOR / INTIMACY / AWE / SADNESS / SATISFACTION / CONFUSION / BOREDOM`. Treat observed flatness as a diagnostic signal, not proof of one predetermined fix.

## FAILURE MODES
- Structure treated as sufficient for engagement.
- Character emotion substituted for reader emotion.
- `Every page must wow` converted into permanent micro-hooks.
- Fixed midpoint / darkest moment / point-of-no-return retrofit.
- Automatic ticking clock or worst-possible-event escalation.
- Sympathy purchased with arbitrary suffering.
- Genre formula mistaken for genre promise.
- Conflict forced into scenes that would be stronger as intimacy, competence, contemplation, discovery or payoff.
- Scene polarity reduced to mandatory positive/negative binary flips.
- Screenplay vertical-writing imported into novel paragraphing.
- Short-sentence density mistaken for motion or suspense.
- Adverb bans and passive-voice bans treated as universal prose laws.
- Symbolism over-signaled until it becomes commentary.
- Every character given a catchphrase instead of a distinct worldview/agenda/rhythm.
- Slang used as the main YA voice differentiator.
- Exposition chopped among several speakers without a real in-world reason.
- Conflict used merely to camouflage information.
- Mystery created by withholding facts the active POV already knows.
- Subtext everywhere, making normal direct relationships impossible.
- Direct speech rejected even when trust or earned climax makes it truthful.
- Dialogue tricks stacked until characters sound authored rather than alive.
- Supporting characters reduced to delivery systems for exposition or witty lines.
- Revision performed as random line tweaking before structure/causality is stable.

## CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON
- **LaPlante / Lodge / Ribó** correct Iglesias's strongest intensity bias: ordinary life, summary, contemplation and non-conflict scenes remain fully dramatic when meaningful states change.
- **McKee / Truby / Brody** overlap on desire, obstacles, turning points and payoff; IVDIVO keeps functions while rejecting station/percentage retrofit.
- **McKee Dialogue / Corbett / Card** support dialogue-as-action and relation-specific voice; they strengthen the move away from catchphrase/slang differentiation.
- **Zunshine / Herman** impose evidence/provenance discipline on Iglesias-style curiosity and surprise: reader manipulation cannot convert interpretation into fact.
- **Vorhaus / Lodge** reinforce modulation, comic contrast and permission for relief rather than permanent maximum intensity.
- **Wolf / Cambridge SF** reinforce the post-money and conscious-robot firewalls: emotional legibility does not require contemporary Earth economics or servant-AI defaults.

## IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION

### LESSON ZERO
No craft source alone reopens the development lock. Iglesias adds a reader-effect validation layer only after current-version structural correctness is verified. If a scene is flat, first diagnose causal/scene/POV/mode problems before touching sentence cadence.

### ORBITAL YOUTH / FUTURE BOOKS
- Familiar emotional stakes: trust, belonging, autonomy, consent, responsibility, mastery, reputation, love, unique opportunity and time — not wages/rent/credits.
- Conscious robots: no petlike sympathy engineering, universal AI voice or service-default characterization. Require independent desire, relationships, refusal rights and consequences.
- Youth voice: distinguish attitude, agenda, rhythm, omissions and social assumptions before slang.
- Technology description: detail must affect human/nonhuman choice or inference; otherwise compress.
- Mystery: anticipation and surprise must respect evidence provenance and POV knowledge.
- Consciousness schools: dramatic claims remain separated into observation, reproducible effect, school interpretation, competing interpretation and possible ontology.

## FINAL SYNTHESIS
The transferable value of Iglesias is not `make everything more intense`. It is to track the reader's experience as a separate craft variable while preserving causal truth, personhood, modulation and medium-appropriate prose.
