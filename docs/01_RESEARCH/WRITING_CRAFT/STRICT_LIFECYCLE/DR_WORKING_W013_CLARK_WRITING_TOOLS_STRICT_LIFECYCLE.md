# 3. PROVISIONAL CLAIMS / MECHANISMS
## W013-M01 — Tools, not rules
Clark explicitly frames the book in cause/effect rather than right/wrong terms and warns against applying too many tools simultaneously while drafting.

IVDIVO transform:
`GENERATE / DISCOVER → STRUCTURAL DIAGNOSIS → TARGETED TOOL PASS → VALIDATE EFFECT`.
This reinforces Lamott/LaPlante and protects the 100-specialist system from generation paralysis.

## W013-M02 — Early subject/verb can reduce processing load
Default clarity pattern:
`ACTOR/SUBJECT → ACTION/VERB → BRANCHING DETAIL`.
But delaying the main clause can be useful for suspense, discovery or deliberate rhythm.

Gate: **clarity default, not syntax monoculture**.

## W013-M03 — Sentence and paragraph edges carry emphasis
Beginning/end positions often receive extra reader attention.

IVDIVO use:
- place decisive/revelatory word where sentence/paragraph architecture supports it;
- do not mechanically end every paragraph on a punch word.

## W013-M04 — Active verbs reveal agency; passive voice redistributes attention
Active voice can clarify who acts. Passive voice can deliberately foreground the receiver or suppress/withhold the actor.

Institutional IVDIVO gate:
When prose says `was allocated / was authorized / was denied`, ask whether missing agency is intentional or bureaucratic concealment.

## W013-M05 — Modifier test
An adverb earns space when it changes/reframes the verb rather than redundantly intensifying a meaning already present.

`MODIFIER → NEW INFORMATION/CONTRADICTION?` If no, candidate for cut.
No universal adverb ban.

## W013-M06 — Progressive/-ing forms are aspect tools
Strategic progressive forms can create ongoing/immediate action; repetitive -ing syntax can blur distinctions and rhythm.
No suffix purge.

## W013-M07 — Long sentences are legitimate structural tools
Long sentences can carry duration, accumulation, chronology, journey, catalog, pressure and layered perception.
Useful conditions include a stable grammatical spine, meaningful order and deliberate variation with shorter forms.

Critical IVDIVO consequence:
**long sentence ≠ weak sentence; suspense/action ≠ short-sentence density.**

## W013-M08 — Pattern creates expectation; variation creates emphasis
`PATTERN → RECOGNITION → STRATEGIC BREAK → EMPHASIS/ENERGY`.
Applies to syntax, dialogue rhythms, recurring images, scene structures and series motifs.

## W013-M09 — Punctuation controls grouping and rhythm
Punctuation choices influence pace, grouping, interruption and emphasis. Effects remain context-dependent; punctuation is not a mechanical speedometer.

## W013-M10 — Cut big before small
Clark's strongest revision mechanism so far:
`FOCUS / FUNCTION → BLOCK CUT / SCENE CUT → PARAGRAPH CUT → SENTENCE CUT → WORD CUT`.
Brevity comes first from **selection**, then compression.

This maps directly to the IVDIVO anti-cosmetic-repair rule.

# 4. EARLY FAILURE MODES
1. **Tools-All-at-Once Paralysis** — applying dozens of gates during sentence generation.
2. **Subject-Verb Monoculture** — every sentence begins identically.
3. **Edge-Emphasis Monoculture** — every ending engineered as a punch line.
4. **Active-Voice Moralism** — passive treated as automatically weak.
5. **Agency Erasure by Accident** — passive bureaucracy hides who decided/acted.
6. **Adverb Purge** — deleting useful contrast/modification.
7. **-ing Purge** — confusing overuse with grammatical prohibition.
8. **Long-Sentence Fear** — producing chopped prose to look clear/fast.
9. **Pattern Without Variation** — rhythmic monotony.
10. **Punctuation Metronome** — assuming a mark has one fixed pacing effect.
11. **Line Cut Before Structural Cut** — polishing/removing words while unnecessary scenes/blocks remain.

# 6. EARLY IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION
## Anti-"обрубки"
Updated line-stage router:
`STRUCTURAL NECESSITY → MODE → PARAGRAPH INTENT → READER-STATE CONTINUITY → SENTENCE SPINE → LENGTH/RHYTHM VARIATION → PUNCTUATION → WORD/MODIFIER`.

Do not repair fragmentary prose by simply joining everything; restore a coherent grammatical/rhythmic unit first, then preserve intentional short blows.

## Institutional language
Passive constructions are audited for hidden actors:
`WHO DECIDED? → WHO ACTED? → WHO RECEIVES CONSEQUENCE? → IS AGENT OMISSION INTENTIONAL?`.
Useful in post-money allocation/access/qualification systems so bureaucratic language does not hide power.

## Orbital Youth voice
Youth voice must not equal permanent short sentences. Sentence length can expand during observation, fascination, embarrassment, technical concentration or emotional accumulation and contract at decision/reversal.

## Conscious robots
No grammatical rule defines personhood. Robot POV/voice may use long, short, active or passive structures according to personality, relation and purpose rather than generic machine syntax.
