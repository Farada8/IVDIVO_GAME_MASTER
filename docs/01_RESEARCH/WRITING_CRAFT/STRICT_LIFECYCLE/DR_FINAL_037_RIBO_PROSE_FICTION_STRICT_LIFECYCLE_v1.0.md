## 3. CLAIM EXTRACTION

### R37-C01 — Narrative is meaningful sequencing, not raw occurrence
A useful diagnostic definition is a semiotic representation of events connected by time/cause and interpreted as meaningful. For production this means that mere chronology is insufficient unless selection/arrangement makes relationships legible.

### R37-C02 — Story and discourse are distinct diagnostic levels
`STORY` concerns events, environments and characters; `DISCOURSE` concerns how they are narrated, styled and themed. Many revision errors come from trying to repair a story problem at discourse/line level.

### R37-C03 — Genre is dynamic convention, not eternal form
Genres classify, guide expectation and evaluation, but their rules evolve culturally. Use genre expectations as contracts/diagnostics, never immutable laws.

### R37-C04 — Novel breadth is not permission for diffusion
Long fiction can support more characters, environments, subplots and commentary than short fiction, but these still need an organising relation to the whole.

### R37-C05 — Plot is an arrangement of story events
Emplotment can alter **order, duration, frequency, connection and relevance**. These five variables are a powerful revision matrix beyond beat-sheet thinking.

### R37-C06 — Conflict is common but not the only organising principle
Ribó explicitly records alternatives such as `story as birth/growth` and `story as illumination`, citing Le Guin's critique of the gladiatorial model. This is an important anti-formula counterweight.

### R37-C07 — Closure and resolution are separable
A story can end coherently while leaving conflicts/questions unresolved. IVDIVO still requires local book-level closure, but closure need not mean every uncertainty disappears.

### R37-C08 — Suspense depends on an information/anticipation gap
Suspense is reader anticipation built from what is known/suspected; surprise depends on ignorance or violated expectation. They are separate tools and can work together.

### R37-C09 — Setting is a meaningful arrangement, not inert backdrop
Space/time/character are entangled. Environment can function topographically, atmospherically, psychologically or symbolically, and should be diagnosed by what it changes in experience/action.

### R37-C10 — Description can be narrative without advancing plot
Description builds storyworld and reader imagination. It need not be defended solely by plot function. Significant detail may connect to character/plot, while some apparently useless detail can create verisimilitude.

### R37-C11 — Chekhov's gun is not a universal object law
A detail may function as world texture/verisimilitude rather than future payoff. The right test is reader attention/prominence, not “every noun must fire later.”

### R37-C12 — Character has both storyworld personhood and discourse function
At discourse level characters serve narrative functions; at storyworld level they may be modelled as potentially complete persons. This distinction is crucial for conscious robots/nonhumans: function must not erase personhood.

### R37-C13 — Individuation is culturally conditioned
Modern Western fiction strongly privileges the autonomous individual. Flat/round, type/archetype and individuation models are useful, but not universal metaphysical truths about personhood or all cultures.

### R37-C14 — Main/secondary importance is discourse allocation, not ontological worth
A story can give one character far more narrative relevance without implying the other inhabitants of the storyworld exist only for that protagonist.

### R37-C15 — Characterisation can be compressed or enacted
Indirect characterisation can convey much quickly; direct methods let readers infer from speech, thoughts, effects, actions and looks. Neither mode should be absolutized.

### R37-C16 — Literary dialogue is stylized speech, not transcript
Real conversation contains fragments/repetition/noise. Fictional dialogue selects and shapes enough irregularity to create the impression of life without reproducing all of it.

### R37-C17 — Polyphony preserves distinct consciousnesses
Strong multi-character fiction can place multiple perspectives/ideologies in relation without merging them into one authorial voice.

### R37-C18 — Narrator, focalisation and knowledge are different variables
`WHO SPEAKS?` is not the same as `WHO PERCEIVES?` or `HOW MUCH IS KNOWN?`. This is a high-value POV diagnostic.

### R37-C19 — Person/tense does not determine intimacy by itself
First/third person, internal/external narrator, focalisation and knowledge configuration combine in different ways. Mechanical pronoun changes do not fix POV.

### R37-C20 — Scene and summary are both legitimate narrative methods
Showing is not inherently superior. Summary compresses duration; scene expands enough detail to simulate unfolding action. Mode should follow function.

### R37-C21 — Commentary can be explicit or structurally ironic
Narrators may interpret/judge/generalise/reflect; implicit commentary can arise through ironic or unreliable narration. Authorial meaning must not be confused with narrator statement.

### R37-C22 — Style is a set of linguistic choices, not a mystical property
Rhythm, syntax, lexical choice, paragraphing, phonology and figurative language all participate. Foregrounding is meaningful only relative to reader/context and can become distracting mannerism.

### R37-C23 — Language carries context as well as information
Because prose lacks a shared physical storyworld, wording must help readers reconstruct context. Sentence rhythm and significant sensory detail are therefore functional, not ornament alone.

### R37-C24 — Figurative language is ordinary language intensified, not a separate substance
Metaphor and related figures exist in everyday speech. Originality is not automatically quality; clichés and far-fetched conceits fail for opposite reasons.

### R37-C25 — Symbol is not theme
A motif/symbol is an existent carrying associations; theme is interpreted meaning at discourse level. Repetition does not automatically create thematic depth.

### R37-C26 — Translation/adaptation are interpretations
Moving a story across language or medium changes discourse even when story events are preserved. Audio adaptation therefore requires function-preserving transformation, not literal transfer.

### R37-C27 — Theme is interpretation, not synopsis
Plot summary answers what happened; theme answers what an interpreter thinks it means. Themes can be multiple and contested.

### R37-C28 — Ideology can be concealed, committed, critical or ambiguous
Every narrative carries assumptions. The most dangerous ideology may be the one treated as neutral/common sense.

### R37-C29 — Narrative identity can humanize or stereotype
Fiction can widen identification with an out-group or reinforce reductive types depending on causal agency, viewpoint and detail.

### R37-C30 — Moral complexity need not end in a thesis
Modern fiction can present competing moral alternatives without a single didactic lesson. Theme should not be downloaded into a final speech.

## 4. MECHANISM EXTRACTION

### M37-01 — STORY / DISCOURSE ROUTER
Before revision classify the defect:
`STORY: event / environment / character causality` or `DISCOURSE: narration / language / theme delivery`.
Do not line-edit a broken story engine.

### M37-02 — EMPLOTMENT FIVE-VARIABLE MATRIX
For any weak chapter/sequence test:
1. order;
2. duration;
3. frequency;
4. connection;
5. relevance/withholding.

### M37-03 — EVENT STATE TEST
`EVENT = meaningful change of state`.
If a scene contains activity but no changed action/information/relationship/emotional/social state, test for compression/merge.

### M37-04 — NON-CONFLICT CAUSALITY
Allow progression by:
`GROWTH / DISCOVERY / ILLUMINATION / RELATIONSHIP SHIFT / ACCUMULATION / PRACTICE` provided state and future choice change.

### M37-05 — SUSPENSE / SURPRISE SPLIT
`SUSPENSE = reader anticipation under uncertainty`.
`SURPRISE = expectation violation`.
Do not manufacture surprise by cheating POV/information.

### M37-06 — SETTING CAUSALITY / ATMOSPHERE
`ENVIRONMENT → PERCEPTION/OPTION/CONSTRAINT → ACTION/EMOTION → CONSEQUENCE`.
Setting may also earn space through mental/social atmosphere, not only physical obstacle.

### M37-07 — DETAIL ROLE TAG
Tag prominent details as:
`CAUSAL / CHARACTER / ATMOSPHERE / SYMBOL / VERISIMILITUDE / REDUNDANT`.
A non-payoff detail is not automatically redundant.

### M37-08 — CHARACTER PERSONHOOD / FUNCTION SPLIT
`STORYWORLD PERSON` and `BOOK FUNCTION` are separate columns.
A supporting character can have limited page-time but independent life/agency.

### M37-09 — DIALOGUE STYLISATION
`REAL-LIFE RAWNESS → SELECT CHARACTER-SPECIFIC IRREGULARITY → REMOVE NONFUNCTIONAL NOISE → PRESERVE TACTIC/SUBTEXT`.

### M37-10 — POV THREE-QUESTION GATE
1. Who speaks?
2. Who perceives/focalises?
3. What can the narration know?
Any contradiction requires an in-world/discourse reason.

### M37-11 — SHOW/TELL MODE ROUTER
`DIRECT SCENE` for precise wording/action that changes state.
`SUMMARY/INDIRECT` for duration, repetition, logistics, less-important conversations and transitions.

### M37-12 — POLYPHONY GATE
For ensemble scenes, each relevant person requires a distinct world-model/goal/tactic—not just different vocabulary.

### M37-13 — STYLE COMPONENT AUDIT
`RHYTHM + SYNTAX + LEXICON + PARAGRAPH + FIGURATION + SOUND`.
Repair the component causing the reader effect rather than globally “making prose more literary.”

### M37-14 — SYMBOL / THEME SEPARATION
`RECURRING EXISTENT → CHANGED ASSOCIATION → INTERPRETIVE PATTERN → POSSIBLE THEME`.
Do not explain the symbol after it works.

### M37-15 — IDEOLOGY REWARD AUDIT
Compare stated theme with:
`WHO ACTS / WHO WINS / WHO IS GLAMORIZED / WHO PAYS / WHO GETS POV / WHO MAY REFUSE`.

## 6. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

### Ribó + Chatman
Strong convergence on `story/discourse` as a foundational diagnostic distinction. Ribó is more concise and production-friendly; Chatman is deeper on structural theory.

### Ribó + Truby/McKee
Truby/McKee are stronger for active causal design. Ribó provides a neutral diagnostic map and an explicit counterweight against treating conflict/Freytag as the only valid architecture.

### Ribó + LaPlante/Lodge
Strong convergence on anti-absolutism: show/tell, description, style and form are contextual tools. This reinforces IVDIVO's mode-routing before line edit.

### Ribó + Rosenfeld
Rosenfeld is stronger on scene craft; Ribó clarifies that scene is a duration/representation choice, not the only respectable narrative mode.

### Ribó + Card/McKee Character
Card/McKee are stronger at applied character construction. Ribó adds the crucial distinction between discourse function and storyworld personhood, useful for robots/nonhumans/supporting cast.

### Ribó + Zunshine
Ribó separates narrator/focalisation/knowledge; Zunshine adds source-monitoring and layered beliefs. Combined they provide a robust epistemic POV system.

### Ribó + Cambridge SF
Cambridge explains SF historical assumptions; Ribó supplies the neutral narrative variables needed to test whether speculative world differences actually alter setting, character, plot or discourse.

## 7. IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION

### Structural revision router
Before changing prose, classify:
`PLOT / SETTING / CHARACTERISATION / NARRATION / LANGUAGE / THEME`.
Then select the appropriate source/gate. This reduces cosmetic repair.

### ONE MAIN STORY
A novel may use many environments, characters and subplots. The test is not minimalism; it is whether their state changes feed a coherent causal/meaningful whole.

### Orbital Youth
- orbital setting must be meaningful arrangement, not neutral stage;
- post-money normality can be shown atmospherically through assumptions/social behaviour as well as explicit plot mechanics;
- not every environmental detail needs a future payoff, but prominent detail must justify reader attention;
- conflict is not mandatory in every quiet youth scene if growth/recognition/relationship state changes future action.

### Conscious robots
Ribó's human-like default is a limitation of the textbook, not IVDIVO ontology. Use its **agency** definition while rejecting the assumption that nonhuman characters should be human-like by default.
For every robot separate:
`BOOK FUNCTION` from `STORYWORLD PERSONHOOD`.

### Consciousness / dimensional schools
POV epistemic discipline:
`WHO SPEAKS? → WHO PERCEIVES? → WHAT CAN THEY KNOW? → WHAT IS INTERPRETATION? → WHAT IS INDEPENDENTLY CONFIRMED?`
Do not let school commentary become world fact.

### Dialogue
Fictional speech should feel alive without reproducing every hesitation/repetition. Use relationship-specific tactics; summary recurring conversations that do not require exact wording.

### Choppy prose / line editing
Repair order:
`STORY/DISCOURSE DIAGNOSIS → SCENE NECESSITY → DURATION/FREQUENCY → SHOW/TELL → FOCALISATION → PARAGRAPH INTENT → SYNTAX/RHYTHM → WORD`.

### Theme
Theme is not a synopsis, motif or final lesson. Audit meaning through consequences, reader alignment and ideological reward structure.

## 8. MANUSCRIPT VALIDATION

### LESSON ZERO Book One
No new defect was established that justifies reopening development lock. Ribó strengthens existing diagnostics rather than requiring retrofit.

### Future books
Add a pre-line-edit diagnostic sheet with six columns:
`PLOT | SETTING | CHARACTERISATION | NARRATION | LANGUAGE | THEME`.
Every reported weakness must first be routed to one column (or an explicit interaction) before repair.

### Specific IVDIVO safeguards strengthened
- post-money world differences can be expressed through atmosphere/normality, not exposition;
- conscious robot narrative function must not erase independent agency;
- schools/practitioners require focalisation/knowledge discipline;
- quiet scenes may be causal through recognition/relationship/ideological shifts;
- description and “useless” detail may create verisimilitude, so anti-clutter edits must consider reader-world reality rather than delete everything without payoff.

## 9. FINAL SYNTHESIS
Ribó is most valuable as a **clean diagnostic operating vocabulary** that prevents the Writers' Room from collapsing distinct problems into one another.

Primary IVDIVO rule from the source:

`FIRST IDENTIFY THE NARRATIVE LAYER → THEN CHOOSE THE REPAIR TOOL.`

A broken causal chain is not a style problem. A focalisation leak is not a character problem. A slow paragraph is not necessarily a scene problem. A descriptive detail is not automatically useless because it does not fire later. A quiet scene is not automatically inert because nobody fights.

**DIAGNOSE THE LEVEL BEFORE EDITING THE TEXT.**
