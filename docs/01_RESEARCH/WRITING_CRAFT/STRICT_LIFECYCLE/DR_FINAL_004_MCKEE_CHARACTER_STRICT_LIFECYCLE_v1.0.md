## 1. SOURCE PASSPORT

### Best use
Character causality, differentiation, cast architecture, relationship-specific behavior, desire and tactics, character/world interaction, reader identification, long-form character management.

### Weak use / caution
McKee frequently presents philosophical, psychoanalytic, evolutionary, neurological and sociological claims as settled fact. Those claims are not scientific authority and are not IVDIVO ontology. His models of `core self`, `hidden self`, subconscious counterdesire, completion of humanity, archetypes, and radical character are craft heuristics only.

### Medium transfer warning
A large part of the book is written for stage/screen and contemporary Earth institutions. Screen economy, job/status examples, money examples, underdog assumptions and Hollywood genre norms must not be back-projected into post-money orbital society.

## 3. HIGH-VALUE CLAIMS AFTER FILTERING

### C01 — Role is not character
A role says what someone does in the story/system. Character emerges from how this particular person wants, interprets, acts, relates, refuses, adapts and pays.

### C02 — Plot and character are reciprocally causal
External events pressure the character; character choices change events. `Plot-driven` and `character-driven` are dominance descriptions, not quality rankings.

### C03 — Character evidence is behavioral
Self-description, reputation and biography are hypotheses. Repeated choices in varied contexts provide stronger evidence.

### C04 — Relationship-specific self is a major differentiator
The same person should not speak, gesture, reveal, bargain or defend themselves identically with a friend, parent, instructor, rival, lover, institution, public audience or conscious robot.

### C05 — Desire and motivation are different tools
A concrete object of desire supplies a trackable external aim. Motives explain why that aim matters. IVDIVO does not require a hidden subconscious counterdesire.

### C06 — Character complexity is patterned tension
Contradiction is useful when it recurs coherently across contexts and changes decisions. Random inconsistency is not depth.

### C07 — World pressure differentiates character
The same rule/event should be appraised differently by different people. `EXTERNAL CONDITION → PERSON-SPECIFIC APPRAISAL → TACTIC → CONSEQUENCE`.

### C08 — Modus operandi is more useful than static trait lists
A strong character has a recognizable method for pursuing goals and avoiding feared outcomes. Under pressure, tactics should evolve without dissolving identity.

### C09 — Cast design is relational architecture
Characters clarify one another through differences in values, methods, access, power, knowledge, loyalties and risk tolerance. Cast size should be governed by story function and available narrative attention.

### C10 — Reader interest combines curiosity and concern
A character becomes compelling when the reader wants both to understand them and to know what happens to them. Likeability is not required.

### C11 — Long form needs renewable character information
A multi-book saga cannot survive by repeating the same wound, joke, flaw or argument. New situations must reveal new facets, choices, relationships or strategy changes.

### C12 — First impressions create a contract
A character entrance creates expectations. Later material may deepen or revise them, but should not make the entrance retrospectively arbitrary.

## 4. TRANSFERABLE MECHANISMS

### M49-01 — CHARACTER MO ENGINE
`CURRENT WANT → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC MO → TACTIC → RESPONSE → TACTIC CHANGE → CONSEQUENCE`

Use this before adding biography. If two major characters would use the same tactic in the same situation, differentiation is weak.

### M49-02 — SELF-STORY TEST
`WHAT CHARACTER SAYS THEY ARE → WHAT THEY EXPECT → WHAT THEY DO → WHAT OTHERS EXPERIENCE → DISCREPANCY`

Do not automatically interpret discrepancy as deceit or pathology. It can be ignorance, context, growth, social role, incomplete information or ordinary inconsistency.

### M49-03 — RELATIONSHIP-SPECIFIC SELF MATRIX
For each major character, test behavior with:
- intimate equal;
- family/attachment figure;
- authority/instructor;
- subordinate/dependent;
- rival;
- public audience;
- conscious nonhuman/robotic person.

Differences should arise from relationship and stakes, not cosmetic slang.

### M49-04 — CHOICE EVIDENCE LADDER
`LOW PRESSURE CHOICE → REPEATED CHOICE → COSTLY CHOICE → CONFLICTING-GOOD CHOICE → AFTERMATH`

No requirement for one maximum-pressure crisis to reveal a metaphysical “true self.” Character inference should remain revisable.

### M49-05 — VALUE-SHADOW PAIR
A strength can produce a cost under different conditions:
- responsibility → reliability / overcontrol;
- autonomy → initiative / refusal to coordinate;
- care → support / paternalism;
- competence → usefulness / overconfidence;
- transparency → accountability / coercive exposure.

This is a diagnostic, not a mandatory flaw generator.

### M49-06 — CAST COUNTERPOINT
`SAME PROBLEM → DIFFERENT MODEL → DIFFERENT TACTIC → COLLISION → RELATIONSHIP CHANGE`

Use this instead of giving each character an isolated subplot.

### M49-07 — CHARACTER ATTENTION BUDGET
Assign per book:
`PRIMARY MAJOR / SECONDARY MAJOR / SUPPORTING MAJOR / MINOR / WALK-ON`.

Saga-level equality does not imply equal POV, trauma, secrets, jokes or climax functions in each book.

### M49-08 — INDEPENDENT LIFE TEST
For any named supporting person, especially a conscious robot/nonhuman:
`What do they want when protagonist is absent? Who else matters to them? What can they refuse? What costs them something?`

If the answer is nothing, they are still a story function, not a person.

### M49-09 — GENRE EXPECTATION / CHARACTER FRESHNESS
Genre conventions are reader contracts, not templates. Keep the useful function; transform contemporary Earth assumptions that do not belong in IVDIVO.

### M49-10 — LONGFORM RENEWAL
Before reusing a character in a later book, require at least one new:
- relationship pressure;
- responsibility;
- social context;
- method conflict;
- identity question;
- domain of competence;
- consequence from prior choices.

Do not reset the character to replay the earlier arc.

## 5. FAILURE MODES / REJECTIONS

### F49-01 — TRUE-SELF ESSENTIALISM
McKee's `true/core/hidden self` is not IVDIVO ontology. A person is not reduced to one final essence supposedly revealed by one crisis.

### F49-02 — MAXIMUM-PRESSURE MONOCULTURE
More pressure is not automatically more truth. Small choices can reveal deep values, especially in youth, intimacy, consent, practice and responsibility.

### F49-03 — SUFFERING AS DEPTH TAX
Reject the claim that depth requires extensive suffering or old age. A young person can have depth through responsibility, practice, observation, relationship history, moral attention, skill, grief, care, curiosity or sustained discipline.

### F49-04 — CONTRADICTION OVERLOAD
Adding opposite adjectives until a character appears “complex” produces written complexity. Contradictions must have causal consequences.

### F49-05 — SUBCONSCIOUS-FLAW REQUIREMENT
Not every protagonist needs a hidden destructive desire, trauma, secret sin or unconscious self-sabotage.

### F49-06 — COMPLETE-HUMANITY CLIMAX
A novel does not need to exhaust everything knowable or feelable about a character. Saga characters should remain alive beyond one book. Local arc closure is enough.

### F49-07 — UNDERDOG REQUIREMENT
Empathy does not require making every protagonist an underdog against overwhelming forces.

### F49-08 — SUPPORTING PERSON AS PROP
McKee's stock/service economy is useful for narrative attention but cannot be used to deny personhood to conscious robots, nonhumans or named supporting humans.

### F49-09 — ARCHETYPE AS PSYCHOLOGICAL FACT
Archetypes/symbolism are craft traditions, not evidence for universal psychology or metaphysical reality.

### F49-10 — GENRE MORAL BINARIES AS LAW
Hero/Villain/Victim and fixed positive/negative binaries are optional conventions, not IVDIVO requirements.

### F49-11 — EMPATHY = CENTER OF GOOD
Readers can identify through goal adoption, vulnerability, curiosity, intimacy, competence, contradiction or viewpoint—not only a moral “center of good.”

### F49-12 — SCREEN ECONOMY IMPORTED INTO PROSE
Do not use screen constraints to justify thin interiority, stock-like supporting roles or short dialogue in novels.

### F49-13 — MODERN ECONOMY CONTAMINATION
Jobs, money, bills, rent and ownership status in McKee examples cannot be imported into mature orbital cities as ordinary motivation.

### F49-14 — PSYCHOLOGY AS CERTAINTY
Claims about PTSD, subconscious drives, suicide, mental disorder, empathy genetics, sexuality and developmental psychology require real scientific sources if used as factual claims.

## 6. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

### With Orson Scott Card — *Characters & Viewpoint*
Convergence: action/motive, relationship-specific behavior, cast hierarchy, reader alignment.
Difference: Card is more flexible about identity and POV; McKee pushes harder toward essential core/hidden self. IVDIVO keeps Card's evidential caution and McKee's relational/cast tools.

### With Nancy Kress — *Characters, Emotion & Viewpoint*
Convergence: motivation must be legible, frustration produces character-specific response, POV distance matters.
Difference: Kress is more practical and less ontologically committed. Kress outranks McKee when McKee's psychoanalytic claims become deterministic.

### With Truby — *The Anatomy of Story*
Convergence: character web, opposition, desire, world/story integration.
Difference: Truby emphasizes moral/structural web; McKee emphasizes layers, dimensions and cast maps. IVDIVO combines them as `CAUSAL WEB + RELATIONSHIP-SPECIFIC SELF` without mandatory moral weakness.

### With McKee — *Story* and *Dialogue*
Character supplies the actor model; Story supplies causal architecture; Dialogue supplies verbal tactics. Production order remains:
`CAUSAL STORY → CHARACTER WANT/MO → SCENE TACTIC → DIALOGUE/SUBTEXT → LINE EDIT`.

### With Bell — *Conflict & Suspense*
Convergence: meaningful resistance makes character legible. IVDIVO rejects both authors' tendency toward relentless escalating pressure; success and ordinary life can also reveal character if state changes.

## 7. IVDIVO PRODUCTION GATES — BATCH 49

**B49-G01 ROLE ≠ PERSON** — function in the plot is never enough to define a named character.

**B49-G02 MO BEFORE BIOGRAPHY** — before adding backstory, identify how the character typically pursues what they want.

**B49-G03 RELATIONSHIP REGISTER** — same voice cannot remain unchanged across friend/family/authority/rival/intimate/robot contexts.

**B49-G04 CHOICE AS EVIDENCE** — infer character from repeated consequential choices, not author labels.

**B49-G05 NO TRUE-SELF CERTAINTY** — one crisis does not reveal an unquestionable final essence.

**B49-G06 NO SUFFERING DEPTH TAX** — youth depth does not require trauma inflation.

**B49-G07 CONTRADICTION MUST PAY** — a contradiction must alter tactic, relationship, choice or consequence.

**B49-G08 SELF-STORY IS A MODEL** — what a character says about themselves remains source-tagged information.

**B49-G09 INDEPENDENT LIFE** — named conscious supporting persons need wants/relations/refusal outside protagonist service.

**B49-G10 CAST COUNTERPOINT** — major cast differentiation must be causal, not cosmetic.

**B49-G11 ATTENTION BUDGET** — explicitly rank character narrative weight per book.

**B49-G12 LONGFORM NO RESET** — sequels require new pressure/strategy, not replay of the same flaw.

**B49-G13 WORLD THROUGH CHARACTER** — environment/institution matters when it changes appraisal/action.

**B49-G14 OBJECTIVE + MO** — every consequential scene should expose both what someone wants now and how this person tries to get it.

**B49-G15 TACTIC CHANGE** — repeated resistance should trigger a new tactic/model, not same argument louder.

**B49-G16 NONHUMAN PERSONHOOD** — conscious robots/nonhumans cannot be compressed into Helper/Tool/Service archetypes.

**B49-G17 POST-MONEY MOTIVE FIREWALL** — ordinary work/rent/credit purchasing-power motivation is forbidden in mature orbital city life unless Founder creates an explicit exception.

**B49-G18 CONSCIOUSNESS-TRANSFER FIREWALL** — do not infer operational human soul/consciousness transfer from synthetic-body or interface language in current orbital civilization.

**B49-G19 PSYCHOLOGY SOURCE FIREWALL** — McKee does not establish clinical/developmental/scientific facts.

**B49-G20 LOCAL CHARACTER CLOSURE** — a book closes its local character problem; it need not “complete” the person for all future saga use.

## 8. MANUSCRIPT / PROJECT VALIDATION

### LESSON ZERO Book One
No new McKee finding justifies reopening the development lock. Existing policy remains: theory alone cannot trigger a whole-book rewrite. Reopen only for a concrete present defect or external reader evidence.

### Orbital Youth / future books
McKee strengthens: character-specific methods, cast counterpoint, relationship-specific voice, independent robot/nonhuman personhood, and sequel no-reset.

### B07 — THE UNLISTED CITY
The current working dossier has a strong character engine in Kyra's conflict between procedural perfection and evidence-based responsibility, plus useful countermodels in Sari, Tem, Iven and Daal. However, it also contains canon regressions that must be repaired before prose:
1. ordinary money/credit/rent/housing-economic motivation reappears;
2. licensed service-body transitions / grid continuity read as operational body/consciousness transfer technology in routine cadet practice;
3. Tem's ontology must be explicit: if a conscious synthetic/robotic person, diagnostic access requires personhood/consent throughout; if an interface/device, do not write it as a person only when convenient.

These are canon defects, not reasons to discard the whole B07 engine.

## 9. FINAL SYNTHESIS

McKee's strongest contribution to IVDIVO is not the doctrine of a hidden “true character.” It is the operational idea that **character is revealed by a recognizable way of pursuing desire across changing relationships and pressures**.

Final IVDIVO character formula:

`CONTEXT → WANT → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC APPRAISAL → MO / TACTIC → OTHER PERSON'S COUNTERMODEL → CHOICE → COST → CONSEQUENCE → UPDATED STRATEGY`

A character is successful when the reader can predict the *kind of reasoning* they may use without being able to predict the exact action they will choose.
