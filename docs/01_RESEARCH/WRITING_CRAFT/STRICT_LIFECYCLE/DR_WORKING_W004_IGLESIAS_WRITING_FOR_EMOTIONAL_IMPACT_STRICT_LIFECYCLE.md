## SCREENWRITING / DATE FIREWALL
This is a 2005 screenwriting book rooted in Hollywood script-reader/market conditions of that period.
- Industry-market claims are historical and not current publishing/film-market authority.
- Screenplay compression/page-format advice does not automatically transfer to novel prose.
- Reader-effect mechanisms can transfer when separated from screenplay-specific formatting.
- Universalizing claims about emotions/human nature are craft rhetoric, not psychological science.
- `Every page must grip`, mandatory three-act turning points, obligatory darkest moment, constant rising stakes, and screen-reader first-page heuristics are not universal novel laws.

## CLAIMS FROM STRICT READ SO FAR

### I-C01 — Story design and reader effect are separate but interdependent problems
A structurally coherent story may still produce a flat reading experience. Craft must diagnose not just event order but what happens in the reader's attention/emotion.

### I-C02 — Character emotion and reader emotion are not identical
A character can be calm while the reader fears; a character can cry while the reader remains unmoved. Emotional labeling on the page is not equivalent to evoking an effect.

### I-C03 — Reader trust is a useful craft model
Confusion, implausibility, cliché, dead exposition or accidental tonal break can pull the reader out of the experience. Reader orientation and causal credibility therefore matter independently of author intention.

### I-C04 — Formula can solve arrangement without solving experience
Iglesias explicitly positions techniques as tools rather than fixed page-number rules. This reinforces IVDIVO's anti-template policy.

### I-C05 — Concept is a promise of an experience, not just a logline
A premise can attract through uniqueness, familiar human relevance, conflict, dilemma, setting, contrast or a transformed known element. The IVDIVO translation is not `high concept at any cost` but `clear specific promise of the book's distinctive experience`.

### I-C06 — Familiarity should be separated from Earth-default social assumptions
For IVDIVO, emotional legibility can coexist with radically unfamiliar post-money or human/robot social arrangements; familiarity must not be achieved by restoring contemporary Earth economics.

### I-C07 — Genre can be treated as reader-effect promise
Genre is useful as an expectation contract, but formula is the repeated cliché used to deliver it. IVDIVO can mix genres while retaining one dominant causal/emotional contract per book.

### I-C08 — Concept-generation techniques are alternatives, not mandatory escalation
Contrast, reversal, setting, dilemma, recombination, time pressure and `worst thing` are idea-generation tools. Many become failure modes if used automatically.

### I-C09 — Theme works best as a tested question rather than an announced answer
Iglesias's strongest theme material is not the moral maxim but the instruction to dramatize competing sides and let the story produce meaning. IVDIVO should formulate a thematic question, then prove/complicate it through consequences.

### I-C10 — Counterargument strengthens theme
A preferred thematic position becomes dramatically credible when a competent character or system embodies the strongest opposing case. This aligns with IVDIVO's `competent countermodel` rule.

### I-C11 — Theme should ride through character, relationship, image and causality rather than lecture
Dialogue, recurring images and subplots can carry thematic resonance, but explicit moral explanation is a weak default. The theme must survive if speeches are removed.

### I-C12 — Character connection has multiple routes
Recognition/empathy, fascination and mystery are distinct reader-entry routes. A protagonist does not need to be conventionally likable if the reader can understand, track or remain fascinated by them.

### I-C13 — Action/inaction under pressure is a stronger character test than trait labels
Iglesias repeatedly returns to decisions, reactions and refusals. For IVDIVO this supports behavior-based character modeling rather than résumé/backstory characterization.

### I-C14 — `Instant empathy` techniques are manipulable and therefore high-risk
Victimization, injury, children, animals, undeserved misfortune, rejection, sacrifice and moral virtue can all create rapid sympathy, but become sentimental manipulation when inserted merely to purchase reader care.

### I-C15 — Reader anticipation is built from future-oriented information
Goals, plans, warnings, appointments, promises, deadlines, foreshadowing and dramatic irony all create forward attention by causing the reader to model a future state.

### I-C16 — Questions are useful at multiple scales, but question-monoculture is not required
Act/sequence/scene/beat questions are useful diagnostics for uncertainty. However, a scene can earn attention through competence, intimacy, revelation, beauty, humor, emotional processing or payoff without an explicit unanswered question at every beat.

### I-C17 — Surprise must be retrospectively fair
Reversal, revelation, red herring and misdirection work when the prior information remains logically valid after the reveal. Surprise that depends on author cheating breaks reader trust.

### I-C18 — Setup/payoff is broader than plot objects
A setup may be a skill, habit, relationship, rule, line, environment constraint or piece of knowledge. Payoff should feel earned and causally usable, not merely echoed.

### I-C19 — Emotional intensity requires modulation
Iglesias's own use of relief, humor, victories/losses and anticipation demonstrates that permanent maximum intensity would flatten the reading experience. Contrast, release and ordinary life are part of emotional design.

### I-C20 — Structure advice in this source is strongly normative and must be decomposed
Inciting event, point of no return, midpoint, darkest moment, recovery and final showdown are useful diagnostic functions, not mandatory stations. Their transferable core is `state change → increased commitment/constraint → altered strategy → consequential resolution`.

### I-C21 — Endings are judged by earned local satisfaction, not by one fixed emotional shape
Happy, tragic, bittersweet, twist and open endings are examples, not exhaustive categories. For IVDIVO, the stronger rule is `local core conflict resolution → cost/aftermath → changed state → optional saga hook`.

## MECHANISMS

### I-M01 — READER-EFFECT DOUBLE ENTRY
For a major scene track both:
`STORY STATE CHANGE` and `READER EFFECT / QUESTION / EXPECTATION CHANGE`.
Neither substitutes for the other.

### I-M02 — CHARACTER / READER EMOTION FIREWALL
`CHARACTER FEELS X ≠ READER FEELS X`.
Ask what evidence, alignment, uncertainty, contrast or consequence produces the intended reader response.

### I-M03 — EXPERIENCE PROMISE
Before architecture:
`WHAT SPECIFIC EXPERIENCE DOES THIS BOOK PROMISE? → WHAT CAUSAL ENGINE EARNS IT? → WHAT WOULD VIOLATE THE PROMISE?`

### I-M04 — UNIQUE / FAMILIAR SPLIT
`DISTINCTIVE WORLD/CONFLICT/RELATIONSHIP + LEGIBLE PERSONAL STAKE`.
Do not translate `familiar` into money, rent, consumer status or AI-servant defaults.

### I-M05 — CONCEPT ALTERNATIVE GENERATOR
When the premise is weak, generate alternatives through character contrast, character/world mismatch, POV shift, setting consequence, meaningful dilemma, reversal of first predictable solution, recombination with a second mechanism, or a more specific disturbance. Choose by character/world truth and causality, not maximum loudness.

### I-M06 — DOMINANT GENRE / SECONDARY MODE
For a hybrid book, identify the dominant reader contract and let secondary genres enrich it without producing multiple independent engines.

### I-M07 — ANTI-FORMULA CHECK
If a technique is present only because a craft source says it increases excitement (`ticking clock`, worst possible event, extreme villain, mandatory inciting shock), require a story-specific reason or remove it.

### I-M08 — THEME QUESTION / CONSEQUENCE TEST
`THEMATIC QUESTION → COMPETING MODELS → CHARACTER CHOICES → CONSEQUENCES → PROVISIONAL ANSWER`.
The answer may remain complicated; it must not depend on a lecture.

### I-M09 — COUNTERMODEL THEME GATE
Before approving a thematic conflict, require the strongest opposing side to possess real benefits, evidence, affected stakeholders and a coherent value model.

### I-M10 — CHARACTER CONNECTION ROUTER
For a major character deliberately decide which combination drives reader attention:
`RECOGNITION / EMPATHY`, `FASCINATION`, `MYSTERY`, `COMPETENCE`, `RELATIONSHIP`, `MORAL/TACTICAL UNCERTAINTY`.
Do not buy all six through victimization.

### I-M11 — EMPATHY MANIPULATION AUDIT
If an injury, child, animal, humiliation, rejection, disability, sacrifice or undeserved loss exists mainly to make a character likable, flag it. Require independent causal/story function and person-specific consequence.

### I-M12 — FUTURE-MODEL ANTICIPATION
`KNOWN GOAL/PLAN/WARNING/PROMISE → READER MODELS FUTURE → CURRENT SCENE GAINS TENSION/HOPE/WORRY`.
Anticipation can exist in quiet scenes without physical danger.

### I-M13 — FAIR SURPRISE
`PRIOR TRUE INFORMATION → READER MODEL A → NEW EVIDENCE → MODEL B → RETROSPECTIVE SUPPORT FOR B`.
Red herrings must be true or legitimately interpretable facts, not fabricated lies from the author.

### I-M14 — SETUP / PAYOFF LEDGER
Track setup by type: `OBJECT / SKILL / RULE / RELATIONSHIP / HABIT / INFORMATION / ENVIRONMENT CONSTRAINT / LANGUAGE`. A payoff must alter action, inference, relationship or outcome; pure callback is optional style, not structural payoff.

### I-M15 — EMOTIONAL MODULATION
`PRESSURE → PARTIAL RELEASE / SUCCESS / HUMOR / INTIMACY / ORDINARY LIFE → NEW PRESSURE`.
The release must often create information, relationship or responsibility, not merely pause the book.

### I-M16 — STRUCTURE-FUNCTION DECOMPOSITION
Translate Iglesias's screenplay stations into functions:
- disturbance = durable state change;
- commitment point = meaningful narrowing/acceptance of responsibility;
- midpoint = model/goal/method/relationship change;
- low point = optional severe constraint, not required despair;
- climax = decisive bounded action that resolves the local engine;
- resolution = aftermath and changed state.
No fixed percentages.

### I-M17 — ENDING SATISFACTION GATE
`LOCAL PROBLEM RESOLVED OR DELIBERATELY BOUNDED → CONSEQUENCE/COST → RELATIONSHIP/KNOWLEDGE STATE UPDATED → SAGA HOOK ONLY AFTER LOCAL CLOSURE`.

## FAILURE MODES
- Structure assumed to guarantee emotional engagement.
- Character emotional display substituted for reader effect.
- Every page required to produce maximum intensity.
- Contemporary Hollywood gatekeeper conditions treated as universal current market truth.
- High-concept pressure overriding character/world truth.
- `Familiar emotion` used to smuggle contemporary Earth institutions into future worldbuilding.
- Automatic `worst thing from hell` escalation.
- Automatic ticking clock.
- Fish-out-of-water used when ordinary local belonging is more truthful.
- Genre contract mistaken for formula.
- One-page-test rhetoric converted into choppy sentence/page micro-hooks.
- Reader manipulation treated as more important than causality/personhood.
- Theme stated as moral answer before the story tests it.
- Hero/antagonist forced into simplistic positive/negative theme embodiments.
- Supporting cast reduced to thematic mouthpieces.
- `Ghost` or trauma inserted because a character book says depth requires it.
- Instant-empathy victimization inflation.
- Child/animal/illness/sacrifice used as sympathy purchase.
- Mystery created by withholding information the POV would naturally disclose.
- Question at every beat converted into permanent hook-writing.
- Stakes inflation interpreted as `hero must lose everything`.
- Victory/loss ping-pong used mechanically.
- Shock mistaken for emotional depth.
- Red herring that becomes false after reveal.
- Coincidence solving the climax.
- Darkest Moment retrofit.
- Point-of-no-return retrofit.
- Midpoint placed by percentage instead of function.
- Final showdown required even when the story's resolution is relational/institutional/epistemic.
- Twist ending added for market buzz rather than causal truth.

## CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON — PROVISIONAL
- **LaPlante / Lodge / Ribó** counter Iglesias's strongest normative claims: ordinary life, contemplation, summary, non-transformative arcs and variable scene forms can remain fully dramatic when they alter meaningful states.
- **McKee / Truby / Brody** overlap with Iglesias on disturbance, desire, obstacles, turning points and payoff; IVDIVO keeps the functions but rejects station-based retrofit.
- **Zunshine / Herman** strengthen fair surprise and evidence provenance: reader uncertainty must not silently convert interpretation into fact.
- **Corbett / Card / McKee Character** support action/decision-based characterization but reject simplistic `virtue + wound + extreme pressure = true self` determinism.
- **Vorhaus / Lodge** support emotional modulation through humor and contrast rather than permanent intensity.

## IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION / LOCAL MANUSCRIPT APPLICATION
- Preserve reader effect as an explicit validation layer after structural correctness.
- For Orbital Youth, emotional familiarity comes from autonomy, trust, belonging, responsibility, consent, love, reputation, mastery, unique opportunity and time — not wages/rent.
- For conscious robots, do not manufacture empathy by making them petlike, helpless or secretly human. Personhood comes from independent desire, relationships, action, cost, refusal and consent.
- For mystery, curiosity is useful only when evidence/source contracts remain fair; `reader inferior position` cannot justify hiding facts already known by the active POV without an in-world reason.
- For style, `reader effect` never means `short-sentence density`; rhythm is chosen by required effect.
- For `LESSON ZERO`, no new craft theory by itself reopens development lock. Use Iglesias only to locate a concrete reader-effect failure that survives current-version verification.
- For future books, formulate a dominant emotional/reader promise, then test whether ONE MAIN STORY causally earns it.
- In ensemble scenes, use theme/countermodel pressure through different wants and tactics, not five characters delivering five essays.
- In post-money settings, `stakes rise` means a more consequential relationship/responsibility/consent/reputation/unique-opportunity cost — never disguised credits.
- In consciousness-school material, emotional mystery must not upgrade a school's interpretation into universal ontology.
