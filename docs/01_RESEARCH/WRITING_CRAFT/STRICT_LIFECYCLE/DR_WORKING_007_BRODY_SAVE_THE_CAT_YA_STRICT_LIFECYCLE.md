# 4. PRELIMINARY OBSERVATIONS — NOT FINAL CLAIMS

## YA category
- Brody correctly frames YA as an age category/lens capable of containing multiple genres rather than one genre.
- Useful IVDIVO implication: Orbital Youth can be social SF, mystery, relationship drama, sport, thriller or adventure without ceasing to be YA if youth developmental stakes remain causal.

## Story-worthiness
Brody’s early hero triad is:
`PROBLEM / WANT / NEED`.

Transferable core:
- protagonist needs a legible present friction/problem;
- protagonist actively wants a concrete outcome;
- external pursuit should pressure a deeper developmental blind spot/need.

IVDIVO correction:
- do not force every hero into one didactic “life lesson”;
- need may be growth in judgment, responsibility, boundaries, trust, autonomy, belonging or self-model, not a slogan.

## Beat sheet
Brody presents 15 beats with approximate percentages:
Opening Image → Theme Stated → Setup → Catalyst → Debate → Break into 2 → B Story → Fun and Games → Midpoint → Bad Guys Close In → All Is Lost → Dark Night → Break into 3 → Finale → Final Image.

This remains **SOURCE MODEL, NOT IVDIVO LAW**.

Preliminary useful value:
- diagnostic attention to setup/catalyst/commitment/midpoint/escalation/low point/final choice;
- beat locations may be used as pacing comparison after story causality exists.

Preliminary rejection:
- fixed percentage positions are not authority over organic causality;
- mandatory Theme Stated, All Is Lost, Dark Night, false victory/false defeat and “hero learns the theme” are not automatically required;
- B Story characters must not exist merely to teach protagonist a theme;
- Finale does not require “bad guys destroyed” or total thematic conquest.

## Commercial claims
Statements about bestseller construction, market popularity and reader expectation are historical/commercial claims and require current evidence if used for market decisions. They are not craft facts merely because examples fit the template.

# 5. PRELIMINARY FAILURE MODES TO TEST DURING FULL READ

- **BEAT-PERCENTAGE FETISH** — moving story events to percentages instead of fixing causality.
- **THEME-STATED LITERALISM** — inserting a line whose only job is to announce author lesson.
- **B-STORY-AS-TUTOR** — supporting character exists to teach hero theme.
- **FUN-AND-GAMES MONTAGE** — premise delivery detached from consequence.
- **FALSE-VICTORY/DEFEAT FORCING** — midpoint categorized before its actual causal function is understood.
- **ALL-IS-LOST MANUFACTURING** — artificial catastrophe added because template requires 75% low point.
- **DARK-NIGHT MONOCULTURE** — every protagonist forced into same reflective breakdown.
- **FINALE MORAL PROOF** — climax becomes demonstration that hero learned author’s lesson.
- **PERCENTAGE AS QUALITY** — structural numbers substituted for reader effect.
- **YA AS ADULT PLOT WITH TEEN AGES** — external plot has no developmental/social consequences specific to youth.
