# 4. PROVISIONAL CLAIM / MECHANISM NOTES — NOT FINAL SYNTHESIS

These are allowed as working extraction because the relevant chapters have been fully read, but the source remains strict-FULL-READ incomplete.

## 4.1 Hero engine
Brody's early `PROBLEM / WANT / NEED` remains useful when translated away from a mandatory moral lesson:

`PRESENT PRESSURE → ACTIVE WANT → DEVELOPMENTAL BLIND SPOT / NEED → CHOICE UNDER CONSEQUENCE`.

## 4.2 Commitment beats
Transferable value of Catalyst / Debate / Break Into 2:
- a disturbance changes the protagonist's available choices;
- the protagonist processes what the change means;
- the story needs an active commitment or accepted responsibility that generates a trackable A-story goal.

IVDIVO rejection:
- Catalyst need not be huge, external or irreversible;
- hero need not enter Act 2 by doing the morally or strategically `wrong` thing;
- percentage placement is diagnostic only.

## 4.3 Trackable middle goal
Brody is strongest when insisting that the middle has an explicit pursuit rather than free-floating episodes.

IVDIVO form:
`ONE MAIN STORY → TRACKABLE OBJECTIVE → LOCAL ATTEMPTS → CHANGED INFORMATION/RELATIONS → STRATEGY CHANGE`.

## 4.4 B Story / relationship correction
SOURCE MODEL:
- B Story character often embodies or teaches the theme.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
- a relationship can pressure the hero's internal development;
- the other person must have an independent want, costs, relationships and right to refuse;
- friendship, romance, mentor, rival or conscious robotic person must not exist as an author's theme-delivery device.

## 4.5 Promise of premise
Useful core:
- a book should actually deliver the distinctive experiences implied by its premise;
- premise-delivery scenes still need consequence and character causality.

IVDIVO correction:
- Orbital Youth must show genuinely orbital adolescence rather than Earth teen life with scenery;
- this is not permission for technological tourism or montage detached from the A-story.

## 4.6 Midpoint
SOURCE MODEL stresses false victory/false defeat, stakes escalation and a wants→needs shift.

IVDIVO TRANSFORMATION:
`MIDPOINT = MATERIAL CHANGE IN MODEL / GOAL / METHOD / RELATIONSHIP / CONSTRAINTS`.

It does **not** require:
- false victory/defeat;
- public spectacle;
- ticking clock;
- secret reveal;
- death;
- romantic stakes.

## 4.7 Pressure after midpoint
Useful core:
- external resistance and the protagonist's internal blind spot can begin interacting more tightly;
- repeated middle scenes should not all apply the same kind of pressure.

Reject mandatory trajectory inversion (`Fun & Games up = BGC down`).

## 4.8 Low point / aftermath
`All Is Lost` and `Dark Night` are not universal requirements.

Transferable core:
- a decisive consequence may invalidate the current strategy or narrow options;
- the protagonist's role in producing the consequence can make it personally costly;
- aftermath is useful when interpretation changes the next choice.

Do not manufacture death imagery, rock bottom, wallowing or an epiphany simply to satisfy the template.

## 4.9 Finale
Brody's five-point Finale is useful only as an optional diagnostic against a rushed ending:

`PREPARATION → ATTEMPT → COMPLICATION → RECONSIDERATION/REPLAN → CONSEQUENCE`.

Reject as universal:
- first plan must fail;
- supporting character must sacrifice;
- High Tower Surprise;
- hero must prove the theme;
- triumph is structurally mandatory.

IVDIVO ending gate remains:
`CAUSAL CONVERGENCE → BOUNDED CHOICE → COST → LOCAL RESULT → AFTERMATH → only then saga hook`.

## 4.10 Visible change
Final Image is useful when read as visible evidence of a changed condition or behavior.

Do not force an exact mirror or add an explanatory moral sentence after the behavior already shows the change.

## 4.11 Multiple POV
Brody's single/multi/blended track vocabulary is useful for diagnosing ensemble structure.

IVDIVO correction:
- `SAGA EQUALITY ≠ BOOK-LEVEL EQUAL POV`;
- every major POV must have a defined causal connection to the one main story;
- tracks may merge/diverge, but five protagonists must not become five unrelated mini-books;
- supporting/minor POV may be justified by information, irony, world pressure or relationship function even without equal transformation/page count.

## 4.12 Early/late disturbance
Useful core:
- early disturbance can make reaction carry setup work;
- later disturbance requires ongoing meaningful pressure/questions, not simply delayed information.

Reject market anecdotes about exact page counts as timeless craft law.

## 4.13 Backstory
Useful:
- backstory/flashback should affect present understanding, action, stakes, relationship or decision.

Correction against excessive necessity test:
- Chatman-style satellites may have legitimate emotional/voice/foreshadowing value without being literal plot prerequisites;
- ask `WHAT FUNCTION DOES THIS PAY FOR?`, not only `CAN THE PLOT SURVIVE WITHOUT IT?`.

## 4.14 Genre taxonomy
Brody's ten genres can be useful as **comparison lenses** or a way to clarify a story's dominant human conflict.

Reject:
- claim that all fiction is reducible to ten universal templates as production truth;
- checking all ingredients because successful examples can be retrospectively fitted;
- using taxonomy to overwrite a stronger organic story engine.

Useful question:
`What kind of human pressure dominates this book, and what promise does that create for the reader?`

## 4.15 Rites of Passage
Useful YA insight:
- adolescence naturally amplifies identity, separation, changing parental worldviews, first responsibility and social reclassification;
- life problem can be ordinary and still story-worthy.

Reject monoculture:
- hero does not have to attack every developmental problem the `wrong way`;
- acceptance is not the only valid resolution;
- competent coping is not inherently dull;
- not every YA arc requires pain avoidance → lesson acceptance.

IVDIVO transformation:
`DEVELOPMENTAL PRESSURE → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC COPING STRATEGY → CONSEQUENCE → REVISED RELATIONSHIP TO SELF/OTHERS/WORLD`.

# 5. PROVISIONAL FAILURE MODES — EXPANDED

- **BEAT-PERCENTAGE FETISH** — moving events to percentages instead of fixing causality.
- **THEME-STATED LITERALISM** — inserting a line whose main job is to announce the author's lesson.
- **B-STORY-AS-TUTOR** — supporting person exists to teach the protagonist.
- **CONSCIOUS-PERSON-AS-THEME-DEVICE** — especially unacceptable for robotic persons.
- **FUN-AND-GAMES MONTAGE** — premise delivery without consequence.
- **FALSE-VICTORY/DEFEAT FORCING** — categorizing midpoint before understanding its causal function.
- **STAKES-INFLATION TOOLBOX** — adding clock/secret/public spectacle/death because the template needs escalation.
- **ALL-IS-LOST MANUFACTURING** — artificial catastrophe at the expected percentage.
- **DARK-NIGHT MONOCULTURE** — every hero forced into reflective breakdown.
- **FINALE MORAL PROOF** — climax becomes a demonstration that the hero learned the author's lesson.
- **PERCENTAGE AS QUALITY** — structural numbers substituted for reader effect.
- **YA AS ADULT PLOT WITH TEEN AGES** — youth developmental/social conditions are noncausal decoration.
- **EQUAL-POV FOG** — ensemble members receive equal weight merely because they are all important to the saga.
- **TRACK WITHOUT CONNECTION** — multiple POVs have separate stories but no causal/common pressure.
- **CATALYST SIZE FETISH** — assuming a story begins only when something huge happens.
- **DOUBLE-BUMP PATCHING** — adding a second disturbance because the first did not generate genuine choice.
- **CRYPTIC-ALLUSION DEBT** — teasing future catastrophe rather than making current pages intrinsically causal/interesting.
- **BACKSTORY NECESSITY OVERREACH** — deleting useful relational/voice/emotional satellites because plot could technically survive.
- **TEN-GENRE TOTALISM** — treating a retrospective taxonomy as exhaustive ontology of story.
- **GENRE-INGREDIENT CHECKBOXING** — inserting conventions instead of diagnosing story promise.
- **WRONG-WAY MONOCULTURE** — requiring competent young people to make avoidable mistakes because growth is assumed to require error.
- **ACCEPTANCE-AS-ONLY-GROWTH** — forcing all coming-of-age arcs into one therapeutic shape.

# 6. PROVISIONAL CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

Not final until FULL READ closes.

- **Truby** is stronger than Brody on organic causality: desire → action → learning → changed decision, without fixed percentage ownership.
- **Rosenfeld** is stronger at scene recursion: prior consequence → intention → complication/support → result → next intention.
- **Bell** is useful for pressure/resistance and concrete stakes, but also requires firewalling against artificial escalation; together with Brody this reinforces the need for a `NO STAKES INFLATION` gate.
- **Card** is stronger on character hierarchy and POV attention budget; this corrects Brody's tendency to associate hero status with roughly equal page/plot/transformation weight.
- **Chatman** protects meaningful satellites from an overly strict `if removable, delete` backstory test.
- **Kress** supplies character-specific motivation/frustration responses, preventing Brody's `flaw → wrong way → lesson` from becoming a generic YA psychology template.

# 7. PROVISIONAL IVDIVO PRODUCTION GATES FROM CH1–5

These gates are operational now but remain subject to revision after the full source is completed.

**B41-G01 — ACTIVE COMMITMENT, NOT ACT-2 PERCENTAGE**  
After disturbance, the protagonist eventually makes/accepts a meaningful choice that generates causal forward motion. Do not place it by percentage.

**B41-G02 — TRACKABLE A-STORY GOAL**  
After the major commitment, the protagonist/team needs a concrete objective that lets scenes accumulate rather than drift.

**B41-G03 — RELATIONSHIP PERSONHOOD**  
No friend, lover, mentor, rival or conscious robot exists primarily to teach theme. Give independent desire, costs and refusal rights.

**B41-G04 — PREMISE MUST PAY OFF**  
Deliver the promised experience of the book (including orbital adolescence) through causal scenes, not exposition tourism.

**B41-G05 — MIDPOINT MODEL CHANGE**  
Mid-book event must materially change model/goal/method/relationship/constraints; false victory/defeat is optional.

**B41-G06 — NO STAKES INFLATION**  
Clock, secret, death, spectacle or catastrophe enters only if generated by existing causality.

**B41-G07 — NO MANDATORY ROCK BOTTOM**  
Use the consequence the story earns; never manufacture All Is Lost / whiff of death.

**B41-G08 — AFTERMATH CHANGES ACTION**  
Reflection/contemplation earns space when it changes interpretation, relation, commitment or next strategy.

**B41-G09 — FINALE RESISTANCE WITHOUT TEMPLATE**  
Ending needs earned resistance, bounded choice and cost; it does not require first-plan failure or sacrifice.

**B41-G10 — VISIBLE CHANGE, NO MORAL EXPLANATION**  
Prefer changed behavior/relationship/status over an authorial lesson statement.

**B41-G11 — MULTI-POV CONNECTION**  
For every major POV, name the causal connection to ONE MAIN STORY and why that mind is needed now.

**B41-G12 — SAGA EQUALITY IS NOT EQUAL SCREEN TIME**  
Book-level attention is hierarchical and functional.

**B41-G13 — BACKSTORY PAYS PRESENT FUNCTION**  
Backstory must change present understanding/action/relationship/reader expectation; not every useful satellite must be plot-essential.

**B41-G14 — TAXONOMY IS A LENS, NOT AN ENGINE**  
Use story-type labels to clarify dominant pressure/promise, never to overwrite stronger causality.

**B41-G15 — YA DEVELOPMENT IS CAUSAL**  
The same external problem should mean something specifically adolescent: autonomy, family authority, peer belonging, first responsibility, identity, first intimacy, future self, status or consent.

**B41-G16 — COMPETENT YOUTH MAY STILL GROW**  
Do not force avoidable stupidity or a `wrong way` merely to create an arc. Growth can come from a correct but incomplete strategy meeting a more complex world.
