# 3. CLAIMS
## W008-C01 — Genre changes the reader contract
Form changes assumptions about truth status, expected payoff, reading speed and attention.

## W008-C02 — Read for decisions and effects
Writerly reading asks: what choice was made, what effect does it produce here, and what transferable principle—not surface imitation—can be tested elsewhere?

## W008-C03 — Genre is repertoire, not prison
Genre boundaries and literary/commercial boundaries are porous. The useful question is what a mode enables or constrains.

## W008-C04 — Selective focus is part of narrative truth
Neither invented worlds nor lived experience require inclusion of all available material. Relevance is designed.

## W008-C05 — Small plots can carry developmental weight
Coming-of-age does not require catastrophe. A first responsibility, changed relation, boundary, embarrassment, loss of trust or unique experience can alter a young person's model of self/world.

## W008-C06 — Hybrid form must produce additional meaning
Image/text, list/prose, transcript/narration or other mixed forms earn their presence when their interaction produces information/effect unavailable from either layer alone.

## W008-C07 — Short form means density, not chopped syntax
Micro/flash work by compression and multifunctional detail. Sentence fragments are optional; completeness of effect remains required.

## W008-C08 — Repetition is valuable when it evolves meaning or structure
A repeated word/image/phrase can mark time, rhythm, escalation or thematic development. Pure restatement wastes attention.

## W008-C09 — Narrative and non-narrative cohesion differ
Narrative work may cohere through plot/arc/conflict; other work may cohere through central concept, image, sense, emotion or patterned juxtaposition. Conflict-first is not universal.

## W008-C10 — Structure changes how material is read
Linear, braided, segmented, borrowed and visual structures are not decoration. Sequence and juxtaposition alter inference and emphasis.

## W008-C11 — Whitespace and breaks are semantic operators
Paragraph/section/chapter breaks cue shifts in time, thought, POV, emotional weight, plot or concept. More space generally signals a larger transition.

## W008-C12 — Character depth accumulates through specific detail
A character becomes dimensional through distributed physical, inner, habitual, relational and speech details that interact with pressure.

## W008-C13 — POV is lens + access + distance
Pronoun alone does not define POV. What matters is who holds the lens, what can be perceived/known/interpreted, and how close narration sits to that consciousness.

## W008-C14 — Setting must be occupied
Effective setting gives place/time through characters doing, sensing and reacting. Setting becomes story when it changes behavior, relation, conflict, theme or possibility.

## W008-C15 — Scene is selective time dilation
Full scene is justified for moments whose exact actions, dialogue, sensory sequence or immediate realization deserve reader time. Important material can also use partial scenic rendering without becoming a full scene.

## W008-C16 — Sentence length must serve cognition/effect
Long, short, complete and fragmentary sentences can all work. Syntax may embody thought, movement or emotional state; no sentence-length percentage is a literary law.

## W008-C17 — Formal rule-breaking must be intentional and legible
A fragment, conjunction opening, cliché inversion or other deviation works when it serves the piece and does not create accidental confusion.

# 4. TRANSFERABLE MECHANISMS
## M01 — GENRE CONTRACT TEST
`FORM/MODE → reader assumptions → promised experience → craft choices → payoff`

## M02 — WRITERLY READING NOTE
`reader effect → exact technique → local reason it works → transferable principle → original experiment`

## M03 — SELECTIVE EXPERIENCE ROUTER
`total available reality → story focus → necessary events/details → omission → coherent experience`

## M04 — ISSUE → PERSON FILTER
`abstract issue → specific person → immediate want/problem → lived detail → choice → consequence → reader inference`

## M05 — HYBRID FUNCTION GATE
`secondary medium/form → unique information/effect → interaction with primary layer → changed meaning/inference`
If no unique work is added, remove the hybrid layer.

## M06 — SHORT-FORM DENSITY TEST
`detail/repetition/sentence → number of simultaneous jobs → keep/compress/cut`
Possible jobs: action, character, relation, setting, time, theme, question, rhythm.

## M07 — PURPOSEFUL REPETITION
`first meaning → recurrence in changed context → altered/expanded meaning → payoff`

## M08 — COHESION ROUTER
`Is this narrative?` If yes, test causal/relational progression. If no, identify the actual organizing concept/image/sense/emotion rather than inventing fake conflict.

## M09 — BREAK INTENT TEST
Before paragraph/section/chapter break: `what changed enough to justify new visual unit?`
No change = likely paragraph confetti.

## M10 — TRANSITION SCALE
`micro shift → sentence/paragraph bridge; larger time/POV/plot shift → stronger break; major discontinuity → section/chapter boundary`.

## M11 — HABIT UNDER PRESSURE
`establish specific habit/tactic → repeat enough to become legible → pressure modifies/interrupts it → reader infers character change/state`.

## M12 — POV LENS MATRIX
`lens holder → observable → knowable → inferred → unknown → narrative distance → reader effect`.

## M13 — SETTING OCCUPANCY
`place rule/detail → character encounters it → physical/social response → choice/relationship/consequence`.

## M14 — SCENE NECESSITY / DURATION ROUTER
`importance of exact moment + need for exact words/actions + reader attention budget → full scene / partial scenic rendering / summary`.

## M15 — LANGUAGE FUNCTION PASS
`meaning → diction → syntax → punctuation → sound → rhythm`.
A line-level choice is retained because of effect, not because a generic rule says it is “strong.”

## M16 — INTENTIONAL FRAGMENT TEST
A fragment is retained only if context makes meaning clear and incompleteness/impact/rhythm performs a specific job. Fragment density itself is never a target.

# 5. FAILURE MODES
1. **Hybrid Decoration** — dossiers, chat logs, transcripts, maps or images add style but no new meaning.
2. **Multimedia Redundancy** — visual/audio/text layers repeat the same information instead of complementing one another.
3. **Micro = Fragment Confusion** — short form mistaken for chopped syntax.
4. **Platform Ignorance** — text moved between media without adapting context, pacing or interaction.
5. **Arc Retrofit** — traditional five-part arc treated as mandatory geometry.
6. **Conflict Monoculture** — every scene forced into overt confrontation or catastrophe.
7. **Theme Declaration** — abstract idea stated instead of carried by concrete pattern/choice.
8. **Structural Novelty Without Purpose** — braid/collage/borrowed form selected because it seems sophisticated.
9. **Whitespace Without Shift** — visual break creates fake emphasis.
10. **Paragraph Confetti** — every sentence or beat isolated into its own paragraph without functional change.
11. **Transition Overexplanation** — “then/meanwhile/after that” used where causal/perceptual continuity could do the work.
12. **Character Blizzard** — biography/details dumped before reader has reason to care.
13. **Checklist Characterization** — physical/mental/habit/speech categories treated as mandatory inventory.
14. **Generic Gesture/Detail** — interchangeable action or setting detail that belongs to no particular person/place.
15. **Pity as Depth** — injury, illness, bullying or vulnerability used as substitute for agency/complexity.
16. **Pronoun = POV Confusion** — changing I/he/she without changing access, lens or distance.
17. **POV Gimmick** — second person/plural/objective used for novelty without reader-effect reason.
18. **Setting Tourism** — future world shown as catalogue rather than occupied constraint/opportunity.
19. **Scene Monoculture** — every process, commute and routine dramatized moment by moment.
20. **Rule Compliance Prose** — adverb bans, no-fragment rules, active-verb quotas or sentence-length targets overriding actual effect.
21. **Choppy Suspense Myth** — tension equated with short-sentence percentage.

# 7. IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION
## ONE MAIN STORY
Keep one dominant book-level causal/emotional engine. Allow local formal variety only when each mode returns value to that engine or a major relationship/character chain.

## Orbital Youth / post-money civilization
Do not explain post-money society in an essay paragraph. Reveal it through ordinary desire and friction: access to a specific role, trust, consent, time, relationship, reputation, expertise, unique experience, shared space or responsibility. Never reintroduce money as credits/points merely to imitate contemporary YA stakes.

## Conscious robots
No generic robot voice or terminal-like characterization. Apply POV lens, character accumulation and relation-specific speech to robots exactly as to other persons, adjusted for embodiment/culture. If a named robot can be replaced with a smart tool without changing consent, desire or relation, personhood is not written.

## Consciousness schools / dimensional knowledge
Use form/POV to keep epistemic layers distinct: practitioner observation, reproducible effect, school interpretation, competing interpretation and possible ontology. A transcript/diagram/ritual record is not world-fact merely because it looks documentary.

## Mystery
Hybrid evidence (recording, log, image, chat, map) earns page space when the medium changes what can be inferred or verified. Do not duplicate the prose explanation of the same clue.

## YA psychology
Developmental movement may be small but irreversible in meaning. Do not inflate ordinary autonomy, belonging, first responsibility or relationship boundary into forced catastrophe.

## Paragraphs / fragments / rhythm
Use the hierarchy:
`story/causality → scene necessity → mode → POV → paragraph intent → syntax → wording`.
A one-sentence paragraph or fragment is protected when it creates a real beat/shift/resonance. It is cut/merged when it merely adds artificial emphasis.

# 10. FINAL SYNTHESIS
Pushman's durable contribution to IVDIVO is not a competing story formula. It is a **form-and-reading diagnostic layer** that prevents the production system from confusing structural rigor with formal uniformity.

The central synthesis is:

`READER CONTRACT → STORY FUNCTION → FORM/MODE → POV/SETTING/SCENE CHOICE → PARAGRAPH/SYNTAX/LANGUAGE → READER EFFECT`.

This sits beneath ONE MAIN STORY rather than replacing it. The book-level causal engine may remain unified while local form, scene duration, POV distance, paragraph size and sentence rhythm vary substantially.

For IVDIVO line editing, the most important rule is:

`DO NOT FIX A STRUCTURAL/MODE PROBLEM WITH SENTENCE COSMETICS.`

And for the long-running “choppy prose” problem:

`SHORT ≠ BAD; UNMOTIVATED FRAGMENTATION = BAD.`

A fragment, one-sentence paragraph, long winding sentence, indirect summary or quiet scene is valid when it performs a precise reader-facing function and belongs to the character/story state.
