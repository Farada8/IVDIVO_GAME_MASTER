## 3. FINAL CLAIM EXTRACTION — TRANSFERABLE CORE

### A12-C01 — Action is a human-state change carried by physical/strategic movement
Spectacle alone is not dramatic value. Movement matters when success/failure changes safety, trust, relationship, authority, responsibility, autonomy, civic state, evidence or future possibility.

### A12-C02 — Object of desire and motivation are separate
`WHAT THE CHARACTER WANTS` and `WHY THIS CHARACTER WANTS IT` are independent design problems.

### A12-C03 — Opposition quality determines protagonist ingenuity
A protagonist looks resourceful only against a coherent problem/countermodel that could genuinely succeed.

### A12-C04 — Tactical specificity creates originality
Characters differentiate through the particular methods they choose under pressure, not through labels alone.

### A12-C05 — Progression means qualitative change, not numeric escalation
A later obstacle should demand a different model, tactic, sacrifice, relationship or type of leverage—not simply more explosions, more enemies or higher volume.

### A12-C06 — Power is asymmetric leverage
Status, information, legitimacy, access, time, technical capacity, numbers, social trust and institutional authority can create stronger pressure than physical force.

### A12-C07 — Useful limitation generates strategy
Unlimited capability creates “why not just...?” holes. Real limits force invention.

### A12-C08 — A decisive solution should belong to established agency
Coincidence may introduce trouble. It should not perform the critical solution.

### A12-C09 — Set piece = tension + sensory/physical activity + causal change
A major action sequence should have an internal tactical problem and altered state, not merely choreography.

### A12-C10 — Rhythm, tempo and intensity are different variables
Scene length/spacing, internal activity level and proximity/severity of danger can be modulated independently.

### A12-C11 — Repetition has diminishing returns
Repeated movement, emotional charge, tactic or set-piece geometry loses effect unless cast, meaning, information or relation changes.

### A12-C12 — A climax removes easy answers and concentrates meaningful choice
Transferable form:
`REMOVE EASY TOOL / CERTAINTY / ACCESS → CONCENTRATE PRESSURE → REAL ALTERNATIVES → EARNED CHOICE → COST → RESULT`.

### A12-C13 — Resolution should show the relevant ripple, then stop
Close the local story before extending the saga. A late extra threat or false ending needs real causal necessity.

### A12-C14 — Genre/subgenre is a focusing device, not a formula
A source of antagonism can focus story design, but conventions are not mandatory beat prescriptions.

### A12-C15 — Formulas reduce possibility
McKee/El-Wakil explicitly reject rigid Whammo/Hero's-Journey imitation. This converges with IVDIVO anti-template production law.

## 4. MECHANISMS

### M-A12-01 — OBJECT / MOTIVE SPLIT
`DISTURBANCE → SPECIFIC OBJECT OF DESIRE → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC WHY → ACTION`.

### M-A12-02 — FAVORITE-TACTIC REMOVAL
Before making danger bigger, remove the protagonist's easiest habitual procedure, access, certainty, instrument, ally or authority.

### M-A12-03 — TACTICAL IMBALANCE, TRANSFORMED
Do not require supervillain immunity. Instead create one **specific opposing method** that bypasses the protagonist's normal competence and one meaningful limit on the protagonist's strongest method.

### M-A12-04 — STRENGTH-TO-VULNERABILITY REINTERPRETATION
Prefer a property that initially functions as strength, then becomes exploitable only after evidence/model revision. Avoid obvious early off-switches.

### M-A12-05 — SET-PIECE MOVEMENT MAP
A pressure sequence may move the protagonist:
- toward a problem;
- away from a problem;
- toward another person who needs help;
- through a contested object/information/access struggle;
- into direct confrontation.
Use as compositional options, not required categories.

### M-A12-06 — PRESSURE VARIATION
Alternate type, rhythm, tempo, intimacy and leverage. Quiet high-leverage scenes may carry more pressure than kinetic scenes.

### M-A12-07 — COUNTERMODEL PLAN TEST
For any opposing system/person/institution:
`COHERENT? → CAPABLE OF SUCCESS? → CHARACTER/INSTITUTION-SPECIFIC? → REAL BENEFIT/REWARD? → REAL COST/BLIND SPOT?`

### M-A12-08 — ACTION REVEAL
A revelation matters only if it alters tactics, risk, access, relationship or decision. Secret facts that do not turn action are decorative.

### M-A12-09 — ACTION RESOLUTION OWNERSHIP
`ESTABLISHED KNOWLEDGE + PREPARATION + RELATIONSHIP + CHOICE + ACTION → DECISIVE RESULT`.

### M-A12-10 — ACTION-PROSE ORDER
`VALUE / CAUSALITY → AGENCY → TACTIC → INFORMATION → CONSEQUENCE → POV → RHYTHM → SENTENCE`.
Line chopping cannot repair a structurally inert action scene.

## 6. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

### With McKee — Story
Convergence: action must turn value/state; progressive complications should change the problem; crisis/climax resolves through choice.
Correction: IVDIVO rejects compulsory binary value changes and maximum-stakes escalation.

### With McKee — Character
Convergence: character emerges through specific action under pressure.
Correction: repeated behavior across contexts outranks one supposed revelation of immutable “true self.”

### With McKee — Dialogue
Convergence: tactics change in verbal and physical action alike; leverage can remain subtextual.

### With Bell — Conflict & Suspense
Strong convergence on intelligent opposition, tactical variation, pressure modulation and earned suspense.
Bell/McKee genre claims about death are both downgraded to **meaningful irreversible or costly stakes** for broader IVDIVO use.

### With Rosenfeld — Make a Scene
Convergence: intention, resistance, changed state and next intention.
Rosenfeld better protects non-action scene legitimacy; Action better articulates tactical asymmetry and capability limits.

### With Truby
Convergence: causal architecture and character-specific opposition.
Truby is stronger on story organism/revelation networks; Action is stronger on tactical pressure, capability limits and physical progression.

## 7. IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION / PRODUCTION GATES

**A12-G01 Human Value Before Spectacle** — what human/relational/civic state changes?

**A12-G02 Agency Preservation** — threatened people/nonhumans retain meaningful agency where possible.

**A12-G03 Favorite-Tactic Removal** — test removal before scale inflation.

**A12-G04 No Obvious Off-Switch** — final solution must not be obvious from an early weakness plant.

**A12-G05 Resolution Ownership** — decisive result is causally earned.

**A12-G06 Excitement Modulation** — vary pressure, rhythm, tempo and intensity.

**A12-G07 Character-Specific Reaction** — same pressure should produce different attention/tactics/costs across characters.

**A12-G08 Convention Function Test** — preserve function; reinvent execution.

**A12-G09 Causal Contradiction** — contradiction must alter behavior/relationship.

**A12-G10 Competent Countermodel** — opposition needs real evidence, benefits and interests.

**A12-G11 Asymmetric Leverage Map** — identify who controls access, information, legitimacy, time, trust and technical capacity.

**A12-G12 Specific Vulnerability** — which exact method bypasses normal competence?

**A12-G13 Power Limits** — eliminate “why not just use the stronger capability?” holes.

**A12-G14 Institutional Personhood** — named institutional actors remain people, not mouthpieces.

**A12-G15 Subtext Leverage** — quiet pressure must be legible without explanation.

**A12-G16 Object/Motive Split** — separately specify what is wanted and why this person wants it.

**A12-G17 Qualitative Progression** — later pressure requires different thought/action, not only more danger.

**A12-G18 Revelation-to-Tactic** — important revelation changes the next move.

**A12-G19 Set-Piece Causal Payoff** — major physical sequence leaves a changed action/information/relationship state.

**A12-G20 Rhythm/Tempo/Intensity Separation** — do not use sentence length as a proxy for all three.

**A12-G21 No Deadline Default** — use time pressure only when the world/story genuinely creates it.

**A12-G22 No Personal-Stakes Shortcut** — do not threaten family/romance merely to intensify conflict.

**A12-G23 Local Closure Before Hook** — resolve book-level problem before saga extension.

## 8. MANUSCRIPT / PROJECT VALIDATION

### LESSON ZERO
No evidence from this source justifies reopening a development-locked book. Action craft applies only where a concrete current defect is observed.

### ORBITAL YOUTH
Preserve post-money stakes and conscious-robot personhood. Action pressure should come from responsibility, access, timing, trust, environment, coordination, unique opportunities and real technical limits—not reintroduced wages/credits/rent or consciousness-transfer technology.

### B03 — THE EMPTY RESCUE
Current B03 architecture is a strong candidate for Action-derived validation because it contains rescue geography, anticipatory calls, real emergency operations and evidence problems. Apply the following before prose:
1. each rescue set piece must change both operational state and evidence/model state;
2. do not personify weather/mountain as a literal villain unless POV language clearly marks this as human perception;
3. avoid repeated `warning → dispatch → empty site` geometry without a new tactic/evidence consequence;
4. preserve Jana/Nika/Smith as different tactical minds;
5. major rescue sequence should not be solved by a late capability or coincidence;
6. use terrain/communications/authority as specific limits and leverage, not generic danger;
7. final local solution must close the immediate rescue/security problem before any larger saga anomaly hook.

## 9. FINAL SYNTHESIS

Best IVDIVO abstraction:

`MEANINGFUL HUMAN STATE → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC OBJECT + MOTIVE → COHERENT OPPOSITION / ENVIRONMENT → SPECIFIC TACTICS → REAL LIMITS → QUALITATIVE PROGRESSION → MODULATED PRESSURE → BOUNDED DECISIVE CHOICE → EARNED RESULT → LOCAL AFTERMATH`.

The source is valuable when treated as a tactical/action laboratory. It becomes harmful when its narrow action conventions are generalized into universal laws of fiction, psychology, YA or IVDIVO worldbuilding.
