# 2. STRUCTURE MAP
Fludernik separates several levels too often collapsed in production work:
1. world/story facts and events;
2. discourse selection/order/duration/frequency;
3. narrator/voice and narrative level;
4. focalization/perceptual and knowledge access;
5. language/speech/thought representation;
6. paratext and reception framing;
7. reader reconstruction/model.

The book is descriptive narratology, not a prescriptive story formula. Competing taxonomies are best treated as diagnostic lenses.

# 4. CORE CLAIMS AND MECHANISMS
## W012-M01 — Story / discourse / reader model are distinct
`WORLD FACT / STORY → DISCOURSE SELECTION & ORDER → POV / ACCESS → READER MODEL`.
A weak chapter may have correct canon facts but poor presentation. Do not rebuild canon to repair a discourse problem.

## W012-M02 — Paratext creates a pre-reading model
`COVER / TITLE / BLURB / GENRE MARKING → EXPECTATION FRAME → OPENING TEXT → READER MODEL`.
Opening promise begins before scene one.

## W012-M03 — Primacy has inertia
`EARLY MODEL → INERTIA → CONTRADICTION THRESHOLD → REFRAME`.
If an early reader model is wrong, later reorientation must be causally/evidentially strong enough to replace it.

## W012-M04 — Who speaks / perceives / evaluates are separable
`WHO SPEAKS ≠ WHO PERCEIVES ≠ WHO EVALUATES`.
Grammatical person is not POV depth. Narrator and focalizer are separate diagnostic variables.

## W012-M05 — Fallibility is not unreliability
A limited or mistaken focalizer may be honest. Unreliability belongs to the narrator/discourse relation, not every incorrect character inference.

## W012-M06 — Narrative levels must retain provenance
A character, embedded narrator, archive, institution, specialist school or official document can canonically assert X without X becoming world fact.

IVDIVO chain:
`WORLD FACT → TRACE/SOURCE → SOURCE STATEMENT → POV INTERPRETATION → DISCOURSE PLACEMENT → READER MODEL`.

## W012-M07 — Multiple strands need causal convergence
Parallel plots do not become one story by meeting and recapping.
`STRAND A CHANGE + STRAND B CHANGE → INTERACTION → NEW SHARED STATE / DECISION`.

## W012-M08 — Possible worlds create non-material tension
Character desire, obligation, knowledge and belief worlds can conflict even without physical danger.
For post-money Orbital Youth this supports stakes in trust, consent, belonging, responsibility, role, reputation and relationship.

## W012-M09 — Order / duration / frequency are separate controls
A story event can be:
- placed earlier/later;
- rendered as scene/summary/slowdown/pause;
- narrated once, repeatedly, or iteratively.
This is a high-level anti-microdramatization router.

## W012-M10 — Scene and summary are equal tools
Showing is not inherently superior to telling. Choose by reader function and causal importance.

## W012-M11 — Etic openings buy immediacy with orientation debt
A future-world POV need not explain normal life to itself. But every unexplained axis raises reader orientation load.

## W012-M12 — Reality-effect detail can be legitimate without payoff
Not every object must become a clue or Chekhov gun. Some details establish lived reality, social texture, POV and memory.

## W012-M13 — Constructed discourse does not make a claim false
Selection and narrative shaping do not automatically invalidate factual content. Truth status still depends on source/evidence.

## W012-M14 — Speech representation is a continuum
`REPORT OF SPEECH ACT → INDIRECT → FREE INDIRECT → DIRECT`.
Use this to route routine/process dialogue away from unnecessary real-time staging.

## W012-M15 — Figurative language has a source
Metaphor and image should usually belong to narrator/POV/personality, not a generic authorial lyricism shared by every character.

## W012-M16 — Mind representation need not be fake transcript
Psycho-narration can render nonverbal affect, motive or cognition more naturally than invented word-for-word inner monologue.

## W012-M17 — POV experience is not ontology
A character may genuinely experience an anomaly without the text proving the character's explanation of it.

## W012-M18 — Typologies are diagnostic tools
Use the smallest terminology set that resolves the production problem. Do not force prose to validate Genette/Stanzel/any one school.

## W012-M19 — Medium affordance changes discourse realization
Novel, audio drama, film, game and interactive narrative cannot share presentation rules mechanically even when story function is identical.

## W012-M20 — Early POV classification is provisional
Do not diagnose narrator/focalizer architecture from one paragraph if later context can change the classification.

## W012-M21 — Terminology must cash out in observable effect
`TERM → OBSERVABLE TEXT EFFECT → DIAGNOSIS → REPAIR`.
Jargon without a repair implication is production noise.

## W012-M22 — Description can be narrative
Description can change subjective experience, spatial orientation, attention, symbolism, available action or relationship to setting.

## W012-M23 — Formal novelty decays
Present tense, unusual pronouns and other once-marked devices become normalized during reading. Novelty alone cannot sustain effect.

## W012-M24 — Chapter break is not a plot beat
External segmentation and internal causal structure are related but not identical.

## W012-M25 — Reader evidence outranks imagined universal response
Editors can diagnose likely effects; actual readers provide stronger evidence of repeated confusion/attention patterns. Reader-proposed repairs remain suggestions, not canon.

# 7. IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION
## A. Mystery / fair play
Maintain:
`WORLD FACT → TRACE/SOURCE → CHARACTER BELIEF → DISCOURSE PLACEMENT → READER MODEL`.
Withholding may manipulate order, not invent false authorial facts. Reframes require retrospective support.

## B. Orbital Youth / post-money life
Use selective reality-effect details rather than lectures. Tension can come from conflicts among want/obligation/knowledge worlds: trust, consent, role, mastery, belonging, time, reputation, responsibility, unique opportunity.

## C. Conscious robots
Anthropocentric narratology is not an ontology. A conscious robot may narrate/focalize and remains a person whether or not it imitates human cognition or speech. Human interpretation of robot behavior is not robot motive.

## D. Consciousness schools / metaphysical knowledge
Enforce nested provenance:
`PRACTITIONER OBSERVATION → REPRODUCIBLE EFFECT → SCHOOL INTERPRETATION → COMPETING INTERPRETATION → POSSIBLE ONTOLOGICAL FACT`.
A canonically real school can canonically be wrong.

## E. Ensemble / ONE MAIN STORY
Parallel lives are permitted, but convergence must alter shared action, relationship, evidence or decision. Avoid council/recap scenes as fake convergence.

## F. Anti-"обрубки" revision
Use:
`ONE MAIN STORY / CAUSALITY → SCENE NECESSITY → SHOW/TELL → POV/FOCALIZATION → DIALOGUE MODE → PARAGRAPH INTENT → READER-STATE CONTINUITY → ORDER/DURATION/FREQUENCY → SENTENCE RHYTHM → WORDING`.

## G. Cross-media production
Preserve causal/story function while redesigning discourse for prose, audiobook/audio drama, film/video and interactive forms. Do not import screenplay white-space or prose POV rules unchanged across media.

# 9. SYNTHESIS
Fludernik's highest-value contribution is a disciplined separation of **what is true in the story-world**, **how it is presented**, **who has access**, and **what the reader currently believes**. For IVDIVO this is both a craft tool and a continuity/safety mechanism: it prevents POV belief, institutional assertion or specialist-school interpretation from silently becoming canon truth.
