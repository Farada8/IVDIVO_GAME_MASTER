# 3. CLAIM EXTRACTION

### H-C01 — WORLDING and STORYING are different operations
`WORLDING THE STORY` concerns how interpreters construct a storyworld from cues.  
`STORYING THE WORLD` concerns how persons use narrative to make sense of experience.  
IVDIVO must distinguish what evidence lets the reader infer from what characters/institutions narrativize inside the world.

### H-C02 — Reasons for action are defeasible models
Observed behavior does not transparently reveal motive. Interpreters build provisional reason-models that can be revised by context, history and later evidence.

### H-C03 — One action can have multiple contributory reasons
Immediate desire, long-term project, relationship, social position, history and belief may all matter. One-cause psychology is usually too clean.

### H-C04 — Stories can become causes without becoming true
A family story, institutional explanation, rumor, archive interpretation or self-narrative can shape expectation and action. Causal influence does not upgrade the story to objective truth.

### H-C05 — Storyworld construction is dimensionally selective
Readers infer WHEN / WHAT / WHERE / WHO / HOW / WHY as needed. Exhaustive explanation at first contact is neither necessary nor desirable.

### H-C06 — Person-level explanation resists feature-vector reduction
Conduct becomes intelligible through embodiment, environment, relationships, history, social positions and projects. Category, diagnosis, demographic label or prediction is not a complete person.

### H-C07 — Perspective is distributed architecture, not a pronoun choice
Narrative effect depends on which viewpoints appear, sequence, scope, distance and strategically absent but possible viewpoints.

### H-C08 — Viewpoint omission can be active but must be fair
An omitted viewpoint can enlarge later cognitive repair. Withholding is legitimate only when the chosen perspective architecture genuinely excludes the information.

### H-C09 — Perspective shifts can force model repair
A new viewpoint or contextual fact can reorganize reader interpretation of earlier events without changing those events.

### H-C10 — Dialogue is multimodal social action
Speech, silence, gesture, gaze, embodiment, spatial arrangement, artifacts and prior status jointly position self and others.

### H-C11 — Local talk creates relationship narratives
A speaker can position another as expert, irresponsible child, ally, threat, victim, trusted operator, outsider, etc. Acceptance/resistance of these roles accumulates into the relationship model.

### H-C12 — Emotion labels are mediated interpretations
Felt state, display, culturally available emotion term, another person's interpretation and narrator label are separate layers.

### H-C13 — Retellings are versioned social artifacts
Selection, omission, context, purpose and medium change accounts. Testimony/archive/rumor is not neutral copying.

### H-C14 — Narrative is a tool for chunking experience
Beginnings/endings bound otherwise continuous experience into manipulable segments. A bounded narrative can be complete without explaining everything beyond its scope.

### H-C15 — Narrative supports causal inference but can overclaim causality
Readers readily connect sequential events into causal structures. `AFTER X` must not silently become `BECAUSE OF X` without adequate support.

### H-C16 — Typification supports understanding but can require reclassification
Narratives rely on expectation/categories, but expectation-violating behavior can pressure interpreters to revise the category rather than force the person into it.

### H-C17 — Narrative can model action over time
Order, duration and frequency allow present behavior to be related to prior patterns and later consequences. Time manipulation is an action-modeling instrument, not merely style.

### H-C18 — Narrative duration allocates attention and implied value
Scene/summary/pause/ellipsis change how much interpretive weight an event receives. Duration is not equivalent to objective importance, but it strongly affects reader attention.

### H-C19 — Repeated narration can mark salience
Frequency can foreground a past act, habit or memory. Repetition should therefore be used intentionally rather than as accidental thematic restatement.

### H-C20 — Narrative enables counterfactual action modeling
A story can compare actual action with plausible alternatives, supporting regret, planning and revised models. Counterfactuals should expose choice architecture rather than announce author lessons.

### H-C21 — Narrative overgeneration is a real epistemic failure
A storyline that can absorb every possible fact and can never be falsified becomes confabulation/conspiracy logic. Strong interpretation retains possible disconfirmation.

### H-C22 — Narrative undergeneration is the opposite failure
A model can also remain too fragmentary to support action or explanation. The goal is neither totalizing story nor disconnected facts, but bounded explanatory sufficiency.

### H-C23 — Transformation motifs do not imply one ontology
The Worked Example comparing metamorphosis narratives demonstrates that superficially similar transformations can have radically different causal, temporal and ontological designs.

### H-C24 — Embodiment changes action space
When embodiment changes, available movement, sensation, social treatment, risk and affordances should change. Otherwise embodiment is decorative.

### H-C25 — Local causality can be knowable while global causality remains unknown
A story can establish that action A caused local effect B while retaining genuine uncertainty about the larger origin/system. This is highly useful for IVDIVO mystery and metaphysical restraint.

### H-C26 — Embedded stories distribute intelligence across people and time
Frames, testimony, lessons, archives and retellings can carry knowledge beyond one person's direct experience.

### H-C27 — Embedded narratives need function
Stories-within-stories may be actional, explanatory or thematic. An archive/history passage that changes nothing in the present is likely lore rather than story.

### H-C28 — Apprenticeship can transmit interpretive skill, not just information
Guided participation, stories and worked cases can teach ways of reasoning. This is especially useful where expertise depends on long practice rather than database access.

### H-C29 — Space becomes place through lived and social relations
A location gains narrative force when attached to practice, memory, rights, conflict, identity or action—not through scenic inventory alone.

### H-C30 — Environment can scaffold cognition
Maps, markers, interfaces, architecture, tools, bodies and social arrangements can carry part of the reasoning load. This permits worldbuilding to occur through use rather than explanation.

### H-C31 — Narrative distributes intelligence
Solutions can emerge from networks of people, records, tools, institutions and environments rather than a single protagonist's cognition.

### H-C32 — Narrative can model reasons across temporal scales
Earlier acts can make current behavior intelligible; later consequences can revise understanding of earlier acts. However, retrospective coherence is not proof of the inferred motive.

### H-C33 — Stories can make behavior intelligible while remaining uncertain
The fact that a storyline explains observed conduct does not prove that storyline is the true causal history.

### H-C34 — Narrative overneatness damages reliability
When a character or investigator smooths rough edges and removes inconvenient transitions, the resulting story may feel more coherent precisely because it is less trustworthy.

### H-C35 — The book itself argues against one-way scientific borrowing
The Coda explicitly warns that narrative scholars can import disputed cognitive-science concepts without registering the debates inside those sciences. Transdisciplinary convergence is preferable to treating one field as final authority.

# 4. MECHANISM EXTRACTION / PRODUCTION GATES

### H-M01 — REASONS MODEL
`OBSERVED ACTION → MULTIPLE POSSIBLE REASONS → CONTEXT/HISTORY → PROVISIONAL MODEL → NEW EVIDENCE → MODEL UPDATE`.

### H-M02 — BEHAVIOR / REASON / CONSENT FIREWALL
`PREDICTED BEHAVIOR ≠ REASON ≠ CONSENT ≠ RIGHT TO ALLOCATE BURDEN`.
A system may predict what someone will do and still lack knowledge of motive or normative authority to use that behavior as a resource.

### H-M03 — PERSON-LEVEL INTEGRITY
Before reducing a character to category/trait/diagnosis/model output, ask what embodiment, environment, relationship, history and project make the action intelligible at person level.

### H-M04 — WORLDING / STORYING SEPARATION
Track separately:
1. what evidence supports;
2. what a character/institution says it means;
3. what story that interpretation causes them to act on.

### H-M05 — STORY-AS-CAUSE
`NARRATIVE MODEL → EXPECTATION → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE → MODEL REVISED OR DEFENDED`.

### H-M06 — TEMPORAL RECONTEXTUALIZATION
Use flashback/flashforward/iteration only when temporal relocation changes motive-model, stakes, relationship, evidence status or later choice.

### H-M07 — MULTICAUSAL MOTIVE GATE
For major surprising behavior, generate several plausible contributory reasons before locking a single explanation.

### H-M08 — READER WORLD-DIMENSION BUDGET
Answer WHEN / WHAT / WHERE / WHO / HOW / WHY only as needed for orientation, consequence and inference. Do not frontload the entire world model.

### H-M09 — RETELLING PROVENANCE
`EVENT → WITNESS/TELLER → VERSION/PURPOSE → OMISSION/EMPHASIS → RECEIVER → MODEL CHANGE → ACTION`.

### H-M10 — PERSPECTIVE MATRIX
`AVAILABLE VIEWPOINTS → ACTUAL VIEWPOINT BY SEGMENT → OMITTED VIEWPOINTS → INFORMATION CONSEQUENCE → LATER REPAIR COST`.

### H-M11 — VIEWPOINT OMISSION FAIRNESS
Do not hide from the reader information that the chosen focal consciousness actually knows solely to manufacture surprise.

### H-M12 — DISTRIBUTED INTELLIGENCE
`PERSON A EVIDENCE + PERSON B INTERPRETATION + TOOL/RECORD + ENVIRONMENTAL CONSTRAINT + PERSON C DECISION → EMERGENT SOLUTION`.

### H-M13 — CATEGORY REVISION
`INITIAL CATEGORY → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC BEHAVIOR → CATEGORY STRAIN → RETYPIFICATION / REVISED PERSON MODEL`.

### H-M14 — POSITIONING LEDGER
`WHO POSITIONS WHOM AS WHAT → ACCEPT / RESIST / REVERSE → RELATIONSHIP CONSEQUENCE`.

### H-M15 — SCENE-OF-TALK MULTIMODALITY
`WORDS + SILENCE + GESTURE + GAZE + BODY + SPACE + ARTIFACT + PRIOR STATUS`.

### H-M16 — EMOTION STATUS FIREWALL
Separate:
`FELT STATE / DISPLAY / CULTURAL LABEL / OTHER'S INTERPRETATION / NARRATOR LABEL`.

### H-M17 — CHUNKING / SCOPE CONTRACT
Define what the current book/case actually starts and ends. Closure of that bounded problem does not require closure of the whole saga ontology.

### H-M18 — CAUSALITY EVIDENCE GATE
For every important `X caused Y` assertion track:
`TEMPORAL ORDER → MECHANISM/BRIDGE → ALTERNATIVE CAUSE → DISCONFIRMING TEST`.
Do not let narrative fluency substitute for causal evidence.

### H-M19 — CANONICITY / BREACH / RETYPIFICATION
`EXPECTED PATTERN → VIOLATION → FIRST EXPLANATION → REPEATED/CONTROLLED EVIDENCE → REVISE CATEGORY OR RULE`.
Useful for conscious robots, unfamiliar cultures and anomaly narratives.

### H-M20 — ACTION-MODELING TIME MATRIX
For an important choice test:
`PRIOR PATTERN / CURRENT ACTION / LATER CONSEQUENCE / COUNTERFACTUAL ALTERNATIVE`.
Use only the temporal layers that materially change interpretation.

### H-M21 — NARRATIVE OVERGENERATION TEST
Ask what evidence could falsify the current theory. If the answer is `nothing`, downgrade it from explanation to unfalsifiable narrative.

### H-M22 — NARRATIVE UNDERGENERATION TEST
If facts remain too disconnected to support a bounded operational decision, specify the next evidence needed rather than inventing a total theory.

### H-M23 — TRANSFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
For any transformation/body-state phenomenon:
`WHAT CHANGES → WHAT PERSISTS → SENSORIUM → ACTION AFFORDANCES → SOCIAL/RISK CHANGE → REVERSIBILITY → EVIDENCE OF IDENTITY CONTINUITY`.
This is a narrative diagnostic, **not permission for human consciousness-transfer technology**.

### H-M24 — LOCAL / GLOBAL CAUSALITY SPLIT
`LOCAL MECHANISM PROVEN` may coexist with `GLOBAL ORIGIN UNKNOWN`.
Protect series mystery from premature ontology answers.

### H-M25 — EMBEDDED-STORY FUNCTION GATE
An archive, testimony, legend or training anecdote earns full dramatization only if it changes present action, interpretation, relationship or evidence status.

### H-M26 — APPRENTICESHIP KNOWLEDGE GATE
For specialist schools:
`LINEAGE → PRACTICE → GUIDED CASE → REPRODUCIBLE EFFECT → INTERPRETIVE MODEL → DISAGREEMENT / LIMIT`.
Knowledge that requires embodied practice cannot be assumed to scale merely because robots/AI can reproduce texts or facilities.

### H-M27 — PLACE-MAKING GATE
`SPATIAL FEATURE → LIVED/SOCIAL MEANING → WHAT IT ENABLES/CONSTRAINS → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE`.

### H-M28 — ENVIRONMENTAL COGNITIVE SCAFFOLD
Permit maps, interfaces, signage, artifacts, robot bodies, architecture and local experts to carry reasoning burden. Avoid protagonist exposition monopoly.

### H-M29 — DISTRIBUTED CLIMAX
Separate functions:
`EVIDENCE HOLDER / INTERPRETER / TOOL OPERATOR / LIVED-EXPERIENCE WITNESS / COUNTERMODEL / DECISION OWNER`.
One protagonist need not discover, understand and solve everything.

### H-M30 — RETROSPECTIVE COHERENCE FIREWALL
`GOOD EXPLANATORY STORY ≠ VERIFIED CAUSAL HISTORY`.
When a late explanation makes everything fit, preserve at least one explicit test/source boundary before upgrading it to fact.

### H-M31 — REASON-MODEL CONFIDENCE
For important motive inferences label:
`PLAUSIBLE / SUPPORTED / CORROBORATED / CONTESTED / UNKNOWN`.
Do not convert a psychologically satisfying explanation into certainty.

### H-M32 — SCIENCE / CRAFT DOUBLE ENTRY
When a narrative framework is useful but its scientific basis may be disputed, store separately:
`CRAFT UTILITY` and `SCIENTIFIC STATUS`.

LaPlante and Ribó protect organic form, POV, scene/summary and causal diagnosis. Herman adds that causality itself is an interpretive construction that can overgenerate.

## Herman ↔ McKee / Truby
McKee/Truby emphasize causal structure; Herman provides an epistemic corrective: coherent causal story is useful for action but not automatically true.  
IVDIVO keeps strong causal prose while distinguishing `story causality required for plot` from `character/investigator causal hypothesis requiring evidence`.

## Herman ↔ Cambridge SF / IVDIVO worldbuilding
SF encourages explanation of unfamiliar systems; Herman shows readers can build partial storyworlds and recruit environmental scaffolds.  
Therefore future-tech explanation should emerge through action, interface, place, comparison and consequences rather than encyclopedia delivery.

# 7. IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION

## ONE MAIN STORY
Use distributed intelligence without fragmenting causal unity. Multiple characters may carry different evidence, interpretations and tools, but all major lines must change the same dominant book-level problem.

## Mystery / evidence
Mandatory ledger fields for consequential inference:
`OBSERVATION / SOURCE / INTERPRETATION / CONFIDENCE / ALTERNATIVE / DISCONFIRMING TEST / ACTION CONSEQUENCE`.

Use a **proof ceiling**. A book can solve the local operational problem while retaining global ontology as UNKNOWN.

## Conscious robots
A robot's predicted behavior, architecture or category does not determine motive. Personhood must be written through projects, relationships, embodiment, choices and refusals. If a robot violates a human category, first consider category revision rather than `malfunction` or anthropomorphic assimilation.

## Consciousness / dimensional schools
Use:
`OBSERVATION → REPRODUCIBLE PRACTICE EFFECT → SCHOOL INTERPRETATION → COMPETING INTERPRETATION → POSSIBLE ONTOLOGY`.
Add apprenticeship/lineage when skill requires long embodied practice. High industrial/AI capability does not eliminate scarcity of deeply trained practitioners.

## Post-money orbital life
Narrative causal defaults must not silently reconstruct Earth money/rent/status-consumption. Show alternative coordination through lived decisions and nonfungible constraints (time, attention, trust, unique access, mission place, consent, relationship, responsibility).

## POV
Track perspective as architecture, not merely pronouns. Omitted minds are allowed only when the focal design fairly excludes them. A late POV expansion should justify the cognitive repair it creates.

## Dialogue / relationships
Track role-positioning in addition to content. A teenager and adult may be arguing about procedure while simultaneously contesting `child vs operator`, `student vs colleague`, or `outsider vs stakeholder` roles.

## Worldbuilding / place
Use physical space as cognitive and social infrastructure. A ring, corridor, gravity regime, school, workshop or transit route earns detail when it changes what characters can infer/do and who has standing there.

## Reader effect / style
Temporal manipulation (duration/order/frequency) should model action and attention, not merely display technique. Suspense can slow down when close reasoning/embodied processing matters.

# 9. FINAL SYNTHESIS

The most important Herman contribution to IVDIVO is a discipline of **modeling without overclaiming**.

Core production loop:

`OBSERVED WORLD / ACTION → PARTIAL STORYWORLD → MULTIPLE REASON MODELS → SOURCE/PERSPECTIVE CHECK → CAUSAL MODEL → TEST / NEW EVIDENCE → MODEL REVISION → ACTION`.

And its central firewall:

`COHERENT STORY ≠ VERIFIED CAUSE ≠ MOTIVE ≠ CONSENT ≠ METAPHYSICAL TRUTH`.

This lets IVDIVO remain strongly causal and readable while preserving epistemic honesty, independent personhood and series-scale mystery.
