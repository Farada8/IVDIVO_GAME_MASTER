# 3. PRELIMINARY OBSERVATIONS — NOT FINAL CLAIMS

## Scene / subtext / tension / intention
- Scene intention = immediate planned action; motivation = why it matters; plot intention = larger desired outcome.
- Strong causal recursion: `prior consequence → immediate intention → action → resistance/support → result → next intention`.
- Subtext works as `surface action + second inferable meaning`; do not decode it immediately afterward.
- Tension is meaningful uncertainty/power/expectation gap, not necessarily argument.
- Suspense can slow down through detail and interior attention; short syntax is not a suspense requirement.
- Allies/support are causal; endless thwarting is not a virtue.
- Contemplative scenes are valid when processing changes interpretation, decision, relationship or readiness.
- Direct dialogue is justified when exact wording changes power, trust, consent, evidence, relation, decision or emotion.

## Flashbacks — Chapter 18
- Flashback is a real scene in past time, not a backstory dump.
- Strong present-value test: after the flashback, the reader/character model of the present must change.
- Good functions: present plot causality, necessary character history, suspense, or a deliberate parallel past line that later converges.
- Time transitions must be legible; memory cue/date/incident/tense shift may help.
- Rosenfeld’s tendency to prefer shorter/faster flashbacks is a heuristic, not a universal law for dual-timeline fiction.
- Failure modes: **BACKSTORY EXCURSION**, **PAST-AS-EXPLANATION**, **TIME-TRANSITION JOLT**, **PARALLEL-STORY ORPHAN**.

## Climactic scenes — Chapter 19
- Useful core: climax should grow from the inciting disturbance and prior causal pressure; it should test the protagonist and permanently alter the decision environment.
- Strong mechanism: preceding scenes can narrow options and create readiness without mandatory cliffhangers.
- A climax needs room for emotional content even under fast action; focused detail may deliberately slow the peak.
- Rosenfeld’s `one major Triumph`, `protagonist reigns victorious`, universally rising intensity, and `all later scenes slower` are **school-specific / overprescriptive**, not IVDIVO laws.
- IVDIVO allows ensemble, procedural, tragic, ambiguous, social-SF, mystery and bounded-decision climaxes where victory is not the correct frame.
- Post-climax should resolve consequences rather than inject unrelated new plot machinery, but series hooks may remain after local closure.

## Epiphany scenes — Chapter 20
- Strong core: insight must be **earned by prior events**, not downloaded by the author.
- Functional chain: `accumulated evidence/pressure → old model fails → recognition → choice/change`.
- Epiphany should cost something or materially change behavior/readiness; otherwise it is commentary.
- Multiple smaller realizations are possible; Rosenfeld’s suggested three-part distribution is heuristic only.
- Identity realization must not be used as a generic dramatic device detached from lived character evidence.
- Failure modes: **LESSON DOWNLOAD**, **EPIPHANY WITHOUT ACTION**, **SYMBOLIC PREANNOUNCEMENT**.

## Final scenes — Chapter 21
- Useful core: final scene should show the consequence of the main story and leave a stable emotional/causal state, not reopen the entire book.
- Final scene may be reflective, image-driven, action-driven, relational or quiet; it need not explain all feeling already demonstrated.
- Final actions/images are strongest when they arise from previously established character/theme rather than being manufactured as a symbolic flourish.
- Final sentences carry disproportionate reader weight; avoid thematic slogan-writing.
- Rosenfeld overstates that final scenes should generally be slower, reflective and low-action. Genre/structure may require a different finish.
- Strong IVDIVO rule: **local conflict closes before saga hook**.

# 4. PRELIMINARY CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS
- Bell + Rosenfeld: suspense is meaningful uncertainty; pace may accelerate or deliberately slow.
- Card/Kress/Hodgins + Rosenfeld: behavior/epiphany requires causal preparation; POV and setting filter what information matters.
- Chatman + Rosenfeld: contemplative/relationship/epistemic causality can justify scenes without large external events.
- Zunshine + Rosenfeld: information release must preserve `source → observation → inference → confidence`.
- IVDIVO correction to Rosenfeld climax doctrine: `CAUSAL CONVERGENCE → BOUNDED CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE`, not mandatory `Triumph`.
