# 3. PRELIMINARY OBSERVATIONS — NOT YET FINAL CLAIMS

These are notes from the actually read material only and remain subject to correction by later chapters.

1. Rosenfeld treats scenes as multi-element systems rather than single conflict units: protagonist, allies/opposition, POV, real-time momentum, new plot information, tension, setting, imagery and controlled summary.
2. She explicitly allows variable mixtures: physical action, dialogue, very small actions, setting interaction, and some narrative summary.
3. `Show, don't tell` is reframed usefully as **demonstrate, don't lecture / trust the reader**, rather than a ban on summary.
4. Scene length is functional and rhythmic; she warns that too many short scenes can become choppy. Numeric page ranges are heuristics, not IVDIVO gates.
5. Scene intention should arise from plot context; she explicitly warns against free-floating intentions/vignettes.
6. A new scene launch should answer where the characters were left, what matters now, what the character wants, and what will help/resist that want.
7. Action launches favor demonstration before explanation, but this must later be cross-checked against contexts where orientation or voice-first openings are superior.

These observations already converge with prior IVDIVO gates on causal intention, reader trust and anti-fragment rhythm, but no new production law is finalized until full read completes.
