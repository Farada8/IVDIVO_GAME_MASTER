## PROVISIONAL CLAIMS FROM STRICT READ
### W005-C01 — Reconnaissance before tactics
A writer should understand the actual constraints and objective of the current task before choosing craft tactics. IVDIVO translation: diagnose book/stage/version first; do not apply a craft trick because it is available.

### W005-C02 — Process discipline can outperform bursts
Regular production creates learning and finished artifacts. However, Bell's quota rhetoric is a process option, not a universal requirement or a reason to preserve low-quality output.

### W005-C03 — Long-term craft orientation is more robust than status chasing
The source repeatedly distinguishes improvement of the page from anxiety about recognition. For IVDIVO: optimize story quality and production evidence, not symbolic completion metrics.

### W005-C04 — Publishing outcomes contain high uncertainty
Bell's `nobody knows anything` discussion usefully warns against reverse-engineering one bestseller's outcome into a deterministic formula. For IVDIVO this supports anti-template and alternative-engine comparison.

### W005-C05 — Market realism and artistic value are separate variables
A work can have artistic strengths while market outcomes remain uncertain. Do not use historical publishing economics as a narrative model for post-money orbital society.

## PROVISIONAL MECHANISMS
### W005-M01 — STAGE-FIRST DIAGNOSIS
`WHAT STAGE IS THIS? → WHAT IS ACTUALLY BROKEN? → WHICH TOOL FITS? → APPLY → RETEST`.

### W005-M02 — PRODUCTION DISCIPLINE WITHOUT METRIC WORSHIP
Use regular production/checkpoints to accumulate craft learning, but never treat word count, chapter count or cadence percentage as proof of quality.

### W005-M03 — OUTCOME UNCERTAINTY FIREWALL
Do not infer universal story laws from a successful title, market event or single reference novel. Extract mechanism, compare alternatives, validate on the manuscript.
