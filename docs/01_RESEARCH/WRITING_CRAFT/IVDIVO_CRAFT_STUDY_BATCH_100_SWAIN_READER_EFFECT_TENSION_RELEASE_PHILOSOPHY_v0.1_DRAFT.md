## 1. What Chapter 5 actually establishes

Swain treats fiction as a designed reader experience. The useful abstraction is not his narrow claim that readers primarily buy fear/tension, but the more general production distinction:

`STORY STATE CHANGE != READER EFFECT`

A scene can be causally correct while emotionally flat; it can also be emotionally vivid while failing to change the story. IVDIVO should track both.

### Reader-effect vector
For every major sequence, identify the intended dominant effect:
- curiosity,
- hope,
- worry,
- intimacy,
- wonder,
- competence pleasure,
- humor,
- beauty with consequence,
- mystery,
- anticipation,
- relational tension,
- ethical uncertainty,
- earned relief,
- partial answer / model revision.

No one effect is mandatory in every scene.

## 2. Tension and release — keep the mechanism, reject the monoculture

Swain's useful mechanism:

`TENSION / UNCERTAINTY -> SUSTAINED ATTENTION -> RESOLUTION / RELEASE`

IVDIVO correction:

`READER ENGAGEMENT -> VARIED PRESSURE / REWARD -> EARNED PAYOFF / CHANGED STATE`

The book's 1965 commercial-fiction assumption that the habitual reader is fundamentally a fear/tension consumer is too narrow to become a universal law. Quiet competence, discovery, romance, humor, ordinary-life specificity, beauty, belonging and successful cooperation can also generate forward pull.

### Production gate — TENSION MONOCULTURE
If three or more adjacent units rely on the same reader effect (danger, worry, withholding, dread), require variation unless repetition is intentionally escalating a single mechanism.

## 3. Reader satisfaction is not mandatory poetic justice

Swain argues for a moral reward structure, but critically he also states that writers with a different philosophy should create a different pattern consistent with their own beliefs. That concession is the key usable principle.

IVDIVO rule:

`READER SATISFACTION != VIRTUE MUST WIN`

Preferred ending model:

`BOOK PROMISE / QUESTION -> DECISIVE CHOICE OR ACTION -> CONSEQUENCE -> COST -> AFTERMATH -> CHANGED STATE`

Possible outcomes include:
- earned success,
- bounded success with cost,
- correct refusal,
- partial answer,
- relationship repair without total agreement,
- tactical failure with epistemic gain,
- unresolved global ontology with resolved local problem.

No automatic reward for virtue, punishment for transgression, black moment, or moral sermon.

---

## 4. Philosophy firewall

Swain's claims about what 'most American readers' believe, the inevitability of social reward for virtue, guilt as universal punishment, and commercial rejection of cynical outcomes are historically situated authorial judgments.

They are **not**:
- modern reader psychology,
- moral science,
- political science,
- IVDIVO social canon,
- proof about consciousness or metaphysics.

### Gate
Whenever a craft source moves from 'this pattern can create reader satisfaction' to 'life/society/human nature works this way', downgrade the latter to `SOURCE WORLDVIEW / HISTORICAL CLAIM` unless independently verified.

## 5. Cross-source synthesis

### With Iglesias
`CHARACTER FEELS X != READER FEELS X`.
Swain's reader-response focus is retained, while Iglesias prevents us from equating displayed emotion with reader emotion.

### With Clark
Swain's tension/release needs Clark's reader rewards. A middle made solely of increasing worry becomes emotionally monotonous.

### With Lodge / Francine Prose / Le Guin / Zinsser
Reader pressure does **not** imply short-sentence density. Syntax follows intended reader effect.

### With LaPlante / Lamott
Generate live material first; diagnose reader effect afterward. Do not run all reader-manipulation gates during sentence birth.

### With McKee / Bell
Local closure is required, but it need not take the form of knockout, final battle, poetic justice or maximal catastrophe.

## 6. New failure modes

1. **Tension Monoculture** — every scene tries to make the reader worry.
2. **Constant Danger Loop** — danger replaces causal variety.
3. **Sympathy/Stress Inflation** — injury, humiliation or helplessness inserted to force caring.
4. **Poetic-Justice Lock** — moral reward/punishment substitutes for earned consequence.
5. **Reader-Effect Substitution** — emotional intensity used to hide weak causality.
6. **Security-as-Universal-Need** — one historical theory of satisfaction treated as human universal.
7. **Resolution-as-Sedation** — ending designed only to remove discomfort instead of resolving the book's contract.
8. **Philosophy-to-World-Fact Leak** — craft author's worldview becomes IVDIVO ontology.
9. **Fear-Only Suspense** — hope, curiosity, anticipation, desire and relationship pressure ignored.

## 7. IVDIVO production gates

### Gate B100-01 — Reader Effect Vector
For each major sequence: intended effect + causal basis + payoff.

### Gate B100-02 — Engagement Palette
No default to danger when competence, curiosity, intimacy, wonder, humor or responsibility can carry the sequence better.

### Gate B100-03 — Tension/Release Rhythm
Track pressure and release across the book; do not maximize pressure continuously.

### Gate B100-04 — Earned Resolution
Resolution must arise from prior choices, evidence, relationships and world rules; not authorial moral reward.

### Gate B100-05 — Philosophy Firewall
Source worldview never upgrades automatically to storyworld truth.

### Gate B100-06 — YA Bond Without Trauma Purchase
Young characters can earn reader bond through responsibility, competence, awkwardness, belonging, first autonomy, relationships, humor and meaningful mistakes.

### Gate B100-07 — Conscious Robot Personhood
Do not manufacture sympathy through petlike helplessness or hidden 'almost-human' qualities. Require own wants, relationships, consent/refusal and consequences.

### Gate B100-08 — Consciousness-School Epistemics
Emotional reassurance or mystical payoff never upgrades school interpretation into ontology.

## 8. Early Chapter 6 provisional extraction — NOT a completed chapter

The opening of Chapter 6 introduces a five-part commercial-story lineup: character, situation, objective, opponent, disaster, then compresses the concept into a statement plus a question.

Useful abstraction for IVDIVO:

`FOCAL AGENT / OWNER + CURRENT SITUATION + TRACKABLE OBJECTIVE + MEANINGFUL RESISTANCE + HIGH-COST FAILURE CONDITION`

Corrections:
- an opponent need not be a villain or even a person;
- a 'disaster' need not be catastrophe or death;
- story question need not be binary if the actual engine is an operational mystery or ethical choice;
- intellectual/mystery pleasure is not subordinate by definition;
- the two-sentence exercise is a compression diagnostic, not a mandatory blueprint.
