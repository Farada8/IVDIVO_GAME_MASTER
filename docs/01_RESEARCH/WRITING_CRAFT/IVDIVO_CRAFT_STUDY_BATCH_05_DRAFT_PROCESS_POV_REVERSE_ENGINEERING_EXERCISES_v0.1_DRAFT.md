# 1. LAMOTT — BIRD BY BIRD

## 1.1 Small execution units reduce cognitive overload

Lamott’s “short assignments” mechanism is production-useful: a writer can become immobilized by the total scale of a novel and recover motion by reducing the immediate task to one bounded piece.

Transferable mechanism:

`LARGE PROJECT → BOUNDED CURRENT UNIT → FINISH CURRENT UNIT → NEXT UNIT`

### IVDIVO LIMIT
This must **not** become “write random little scenes and hope a novel appears.” The Founder’s specification-first law remains higher-order:

`BOOK CONTRACT / MAIN STORY FROZEN → CURRENT APPROVED UNIT → EXECUTE COMPLETELY`

Short assignments solve execution overload, not story architecture.

## 1.2 Exploratory first drafts are discovery instruments

Lamott argues that first drafts are often rough because the writer is still discovering what the material is. The useful mechanism is separation of generative and critical modes.

### IVDIVO PHASE SPLIT
**DISCOVERY LAB:** rough exploratory prose, voice tests, scene prototypes, alternative POVs are permitted.  
**SPECIFICATION FREEZE:** main story, ending obligations, clue logic, continuity, character trajectories are locked.  
**PRODUCTION DRAFT:** prose executes approved design.  
**CHANGE REQUEST:** if drafting reveals a structural truth that invalidates the frozen design, the writer does not silently improvise a new book; the issue is escalated to architecture and deliberately re-frozen.

This reconciles Lamott’s discovery process with IVDIVO’s specification discipline.

## 1.3 Plot grows from character—but still requires structure

Lamott strongly argues that characters should not be pawns of an imposed plot. Yet her own “Plot Treatment” chapter demonstrates the opposite failure mode: vivid characters, tone, and prose can still produce a novel with no structural foundation.

The combined lesson is therefore not “ignore plot.” It is:

`SPECIFIC CHARACTER → CHARACTER-TRUE CHOICES → CAUSAL PLOT → STRUCTURAL TREATMENT → REWRITE`

### IVDIVO TEST — CHARACTER / STRUCTURE DOUBLE GATE
A plot turn must pass both:
1. **Character truth:** why does this person choose this action?
2. **Story necessity:** how does this action cause or alter the main-story chain?

Passing only one is insufficient.

## 1.4 Chapter A→B treatment

Lamott describes rebuilding a novel through a chapter-by-chapter treatment that identifies where a chapter starts, where it ends, what moves the characters between the two, and how the end of one chapter leads organically into the next.

This is directly operational:

`CHAPTER ENTRY STATE A → CAUSAL MOVEMENT → EXIT STATE B → B CREATES NEXT CHAPTER A`

### IVDIVO CHAPTER CAUSALITY TEST
For every chapter:
`ENTRY STATE | CHAPTER OBJECTIVE | MAJOR TURN | EXIT STATE | NEXT-CHAPTER CAUSE`

If the next chapter begins because “the outline says so,” rather than because the previous state generates it, the chapter chain is weak.

## 1.5 False starts are model correction

Lamott treats false starts as evidence that the writer’s first model of a character/story may be wrong. Repeated observation can strip away stereotype and surface assumption.

### IVDIVO USE
False starts belong in **Discovery Lab**, where they can test:
- wrong character conception;
- false scene purpose;
- wrong POV;
- false climax image;
- tone mismatch.

### Failure mode
After freeze, “the character surprised me” is not authorization for uncontrolled continuity/story drift. Structural surprise becomes a Change Request.

## 1.6 Dialogue is compressed, dramatized speech

Lamott emphasizes that fiction dialogue is not a transcription of actual speech. It translates the sound/rhythm of a character into more concise and dramatic language.

### IVDIVO DIALOGUE SOUND TEST
Read important dialogue aloud and check:
- character-specific rhythm;
- unnecessary exposition;
- dead literal transcription;
- whether each line feels created in response to the previous pressure;
- whether the exchange contains action, not only information.

## 1.7 Set research creates credible specificity

Lamott’s “Set Design” process shows that the writer need not personally possess expertise; they can research and consult people who do.

### IVDIVO WORLD-SPECIFICITY RULE
When a scene depends on a profession/place/technology:
`KNOWLEDGE GAP → SOURCE/EXPERT/REFERENCE → CONCRETE DETAILS → POV-FILTERED USE`

Do not fill gaps with generic “futuristic” prose.

## 1.8 Voice cannot be borrowed as final product

Lamott explicitly treats imitation of admired writers as a temporary prop: it may help a writer learn, but it must be relinquished.

### IVDIVO SOURCE-DISTANCE RULE
Study source voice at mechanism level:
- sentence length distribution;
- distance;
- compression;
- humor placement;
- image density;
- dialogue/narration ratio;
without reproducing distinctive diction, syntax patterns, imagery, or signature cadence.

## 1.9 “Done” requires more than intuition in production

Lamott describes completion partly as the point where repeated revision has solved major problems and the writer has done the best possible work for now.

IVDIVO needs a stricter production variant:

`AUTHOR COMPLETION SIGNAL + OBJECTIVE GATES + RED TEAM + CONTINUITY PASS + RESIDUAL ISSUE LOG`

Subjective exhaustion cannot certify a book.

# 2. BERNAYS & PAINTER — WHAT IF?

## 2.1 Writing like a writer vs thinking like a writer

The authors distinguish:
- **craft control**: dialogue vs summary, modifiers, specificity, time/place, technique;
- **writerly thinking**: curiosity, imagination, skepticism, looking beneath apparent meaning.

This distinction is valuable for IVDIVO’s “100-specialist” model because technical correctness alone can produce dead fiction.

### IVDIVO DOUBLE AUDIT
For a scene ask:

**CRAFT:** Is POV controlled? Is dialogue functional? Is time/place clear? Is prose specific?  
**DISCOVERY:** What is not what it seems? What motive/meaning lies below the first explanation? What contradiction deserves pressure?

## 2.2 Character + unstable situation + alternatives → plot

A central plotting exercise places a particular character in a situation with opposing forces and real alternatives. The character’s way of perceiving and choosing drives the plot.

Operational mechanism:

`SPECIFIC CHARACTER + UNSTABLE SITUATION + OPPOSING FORCES + REAL ALTERNATIVES → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE → MORE GRAVE SITUATION → CRISIS → CHANGE`

This triangulates Lamott, Egri, Hodgins, Storr, and the Founder’s story-first law.

## 2.3 Plot is arranged causality, not chronology

The book draws on Burroway/Forster to distinguish simple event sequence from plot that reveals dramatic, emotional, and thematic significance.

### IVDIVO “AND THEN / WHY” TEST
Between major beats, replace “and then” with:
- because;
- therefore;
- but because;
- which made X decide.

If causal connectors repeatedly fail, the manuscript is episodic.

## 2.4 Psychic distance is a controllable variable

Using Gardner’s model, the book demonstrates that narrative distance can move from remote/formal to close/interior, but careless jumps are jarring.

### IVDIVO DISTANCE MAP
For each scene define:
`ENTRY DISTANCE | TARGET CLOSEST DISTANCE | TRIGGER FOR ZOOM-IN | EXIT DISTANCE`

A sudden shift into interiority must be motivated by perspective and syntax, not accidental head movement.

## 2.5 Style can be measured diagnostically

The style exercise proposes objective checks:
- sentence-length variation;
- modifier density;
- sentence-structure variation;
- diction/syllable profile;
- verb/passive patterns.

These are not quality formulas. They are excellent **symptom detectors**.

### IVDIVO PROSE FINGERPRINT
For a sample from each core POV calculate/inspect:
`SENTENCE LENGTH RANGE | PARAGRAPH LENGTH RANGE | MODIFIER RATE | PASSIVE RATE | REPEATED OPENINGS | DOMINANT SYNTAX | ABSTRACT/CONCRETE BALANCE`

Use comparisons to find monotony or accidental sameness, never to imitate an external author’s numerical fingerprint.

## 2.6 Revision should isolate variables

The revision section explicitly recommends evaluating elements separately: structure, POV, tense, characters, language, scene length, imagery, body language, description.

### IVDIVO REVISION ORDER
This supports staged editing rather than chaotic “improve everything at once”:

`MAIN STORY → STRUCTURE → CHARACTER/RELATIONSHIP → POV/KNOWLEDGE → SCENE FUNCTION → DIALOGUE → DESCRIPTION/IMAGE → RHYTHM/STYLE → COPYEDIT`

## 2.7 Exercises are laboratories, not commandments

The deeper value of *What If?* is not the specific prompt list; it is the idea of varying one parameter to discover what changes.

### IVDIVO CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT FORMAT
`KEEP CONSTANT: story fact / character goal / canon`  
`CHANGE ONE VARIABLE: POV / tactic / distance / line opening / ending mode / scene order`  
`COMPARE: emotional force / clarity / causality / suspense / voice`  
`ACCEPT OR REJECT`.

This turns writing exercises into an auditable design method.

# 3. PUSHMAN — HOW TO READ LIKE A WRITER

## 3.1 Read mechanisms, not prestige

Pushman’s core instruction is to read like a mechanic studies an engine: identify how the text works.

A writer should inspect:
- genre/form;
- plot/arc;
- conflict;
- structure;
- character;
- POV;
- setting;
- scene;
- language/voice.

This gives IVDIVO a formal method for reverse engineering fiction references.

## 3.2 “Favorite passage” must become a causal explanation

Pushman recommends rereading passages that feel masterful and determining **why** they work.

### IVDIVO REFERENCE DISSECTION
For any “wow” passage:

`READER EFFECT → TEXTUAL DECISION → MECHANISM → PRECONDITIONS → TRANSFERABLE ABSTRACTION → DO-NOT-COPY SURFACE`

“Great atmosphere” is not an extraction. “The writer delays naming the object until the focal character learns it, synchronizing reader knowledge with character discovery” is an extraction.

## 3.3 Structure includes physical page organization

Pushman treats paragraph, section, chapter, and white-space breaks as reader-orientation and pacing devices, not merely formatting.

### IVDIVO BREAK TEST
For each chapter/section break ask what it signals:
- time shift;
- POV shift;
- conceptual shift;
- escalation;
- withheld answer;
- emotional pause;
- location shift.

A break without function can create false emphasis; missing breaks can blur transitions.

## 3.4 Scene = specific time, slowed enough for lived experience

Pushman defines scene as writing that narrows to a specific action/interaction and renders it moment-to-moment or nearly so through action, sensory detail, and often dialogue.

This creates a useful contrast:

**SCENE:** lived specific time.  
**SUMMARY:** compressed/general time.

### IVDIVO SCENE/SUMMARY ALLOCATION TEST
Use scene when the reader must:
- experience a choice;
- witness relationship change;
- inspect evidence;
- feel danger/embodiment;
- observe a reveal being interpreted.

Use summary when the dramatic value lies in compression, transition, repeated pattern, or elapsed time rather than beat-by-beat experience.

## 3.5 POV is a lens that selects and interprets

Pushman’s lens model reinforces Rasley/Card/Storr: POV is not only pronoun. It determines what appears salient and how the scene is interpreted.

### IVDIVO LENS TEST
Write a neutral event inventory, then the scene’s POV rendering. Every selected detail should be explainable through:
`LOCATION/ACCESS | KNOWLEDGE | ATTENTION | VALUES | FEAR/DESIRE | PROFESSIONAL HABIT | CURRENT EMOTION`.

## 3.6 Syntax can embody experience

Pushman’s language chapter emphasizes diction, syntax, punctuation, sentence length, sound, and rhythm. Crucially, syntax can reflect the motion of thought/body rather than merely decorate it.

### IVDIVO PROSE-EXPERIENCE TEST
At high-intensity moments ask whether sentence movement supports:
- breath;
- hesitation;
- sensory overload;
- careful reasoning;
- acceleration;
- intimacy;
without becoming an automatic stylistic gimmick.

# 4. RASLEY — THE POWER OF POINT OF VIEW

## 4.1 POV is reader-oriented but author-controlled

Rasley defines POV as the perspective through which the reader experiences story action. The author selects whose perception, thought, and emotion become the reader’s access route.

### IVDIVO POV FUNCTION
POV selection is therefore a structural decision about:
- reader knowledge;
- intimacy;
- suspense;
- interpretation;
- theme;
not a cosmetic pronoun choice.

## 4.2 Four working components of POV

Rasley separates:
1. **Narration** — how action is told;
2. **Perception** — what/how the focal character senses;
3. **Introspection** — thoughts/feelings;
4. **Voice** — diction, style, attitude.

### IVDIVO POV PASSPORT
For each core POV character record:
`KNOWLEDGE LIMIT | PHYSICAL ACCESS | PERCEPTUAL BIAS | INTERPRETIVE BIAS | INTROSPECTION RANGE | VOICE | WHAT READER MUST NOT KNOW YET`.

## 4.3 Choose POV by story purpose

Rasley distinguishes a tightly personal novel from a social/group novel.

- Personal story → often benefits from fewer, tighter POVs.
- Social/group story → may need multiple/omniscient access to reveal interactions and simultaneous systems.

### IVDIVO POV SCOPE TEST
Ask before freezing POV:
`IS THE CORE EXPERIENCE ONE PERSON’S JOURNEY OR A SYSTEM/GROUP INTERACTION?`

Then use the **minimum number of POVs that can deliver the intended experience**.

## 4.4 Multiple POV trades intimacy for scope—and can leak suspense

Rasley identifies major risks of multiple POV:
- head-hopping;
- reduced reader involvement;
- redundancy;
- revealing too much too quickly;
- fragmenting sustained narrative focus.

Benefits:
- simultaneous locations;
- conflicting interpretations;
- group/team stories;
- social novels;
- relationship duality.

### IVDIVO POV-ADMISSION GATE
A new POV character must provide at least one unique function that cannot be delivered cleanly through an existing POV:
- inaccessible action;
- necessary conflicting interpretation;
- essential emotional stake;
- independent causal line.

“Interesting character” alone is insufficient.

## 4.5 POV switching requires duration and purpose

Rasley’s practical preference is to stay in one viewpoint long enough for the reader to inhabit it, switching for a clear reason rather than sentence-by-sentence head hopping.

### IVDIVO SWITCH TEST
Before a POV shift state:
`WHAT BECOMES POSSIBLE AFTER THE SHIFT THAT WAS IMPOSSIBLE BEFORE?`

If no answer, stay with the current POV.

## 4.6 Individualized POV includes value system and sentence construction

Different POV characters perceive the same event differently not only because they see different facts, but because they assign different meaning, probability, trust, threat, and value.

### IVDIVO PERCEPTION PROFILE
For each core POV:
- what do they notice first?
- what sensory channel dominates?
- what do they ignore?
- how do they decide trust?
- optimist/pessimist/other prediction style?
- past/future/present orientation?
- what social signals matter?
- how do profession and class shape vocabulary?
- what is their default explanation when evidence is ambiguous?

This profile should alter narration itself.

## 4.7 POV is a suspense-control system

Because POV determines knowledge, it can create or destroy mystery. Multiple POV can prematurely explain uncertainty; a restricted POV can create fair suspense when evidence remains available but interpretation is incomplete.

### IVDIVO KNOWLEDGE BUDGET
For each scene:
`READER KNOWS | POV KNOWS | OTHER CHARACTERS KNOW | AUTHOR KNOWS | WITHHELD FAIRLY | WITHHELD UNFAIRLY`.

Withholding is fair when the POV genuinely lacks/accesses incompletely the information. It is unfair when the narrator consciously knows and artificially refuses to think about it only to trick the reader.

# 5. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

## 5.1 Discovery and specification are different phases

Lamott encourages discovery; IVDIVO requires specification lock. These do not conflict if phase boundaries are explicit:

`SOURCE STUDY / DISCOVERY LAB`
`→ STORY & CHARACTER EXPERIMENTS`
`→ BOOK CONTRACT`
`→ FREEZE`
`→ PRODUCTION EXECUTION`
`→ STRUCTURAL FINDING? CHANGE REQUEST`
`→ RE-FREEZE`
`→ CONTINUE`

This prevents two opposite failures:
- sterile planning that never discovers living character truth;
- endless improvisation that destroys continuity and ending design.

## 5.2 Reverse engineering is now a formal research discipline

Pushman + Bernays/Painter + Rasley support a stronger source-study protocol:

For a fiction reference:

`EFFECT → GENRE PROMISE → STORY FUNCTION → SCENE FUNCTION → POV DECISION → INFORMATION CONTROL → CHARACTER CHOICE → PROSE DEVICE → READER RESPONSE → ABSTRACT MECHANISM → LIMITS → DO-NOT-COPY`.

No source enters IVDIVO because “I liked it.” It must yield a precise mechanism.

## 5.3 POV and voice are coupled but not identical

Rasley + Pushman + Lamott:

`POV = ACCESS + SELECTION + INTERPRETATION`

`VOICE = LANGUAGE EXPRESSION OF THAT PERSPECTIVE`

Thus changing pronouns without changing selection, assumptions, vocabulary, syntax, and rhythm does not create a new POV experience.

## 5.4 Character truth and story architecture must constrain each other

Lamott + What If? resolve a persistent false dichotomy:

**Wrong A:** force characters through predetermined plot regardless of plausibility.  
**Wrong B:** let characters wander and call the accumulation a novel.

**IVDIVO synthesis:**
`FROZEN MAIN STORY OBLIGATIONS + CHARACTER-TRUE CAUSAL EXECUTION`.

If they conflict, return to architecture; do not sacrifice either silently.

## 5.5 Revision is controlled re-vision, not cosmetic polishing

Lamott + What If? + Rasley:

A manuscript can require changes to:
- chapter order;
- POV;
- scene existence;
- character function;
- plot treatment;
- knowledge distribution;
- voice;
long before commas matter.

This reinforces IVDIVO’s rule that structural defects in Book 1 must be addressed before literary line polish.

# 6. NEW PRODUCTION GATES FROM BATCH 05

## GATE R — DISCOVERY / PRODUCTION SEPARATION
Exploratory drafting is permitted only in explicit Discovery Lab mode. Production prose cannot silently rewrite frozen story architecture.

## GATE S — CHAPTER CAUSAL LINK
Every chapter must name its entry state, exit state, and causal bridge into the next chapter.

## GATE T — POV ADMISSION
Every additional POV must earn its existence through unique causal, informational, or emotional access.

## GATE U — KNOWLEDGE BUDGET
Every mystery scene must track what reader/POV/others know and verify that withheld information is fair.

## GATE V — PSYCHIC DISTANCE CONTROL
Distance changes must be deliberate, gradual enough for the intended effect, and compatible with the scene’s focal consciousness.

## GATE W — REFERENCE DISSECTION
No external work may be cited as an influence without at least one written transferable mechanism and explicit do-not-copy surface list.

## GATE X — PROSE FINGERPRINT
Before line-edit signoff, each major POV receives a sentence/paragraph/rhythm/lexicon audit for accidental uniformity and repeated AI-like cadence.

## GATE Y — SCENE/SUMMARY ALLOCATION
A dramatic choice, relationship turn, decisive clue interpretation, or embodied danger may not be summarized away without an explicit reason; routine transition need not be dramatized at full length.

## GATE Z — COMPLETION
A manuscript is not “done” because the writer is tired. It requires all designated structural, continuity, prose, and red-team gates plus a residual-risk log.

# 8. LIMITS / CONFLICTS BETWEEN SOURCES

1. **Lamott’s discovery-first ethos vs specification-first production.** Resolution: phase separation; discovery before freeze, Change Request after freeze.
2. **Rasley’s flexible POV options vs “keep POV minimal.”** Resolution: minimality is a cost principle, not dogma; social/system novels may require multiple perspectives.
3. **Gardner-style psychic-distance rules as presented in What If? vs published exceptions.** Resolution: use distance as conscious control, not an absolute prohibition against artistic shifts.
4. **Measurable style diagnostics vs literary quality.** Sentence-length/modifier/passive metrics detect patterns; they do not score beauty.
5. **Pushman’s “read like a writer” method can tempt imitation.** IVDIVO requires mechanism abstraction plus source-distance firewall.
