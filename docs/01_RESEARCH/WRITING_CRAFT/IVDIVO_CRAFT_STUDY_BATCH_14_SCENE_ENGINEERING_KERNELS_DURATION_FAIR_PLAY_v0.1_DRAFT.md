# 1. SANDRA SCOFIELD — SCENE AS EVENT + MEANING

## 1.1 Scene is not merely a time-place container

A usable scene must have an event large enough to merit dramatization, a character experience of that event, and a changed state at the end.

Operational form:

`ENTRY STATE → EVENT → ACTION BEATS → EMOTIONAL RESPONSE BEATS → FOCAL POINT/TURN → EXIT STATE`

The scene must "add up": if nothing meaningful is different after the scene, it may be a nonevent, redundant scene, or material better handled by summary.

## 1.2 Action and emotion must be causally paired

Scofield's evaluation method is especially useful for IVDIVO because our drafts can become technically intelligent while emotionally under-coupled.

Production table:

`ACTION BEAT | CHARACTER RESPONSE | WHY THIS RESPONSE | NEXT ACTION CAUSED`

The action and emotional tracks should accelerate together toward a focal point rather than run as separate channels.

## 1.3 Focal point

A strong scene has a place where its accumulated action and response converge and the scene turns.

The focal point is not necessarily:
- a revelation;
- a loud argument;
- a twist;
- a climax.

It can be a decision, recognition, surrender, refusal, changed relation, new interpretation, or concrete act that alters the state.

### IVDIVO test

`WHAT IS THE ONE MOMENT AFTER WHICH THIS SCENE CANNOT RETURN TO ITS ENTRY STATE?`

If no answer exists, scene architecture requires review.

## 1.4 Scene utility and economy

For every scene:

`WHY HERE?`
`WHY SCENE, NOT SUMMARY?`
`WHAT WOULD BREAK IF REMOVED?`
`WHAT DOES IT ADD THAT NO PREVIOUS SCENE ADDED?`
`WHAT DOES IT CAUSE NEXT?`

A scene may be well written and still be unnecessary.

# 2. SANDRA SCOFIELD — PULSE

Pulse is the recurring force carrying action and feeling through a scene.

For IVDIVO, pulse is useful because many procedural scenes contain good information but can feel static.

### Pulse model

`LIVE WANT / FEAR / QUESTION`
→ repeated pressure on that concern
→ altered tactics / interpretation
→ stronger pressure
→ focal point
→ consequence.

A scene with many facts but no pulse reads like briefing material.

### Pulse questions

- What does the POV character keep checking, wanting, fearing or resisting?
- How does each new beat alter that pressure?
- Does the pulse intensify rather than merely repeat?
- Does the focal point answer or transform the initial question?

# 3. NEGOTIATION AS AN ALTERNATIVE TO GENERIC CONFLICT

Not every effective scene needs overt hostility.

A large amount of IVDIVO material is institutional, relational and procedural. Treating every scene as an argument would falsify the world.

Use negotiation architecture:

`A WANTS X FROM B`
`B WANTS Y / PROTECTS Z`
`A OFFERS / WITHHOLDS / TESTS`
`B COUNTERS / REDIRECTS / SETS BOUNDARY`
`NEW INFORMATION CHANGES LEVERAGE`
`TERMS CHANGE`
`SCENE ENDS WITH AGREEMENT / REFUSAL / PARTIAL DEAL / NEW IMBALANCE`

This preserves dramatic movement without manufacturing villains.

# 4. SEYMOUR CHATMAN — KERNELS AND SATELLITES

Chatman's distinction is highly useful for manuscript compression.

## Kernel

A kernel is a major story event where a meaningful branch or choice exists. Removing it damages the causal logic of the story.

## Satellite

A satellite elaborates, fills out, delays, enriches, characterizes, or aesthetically develops the kernel. It may be excellent, but is structurally expendable.

### IVDIVO Kernel Test

For each chapter beat ask:

`IF THIS EVENT DISAPPEARS, CAN THE NEXT MAJOR EVENT STILL HAPPEN WITH THE SAME LOGIC?`

- **NO** → likely KERNEL.
- **YES, but texture/character/world becomes poorer** → likely SATELLITE.

### Why this matters

A long novel can be rich in satellites while still having too few kernels. The reader then experiences "a lot happening" without enough irreversible progress.

### Production ledger

`BEAT | KERNEL/SATELLITE | CHOICE/BRANCH | CONSEQUENCE | READER VALUE | CUT/MERGE/KEEP`

# 5. CHATMAN — NARRATIVE DURATION AS ATTENTION ALLOCATION

The story and the discourse do not need to occupy the same amount of time.

Useful modes:

- `SUMMARY` — discourse time shorter than story time;
- `ELLIPSIS` — story event omitted;
- `SCENE` — approximate parity / lived event;
- `STRETCH` — discourse takes longer than the represented event;
- `PAUSE` — discourse continues while story-time effectively stops.

### IVDIVO application

These are not academic labels only. They are an editing control surface.

Use **SCENE / STRETCH** for:
- consequential choice;
- uncertainty;
- bodily danger;
- relationship turn;
- clue interpretation;
- consent / authority decision;
- moment where exact behavior matters.

Use **SUMMARY / ELLIPSIS** for:
- routine setup;
- repeated calibration;
- travel without consequence;
- procedural steps already established;
- world facts that do not require lived discovery.

Use **PAUSE** sparingly when description/thought creates genuine meaning or suspense. Too many pauses inside procedure-heavy scenes produce conceptual drag.

# 6. JAMES N. FREY — PLAYING FAIR

The reader contract requires that the story not solve itself by authorial manipulation.

Operational fair-play principles extracted:

1. Mystery evidence must be available enough that the reader has a genuine chance to reason.
2. Romantic separation/misunderstanding must be motivated rather than artificially prolonged.
3. Characters should operate near their established capacity; suspense should not depend on sudden stupidity.
4. Coincidence may plausibly ignite a story, but should not repeatedly solve central problems.
5. Contrivance = author provides the solution rather than character action/resources causing it.
6. Obstacles must be worthy of the competence of the protagonists.

### IVDIVO Fair-Play Gate

`PROBLEM → AVAILABLE INFORMATION → CHARACTER CAPACITY → CHOICE → ACTION → CONSEQUENCE`

Reject:

`PROBLEM → CHARACTER FORGETS OBVIOUS THING / AUTHOR HIDES NECESSARY FACT / MIRACLE RESOURCE APPEARS → SOLUTION`.

# 7. CROSS-CHECK — MCKEE BEAT / TURNING POINT

McKee's scene progression aligns usefully with Scofield:

`ACTION → REACTION → STRONGER ACTION → STRONGER REACTION → TURNING POINT`.

But IVDIVO should not force every scene into an overtly escalating duel. Some scenes progress by negotiation, investigation, withholding, misinterpretation or restraint.

The common principle is **state change under pressure**.

# 8. NEW PRODUCTION GATES

[OUR SYNTHESIS / DRAFT — NOT CANON UNTIL FOUNDER APPROVAL]

### Gate DJ — Scene Event
Every full scene must contain an event significant enough to justify dramatization.

### Gate DK — Scene Add-Up
The exit state must differ meaningfully from the entry state.

### Gate DL — Action/Emotion Coupling
Important action beats and emotional/interpretive responses must causally interact.

### Gate DM — Focal Point
Identify the moment after which the scene cannot return to its opening state unchanged.

### Gate DN — Pulse
A live want/fear/question must recur with increasing pressure through the scene.

### Gate DO — Negotiation
Where overt conflict would be false, build dramatic exchange through competing terms, boundaries, leverage and concessions.

### Gate DP — Kernel/Satellite
Every chapter must identify its kernels. Satellite density may enrich but cannot substitute for irreversible story progress.

### Gate DQ — Narrative Duration
Allocate page-time by importance; do not dramatize routine and summarize decisive experience by accident.

### Gate DR — Fair-Play Competence
No suspense may depend on a competent character behaving stupidly without established cause.

### Gate DS — No Author Rescue
Central problems must be solved through character resources, choices and earned consequences, not contrivance.

### Gate DT — Evidence Availability
Mystery/reveal architecture must give the reader access to relevant evidence before or at the point where its inference matters, unless deliberate withholding is itself clearly motivated by POV/discourse.

### Gate DU — Scene Sequence Progression
A scene should arise from the previous state and make the next state possible or necessary.

# 9. MANUSCRIPT VALIDATION — BOOK TWO CHAPTERS 1–6

## Chapter 1 — THE BADGE

### Kernel A
Route divergence reveals that the public system predicts companion correction rather than merely issuing neutral paths.

### Kernel B
Rover boundary drift + rerouting puts a child on a path into the course; Ethan uses the stop correctly.

### Kernel C
Rivergate invitation arrives; Samir recognizes a structural shape.

### Focal point
Ethan presses the stop before anyone else acts. His tiny authority becomes real through action.

### Pulse
`Ethan wants real authority → must restrain management impulse → receives a real stop condition → acts correctly → is immediately offered a more consequential authority problem.`

### Finding
Strong chapter, but satellite density before the first routing divergence is high. Maker banter and one-eye rover detail are good characterization; retain, but do not add more pre-kernel setup.

### Revision rule
No additional exposition before Taven/Mel. If expanding, expand the consequence of the stop or relation-state change, not environmental setup.

## Chapter 2 — YOUR CALL

### Kernel
Ethan receives genuine Youth-Scope Hold authority and learns both action and non-action are accountable decisions.

### Focal point
Voss states that a hold can be correct and still produce harm; Samir then establishes that not using the hold is also logged.

### Pulse
`Ethan wants authority → discovers authority is bounded → discovers using it harms something → discovers not using it is also a decision.`

### Finding
The chapter earns its briefing material because the information changes Ethan's responsibility model. However, procedural explanation is close to maximum tolerable density.

### Revision rule
Future chapters must not restate the hold rules unless a new edge case changes their meaning. Repetition becomes satellite overload.

## Chapter 3 — THE PEOPLE IN THE MODEL

### Kernel A
Lattice proves it can solve a real multi-party allocation conflict while carrying the displaced cost.

### Kernel B
The group discovers inferred relations.

### Kernel C
Actor-order similarity connects the system to unresolved historical relational-model provenance.

### Focal point
Voss says she wants to know whether Rivergate inherited a design assumption it no longer understands.

### Pulse
`What does the model think a person/relation is? → declared relation → inferred relation → order-sensitive state → older structural similarity → responsible provenance question.`

### Finding
This is information-heavy but it **does add up**. The danger is not lack of structure; it is narrative duration. Several explanatory lists are satellites around the three kernels.

### Revision rule
When line editing, compress lists and repeated clarification before cutting the core simulation, Samir boundary-setting or Voss provenance turn.

## Chapter 4 — FIRST HOLD

### Kernel
Ethan is given an arguable but insufficient trigger and chooses **not** to use the hold.

### Focal point
The recorded decision `NO HOLD INVOKED — THRESHOLD NOT ESTABLISHED` makes restraint an operational act rather than absence of action.

### Pulse
`Ethan expects authority to become real by use → evidence tempts him → threshold fails → he does not hold → authority becomes real through restraint.`

### Finding
Very strong scene architecture. Protect.

### Fair-play check
Pass: Ethan is not made stupid to create drama; he uses available evidence and process.

## Chapter 5 — NICO

### Kernel A
Maya receives bounded responsibility for Nico and resists the control impulse.

### Kernel B
She lets him choose his own permitted route.

### Kernel C
When Nico finds the signage error, Maya credits **Nico Alvarez**, not the generic role `observer`.

### Focal point
Deleting `OBSERVER FOUND...` and replacing it with `NICO ALVAREZ FOUND...`.

### Pulse
`Responsibility sounds like control → Maya wants to control → she chooses distance → Nico proves competent → Maya changes the institutional record to preserve his agency.`

### Finding
Excellent human-scale scene and important antidote to institutional abstraction. Protect almost entirely.

## Chapter 6 — THE SYSTEM WORKS

### Kernel
Lattice anticipates a developing environmental/translation conflict before staff and redistributes resources to prevent an actual incident.

### Focal point
The system's preventive action succeeds before the protagonists can claim the discovery as theirs.

### Story-level function
This chapter makes the later hold morally expensive. Without it, Lattice could become a straw-man algorithm the heroes merely shut down.

### Pulse
`The team watches the system → Samir notices unusual reservation → causal chain becomes visible → intervention prevents harm → protagonists must update their model: the system is genuinely useful.`

### Finding
Strong kernel, but because the protagonist initially misses it, the chapter must keep Samir's noticing and the tangible prevented outcome extremely legible.

### Revision rule
Do not add theoretical praise of Lattice after the incident. Let the prevented harm and displaced costs prove value.

# 10. BLOCK-LEVEL DIAGNOSIS — CH1–6

The six chapters contain enough kernels. The larger risk is **procedural clustering**:

- Ch2 briefing;
- Ch3 model demonstration;
- Ch4 threshold exercise;
- Ch6 dry run.

Ch1 and Ch5 provide needed public/ordinary-life variation.

Therefore Chapter 7 should not be another room with a screen and a lesson.

### Required Ch7 design constraint

`SYSTEM CONSEQUENCE → ORDINARY/SOCIAL ARENA → HUMAN COST OR BENEFIT → CHOICE → RETURN TO AUDIT WITH CHANGED QUESTION`

Possible arenas already allowed by architecture: school, transport, family, maker obligations, youth observer peer status, media/public transparency, food court/public opening prep.

The point is not to add filler. The ordinary arena must **cause a new audit question**.
