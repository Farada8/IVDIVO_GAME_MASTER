## 1. WHY THIS BATCH MATTERS
Swain is often inherited downstream as a source of mechanical rules: active verbs, stimulus-response chains, scene/sequel. Chapter 2 is more useful when read less dogmatically. It contains a strong anti-formula undercurrent: brevity is conditional, vividness can outrank brevity, syntax should vary, grammatical rules may be broken deliberately, and clarity is a reader-facing obligation.

For IVDIVO this produces a prose-control layer that sits *after* structural correctness and before cosmetic polishing.

## 2. CORE SYNTHESIS
### 2.1 Selection / Arrangement / Description
`STORYWORLD MATERIAL -> POV/SCENE SELECTION -> DISCOURSE ORDER -> RENDERING -> READER MODEL`

Worldbuilding is not neutral transcription from the Saga Bible. What is selected, who notices it, and in what order the reader receives it already shapes interpretation.

### 2.2 Agency without active-voice dogma
Swain strongly favors active verbs and often attacks `to be`. Keep the useful question:
`WHO/WHAT ACTS, CHANGES, DECIDES, RECEIVES, OR REMAINS IN A STATE?`

Reject the blanket rule. Passive voice and state verbs are correct when the receiver/state/identity is the real subject of attention.

### 2.3 Temporal forward pressure without banning past perfect
Past material should alter present understanding, relationship, evidence or action. Swain himself allows legitimate uses of `had`; therefore:
`PAST PERFECT = temporal routing tool`, not defect category.

### 2.4 Pronoun provenance
In ensemble/mystery prose, vague pronouns can become causal/evidentiary errors.

Gate:
`PRONOUN -> REFERENT -> AGENT/SOURCE -> ACTION/CLAIM -> READER MODEL`.

If `they decided`, `it showed`, `this proved` hides who decided, what showed, or what exactly was proved, repair before style polishing.

### 2.5 Modifier function
Adjectives/adverbs earn their place when they change meaning, POV attitude, relation, rhythm or precision. Redundant intensification is a deletion/rewrite candidate. Do not create an adverb/adjective purge.

### 2.6 Connotation as hidden evaluation
`DENOTATION -> CONNOTATION -> SPEAKER/INSTITUTION -> IMPLIED VALUE JUDGMENT`.

Words such as `efficient`, `obedient`, `deviant`, `advanced`, `primitive`, `safe`, `pure`, `blocked`, `higher`, `lower` can masquerade as neutral description while actually encoding a character's or institution's model.

### 2.7 Syntax by effect
Swain explicitly warns against monotonous sentence construction and also against conspicuous variety for its own sake.

IVDIVO rule:
`READER EFFECT -> THOUGHT SHAPE -> SENTENCE SHAPE`.

Do not optimize average sentence length. Use short, medium and long structures according to continuity, accumulation, shock, reflection, technical reasoning, voice and pressure.

### 2.8 Content load before length
A difficult sentence may not be too long; it may be doing too many unrelated logical jobs or separating its governing subject/action beyond reader-state continuity.

Diagnosis:
`HOW MANY LOGICAL JOBS? -> WHAT IS THE GOVERNING RELATION? -> CAN THE READER HOLD IT? -> SPLIT / REORDER / KEEP`.

### 2.9 Intentional repetition vs accident
Repeated language can create motif, rhythm, escalation or changed meaning. Accidental lexical echo simply exposes the drafting machinery.

`REPETITION -> NEW FUNCTION/MEANING? -> KEEP; NO -> CUT/VARIATE`.

### 2.10 Fragment intent
Swain permits fragments, one-word paragraphs and nonstandard punctuation when deliberately used. Therefore the defect is not fragmenthood; it is accidental/monotonous fragmentation.

`FRAGMENT -> CONTEXT CLEAR? -> SPECIFIC EFFECT? -> CONTRAST WITH SURROUNDING RHYTHM?`

If all three fail, merge or rewrite.

### 2.11 Basic clarity vs productive mystery
Swain's insistence on immediate clarity is useful only at the level of basic orientation.

Mystery may intentionally withhold:
- answer,
- motive,
- source reliability,
- causal interpretation.

It should not accidentally withhold:
- who acted,
- what pronoun refers to,
- when the event occurred,
- whether a sentence is observation or inference.

## 3. CHAPTER 3 OPENING — RELATIONAL EVALUATION
Swain's opening argument in Chapter 3 is stronger than a simple `good/bad` polarity. A specific event only gains evaluative significance relative to a particular somebody and a yardstick.

Operationalization:
`EVENT / RULE / TECHNOLOGY -> PERSON/AGENT -> GOAL/VALUES/YARDSTICK -> BENEFIT/COST -> RESPONSE`.

This is extremely useful for IVDIVO because it blocks authorial declarations such as `the system was good`, `the technology was dangerous`, or `the school was advanced` without specifying for whom, under what conditions, by what evidence, and at whose cost.

## 4. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON
### Swain vs Le Guin / Francine Prose / Pushman / Clark
Strong convergence: rhythm requires functional variation; fragments and long sentences are tools, not moral categories. Swain's own `vividness outranks brevity` corrects later caricatures of his school.

### Swain vs Zinsser
Productive tension: Zinsser aggressively removes clutter; Swain warns that over-compression can kill vividness. Synthesis: delete nonfunctional material, preserve complexity required for exact experience.

### Swain vs Williams-Bizup / Pinker / Clark
These later style systems correct Swain's strongest prescriptions about active voice / `to be`. Agency and emphasis matter more than grammatical ideology.

### Swain vs Fludernik / Chatman
`Arrangement` maps directly to discourse: same story facts can create different reader models depending on order.

### Swain vs Zunshine / Herman
Necessary epistemic correction: wording/order may shape interpretation but cannot upgrade a hypothesis, institutional label or subjective evaluation into world fact.

### Swain vs Lamott / LaPlante
Strong convergence on separating creation from critique. This supports staged Writers' Room passes rather than 100 simultaneous vetoes during discovery.

## 5. IVDIVO PRODUCTION GATES
### Gate B96-01 — AGENT VISIBILITY
For every institutional sentence, identify who decides/acts/authorizes/refuses/appeals.

### Gate B96-02 — PRONOUN PROVENANCE
No ambiguous `they/it/this` where agency, source, evidence or consent matters.

### Gate B96-03 — LOADED LABEL
Any evaluative term must be attributed to a speaker/model unless independently established.

### Gate B96-04 — TECHNICAL TERM LEGIBILITY
A precise term must be authentic to POV and inferable enough for the intended reader.

### Gate B96-05 — FRAGMENT INTENT
One-line/fragmented prose must earn a concrete effect. Do not optimize fragment percentage.

### Gate B96-06 — CONTENT-LOAD DIAGNOSIS
Before shortening a long sentence, identify whether the problem is length, logical overload, referent distance or reader-state transition.

### Gate B96-07 — REPETITION DEVELOPMENT
Repeated word/image/phrase must return with new pressure, meaning, rhythm or payoff.

### Gate B96-08 — TEMPORAL ROUTING
Past tense/past perfect is evaluated by orientation and present relevance, not banned mechanically.

### Gate B96-09 — RELATIONAL VALUE
Every `good/bad/safe/fair/efficient/advanced` claim must answer: for whom, by what yardstick, under what conditions, at whose cost?

### Gate B96-10 — BASIC CLARITY / HIGHER-ORDER UNCERTAINTY
Keep basic syntax/source/time clear while allowing genuine mystery about explanation and motive.

## 6. IVDIVO APPLICATIONS
### Post-money orbital society
Words like `allocation`, `priority`, `contribution`, `access`, `assignment`, `qualification` require mechanism-level grounding:
`WHO DECIDES -> WHAT RESOURCE/ROLE IS ACTUALLY LIMITED -> WHO CAN REFUSE/APPEAL -> WHO BEARS COST`.
Otherwise they risk smuggling salary/rent/credits back into the world as euphemisms.

### Conscious robots
Human descriptors are not robot motives. `Efficient`, `obedient`, `erratic`, `helpful`, `unsafe` are human evaluations until linked to the robot's own goal, relation, evidence and choice.

### Consciousness-practice schools
Preserve:
`PRACTITIONER OBSERVATION -> REPRODUCIBLE EFFECT -> SCHOOL INTERPRETATION -> COMPETING INTERPRETATION -> POSSIBLE ONTOLOGICAL CLAIM`.
Terms such as `higher/lower/pure/blocked/advanced` must stay attributed when they belong to a school's vocabulary.

### Mystery
Preserve:
`WORLD FACT -> TRACE/SOURCE -> WORDING/FRAME -> POV INTERPRETATION -> DISCOURSE ORDER -> READER MODEL`.
The order can manipulate suspense; it cannot alter what evidence actually proves.

### Anti-fragmentation
Final router remains:
`ONE MAIN STORY / CAUSALITY -> SCENE NECESSITY -> ORDER/DURATION/FREQUENCY -> SHOW/TELL -> POV/FOCALIZATION -> DIALOGUE MODE -> PARAGRAPH INTENT -> READER-STATE CONTINUITY -> SENTENCE RHYTHM -> WORDING`.
