## IMPORTANT NEW IGLESIAS FINDINGS
1. Iglesias explicitly states that not all scenes require conflict; this is retained as a counterweight to conflict-monoculture.
2. Scene polarity is decomposed into meaningful state change rather than mandatory positive/negative flips.
3. Screenplay description advice is split into transferable specificity/active-perception mechanisms versus screenplay-only vertical layout/page-density rules.
4. Motion is not short-sentence density. Action progression, duration, attention, paragraphing and expectation also control speed.
5. Dialogue voice requires rhythm, vocabulary, agenda, emotional tempo and relation-specific behavior; catchphrases/slang are insufficient.
6. Direct/indirect dialogue are both valid; exact wording earns direct dramatization when it changes evidence, consent, trust, power, relationship, decision or emotion.
7. Exposition must have an in-world need and consequence. Conflict is only one carrier, not universal camouflage.
8. Subtext is useful when directness has a believable cost; Iglesias explicitly allows direct speech where safety, earned climax or simplicity makes it truthful.
9. Context, silence and physical behavior can carry subtext without ornamental clever dialogue.
10. Rewrite passes should be diagnostic and element-specific, not random sentence tweaking.
