## 1. CENTRAL SYNTHESIS
The most useful part of Swain's character chapter is not its dated psychological typology. It is the practical movement from cast function to observable behavior under different pressures.

IVDIVO form:

`STORY FUNCTION + INDEPENDENT PERSONHOOD → WANT → RELATIONSHIP CONTEXT → PRESSURE → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC TACTIC → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE → UPDATED CHARACTER MODEL`

## 2. CAST ECONOMY WITHOUT PERSONHOOD LOSS
Swain is right that too many functionally redundant characters overload reader attention. He is not licensed to reduce people to tools.

### Gate B104-G01 — FUNCTION ECONOMY
Merge or remove redundant book functions when possible.

### Gate B104-G02 — PERSONHOOD FIREWALL
For any named conscious person, especially a conscious robot/nonhuman:
`BOOK FUNCTION != STORYWORLD PERSONHOOD`.

A viable person must retain some combination of:
- own want;
- own relationships;
- own evidence/model;
- preferences;
- consent/refusal;
- independent choices;
- consequences not reducible to protagonist growth.

## 3. CHARACTER ATTENTION BUDGET
Not every character needs equal page depth.

Per-book hierarchy may include:
- PRIMARY MAJOR;
- SECONDARY MAJOR;
- SUPPORTING MAJOR;
- MINOR;
- WALK-ON.

Saga-level importance does not imply equal POV, secrets, trauma, humor, backstory or climax ownership in every book.

## 4. DOMINANT IMPRESSION — USE AS ORIENTATION, NOT REDUCTION
A reader benefits from rapid differentiation, but a dominant impression is only an entry model.

### Gate B104-G03 — FIRST MODEL MUST BE REVISABLE
Early tags/traits may orient the reader, but later behavior must be allowed to complicate or revise that first model.

## 5. BEHAVIOR BEFORE EXPLANATION
Prefer rendering patterns of choice and conduct before assigning one clean psychological cause.

`BEHAVIOR ACROSS CONTEXTS → PLAUSIBLE MOTIVE MODELS → NEW EVIDENCE → UPDATED MODEL`

This prevents:
- one-trauma explanations;
- diagnostic labeling as characterization;
- exposition substituting for behavior;
- false certainty about another person's motives.

## 6. RELATION-SPECIFIC SELF
A major character should not use identical tactics with every interlocutor.

Test the same person with:
- close friend;
- parent/family;
- instructor/authority;
- rival;
- romantic/intimate partner;
- public audience;
- institution;
- conscious robot/nonhuman;
- person who needs help;
- person whose consent is required.

Identity remains continuous, but stakes, trust, history and power alter tactics.

### Gate B104-G04 — NO UNIVERSAL WIT
Character differentiation cannot be achieved merely by giving everyone clever lines or different slang.

## 7. SELF-IMAGE VS OBSERVED ACTION
Swain's attention to self-image is useful when treated as one data layer.

Maintain distinction:
`WHAT CHARACTER THINKS THEY ARE → WHAT THEY DO → WHAT OTHERS THINK → WHAT CONSEQUENCES SHOW`.

No one layer automatically becomes the author's final verdict.

## 8. CONTRADICTION WITH CAUSALITY
Contradiction is valuable only when it emerges from competing wants, relationships, values, contexts or strategies.

`trait A + random opposite trait B` is not depth.

### Gate B104-G05 — CONTRADICTION MUST HAVE CONDITIONS
Ask: under what conditions does each side appear, and why?

## 9. MINOR CHARACTER DEPTH
Do not write full dossiers onto the page for every minor person.

But preserve the possibility that the minor character has a life outside the protagonist's sight.

For a conscious robot, this is mandatory: do not use narrow page function as proof of narrow consciousness.

## 10. CHARACTER-IN-DEPTH THROUGH CONTEXT VARIATION
Depth grows when a person is observed under qualitatively different pressures, not merely when the biography grows longer.

Useful pressure rotation:
- competence;
- embarrassment;
- intimacy;
- uncertainty;
- authority;
- dependence;
- consent;
- public scrutiny;
- moral ambiguity;
- successful responsibility;
- error without stupidity.

## 11. HISTORICAL PSYCHOLOGY FIREWALL
Do not operationalize as scientific or universal claims:
- lack/compensation as universal personality engine;
- fixed gender roles;
- heroine as reward;
- reader need for scapegoat;
- sexual-role stereotypes;
- mandatory courage/wish-fulfillment/disaster;
- `secret self` as an objective psychological essence.

Use only if a specific story/character independently supports such a mechanism.

## 12. ROBOT PERSONHOOD
No default `robot voice` or `robot psychology`.

For a named conscious robot:
`EMBODIMENT → PERCEPTION/ABILITY → OWN WANT → RELATIONSHIP-SPECIFIC TACTIC → CONSENT/REFUSAL → DECISION → CONSEQUENCE`.

A robot may temporarily perform mentor, ally, guardian, opponent or witness functions, but no archetype/function owns its identity.

## 13. CONSCIOUSNESS-SCHOOL EPISTEMICS
A character's internal experience is valid POV material but not proof of ontology.

Maintain:
`PRACTITIONER OBSERVATION → REPRODUCIBLE EFFECT → SCHOOL INTERPRETATION → COMPETING INTERPRETATION → POSSIBLE ONTOLOGICAL CLAIM`.

Swain's emphasis on feeling must never collapse this ladder.

## 14. YA / ORBITAL YOUTH APPLICATION
Strong young characters need not be made deep through trauma.

Prefer:
- first real responsibility;
- competence under unfamiliar constraints;
- intelligent mistake;
- relationship asymmetry;
- autonomy vs coordination;
- trust gained and then tested;
- consent boundary;
- reputation after a public decision;
- belonging without total conformity;
- ordinary life that demonstrates future norms.

## 15. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON
### Converges with
- Card: character hierarchy, relationship network, reader contract.
- Corbett: pressure-tested character hypotheses and independent personhood.
- Kress: motivation/frustration fingerprints and POV distance.
- McKee Character: modus operandi, but IVDIVO rejects `true self` absolutism.
- Herman/Zunshine: behavior does not uniquely identify motive.

### Corrects/limits
- formulaic archetype systems when function replaces person;
- trauma-first character design;
- universalized gender/moral assumptions in older craft manuals.

## 17. PRODUCTION SUMMARY
Design cast size economically, but evaluate major characters through independent desire, relation-specific tactics and observable behavior under varied pressure. Page-time economy must never erase personhood.
