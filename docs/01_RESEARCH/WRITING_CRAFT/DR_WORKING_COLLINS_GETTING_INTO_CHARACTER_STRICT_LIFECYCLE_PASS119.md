## What materially changed this pass
The previous pointer sat immediately after Collins's broad claim that suspense/action usually calls for more staccato sentence rhythm. This pass recovered a continuous chain from that point through the crucial exception: Collins herself later recommends long, strung-together sentences for chaotic simultaneous action, then shifts sentence rhythm toward INNER rhythm when inner experience rather than external motion carries the scene. Her Dread Champion analysis also uses a long sentence to make subjective time slow during a crash.

This means Collins cannot responsibly be operationalized as ACTION = SHORT SENTENCES. The stronger abstraction is:

`READER EFFECT -> SUBJECTIVE DURATION / CONTINUITY -> PARAGRAPH INTENT -> SENTENCE SHAPE / LENGTH -> CONTRAST`

## Cross-source correction
Collins's local heuristic is bounded by stronger cross-source convergence:
- Ursula K. Le Guin: all-short simple prose becomes monotonous/choppy; no optimum sentence length; function and contrast govern.
- Roy Peter Clark: short sentences can function as brakes because periods are stop signs; long sentences can carry continuous motion and suspense.
- McKee Dialogue / Hodgins / clarity-and-style sources: sentence design is a repertoire; monotonous recurrence of one design degrades rhythm.

Therefore:

`ACTION / SUSPENSE != SHORT-SENTENCE DENSITY`

## Compression status
Collins's useful core:
`REMOVE REPEATED MEANING / PROCESS -> CHOOSE SPECIFIC LOAD-BEARING LANGUAGE -> USE SUBTEXT / PRIOR CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE -> RETEST RHYTHM`

IVDIVO correction:
`COMPRESSION != CHOPPING`

Compression may mean merge, summary, indirect dialogue, deletion, or one more precise sentence. It must not create paragraph confetti or destroy setup/payoff, clue provenance, relationship beats, or reader orientation.

## Evidence-language firewall
Strong verbs often contain interpretation. In mystery / forensic passages:
`RAW TRACE -> NEUTRAL OBSERVATION -> POV INTERPRETATION -> CORROBORATED CLAIM`

Words such as hesitated, resisted, obeyed, flinched, stalked, lunged may be excellent narrative choices but cannot silently elevate interpretation into evidence.
