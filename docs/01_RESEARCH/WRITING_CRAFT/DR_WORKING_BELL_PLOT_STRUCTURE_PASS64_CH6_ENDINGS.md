## DIRECTLY SUPPORTED CH6 MAP

The source directly confirms:

1. Strong endings should feel appropriate to the specific novel while avoiding stale predictability.
2. Bell distinguishes three basic objective outcomes: gain, loss, ambiguity.
3. Bell complicates this with cross-sign outcomes: gain + bad consequence; loss + better consequence.
4. Endings may center on a final battle or a final choice.
5. Sacrifice can intensify an ending.
6. Plot twists and loose ends are explicit ending concerns.
7. Resonance is an explicit ending function.
8. Bell treats language, dialogue and description as ending-level tools.
9. Bell warns against summing up and rushing an ending.

## PRIMARY PROVISIONAL MECHANISMS — IVDIVO ADAPTATION

### 1. Ending Contract

Bell's knockout language is translated into a less formulaic IVDIVO contract:

`DOMINANT BOOK QUESTION → DECISIVE ACTION / CHOICE → LOCAL OUTCOME → COST → AFTERMATH → OPTIONAL SAGA HOOK`

The ending owes the reader closure on the book's dominant causal contract. It does NOT owe a total explanation of the saga or the universe.

### 2. Multi-Axis Outcome Matrix

Bell's gain / loss / ambiguous model becomes:

`OBJECTIVE OUTCOME × RELATIONSHIP OUTCOME × ETHICAL OUTCOME × KNOWLEDGE OUTCOME × COST`

Examples:
- objective gained + trust lost;
- objective lost + autonomy preserved;
- local rescue succeeds + ontology remains unknown;
- institutional recognition won + relationship altered;
- technical problem solved + a larger series question remains open.

This prevents simplistic `win = happy / lose = tragic` flattening.

### 3. Final Battle vs Final Choice — Mode Router

Bell explicitly allows both. IVDIVO treats them as alternate architectures:

**FINAL BATTLE MODE** — concentrated opposition requires coordinated action.

**FINAL CHOICE MODE** — evidence, values and consequences converge into a bounded decision.

A battle can be technical, procedural, institutional, relational or evidentiary. It does not require violence.

For ethical YA/SF, an earned final choice may be stronger than a larger disaster.

### 4. Sacrifice — Voluntary Value Trade, Not Mandatory Trauma

Use sacrifice only when:

`ESTABLISHED VALUE → REAL ALTERNATIVE → VOLUNTARY FORECLOSURE → STRONGER CHOSEN VALUE → VISIBLE CONSEQUENCE`

Do not kill, maim, impoverish or destroy a relationship merely because a craft template expects sacrifice.

### 5. Loose Ends → Promised Causal Contracts

Bell's loose-end warning is retained but narrowed:

**MUST CLOSE:** promises the book taught the reader to treat as part of the local causal engine.

**MAY REMAIN OPEN:** saga-scale ontology, distant history, larger civilization questions, future relationships not promised as current-book resolution.

**SHOULD CUT / DOWNGRADE:** a thread that receives enough emphasis to promise payoff but has no intended resolution.

### 6. Series Boundary Gate

`LOCAL RESOLUTION FIRST → CHANGED STATE → NEW SERIES VECTOR SECOND`

A sequel hook may create a new problem or enlarge a remaining mystery only AFTER the current book has delivered its promised answer/action.

Do not withhold the present book's answer merely to manufacture the next book.

### 7. Twist = Retrospective Reclassification

A last-act twist is useful when:

`EARLIER TRUE MATERIAL → READER MODEL A → NEW EVIDENCE → MODEL B → RETROSPECTIVE SUPPORT → CHANGED ACTION / MEANING`

A twist that only shocks but does not alter action, interpretation or consequence is decorative.

Authorial lying is not a twist.

### 8. Resonance Without Theme Summary

Preferred:

`EARLIER IMAGE / RELATION / RULE / OBJECT → RETURN IN CHANGED CONTEXT → NEW MEANING`

Avoid ending with an authorial thesis statement explaining what the book meant.

### 9. Aftermath Space

Do not rush immediately from decisive action to final slogan / sequel hook.

Reader needs enough space to register:
- who paid the cost;
- what relationship changed;
- what rule/authority/evidence status changed;
- what remains unresolved and why;
- what ordinary life looks like now.

But aftermath should stop when the local contract is legible; it must not re-summarize the novel.

### 10. Ending Language Gate

Final dialogue / description should reveal changed state, not explain it twice.

`ACTION / IMAGE / RELATIONSHIP BEAT → READER INFERS CHANGE`

Do not append:

`NARRATOR EXPLAINS CHANGE → SHORT THEMATIC SENTENCE REPEATS IT`.

## FAILURE MODES ADDED

1. Climax-by-Volume — bigger spectacle substitutes for a harder decision.
2. Sequel-Withholding — current-book answer is withheld solely to sell the next book.
3. Theme-Summary Ending — final paragraphs explain the meaning already demonstrated.
4. Universal-Loose-End Closure — saga/world questions are overexplained because every open question is treated as a defect.
5. Sacrifice Inflation — death/trauma/loss inserted to manufacture significance.
6. Twist-for-Last-Page — shock without retrospective support or causal effect.
7. Rushed Aftermath — decisive event lands but relational/institutional/evidence consequences are unreadable.
8. Epilogue-as-Repair-Patch — missing causal closure is patched by retrospective explanation.
9. Outcome Binary Flattening — success/failure treated as one axis.
10. Final Speech Explains Arc — character verbalizes exactly what prior choices already proved.
11. Ontology-Dump Finale — local mystery becomes a lecture resolving universe-scale metaphysics.
12. Hero-Solves-Everything — distributed evidence and agency collapse into one protagonist's omniscient solution.
