# 1. HODGINS — PLOT AS CAUSAL CHAIN

## 1.1 Events matter because of their relations

Hodgins treats reader interest as more than momentary fascination with isolated incidents. The reader asks what the incidents have to do with one another and whether one caused the next.

Operational abstraction:

`A → causes/forces B → causes/forces C → causes/forces D`

The chain can be less visible in literary fiction, but hidden causality is still causality. Complexity does not license randomness.

### IVDIVO CAUSAL-CHAIN TEST
For every major event, identify at least one upstream cause in:
- a prior character decision;
- prior opposition;
- an earlier piece of evidence;
- a resource change;
- a relationship change;
- a world/system consequence.

If the answer is only `the author needed it now`, the event is structurally suspect.

## 1.2 Plot truly begins when equilibrium becomes actionable

Hodgins emphasizes a threatened status quo and the protagonist’s first movement toward a goal.

This sharpens the distinction between:
- background unease;
- anomaly;
- actual story activation.

### IVDIVO ACTIVATION RULE
A story is not fully activated until:

`DISTURBANCE → CHARACTER INTERPRETATION → GOAL/INTENTION → FIRST PURPOSEFUL STEP`.

An unexplained strange event may create atmosphere, but until it changes what someone is trying to do, it is not yet the engine.

## 1.3 Obstruction transforms anecdote into plot

Once the character moves toward a goal, another person, object, system, situation, value conflict, time limit or internal contradiction blocks the path.

### IVDIVO OBSTRUCTION RULE
Each large obstruction must do more than delay. It should alter at least one:

`METHOD | COST | TIME | KNOWLEDGE | TRUST | STATUS | TARGET | LEGALITY | MORAL ACCEPTABILITY | RELATIONSHIP`.

This converges with CRAFT-008 Middle Variable Change.

# 2. LE GUIN — STORY MOVES; PROSE SELECTS

## 2.1 Story is change at every scale

Le Guin’s workbook insists that narrative must move and end somewhere different from where it began, even when the action is internal or apparently mundane.

### IVDIVO MULTISCALE CHANGE TEST
A unit of narrative should be able to state its delta:

- sentence cluster: perception/emphasis shifts;
- paragraph: image/thought/action advances;
- scene: state changes;
- chapter: objective/model/relationship changes;
- act/part: strategy or world model changes;
- novel: local human problem reaches a new state.

Not every unit needs a dramatic reversal. It does need motion appropriate to its scale.

## 2.2 Craft knowledge must become procedural skill

Le Guin’s point is not that a writer should consciously juggle hundreds of technical rules while drafting. Exercises make the writer aware of technique until it becomes usable skill.

### IVDIVO IMPLICATION
The 100-book knowledge system must remain backstage.

Do not burden a drafting pass with all gates simultaneously. Route craft by stage:

`STORY → SCENE → CHARACTER/RELATIONSHIP → POV/KNOWLEDGE → DIALOGUE → DETAIL → RHYTHM → LINE`.

A heavy framework that makes the prose stiff has failed its purpose.

## 2.3 Crowding: concentrate meaning and sensation

Le Guin’s “crowding” is not maximal description. It is dense relevance: exact words, vivid concrete detail, internal echoes, meaningful links.

### IVDIVO CROWDING TEST
Selected detail should preferably perform multiple functions:

`ORIENTATION + CHARACTER + WORLD + TENSION + MOTIF + ACTION POSSIBILITY`.

The goal is not maximal density in every line, but eliminating inert inventory.

## 2.4 Leaping: omit boldly

What is omitted is vastly larger than what is included. Listing is not description; blow-by-blow is not action.

### IVDIVO LEAP TEST
Before dramatizing a transition or action beat, ask:

- Does the reader need to witness it?
- Does it alter state?
- Does it contain choice/resistance/reveal?
- Does it create necessary emotional accumulation?
- Would summary/ellipsis preserve the causal bridge better?

If no, leap.

## 2.5 Action becomes clearer when selection is stronger

Le Guin explicitly warns that exhaustive action description can produce sameness, confusion and boredom.

### IVDIVO ACTION-SELECTION CARD
For each action sequence preserve:

`SPATIAL ORIENTATION | CURRENT OBJECTIVE | DECISIVE ACTIONS | CONSEQUENCE | SENSORY ANCHOR | TACTIC CHANGE`.

Compress repetitions that do not alter the problem.

# 3. ROSENFELD — SCENES MUST INHERIT AND CREATE PRESSURE

## 3.1 Scene is a living component of the whole

Rosenfeld’s scene model treats each scene as both an independent dramatic unit and a contributor to adjacent scenes and the larger storyline.

### IVDIVO SCENE-INTEGRITY TEST
A scene card must answer:

`WHAT PRESSURE ENTERS FROM BEFORE?`
`WHAT DOES THE POV CHARACTER WANT HERE?`
`WHAT COMPLICATION/OPPOSITION APPEARS?`
`WHAT CHANGES?`
`WHAT NEW PRESSURE LEAVES WITH THE READER?`

A scene that works locally but leaves no causal inheritance is a side sketch, not necessarily part of the story engine.

## 3.2 Dramatic tension is potential for things to go wrong

Rosenfeld distinguishes mere emotion from tension. Tension is anticipation that an intention/relationship/situation may fail or worsen.

### IVDIVO TENSION OBJECT TEST
Name the threatened thing:

`RELATIONSHIP | SECRET | GOAL | BODY | STATUS | TRUST | TIME | RESOURCE | MORAL LINE | FUTURE OPPORTUNITY`.

“Tense atmosphere” without a threatened object is weakly specified.

## 3.3 Strong scenes transfer tension rather than simply erase it

A dramatic scene can pay off one pressure while creating another consequence.

Operational chain:

`OLD TENSION → CONFRONTATION/REVEAL → PARTIAL RELEASE → NEW CONSEQUENCE → NEW TENSION`.

### IVDIVO PRESSURE-TRANSFER TEST
When a scene answers a question, ask what caused question now becomes more urgent.

Do not mechanically force a cliffhanger after every scene. But major scenes should usually change the future pressure field.

## 3.4 Emotional scenes need intention plus interaction

Rosenfeld’s dramatic-scene treatment stresses emotional intention, interaction, opposition and escalating complications that drive the protagonist toward change.

### IVDIVO EMOTIONAL-SCENE TEST

`EMOTIONAL WANT → OTHER PERSON/FORCE RESISTS → INTERACTION ESCALATES → PERSON MUST REVEAL/CHOOSE/ACT → RELATIONSHIP/SELF STATE CHANGES`.

Emotion without interaction or consequence risks becoming static introspection.

# 4. BELL — SUSPENSE AS DELAYED RESOLUTION

## 4.1 Suspense requires an unresolved outcome the reader cares about

Bell frames suspense as delayed resolution and pleasurable uncertainty. Delay alone is not enough; the reader must be emotionally invested in the hanging question.

### IVDIVO SUSPENSE EQUATION

`VALUED OUTCOME + UNCERTAINTY + EMOTIONAL INVESTMENT + DELAY/OBSTACLE = SUSPENSE`.

This refines CRAFT-008 by explicitly separating:
- unresolved fact;
- unresolved outcome;
- reader investment.

## 4.2 Macro suspense must survive between local scenes

Bell’s “macro suspense” concept is useful because spectacular local scenes cannot compensate for an absent book-level forward question.

### IVDIVO MACRO-SUSPENSE TEST
At any 10–15% interval of the novel, the reader should be able to articulate a live future-oriented question that matters.

Examples of form, not content:
- Will they find the missing person before X?
- Can she stop the intervention without losing Y?
- Will he expose the truth before the institution closes access?

If the only open question is “what else exists in this world?”, plot propulsion is probably too dependent on lore.

## 4.3 Suspense can be braided in strands

Bell’s cable metaphor is useful: one main suspense line can be supported by shorter unresolved pressures in relationship, investigation, social life, time, body, institution.

### IVDIVO SUSPENSE-BRAID RULE
One novel may track:

`MAIN OUTCOME QUESTION`
+ `RELATIONSHIP QUESTION`
+ `INFORMATION QUESTION`
+ `CLOCK QUESTION`
+ `PERSONAL CONSEQUENCE QUESTION`.

But secondary suspense strands must intersect the main story rather than become five independent novels.

# 5. MCKEE — INFORMATION RELATIONSHIP AS DESIGN VARIABLE

## 5.1 Mystery, suspense and dramatic irony differ by knowledge distribution

McKee’s most transferable mechanism here is not genre taxonomy but audience-information architecture:

- **Mystery:** audience knows less than key characters / truth is concealed.
- **Suspense:** audience and protagonist discover the problem roughly together; uncertainty centers on outcome.
- **Dramatic irony:** audience knows more and fears the moment the character catches up.

### IVDIVO KNOWLEDGE-RELATIONSHIP CARD
For every major sequence specify:

`READER KNOWS | POV KNOWS | OTHER CHARACTER KNOWS | READER SUSPECTS | POV SUSPECTS | TRUTH`.

Then choose the intended reader effect:

`CURIOSITY | ANXIETY | DREAD | SURPRISE | ANTICIPATION | REINTERPRETATION`.

Do not conceal information accidentally. Information asymmetry must be designed.

## 5.2 Closed vs open mystery are different engines

McKee’s closed mystery withholds culprit/solution; open mystery may show the perpetrator and shift the reader’s pleasure toward how detection/proof will succeed.

### IVDIVO APPLICATION
Before plotting a case, choose whether the book’s primary engine is:

- `WHO/WHAT REALLY HAPPENED?`
- `HOW CAN IT BE PROVED/STOPPED?`
- a hybrid that deliberately changes mode.

If this is undecided, clues and reveals tend to pull in contradictory directions.

## 5.3 Red herrings are not permission to lie unfairly

McKee treats red herrings as a curiosity-management technique, but his formulation is narrow and tied strongly to murder mystery conventions.

### IVDIVO LIMIT
Do not convert this into a universal “mislead the reader” rule.

Until dedicated clue-fairness sources are processed, IVDIVO should use the safer principle:

`FALSE INTERPRETATION SHOULD PREFERABLY ARISE FROM TRUE OBSERVATIONS + PLAUSIBLE ASSUMPTIONS`.

The text should not rely on authorial cheating, POV suppression of thoughts the character would naturally have, or impossible-to-infer retroactive facts.

# 6. BRODY — YA WHYDUNIT AS PERSONAL AND MORAL PRESSURE

## 6.1 The detective must be connected to the case

Brody’s YA Whydunit analysis emphasizes that an investigator who has no personal connection and nothing to lose becomes a clue-delivery mechanism.

### IVDIVO WHY-THIS-HERO TEST
The case must intersect at least one of the hero’s:

`RELATIONSHIP | FAMILY | STATUS | FUTURE | IDENTITY | SAFETY | MORAL BELIEF | COMMUNITY | SECRET | DESIRE`.

This does not require “chosen one” destiny. It requires personal consequence.

## 6.2 The detective should be meaningfully unprepared

In YA especially, the investigator gains reader identification partly through being outmatched by the problem.

### IVDIVO COMPETENCE-ASMMETRY TEST
Define:

`WHAT HERO CAN DO | WHAT HERO THINKS THEY CAN DO | WHAT CASE REQUIRES | WHERE THE GAP HURTS`.

Competence should produce progress; inexperience should produce cost, error, dependency or growth.

## 6.3 The secret should illuminate more than logistics

Brody’s “secret” is not just the factual answer to a puzzle. In her genre framing, the final truth reveals something about human motives/darkness.

### IVDIVO SECRET-DEPTH TEST
A major mystery solution should ideally resolve three levels:

1. **FACTUAL:** what happened;
2. **CAUSAL:** why/how it happened;
3. **HUMAN/THEMATIC:** what desire, fear, institution or value conflict made it possible.

## 6.4 The dark turn makes investigation cost identity

Brody describes a point where the investigator becomes so committed that rules, loyalties, ethics, integrity or safety are compromised.

### IVDIVO DARK-TURN TEST

`OBSESSION / NECESSITY → BOUNDARY CROSSED → EVIDENCE/ACCESS GAINED → COST → SELF/RELATIONSHIP CONSEQUENCE`.

A dark turn is not simply “danger increases.” The hero must do something consequential that changes what solving the case means about them.

# 7. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

## 7.1 Story propulsion has three different engines

The processed sources clarify three distinct but combinable engines:

### CAUSALITY
`WHAT HAPPENED BECAUSE OF WHAT?`

Hodgins / Rosenfeld.

### CURIOSITY
`WHAT IS TRUE?`

McKee Mystery / Brody Whydunit.

### SUSPENSE
`WHAT WILL HAPPEN?`

Bell / McKee Suspense.

Strong novels often braid all three, but they should not be confused.

A reveal can satisfy curiosity yet kill suspense if it closes the only forward question. A chase can provide suspense but feel empty if it is not caused by earlier choices. A causal chain can be coherent but dull if the reader has no valued uncertain future.

## 7.2 The ideal reveal has metabolic consequences

Combining Truby from earlier batches with Brody/McKee here:

`CLUE/EVENT → INTERPRETATION → REVEAL → MODEL CHANGE → DECISION → TACTIC CHANGE → CONSEQUENCE → NEW QUESTION`.

If revelation changes no plan, relationship, risk or moral position, it is informational ornament.

## 7.3 Good scene endings do not merely “hook”; they alter the pressure field

Rosenfeld + Bell + Hodgins:

`SCENE OBJECTIVE → COMPLICATION → TURN/ANSWER → CAUSED NEW PRESSURE`.

This is stronger than random interruption because the next scene is born from the previous one.

## 7.4 Selection controls pace without amputating meaning

Le Guin + Hodgins + Rosenfeld:

- causality tells us what cannot be omitted;
- scene function tells us what deserves dramatization;
- leaping tells us what can be skipped;
- crowding tells us which remaining details need density.

Therefore pacing is not “make everything shorter.”

### IVDIVO SELECTION ORDER

1. Preserve causal bridge.
2. Preserve motive/decision.
3. Preserve emotional accumulation required for payoff.
4. Dramatize turns and consequential interactions.
5. Summarize routine transitions/repetition.
6. Use exact detail where it creates orientation, character, tension or world consequence.

## 7.5 Mystery must pressure the hero, not merely test intelligence

Brody + Bell + McKee + prior Storr/Egri:

The investigation becomes dramatically strong when new knowledge threatens:

`SELF-MODEL | RELATIONSHIP | MORAL LINE | FUTURE | STATUS | BELONGING | SAFETY`.

This is the difference between “clue acquisition” and a human story.

# 8. NEW PRODUCTION GATES — BV–CI

## GATE BV — CAUSAL INHERITANCE
Every major event must have an identifiable upstream cause in prior action, evidence, opposition, resource or relationship state.

## GATE BW — STORY ACTIVATION
An anomaly does not fully activate the plot until it changes a character’s goal and produces a purposeful step.

## GATE BX — MULTISCALE CHANGE
Scene/chapter/sequence must end in a meaningfully changed state appropriate to its scale.

## GATE BY — CROWD / LEAP SELECTION
Keep exact, high-function detail; omit routine or repetitive material that does not alter causality, emotion, knowledge or choice.

## GATE BZ — ACTION SELECTIVITY
Action sequences preserve objective, orientation, decisive beats, consequence and tactic changes; repetitive blow-by-blow is compressed.

## GATE CA — PRESSURE TRANSFER
A major scene should resolve or transform inherited pressure and create a caused future pressure.

## GATE CB — TENSION OBJECT
Every important tension sequence names what concrete valued thing may be lost, damaged, exposed or denied.

## GATE CC — MACRO SUSPENSE
The novel maintains a live valued future-oriented question across the full book, not merely local scene excitement.

## GATE CD — KNOWLEDGE RELATIONSHIP
Mystery/suspense/dramatic-irony sequences specify reader knowledge, POV knowledge, other-character knowledge and intended information effect.

## GATE CE — MYSTERY MODE
Before clue design, define whether the primary engine is hidden truth, proof/capture/prevention, or a deliberate hybrid/mode shift.

## GATE CF — FAIR MISINTERPRETATION [PROVISIONAL]
Prefer misleading interpretations generated by true evidence plus plausible assumptions; do not rely on retroactive impossible facts or unnatural POV concealment.

## GATE CG — WHY THIS INVESTIGATOR
The investigator must have a personal stake/connection that makes this case consequential beyond intellectual curiosity.

## GATE CH — SECRET DEPTH
A major mystery payoff should address factual truth, causal truth and human/thematic truth.

## GATE CI — DARK TURN COST
A major investigation should pressure the hero toward a consequential boundary crossing whose cost survives beyond the clue gained.

# 10. DIRECT CONSEQUENCE FOR BOOK 2 — [OUR SYNTHESIS / DRAFT — NOT CANON UNTIL FOUNDER APPROVAL]

The current strongest Book-2 case candidate benefits from a clearer separation of engines:

- **Causality:** a teen disappears/returns changed; that event forces action.
- **Mystery:** what was done and by whom?
- **Suspense:** another teen may undergo the intervention before the group can stop it.
- **Personal connection:** the heroine’s investigation leads toward her father/Smith and therefore threatens family identity/trust.
- **Potential dark turn:** to reach/save the next target, she may have to violate a serious boundary—family trust, institutional rule, privacy, legality or another young person’s consent. Exact choice is not frozen.

This is stronger than treating “Smith’s true nature” as the only question. The book-level mystery must remain attached to a concrete case that can close locally.

# 11. LIMITS / CONFLICTS

1. McKee’s closed/open mystery taxonomy is useful as an information-design lens but should not be treated as exhaustive modern mystery taxonomy.
2. Bell’s strong commercial suspense orientation can over-pressure quieter fiction if applied indiscriminately.
3. Brody’s Whydunit model is YA/commercial and does not define all detective fiction.
4. “Dark turn” must not become a mandatory crime/ethical breach in every mystery; the transferable mechanism is consequential boundary pressure.
5. Le Guin’s “crowding” can be misread as constant density. White space, quiet and simple language remain valid tools.
6. A scene does not need an artificial cliffhanger merely to satisfy pressure-transfer logic.
7. Current clue-fairness rule remains provisional until specialist detective/fair-play sources are processed.
