# 1. SOURCE PASSPORT

Herman connects narratology with cognitive science and asks two linked questions:

1. how readers construct storyworlds from narrative cues;
2. how narrative itself helps people make sense of persons, actions, reasons and experience.

The book architecture is especially relevant to IVDIVO:

- reasons for action;
- persons embedded in environments and relationships;
- worldmaking;
- perspective taking;
- character/person categorization;
- scenes of talk;
- narrative as instrument of mind;
- distributed intelligence;
- storied minds / folk psychology.

This complements Chatman's structural story/discourse framework with a stronger **person-and-reason** layer.

---

# 2. REASONS FOR ACTION — ACTION IS NOT JUST MOTION

A central Herman mechanism is that narratives support provisional attribution of **reasons for action**. Readers do not understand characters merely by registering observable motion; they infer why a person acts from context, goals, beliefs, relations, norms, history and situation.

## IVDIVO translation

For important character action, record separately:

`OBSERVED BEHAVIOR`
→ `AVAILABLE CONTEXT`
→ `CHARACTER'S REASON(S)`
→ `OTHER CHARACTERS' INFERENCE`
→ `READER'S CURRENT INFERENCE`
→ `WHAT MAY STILL BE WRONG`.

### Production consequence

Do not collapse:

`PREDICTED BEHAVIOR = REASON`

or

`LIKELY COOPERATION = CONSENT`.

This becomes exceptionally important in Book Two because Lattice can predict that a person will probably yield, wait, assist or alter route. A prediction about behavior does **not** establish the person's reason, willingness or right to be treated as a resource.

---

# 3. PERSON LEVEL — DO NOT REDUCE PEOPLE TO VARIABLES

Herman explicitly develops an antireductionist “person-level” approach: persons should not be exhausted by lower-level properties or externally assigned categories. He emphasizes ecological and intersubjective embedding — people exist and act in environments, relationships and social contexts.

## IVDIVO Person Model Gate

Any system, institution, narrator or character who models another person should distinguish:

- body / access conditions;
- observed behavior;
- declared preference;
- inferred probability;
- social relation;
- legal authority;
- consent;
- goals;
- reasons;
- uncertainty about all of the above.

### Failure mode

A fictional “model of a person” becomes morally and dramatically weak when it is merely a trait vector that the story treats as equivalent to the person.

This is also a craft rule for character bibles: **the profile is not the person**. The manuscript must keep generating behavior that is contextual, relational and revisable.

# 4. STORYWORLD CO-CONSTRUCTION

Herman argues that readers use textual cues to construct answers to WHO / WHAT / WHERE / WHEN / HOW / WHY dimensions of a storyworld, including its entities, properties and relations.

## IVDIVO Worlding Test

Every major SF/future element should allow the reader to construct:

`WHAT EXISTS`
`WHERE IT IS`
`WHAT IT CAN/CANNOT DO`
`WHO USES IT`
`WHAT RELATIONS IT CHANGES`
`WHAT ORDINARY BEHAVIOR IT PRODUCES`
`WHAT CONSEQUENCE FOLLOWS IF IT FAILS`.

### Anti-exposition consequence

Worldbuilding is stronger when the reader infers the system through use, friction and consequence rather than receiving a detached encyclopedia paragraph.

For Rivergate, variable-height handrails, refuge-room atmosphere markers, route widths, translation clusters and mixed-family movement are stronger than a paragraph saying “Rivergate was designed for multiple species.”

# 5. PERSPECTIVE SHAPES THE WHOLE WORLD MODEL

Herman treats perspective not merely as camera position but as a world-shaping factor influencing how readers build the storyworld's people, norms, conflicts and trajectories.

## IVDIVO application

A POV pass should ask not only:

`WHAT DOES ETHAN SEE?`

but:

- which system properties does Ethan notice because of his authority obsession?
- what does Maya notice because of autonomy/control?
- what does Aoife notice because of framing/context?
- what does Samir notice because of model/interpretation boundaries?
- what does Ollie notice because of failure modes and physical fallback?

The same Rivergate should therefore become **five meaningfully different cognitive environments**, not one authorial setting viewed by five interchangeable cameras.

# 6. DISTRIBUTED INTELLIGENCE — GROUP SOLVING WITHOUT ONE GENIUS

Herman's perspective work and later chapter on narrative embedding/distributed intelligence support a model in which intelligent behavior can emerge from transindividual systems distributed across people, tools, records, environments and time.

## IVDIVO Ensemble Intelligence Engine

For a complex problem, map:

`WHO NOTICES`
`WHO HAS HISTORY`
`WHO HAS ACCESS`
`WHO HAS A TOOL`
`WHO HAS AUTHORITY`
`WHO HAS CONTEXT`
`WHO HAS COUNTEREVIDENCE`
`WHO CAN ACT`
`WHO PAYS THE COST`.

No one character needs to contain the whole solution.

### Book Two fit

The climax architecture already naturally distributes cognition:

- **Samir** → derived adversarial evidence + limits;
- **Aoife** → contextual incident record;
- **Maya / Nico** → lived agency evidence;
- **Ollie** → workable physical fallback;
- **Aaron** → strongest argument for keeping system live;
- **Ethan** → bounded legal authority to decide.

This is stronger than making Ethan the person who discovers, understands, proves and solves everything.

# 7. SCENES OF TALK AS SOCIAL ACTION

Herman's worked example on scenes of talk treats communication not as information transfer alone but as positioning: speakers place themselves relative to each other and to contested topics. Importantly, **withholding talk can itself be causal**.

## Dialogue extension

For important dialogue, add to the existing verbal-action model:

`WHAT IS SAID`
`WHAT REASON IS OFFERED`
`WHAT POSITION TOWARD OTHER PERSON IS CREATED`
`WHAT POSITION TOWARD THE TOPIC IS CREATED`
`WHAT IS WITHHELD`
`WHAT CONSEQUENCE DOES WITHHOLDING CREATE`.

This complements McKee's tactic/subtext model rather than replacing it.

# 8. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

## Herman + McKee
McKee: action/dialogue as purposeful effort toward change.  
Herman: readers make sense of action by provisionally attributing reasons in ecological/social context.

**Synthesis:** `ACTION TACTIC` and `REASON FOR ACTION` are related but not identical.

## Herman + Chatman
Chatman: structural distinction between events/existents and discourse.  
Herman: storyworld construction and person-level sense making.

**Synthesis:** Chatman helps identify **what information/event function exists**; Herman helps test **whether the reader can build a coherent human/world model from how it is presented**.

## Herman + Storr / Zunshine
All emphasize readers' construction of other minds, but Herman adds stronger warnings against reducing persons to explicit belief/desire lists and against overextending narrative explanation.

# 9. APPLICATION — BOOK TWO / LATTICE

## Structural confirmation

The core ethical problem of Lattice becomes more precise:

**Lattice is not evil because it predicts behavior. Its danger is treating behavioral probability as if it exhaustively represents a person-level reason or entitlement.**

The exact fault line is:

`OBSERVED PATTERN`
→ `PREDICTED ACCOMMODATION`
→ **ILLICIT STEP** → `ASSUMED AVAILABLE COOPERATION`.

The novel should keep this distinction legible without turning into a philosophy lecture.

## Recommended scene evidence

Show at least three different cases where identical outward compliance has different reasons:

1. person yields because they genuinely prefer to help;
2. person yields because they misunderstand the recommendation as mandatory;
3. person yields because refusing would impose social/translation cost on a companion.

If Lattice treats all three as the same “high accommodation” signal, the reader experiences the moral problem concretely.

## Important constraint

Do not make the system obviously malicious or stupid. Its predictions may be highly accurate. The problem is that **accuracy about likely behavior is insufficient authority to allocate burdens**.

# 10. APPLICATION — POV / ENSEMBLE

Book Two should use cognitive specialization more aggressively.

### Ethan
Notices permission, scope, thresholds, responsibility, timing, escalation paths.

### Maya
Notices whether choice is real, pressured, technically allowed, or paternalistically overridden.

### Aoife
Notices framing, omitted context, sequence, what a clip makes viewers infer.

### Samir
Notices classification boundaries, evidence status, model-versus-person substitutions, provenance claims.

### Ollie
Notices physical constraints, maintenance, failure state, recoverability, simple alternatives.

**Gate:** if any of these five could own a paragraph unchanged merely by swapping names, the POV/attention architecture is under-differentiated.

# 11. NEW PRODUCTION GATES

### Gate II — Behavior/Reason Separation
Never treat observed or predicted behavior as equivalent to the person's reason for acting.

### Gate IJ — Prediction/Consent Firewall
Likelihood of cooperation cannot be narratively or institutionally treated as consent without an explicit bridge.

### Gate IK — Person-Level Integrity
A model/profile/category must not be treated as exhaustive of a person.

### Gate IL — Ecological Characterization
Character behavior must be interpreted in relation to environment, relationships, norms and available options.

### Gate IM — Storyworld Constructibility
Major SF elements must provide enough enacted cues for readers to construct entity, relation, capability and consequence models.

### Gate IN — POV World-Bias
Each POV must organize attention according to character-specific concerns, not only filter vocabulary.

### Gate IO — Distributed Intelligence
For ensemble problems, knowledge, tools, authority and action should be distributed when the story has already established distinct competencies.

### Gate IP — No Protagonist Knowledge Monopoly
The protagonist may own the final decision without owning every discovery, interpretation, proof and solution.

### Gate IQ — Communication Positioning
Important dialogue must be audited for how it changes speaker/hearer positions, not only information content.

### Gate IR — Withholding Consequence
Silence/non-disclosure counts as action when it changes relationship, interpretation or later options.

### Gate IS — Model/Person Dramatic Proof
If a story argues that a system mis-models people, demonstrate the gap through lived cases, not only thematic dialogue.

### Gate IT — Accurate Prediction Is Not Moral Authorization
A system may be predictively successful and still lack justification for burden allocation; preserve this distinction to avoid villainizing technology.
