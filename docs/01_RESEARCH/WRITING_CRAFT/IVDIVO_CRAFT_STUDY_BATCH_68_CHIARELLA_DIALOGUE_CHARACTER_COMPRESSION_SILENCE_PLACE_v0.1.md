# TRANSFERABLE MECHANISMS
1. **Listen → select → shape.** Real speech is raw material, not a transcript target.
2. **Dialogue pressure vector.** Track direction, speed and relational distance, not only overt conflict.
3. **All, but not everything.** Preserve enough speech to infer the whole person; remove repeated filler/tics.
4. **Dialogue load budget.** A conversation should not carry exposition + theme + twist + climax + resolution merely for efficiency.
5. **Silence is action.** Refusal, uncertainty, intimacy, status and protection can be expressed by not answering.
6. **Place participates.** Ambient movement, objects and interruptions can alter meaning and rhythm.
7. **Narrator modulation.** Turn narration up/down according to the effect; avoid both wall-of-dialogue and microreaction clutter.
8. **Character voice stack.** Worldview → attention → diction → syntax → rhythm → directness/evasion → relation-specific change.
9. **Dialect integrity.** Prefer grounded diction/syntax/rhythm over phonetic caricature.
10. **Tags are rhythm tools.** Neutral `said` is often better than theatrical synonyms; tag position can change emphasis.
11. **Discovery ≠ validation.** Overheard lines can generate a scene, but the discovered scene must still earn its place in ONE MAIN STORY.

# PRODUCTION GATES — B68
## B68-G01 — EXACT-WORD NECESSITY
If exact wording does not change trust, consent, evidence, relationship, decision, power or emotion, consider indirect dialogue/summary.

## B68-G02 — RELATION-SPECIFIC VOICE
A major character must not sound identical with friend / parent / instructor / lover / public audience / conscious robot.

## B68-G03 — ALL-BUT-NOT-EVERYTHING
Repeated signature words may establish voice once or twice; later repetition requires new function.

## B68-G04 — DIALOGUE LOAD BUDGET
Assign one primary change and limited secondary functions. Split/compress overloaded exchanges.

## B68-G05 — SILENCE FUNCTION
Silence must have a traceable reason/effect. Never use it merely to avoid writing a difficult answer.

## B68-G06 — PLACE INTEGRATION
At least when relevant, let the physical/social environment interrupt or reframe talk instead of inserting decorative stage business.

## B68-G07 — MODULATION BEFORE MICROREACTION
Before `quote → reaction → quote → reaction`, test whether one coherent paragraph of POV/place/memory produces better rhythm.

## B68-G08 — DIALECT DIGNITY
Do not infer psychology from accent/class shorthand. Do not use phonetic spelling without strong linguistic/cultural grounding.

## B68-G09 — NO ROBOT VOICE DEFAULT
Conscious robots require individual and relation-specific speech strategies, including refusal and silence.

## B68-G10 — POST-MONEY VOICE
Do not make future youth sound contemporary by reintroducing rent/credits/consumer/career slang. Their normal assumptions must reveal the civilization.

## B68-G11 — EPISTEMIC SPEECH STATUS
For consciousness schools/mystery: `observed / reproduced / interpreted / suspected / known` must remain verbally distinct.

## B68-G12 — TAG/PUNCTUATION LATE PASS
Do not fix dead dialogue with clever tags, adverbs, italics or exclamation marks. Repair want/tactic/response first.

Unified dialogue pipeline:
`SCENE NECESSITY → EXACT-WORD NECESSITY → WANT/RELATION → PRESSURE/TAC­TIC → RESPONSE → SILENCE/PLACE/MODULATION → COMPRESSION → TAGS/PUNCTUATION → LINE EDIT`.

# ANTI-CHOP CONSEQUENCE
Short-line density is not a dialogue-quality metric. First test whether the material should be summarized, modulated, allowed silence, or grouped into coherent paragraphs. Sentence-length repair comes later.
