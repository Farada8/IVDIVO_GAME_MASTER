# IVDIVO CRAFT STUDY — BATCH 207
## Roy Peter Clark — Grammar of Intent · Punctuation · Sentence Rhythm · Reader Effect

**Source:** Roy Peter Clark, *The Glamour of Grammar* (2010)  
**Lifecycle:** strict-complete / WIDER  
**Pass:** 172

## Why this source matters
The source supplies a micro-scale control layer for prose. Its strongest contribution is not prescriptive grammar; it is the idea that language choices encode pace, emphasis, hierarchy, agency, stance and reader inference.

## Production laws
### B207-G01 — Grammar of Intent
`READER EFFECT → LANGUAGE OPTION → LOCAL CONSEQUENCE → RETEST`.

### B207-G02 — No short-sentence KPI
`ACTION / SUSPENSE / MYSTERY / EMOTION / SHORT FORM ≠ SHORT-SENTENCE DENSITY`.

### B207-G03 — Functional Fragment
A fragment must buy a specific effect: shock, pause, inventory, intimacy/informality, focus, or release. If it merely replaces a normal clause, merge/rewrite.

### B207-G04 — Period Overbraking Audit
Multiple consecutive short independent sentences can create repeated full-stop friction. Test whether the reader-state should continue across them.

### B207-G05 — Sentence Contrast
`SHORT ↔ LONG` derives force from contrast and surrounding syntax. Neither length is inherently faster or stronger.

### B207-G06 — Punctuation Encodes Relation
Periods, commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, parentheses, ellipses and quotation marks should map idea relation / pause / omission / stance—not decorative habit.

### B207-G07 — Dependency Distance
Keep semantically/grammatically dependent elements close enough for the reader to process them, unless separation produces a deliberate effect.

### B207-G08 — Agency Visibility
Passive voice is legitimate when the receiver or consequence deserves emphasis. It is suspect when it hides who decided, acted, authorized, allocated or bears responsibility.

### B207-G09 — No `to be` Purge
Linking/state constructions perform real semantic work. Replace only when the alternative better serves the sentence's job.

### B207-G10 — Strategic Tense Shift
Tense changes must buy immediacy, reflective distance, foregrounding or another legible effect. Otherwise stabilize.

### B207-G11 — Productive vs Accidental Ambiguity
`CLEAR CONTEXT + MULTIPLE INTENDED MEANINGS = PRODUCTIVE`.
`UNCLEAR REFERENT / MODIFIER / CONNOTATION = DEFECT`.

### B207-G12 — Essential / Nonessential Information
Sentence structure and punctuation should reveal what the reader must hold as load-bearing versus supplementary.

### B207-G13 — Shared-Ground Vocabulary
`SPECIAL TERM → USER/COMMUNITY → READER CONTEXT → LEGIBILITY → STORY FUNCTION`.

### B207-G14 — Acronym/Jargon Ceiling
Abbreviations that save insiders time may cost outsiders comprehension. Do not let institutional shorthand conceal actual mechanism, authority or money/status assumptions.

### B207-G15 — Quotation/Stance Firewall
`SCARE QUOTE / WORD CHOICE → STANCE CUE`, not evidence.

### B207-G16 — Analogy Firewall
`METAPHOR / SIMILE / ANALOGY → ASSOCIATION / HYPOTHESIS`, not proof.

### B207-G17 — Particularity Ladder
Move between concrete and abstract:
`PARTICULAR → PATTERN → PROVISIONAL MEANING` or `ABSTRACTION → LOAD-BEARING EXAMPLE → READER INFERENCE`.

### B207-G18 — Character-Owned Register
`WORLDVIEW + RELATIONSHIP + WANT + STATUS + ATTENTION + OMISSION → DICTION / SYNTAX / RHYTHM`.
No universal authorial cleverness.

### B207-G19 — New Paragraph Requires New Work
A paragraph break should correspond to a genuine shift in speaker, focus, time/place, tactic, evidence status, relation, emphasis or unit of thought.

### B207-G20 — Exact Wording Evidence Lock
If wording, punctuation, spelling or quotation is itself evidence, adaptation/copyedit must preserve provenance and meaning.

## Cross-source synthesis
### Le Guin
Strong convergence: sentence length and fragments derive force from contrast/function; persistent short syntax becomes choppy.

### Williams/Bizup
Clark's dependency distance and punctuation choices fit the reader-state/topic-chain model. Both put processing clarity above slogans about shortness.

### Hodgins
Converges on sentence variation and paragraph-level coherence as the repair for jerky prose.

### Zinsser
Converges on clarity and removal of clutter, but Clark is more explicit that `to be`, passive voice and longer syntax can be fully functional.

### Genette / Chatman
Clark operates downstream: discourse order/duration/frequency and POV determine the information architecture; grammar and punctuation execute it at sentence scale.

`BOOK ENGINE → CHAPTER FUNCTION → SCENE / NARRATIVE MODE → ORDER/DURATION/FREQUENCY → POV/EVIDENCE → PARAGRAPH INTENT / READER-STATE → SENTENCE CONTRAST / RHYTHM → PUNCTUATION → WORDING / COPY`.

## Red Team limits
- 2010 usage guidance is not automatically 2026 inclusive-language policy.
- Journalism examples are references, not novel house style.
- Etymology and analogy are not scientific proof.
- Old Internet/Twitter recommendations are historical.
- Clark's preference for short words in some contexts must not override his own evidence for long syntax, run-ons and fragments used by function.

## Final Batch 207 synthesis
**Structure decides what language must do. Paragraphs maintain reader-state. Sentence shape and punctuation deliver local force.**
