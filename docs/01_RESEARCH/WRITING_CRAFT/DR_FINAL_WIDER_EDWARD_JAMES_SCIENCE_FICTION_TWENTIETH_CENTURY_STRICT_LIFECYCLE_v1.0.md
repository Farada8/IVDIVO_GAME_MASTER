## 3. Core claims retained as craft mechanisms

### M1 — Genre is a historical reader contract, not a metaphysical category
Definitions of SF repeatedly function as prescriptions for what writers and readers want the genre to become.

`GENRE LABEL → EXPECTATION SET`, not `GENRE LABEL → MANDATORY STORY FORM`.

### M2 — SF-specific reader cognition is active model construction
SF frequently withholds ordinary-world explanation and asks readers to infer rules from contextual clues.

`TEXTUAL TRACE → READER HYPOTHESIS → MORE CONTEXT → WORLD MODEL UPDATE`.

The reader can enjoy not yet knowing what a term means if the uncertainty is legible, context permits eventual inference, and the author is not cheating by hiding an already-known POV fact.

### M3 — Familiarity compression
A future-world fact may need little explanation when its meaning can be recovered from POV action, context, causal consequence or genuinely shared genre knowledge.

**Gate:** omit explanation only when the intended reader can reconstruct enough to continue.

### M4 — Novum causalization
A novum is not a decorative device.

`NOVUM → AFFORDANCE / CONSTRAINT → SOCIAL OR HISTORICAL RIPPLE → CHARACTER CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE`.

If the new technology or social rule can be removed without changing action, relations, institutions or future options, it is spectacle/world-tourism rather than structural SF.

### M5 — The lived future beats the technology catalogue
Strong SF often makes extraordinary conditions ordinary to inhabitants. The reader learns through what characters treat as routine, strange, shameful, prestigious or actionable.

`WORLD DIFFERENCE → PERSON-SPECIFIC NORMALITY → BEHAVIOR → READER INFERENCE`.

### M6 — Sense of wonder is an effect, not a content category
Scale, alienness or a Big Dumb Object can create wonder without complete explanation. Wonder is strongest when it changes perceived scale, a working model, emotional/ethical position or future choice.

`WONDER → MODEL / VALUE / OPTION-SPACE CHANGE`.

### M7 — Reader uncertainty can be a feature
Dune, Vance, Gibson and other examples show that readers can hold unexplained terms/data in a mental waiting room.

`PRODUCTIVE UNCERTAINTY = CLEARLY UNKNOWN`  
`CONFUSION = READER CANNOT TELL WHAT QUESTION OR STATE EXISTS`.

### M8 — Shared mega-text is both compression and risk
Genre communities accumulate shared assumptions and shorthand. That permits abbreviated world-building, but can also fossilize conventions, exclude new readers and confuse inherited trope with causal necessity.

### M9 — Market ecology shapes “craft”
Magazine length, pulps, paperback economics, fandom, workshops, awards and media franchises all influenced what later looked like natural genre form.

`HISTORICAL CONVENTION → ASK WHAT ECOSYSTEM CREATED IT → KEEP ONLY IF CURRENT STORY EFFECT NEEDS IT`.

### M10 — Community feedback is high-signal but nonrepresentative
Fandom gives writers unusually articulate feedback and tradition knowledge, but highly involved fans are not the entire audience.

`FAN RESPONSE = SEGMENT EVIDENCE`, not universal reader truth.

### M11 — Recursive/intertextual SF requires payment
References to earlier SF can create resonance and tradition depth, but become insider noise when they do not pay in story function.

### M12 — Future society must actually be allowed to change
A recurring strength of SF is its ability to make present social arrangements contingent rather than permanent. For IVDIVO this directly protects post-money urban life, nonhuman personhood, transformed work/training/status and altered institutional norms.

## 4. Major limits and rejected universals

1. **Anglo-American centrality:** not an exhaustive global genre history.
2. **Historical market data:** sales/publishing data are 1994 evidence only.
3. **Sense-of-wonder nostalgia:** habituation is not cured by endless scale inflation.
4. **Technocratic competence:** `COMPETENCE ≠ LEGITIMACY ≠ CONSENT ≠ DECISION OWNERSHIP`.
5. **Pseudo-science / psi leakage:** ESP, telepathy, UFO and occult history are not scientific evidence.
6. **SF-as-education infodump:** Gernsback didacticism is historical, not mandatory.
7. **Style movement essentialism:** pulp velocity, New Wave opacity and cyberpunk density are historical programs, not prose laws.
8. **Alien-as-human-mirror reduction:** allegory does not replace literal nonhuman personhood.

## 6. Cross-source comparison

### vs Cambridge Companion to Science Fiction
Cambridge supplies specialized theory by multiple scholars; James gives a diachronic account of how genre protocols and institutions formed. Use James to audit historical inheritance; Cambridge for specialist lenses.

### vs Wolf — Building Imaginary Worlds
Wolf: world database, transmedia, infrastructure. James: how readers decode unfamiliar worlds and how shared genre knowledge changes the explanation budget.

### vs Chatman / Genette
Chatman/Genette model discourse generally. James adds SF-specific inference:
`UNKNOWN TERM / NOVUM → CONTEXTUAL CLUES → READER WORLD MODEL`.

### vs Zunshine / Herman / le Carré
Those sources discipline mind-reading, causal inference and source lineage. James supplies the SF reader’s appetite for model-building, constrained by:
`TRACE ≠ INTERPRETATION ≠ VERIFIED FACT`.

### vs Weir / Crouch
Weir/Crouch are mechanism labs for technical/model-update suspense. James explains the historical reader tradition that makes this pleasure recognizable as SF.

### vs Le Guin / Prose / Hodgins / Clark
James’s history demonstrates that pulp action, New Wave experiment and cyberpunk density are competing historical styles. This reinforces:
`ACTION / SF / SUSPENSE ≠ SHORT-SENTENCE DENSITY`.

## 7. IVDIVO operationalization

### ONE MAIN STORY
SF is a mode/contract, not a causal engine.

`SF WORLD / NOVUM + MYSTERY / RESCUE / RELATIONSHIP / COMING-OF-AGE ENGINE → ONE MAIN STORY`.

### Orbital Youth
- Show post-money normality through lived defaults, not a lecture.
- A new technology earns page space when it changes affordances, responsibility, relationships or choices.
- Do not import salary/rent/consumer status merely because old SF did.
- Allow future culture to be historically transformed.
- Protect reader orientation: unfamiliar term clusters need contextual anchors.

### Conscious robots / nonhuman civilization
Historical SF often used robots/aliens as mirrors for humanity. IVDIVO must add:
`OWN WANT → OWN MODEL/EVIDENCE → OWN RELATIONSHIPS → CONSENT/REFUSAL → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE`.

### Consciousness-practice schools
Historical SF fascination with psi is a warning, not permission:
`PRACTITIONER OBSERVATION → REPRODUCIBLE EFFECT → SCHOOL INTERPRETATION → SOURCE LINEAGE → COMPETING INTERPRETATION → INDEPENDENT TEST → ONTOLOGY CEILING`.

### ROOM 917
Use SF-style inference discipline only where relevant:
`TRACE → PARTIAL MODEL → NEW CLUE → MODEL UPDATE`.
Uncanny atmosphere must not mutate evidence.

### LESSON ZERO
No global rewrite. James provides no new concrete current defect evidence.
`NO NEW EVIDENCE → NO NEW GLOBAL REWRITE`.

## 8. Manuscript validation conclusions

1. Current IVDIVO post-money and nonhuman-personhood firewalls are strengthened, not weakened.
2. Technical exposition should be routed through present questions and consequences, not removed categorically.
3. Reader inference is a feature; confusion is not.

5. An SF book still needs one dominant causal engine.
6. Genre-history conventions are candidates for diagnosis, not production commands.
7. The current anti-choppiness architecture remains correct.

## 9. Final synthesis

The highest-value contribution of Edward James is historical de-naturalization: many things writers experience as “what science fiction does” are products of magazines, editors, fandoms, movements, markets and inherited reading protocols.

The production question is not “Is this sufficiently science-fictional?” It is:

`WHAT DOES THE WORLD DIFFERENCE MAKE POSSIBLE / IMPOSSIBLE → WHAT DOES THE READER NEED TO INFER → WHAT CHANGES FOR A PERSON → HOW DOES THAT CHANGE THE MAIN CAUSAL ENGINE?`

That mechanism is retained. Historical prescriptions are not.
