## High-value provisional findings (not final gates yet)

1. Scene launches can legitimately be action, narrative-summary, or setting launches. Action-first is a tool, not a universal rule.
2. Narrative summary is legitimate when it saves dramatized logistics, supplies prerequisite information, or gives access to otherwise unavailable interior state.
3. Complication must be causal: it should reveal character or move plot; difficulty `just because` is a failure mode.
4. Rosenfeld’s recommendation that every scene withhold plot information is too strong for IVDIVO. Disclosure, competence, intimacy, consequence, decision, or relationship change can power a scene without artificial withholding.
5. Scene endings need modulation. Her zoom-in / zoom-out distinction is useful, but cliffhangers are explicitly not mandatory.
6. Ending summary is dangerous when it merely restates what the scene already demonstrated; existing IVDIVO `No End-of-Scene Restatement` stays stricter.
7. Setting should be causal and character-filtered, not tourism. Physical interaction with place is stronger than static observation.
8. Object emphasis creates an implicit reader contract: description weight predicts expected significance/payoff.
9. Sensory writing should be POV-specific; `saw/heard/felt` filter phrasing is often unnecessary when POV already identifies the sensor.
10. Touch carries consent, cultural, relationship, threat, and intimacy information and should be treated as story data, not decoration.
11. Character and plot should be mutually dependent. If the story proceeds unchanged without the protagonist’s participation, story ownership is suspect.
12. New information works best when it changes the reader/character model and causes changed action, feeling, interpretation, or consequence.
13. Chapter 8 strongly supports reviewing previous scenes before inventing the next one, specifically to prevent repeated revelations and disconnected scene invention.
14. Rosenfeld’s `Energetic Markers` architecture is school-specific. IVDIVO retains the transferable mechanism — pressure should force strategy/commitment change — but rejects mandatory Rededication/Dark Night/Triumph template lock.

## Cross-school tension already identified

Rosenfeld converges strongly with Card/Bell/Hodgins/Kress on:
- character shown in action;
- scene consequence feeding the next scene;
- protagonist-specific response under pressure;
- selective summary;
- concrete setting;
- gradual character change.

Potential conflicts requiring later resolution:
- `every scene withholds information` versus IVDIVO fair-play/source-monitoring;
- `every scene pertains to plot` versus relationship/emotional causality and quiet recovery;
- escalation toward a singular Dark Night versus ensemble/social/procedural structures;
- frequent complication rhetoric versus IVDIVO success/competence/reward modulation.
