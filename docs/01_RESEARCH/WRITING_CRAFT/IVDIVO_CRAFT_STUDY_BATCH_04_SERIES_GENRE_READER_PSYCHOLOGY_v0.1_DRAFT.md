# 1. YORKE — INTO THE WOODS

## 1.1 Story begins when normality is disturbed and a problem demands movement

Yorke’s simple formulation is useful because it removes decorative complexity. A story introduces a person in a world, something happens that puts the world out of balance, and the resulting problem demands a journey of action.

Transferable mechanism:

`NORMAL / CURRENT MODEL → DISTURBANCE → PROBLEM → GOAL → JOURNEY THROUGH OBSTACLES → CHANGE`

### IVDIVO TEST — STORY EXISTENCE
Before worldbuilding or chapter design, answer:
- what happened that should not have happened?
- who is personally forced to deal with it?
- what concrete problem now exists?
- what would count as “dealt with” by the end?

If these answers are absent, there is premise/worldbuilding but not yet a story.

## 1.2 Change is the chassis of drama

Yorke repeatedly treats dramatic structure as an architecture of change. Full stories express large change; acts express major intermediate change; scenes express local change.

This triangulates strongly with Coyne’s value shift and IVDIVO’s existing `scene = unit of change` law.

### Operational synthesis
Do not ask only, “What happens in this scene?” Ask:

`WHAT CHANGES? → WHO CAUSED IT? → WHAT NEW ACTION DOES THAT CHANGE DEMAND?`

## 1.3 Fractal structure

Yorke’s “fractal” claim: story, act, scene, and beat can reproduce analogous structural functions at different scales. The most useful aspect is not literal mathematical fractality but **nested dramatic completeness**.

A chapter/episode can have its own:
- setup;
- disturbance;
- development;
- turn/crisis;
- outcome;
while remaining a functional part of a larger whole.

### IVDIVO TEST — NESTED COMPLETENESS
For a long novel/serial arc verify three layers separately:

**BOOK:** main problem → book climax → book result.  
**SEQUENCE/ACT:** local objective → turn → state change.  
**SCENE:** immediate objective → opposition → turn → consequence.

A strong macro arc does not excuse shapeless scenes; strong scenes do not create a book if the book lacks a main action.

## 1.4 Inciting incident is a function, not merely one explosion

Yorke notes that a first act may contain a chain of disturbances. The useful question is not “Which exact second is THE inciting incident?” but which event/sequence actually gives the protagonist a story-driving problem and pushes commitment.

### IVDIVO TEST
Separate:
- first anomaly / disturbance;
- recognition of the problem;
- assignment/choice of concrete goal;
- irreversible commitment.

They may coincide or occur across multiple scenes.

## 1.5 Midpoint as irreversible knowledge/change

Yorke treats the midpoint as a point where a character encounters a truth they cannot fully unknow and begins to embrace what was previously feared/resisted.

This aligns with Weiland’s reactive→proactive transition and Storr’s model-break/update concept.

### IVDIVO MIDPOINT GATE
A midpoint must do more than increase danger. It should alter at least one deep variable:
- the true problem;
- the protagonist’s role;
- the identity of the target/opponent;
- the meaning of prior evidence;
- the moral interpretation;
- the relationship map;
- the hero’s model of self/world;
- the strategy.

## 1.6 Series vs serial

Yorke’s distinction is highly useful for IVDIVO:

**SERIAL:** one larger story is distributed across episodes/installments; episodes function partly like acts of the macro story.

**SERIES:** each installment has a self-contained story-of-the-week/book while a renewable engine and stable format allow recurrence.

Many modern works hybridize the two.

### IVDIVO DOUBLE-CONTOUR SYNTHESIS
This directly strengthens the existing IVDIVO rule:

`LOCAL COMPLETE STORY + CONTINUING LIFE / META-ARC`

Each book/episode must close its principal human problem before relying on a franchise hook.

## 1.7 Renewable story engine

Yorke identifies repeatable series as having:
- a self-contained weekly/story unit;
- a clear renewable engine;
- format rules / POV rules;
- a recognizable social/status world;
- characters audiences want to revisit;
- a stable “home” or precinct that generates stories.

### IVDIVO SERIES ENGINE PASSPORT
Every long-running line must define:

`HOME/PRECINCT | RECURRING JOB/NEED | INTAKE OF NEW PROBLEMS | CORE POV RULE | REPEATABLE ACTION LOOP | RELATIONSHIP HOME | LOCAL CLOSURE TYPE | VARIABLES THAT CHANGE | VARIABLES THAT STAY FAMILIAR`

This is a stronger test than saying “the universe can support many books.”

## 1.8 Returning-character mortality problem

Yorke points to the contradiction of returning drama: drama needs change, but a successful recurring character must remain recognizable. Endless complete transformations exhaust the character; refusing change creates repetition.

### IVDIVO SOLUTION — MULTI-SCALE CHANGE
Distinguish:
- **episode/book change** — local belief, relationship, competence, status, responsibility;
- **phase change** — major developmental arc across several books;
- **identity invariants** — core traits/values/voice that preserve recognizability;
- **generation rotation** — when a character’s major arc is complete, story energy may pass to new protagonists rather than artificially resetting them.

# 2. STORR — THE SCIENCE OF STORYTELLING

## 2.1 Unexpected change switches attention on

Storr’s opening mechanism is clear: unexpected change interrupts the current model of the world and creates the question “What is happening?”

### IVDIVO OPENING TEST
An opening need not explode, but within its early movement there should be a **meaningful discrepancy**:

`EXPECTED STATE ≠ OBSERVED STATE`

The discrepancy should matter to a character, not just to the cosmology.

## 2.2 Curiosity grows from incomplete but partially knowable information

Storr, drawing on information-gap research, stresses that curiosity is weak when the reader knows nothing and weak when the reader thinks the answer is already certain. It rises when the reader has enough context to build a hypothesis but not enough to close it.

### IVDIVO CURIOSITY WINDOW
For each active mystery/question track:

`QUESTION | WHAT READER KNOWS | PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESES | WHAT IS MISSING | NEXT TEST | PAYOFF HORIZON`

Reject “mystery” that consists only of unexplained terminology or information deliberately withheld without a fair inferential path.

## 2.3 Specific salient detail is selected by meaning

Storr’s discussion of salience supports Burroway’s significant detail. Detail attracts attention when it suggests human change, danger, disorder, memory, intention, or consequence.

### IVDIVO SALIENCE TEST
Do not describe everything equally. Prefer the detail that:
- violates expectation;
- carries human history;
- implies an unseen cause;
- exposes status/relationship;
- gives physical evidence;
- changes the reader’s hypothesis.

## 2.4 The character is a model-defender

Storr’s most useful character mechanism: people build models of self/world and later defend them against contradictory evidence. A flaw is therefore not merely a bad trait; it can be an **operating theory of control** that once helped the character and now distorts action.

Operational chain:

`SELF/WORLD MODEL → EXPECTATION → CONTRADICTORY EVENT → DEFENSE/RATIONALIZATION → ACTION → CONSEQUENCE → MODEL UNDER GREATER PRESSURE`

### IVDIVO CHARACTER FIELD — ADD
For each core character:

`CONTROL THEORY: “If I do/believe X, the world/people will behave Y.”`

Then test it repeatedly with evidence the character cannot easily absorb.

## 2.5 Plot should attack the character’s operating model

Storr’s distinction between surface plot and subconscious character process is highly transferable. Surface events matter because they happen to **this specific person with this specific model**.

### IVDIVO PLOT-CHARACTER COUPLING TEST
For every major complication ask:
- why is this event especially difficult for this person?
- what belief/strategy does it invalidate?
- what old behavior does the character use to regain control?
- how does that behavior worsen/change the problem?

If the same plot works essentially unchanged after replacing the protagonist with a different personality, coupling is weak.

## 2.6 Dramatic question: “Who is this person?”

Storr frames drama around a repeated test of identity. The protagonist’s choices under pressure keep updating the reader’s answer to who the person really is.

This triangulates with McKee/Egri/Stanislavski/Corbett: character is exposed in action under pressure.

### IVDIVO DRAMATIC-IDENTITY TEST
At major turns record:

`PRESSURE → EXPECTED RESPONSE → ACTUAL RESPONSE → WHAT THIS REVEALS/COMPLICATES ABOUT CHARACTER`

A twist that changes external facts but reveals nothing about human behavior may have lower emotional value.

## 2.7 Want vs need

Storr uses the conscious want / deeper need contradiction as a major source of dimensionality. The useful mechanism is not forcing every character into a therapeutic template; it is creating a mismatch between the strategy the character consciously pursues and the deeper change required to live/act better.

### IVDIVO TEST
`WANT` must drive visible action.  
`NEED` must be tested by consequences.  
The climax should expose whether the hero chooses the old strategy or a changed one.

## 2.8 Social status as story fuel

Storr argues that status, belonging, reputation, humiliation, exclusion, alliance, and moral standing are powerful human motivators. This is especially relevant to YA because school/friend-group status can be existentially intense without physical danger.

### IVDIVO STATUS LEDGER
For important social scenes track:

`PUBLIC STATUS | PRIVATE STATUS | DESIRED GROUP | WHO CAN GRANT/REMOVE BELONGING | HUMILIATION RISK | FACE-SAVING TACTIC | STATUS CHANGE AFTER SCENE`

This must not replace deeper motives; it adds a social-causal layer.

## 2.9 Active protagonist = principal causer of effects

Storr explicitly warns against protagonists who only absorb events. A protagonist reacts, chooses, attempts control, and thereby causes new effects.

### IVDIVO AGENCY TEST
At least a substantial share of major turns after the initial disturbance should be traceable to protagonist decisions.

Bad chain:
`EVENT → EVENT → EVENT → HERO OBSERVES`

Better chain:
`EVENT → HERO INTERPRETS → CHOOSES → CAUSES NEW EVENT → MUST RESPOND TO CONSEQUENCE`

## 2.10 “Symphony of change” rather than rigid recipe

Storr is useful precisely because, after discussing structural schemes, he warns against letting them dictate a character-driven story. Structural models can be waymarkers; scenes must be worked through beat by beat according to character causality.

### IVDIVO SYNTHESIS
Use story models as:
- specification tests;
- missing-function detectors;
- comparison lenses;
not as an automatic beat generator.

## 2.11 Transportation / simulated consciousness

Storr synthesizes research on narrative transportation: prose can simulate a consciousness moving among perception, speech, thought, memory, and action. When readers are deeply transported, attention and self-processing shift toward the story model.

### Important evidence caution
Storr is a secondary synthesis of cognitive/neuroscientific research. Before promoting specific neuroscience claims to high-confidence project doctrine, IVDIVO should verify the cited primary studies/meta-analyses. The craft-level observation—coherent embodied POV increases immersion—can be used provisionally.

### IVDIVO TRANSPORTATION TEST
In a close-POV sequence verify a natural alternation among:
`PERCEPTION → INTERPRETATION → FEELING/BODY → MEMORY/ASSOCIATION → DECISION → ACTION`
without turning every beat into explicit introspection.

# 3. TRUBY — THE ANATOMY OF GENRES

## 3.1 Genre is not decoration; it changes the story’s action logic

Truby treats each major genre as a different strategy for exploring human life. Whatever one thinks of his philosophical claims, the operational lesson is valuable: genre creates **specific reader promises, action patterns, opposition forms, reveal patterns, and climax expectations**.

### IVDIVO GENRE PASSPORT
Before outlining each novel identify:

`PRIMARY GENRE | SECONDARY GENRES | PRIMARY HUMAN QUESTION | GENRE DESIRE | GENRE OPPOSITION | REQUIRED READER PROMISES | EXPECTED PAYOFF TYPE | ELEMENTS DELIBERATELY SUBVERTED`

No book should become “SF + YA + mystery + romance” merely as a pile of labels.

# 4. TRUBY — COMING-OF-AGE

## 4.1 Coming-of-Age = creation of the self under social pressure

Truby emphasizes the young person’s movement from a self defined by external expectations toward a self they consciously take responsibility for.

Useful operational contrast:

`SELF AS ASSIGNED BY OTHERS → TESTS / CONSEQUENCES → SELF-REVELATION → RESPONSIBILITY FOR CHOSEN SELF`

### IVDIVO YA CONSEQUENCE
The youth line cannot consist only of “young heroes doing adult missions.” A strong youth book should contain a real self-definition problem:
- family expectation;
- school/status identity;
- vocation;
- belonging;
- sexuality/romance;
- body/capability;
- moral responsibility;
- independence vs dependence;
- technology/ability that changes who one is allowed to become.

## 4.2 Inner growth should be mirrored by outer world/relationship change

Truby praises coming-of-age works where the hero’s development connects metaphorically/causally to community, institution, family, or changing place.

### IVDIVO TEST
Do not write “internal arc” and “SF plot” as separate documents. Ask what outer change is the physical/social expression of the identity problem.

Example abstraction:
`WHO AM I ALLOWED TO BECOME?` may be dramatized through access to Orbit, legal adult status, synthetic embodiment, school selection, family control, or professional authorization.

## 4.3 Freedom implies responsibility, not only escape

Truby’s useful distinction: maturation is not merely escaping others’ constraints. The character must accept consequences of self-authorship.

### IVDIVO COMING-OF-AGE CLIMAX TEST
The decisive act should answer both:
- “What do I choose?”
- “What cost/responsibility do I accept because I chose it?”

# 5. TRUBY — SCIENCE FICTION

## 5.1 SF world = land/physics + society/culture + technology

Truby’s worldbuilding triad is operationally useful:

`PHYSICAL RULES / ENVIRONMENT + SOCIAL SYSTEM / CULTURE + TECHNOLOGY`

The important point is interaction. A technology without social/economic consequences is prop design; a society that ignores its environment/technology is schematic.

### IVDIVO SF WORLD CAUSALITY MATRIX
For each major technology/embodiment system:

`TECH/PHYSICS CHANGE → WHO GAINS CAPABILITY → WHO LOSES POWER → NEW JOB/LAW/MARKET → NEW EVERYDAY HABIT → NEW INEQUALITY → NEW RELATIONSHIP PROBLEM → NEW CRIME/ABUSE → COUNTERMEASURE`

## 5.2 Fulcrum technology

Truby recommends one or a small number of technologies that strongly determine how the world operates.

### IVDIVO TEST
A book should not foreground twenty new technologies equally. Identify which technological change is causally central to the human problem of this book.

## 5.3 Avoid “clean-slate human oppressed by system” shallowness

Truby warns that SF’s huge system can dwarf the protagonist, leaving a morally simple innocent victim. His corrective is to give the hero genuine psychological/moral weakness.

### IVDIVO TEST
Systemic injustice does not exempt the hero from character causality. The hero must still make consequential mistakes, compromises, or biased interpretations.

## 5.4 Minor characters build society

Truby argues that minor characters are a major mechanism for expressing a social system. He warns against alien/species shorthand where an entire people are reduced to one trait.

### IVDIVO XENOSOCIAL TEST
Every important nonhuman/synthetic group requires internal variation:
- roles;
- status;
- subcultures;
- disagreement;
- incentives;
- family/kinship or equivalent structures;
- exceptions.

“Logical species / warrior species / spiritual species” alone fails.

## 5.5 SF desire must connect hero to the special technology/world

The genre-specific technology should not remain background décor. The protagonist’s concrete goal must force practical interaction with it.

### IVDIVO TEST
If the same main story could happen unchanged in present-day Dublin after changing a few nouns, the SF mechanism may be ornamental.

## 5.6 Subworlds must serve the main spine

Truby notes SF often moves through multiple subworlds; each subworld should contain local action that advances the overall goal.

### IVDIVO ANTI-TOURISM TEST
Every new planet/habitat/body/environment must alter:
- objective;
- obstacle;
- rule;
- relationship;
- clue;
- cost;
or it is likely worldbuilding tourism.

## 5.7 SF reveal should create a future choice

A particularly valuable Truby mechanism: the largest reveal often confronts the hero with a choice that generates a radically different future.

### IVDIVO SF REVEAL TEST
`REVEAL ABOUT WORLD/TECH → NEW POSSIBLE FUTURES → MORAL/PERSONAL CHOICE → IRREVERSIBLE ACTION`

The reveal must not end as “wow, the universe is bigger.”

## 5.8 Battle can be flipped

Truby explicitly discusses SF examples where the climax prevents battle rather than wins battle. This supports IVDIVO’s need for many noncombat climaxes.

A valid SF climax may be:
- negotiation;
- translation;
- scientific proof;
- refusal;
- rescue;
- shutdown;
- legal recognition;
- public disclosure;
- construction;
- sacrifice of access/power;
provided it decisively answers the main story.

# 6. TRUBY — DETECTIVE / THRILLER

## 6.1 Detective = reconstructing the hidden causal story

Truby’s central mechanism is valuable: the crime/opponent’s plan happens first; investigation runs in the opposite direction, moving from traces toward reconstruction.

Two chains:

`OPPONENT: PLAN → ACTION → COVER-UP → FALSE STORY / RED HERRINGS → RESIDUAL TRACES`

`DETECTIVE: TRACE → QUESTION → HYPOTHESIS → TEST → REINTERPRETATION → RECONSTRUCTED STORY`

### IVDIVO MYSTERY DESIGN RULE
Design the hidden event/opponent plan **before** designing the clue sequence. Otherwise clues risk feeling arbitrary.

## 6.2 Investigation is action, not static information collection

Truby defines Detective as a “learning story,” but the plan is still action: visiting places, observing, questioning, testing, remembering, comparing, risking.

### IVDIVO CLUE-ACTION TEST
Every major clue should open/close a concrete action path:
`CLUE → HYPOTHESIS → NEXT MOVE → RISK/COST → NEW EVIDENCE`

## 6.3 Red herrings belong to a causal false-story system

A red herring is strongest when it arises from someone’s plan, lie, limited POV, or ambiguous evidence—not because the author randomly inserts a misleading object.

### IVDIVO FAIRNESS TEST
For each false clue record:
- why it exists in-world;
- who benefits from the false interpretation;
- why a competent investigator might believe it;
- what later evidence legitimately reclassifies it.

## 6.4 Suspect web requires competing plausible stories

The hidden opponent creates complexity because several possible causal stories compete. Mystery fairness depends on the reader having enough evidence to compare them.

### IVDIVO SUSPECT STORY MATRIX
For each serious suspect:
`MOTIVE | MEANS/CAPABILITY | OPPORTUNITY/ACCESS | WHAT THEY HIDE | TRUE SECRET | EVIDENCE FOR | EVIDENCE AGAINST | WHY INVESTIGATOR MISREADS IT`

Not every suspect’s secret must be the central crime.

## 6.5 Investigation can change point of view on the past

Truby’s memory/reinterpretation mechanism is particularly useful: new knowledge can make the same past event tell a different causal story.

### IVDIVO RETROSPECTIVE REVEAL TEST
A strong reveal should cause the reader to mentally replay earlier evidence with a new interpretation while preserving factual continuity.

## 6.6 Thriller adds attack pressure to truth-seeking

Detective desire: solve mystery/find truth.  
Thriller variation: solve/find truth **while the search itself creates lethal or existential danger**.

### IVDIVO PRESSURE RULE
Danger should be causally generated by investigation:
`HERO GETS CLOSER → OPPONENT/SYSTEM REACTS → OPTIONS NARROW → COST OF KNOWING RISES`

Random attacks unrelated to investigative progress are weaker.

# 7. TRUBY — LOVE STORY

## 7.1 The lover is simultaneously desire and opposition

Truby’s strongest romance mechanism: the person the protagonist wants is also a primary obstacle to forming the relationship. This gives romance plot without inventing a cartoon external villain.

### IVDIVO RELATIONSHIP ENGINE
`ATTRACTION / NEED FOR CONNECTION + INCOMPATIBLE SELF-PROTECTION STRATEGIES → APPROACH → FRICTION → DISCLOSURE/INITIATION → WITHDRAWAL → NEW APPROACH`

## 7.2 Love must affect the rest of life

Truby argues stronger love stories connect relationship desire to life desire rather than isolating romance in a bubble.

### IVDIVO TEST
Romance must interfere with or alter:
- mission;
- status;
- family;
- work/school;
- ethics;
- future plan;
- risk;
not pause the main story.

## 7.3 Dialogue/banter as relationship action

Love stories often place unusually heavy load on verbal play. Useful principle: banter combines attraction, testing, defense, status, and opposition.

### IVDIVO SUBTEXT TEST
For flirtation/romantic dialogue record:
`SURFACE TOPIC | ATTRACTIVE SIGNAL | DEFENSIVE SIGNAL | STATUS MOVE | RISK OF DISCLOSURE | WHAT CHANGES BETWEEN THEM`

## 7.4 Relationship deepens from superficial preference toward values/vulnerability

Truby’s “initiation” mechanism moves discovery from tastes and interests toward family, dreams, wounds, values, and moral character.

### IVDIVO RELATIONSHIP DEPTH LADDER
`NOTICE → SHARED ACTIVITY → PRIVATE PREFERENCE → PERSONAL HISTORY → FUTURE DREAM → VALUE → VULNERABILITY → COSTLY TRUST`

Do not jump directly from physical attraction to “soulmate” without earned evidence.

# 8. GENRE MIXING — OPERATIONAL SYNTHESIS

Truby predicts increasingly mixed genres. IVDIVO already requires multi-genre books, but mixture must be hierarchical.

## 8.1 Primary genre controls main action

Define:

`PRIMARY GENRE = what problem must be solved for this book to be over?`

Secondary genres deepen other dimensions.

Example abstraction for an IVDIVO YA-SF mystery:
- **Primary:** Mystery — identify what happened / stop next intervention.
- **SF:** defines the mechanism and social consequences.
- **Coming-of-Age:** defines the self-responsibility change.
- **Romance:** changes trust, allegiance, vulnerability, and choice.
- **Thriller:** increases pressure as investigation becomes dangerous.

If all genres demand equal plot dominance, the book risks fragmentation.

## 8.2 Genre braid instead of genre blocks

Bad:
`school chapter → romance chapter → mystery chapter → lore chapter → action chapter`

Better:
one event simultaneously advances 2–4 genre functions.

### IVDIVO GENRE-BRAID TEST
For each major scene mark its active genre functions. Prefer causal overlap, but avoid artificially making every scene do everything.

# 9. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS — YORKE × STORR × TRUBY

## 9.1 Nested Change Engine

Yorke gives scale; Storr gives psychology; Truby gives genre-specific expression.

`DISTURBANCE`
`→ CHARACTER MODEL IS CHALLENGED`
`→ GENRE-SPECIFIC GOAL FORMS`
`→ ACTION`
`→ OPPOSITION / EVIDENCE / RELATIONSHIP PRESSURE`
`→ MODEL DEFENSE / ERROR`
`→ CONSEQUENCE`
`→ NEW INFORMATION`
`→ MIDPOINT REINTERPRETATION`
`→ MORE ACTIVE NEW STRATEGY`
`→ FINAL IDENTITY/MORAL TEST`
`→ GENRE-SPECIFIC CLIMAX`
`→ LOCAL CLOSURE`
`→ NEW STABLE/UNSTABLE STATE`

## 9.2 Three simultaneous questions for a YA-SF book

Every core book should be capable of generating three different reader questions:

1. **ACTION QUESTION:** What are they trying to accomplish and will they do it?
2. **MODEL/MYSTERY QUESTION:** What is really happening / what have they misunderstood?
3. **IDENTITY/RELATIONSHIP QUESTION:** Who will this person become, and what will it cost their relationships/status/future?

If only #2 exists, the book becomes lore/mystery exposition.  
If only #3 exists, the speculative engine may be decorative.  
If only #1 exists, the book may be kinetic but emotionally thin.

## 9.3 Reader knowledge should oscillate between hypothesis and revision

Storr’s information gaps + Truby’s Detective reconstruction + Yorke’s change structure yield:

`QUESTION → PARTIAL MODEL → EVIDENCE → CONFIDENCE → CONTRADICTION → REVISED MODEL → ACTION TEST → PAYOFF`

This becomes the default IVDIVO mystery/reveal rhythm.

## 9.4 Series engine + character development must coexist

Yorke’s returning-character contradiction + Truby genre renewal + Storr model change yield a long-series solution:

`STABLE SERIES ENGINE + ROTATING HUMAN PROBLEMS + LIMITED LOCAL CHANGE + PERIODIC DEEP ARC CHANGE + CHARACTER ROTATION`

This protects a 70-book-capacity line from both repetition and artificial reset.

# 10. NEW PRODUCTION GATES FROM BATCH 04

## GATE I — RENEWABLE ENGINE
Before approving a long series line, demonstrate at least 12 substantially different local stories generated by the same engine without repeating protagonist problem + mechanism + resolution.

## GATE J — CURIOSITY WINDOW
Every major mystery must provide enough context for at least one plausible reader hypothesis. Pure obscurity is not suspense.

## GATE K — MODEL PRESSURE
Every major complication should pressure a specific belief, strategy, relationship model, or self-concept of the protagonist.

## GATE L — AGENCY
After the initial disturbance, the protagonist must become a significant causer of plot effects through decisions.

## GATE M — GENRE HIERARCHY
One primary genre owns the book’s closure. Secondary genres must reinforce, complicate, or deepen it rather than competing for unrelated climaxes.

## GATE N — SF CAUSALITY
A foreground technology/world rule must alter actual human incentives, access, law, work, status, relationships, or risk.

## GATE O — MYSTERY BACKWARD DESIGN
The hidden causal event/opponent plan must be built before the final clue sequence is frozen.

## GATE P — COMING-OF-AGE RESPONSIBILITY
Youth transformation must culminate not merely in freedom/ability but in chosen responsibility and consequence.

## GATE Q — RELATIONSHIP CAUSALITY
Romance/friendship/family turns must alter decisions in the main story; they cannot be detachable decorative interludes.

# 11. EVIDENCE AND THEORY CAUTIONS

1. **Yorke’s fractal/symmetry claims** are powerful craft models but should not be treated as mathematical laws of all narrative.
2. **Storr’s neuroscience/cognitive claims** often summarize secondary scientific sources. Before elevating neurological specifics to high-confidence doctrine, verify primary research; craft mechanisms may remain provisional.
3. **Truby’s genre philosophy** is strongly prescriptive and interpretive. Genre beats are best used as obligation/promise generators and comparison lenses, not as universal truths.
4. Apparent agreement among Yorke/Storr/Truby is not independent empirical confirmation when authors cite/interact with overlapping traditions. Cross-source synthesis must preserve source genealogy.
