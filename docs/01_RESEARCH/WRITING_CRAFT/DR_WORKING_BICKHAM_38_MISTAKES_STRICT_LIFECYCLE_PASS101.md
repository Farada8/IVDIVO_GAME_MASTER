## Main mechanisms extracted in this pass

1. Exposition requires a present narrative owner and reason.
2. Dialogue cannot serve as a disguised research dump.
3. Conversation benefits from a character-specific verbal objective/tactic.
4. Fictional dialogue is shaped rather than transcribed.
5. Character voice should not depend on phonetic spelling or fashionable slang.
6. Dialogue needs enough POV/physical orientation to prevent abstract-space reading.
7. Orientation must not be overcorrected into microgesture clutter.
8. Attribution should preserve reader transparency rather than display thesaurus range.

## Important cross-source corrections

- Bickham's one-on-one preference is a simplification tool, not an ensemble law. Baxter/Chiarella/Fludernik and existing IVDIVO listener-attention work justify multi-person scenes when asymmetry matters.
- His anti-dialect and profanity/editor advice contains 1992-era market assumptions; retain craft concerns about distraction/stereotype, not historical market certainty.
- His numeric preference for `said` is not a production KPI.
- `Show information through action/dialogue` does not override LaPlante/Card/Rosenfeld/Scofield: summary and indirect discourse remain legitimate when real-time wording/action is not the point.
