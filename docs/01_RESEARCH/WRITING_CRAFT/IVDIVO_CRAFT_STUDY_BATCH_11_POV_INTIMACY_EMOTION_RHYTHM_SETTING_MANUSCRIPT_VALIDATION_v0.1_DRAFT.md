# 1. ROSENFELD — POV IS NOT A PRONOUN; IT IS ACCESS

## 1.1 POV controls three different things

Rosenfeld’s useful reframing is to treat POV as the reader’s level of **intimacy** with a character, not merely grammatical person.

For production, POV controls:

1. **KNOWLEDGE ACCESS** — what facts can reach the reader through this consciousness;
2. **EXPERIENTIAL ACCESS** — what the reader can feel/sense/think with the character;
3. **WORLDVIEW FILTER** — how the same event is selected, named, valued and interpreted.

Operational model:

`EVENT → CHARACTER ATTENTION → SENSORY/PERCEPTUAL FILTER → INTERPRETATION → EMOTION → THOUGHT → ACTION → READER EXPERIENCE`

This is stronger than a simple `POV = first/third` field.

## 1.2 Intimacy is a design variable

The closer the viewpoint, the more directly the reader shares inner experience. Greater distance can create objectivity, restraint, mystery or protection from overwhelming material.

### IVDIVO POV-INTIMACY CONTRACT
Before prose drafting, define:

`POV CHARACTER | PERSON/TENSE | INTIMACY LEVEL | ALLOWED KNOWLEDGE | FORBIDDEN KNOWLEDGE | INTERNAL ACCESS | SENSORY ACCESS | NARRATIVE DISTANCE | WHY THIS DISTANCE FITS THE MATERIAL`.

Do not choose close POV merely because contemporary YA often uses it. The distance must serve the human experience and information design.

## 1.3 Limited POV creates tension by limiting knowledge

Rosenfeld explicitly values first-person and third-person intimate limitations: readers know only what the viewpoint character can know, which can sharpen information control.

### IVDIVO KNOWLEDGE-FILTER RULE
A close-POV sentence must be traceable to at least one legitimate source:

`OBSERVED | REMEMBERED | INFERRED | TOLD | KNOWN BY TRAINING | FELT IN BODY | IMAGINED/SUSPECTED`.

If none applies, it is an omniscient leak.

This connects directly to CRAFT-012 Knowledge Relationship and the mystery engine.

# 2. ROSENFELD — EMOTION MUST HAVE EVIDENCE

## 2.1 Surface feeling vs subset feeling

Rosenfeld distinguishes an immediately legible **surface emotion** from additional emotions carried underneath it. The useful production idea is not the exact taxonomy; it is emotional layering.

A scene may externally read as anger while cues also carry fear, shame, grief, attraction or helplessness.

### IVDIVO EMOTIONAL-LAYER CARD

`SURFACE FEELING | SUBSET FEELING 1 | SUBSET FEELING 2 | WHAT CHARACTER ADMITS | WHAT CHARACTER MISREADS | WHAT READER CAN INFER`.

This prevents one-note emotional writing.

## 2.2 Emotion cues are evidence, not labels

Rosenfeld repeatedly routes emotion through:

- physical action or inability to act;
- sensory perception;
- dialogue;
- visual imagery;
- thought;
- interaction with setting.

### IVDIVO EMOTION-EVIDENCE RULE
Prefer:

`EMOTION → BODY / PERCEPTION / CHOICE / SPEECH / ATTENTION`

over repeated explanatory labels.

But do not turn "show, don’t tell" into dogma. A precise emotional label can be useful when it advances cognition or compresses what the scene does not need to dramatize.

## 2.3 Interior thought must remain braided with the world

Rosenfeld warns that visceral first-person or close-third becomes static when thought/memory/observation is not interwoven with physical action or setting.

### IVDIVO THOUGHT-ACTION BRAID

`EXTERNAL STIMULUS → INTERNAL OBSERVATION → PHYSICAL RESPONSE/ACTION → NEW EXTERNAL INPUT → REVISED THOUGHT/DECISION`.

Long interior passages are permitted when intentionally contemplative, but they still require a designed reason for suspending outward action.

# 3. ROSENFELD — POV CHOICE CHANGES PLOT DELIVERY

## 3.1 Third-person intimate is not omniscient

Rosenfeld treats third-person intimate as a close camera limited to one character’s eyes, mind and heart per scene/chapter. Pronouns alone do not make a narrator omniscient.

### IVDIVO CLOSE-THIRD RULE
Inside a close-third scene:

- no unexplained access to another character’s thoughts;
- no information inserted solely because the author knows it;
- other people are known through behavior, speech, prior history and inference;
- setting is selected through viewpoint relevance.

## 3.2 Changing POV is deep revision

Rosenfeld’s practical point is crucial: changing POV is not a pronoun substitution. It changes access, information, experience, voice and often plot mechanics.

### IVDIVO POV CHANGE REQUEST
Any global POV change must trigger a structural change request covering:

`KNOWLEDGE DISTRIBUTION | CLUE TIMING | SCENE OWNERSHIP | EMOTIONAL ACCESS | VOICE | EXPOSITION | SUSPENSE | REVEALS`.

No line editor may silently change viewpoint strategy.

## 3.3 Multiple POV must be earned

The value of multiple viewpoint is not “more cameras.” Each additional consciousness should contribute something the existing POV cannot provide without damaging the story.

### IVDIVO POV-ADMISSION TEST
For every recurring POV character:

`UNIQUE KNOWLEDGE | UNIQUE EMOTIONAL EXPERIENCE | UNIQUE SOCIAL POSITION | UNIQUE VOICE/FILTER | UNIQUE STORY CONSEQUENCE`.

If a POV adds only duplicate coverage, demote or remove it.

# 4. HODGINS — VOICE IS A WAY OF SEEING MADE VERBAL

## 4.1 Same event, different language

Hodgins’s POV/voice exercises ask writers to retell the same event through different people while changing vocabulary, sentence structure, speech rhythm, attitude and mood. The expected result is not merely different opinions; the wording itself changes.

### IVDIVO VOICE FINGERPRINT
For each major POV/narrator define:

`ATTENTION BIAS | VOCABULARY RANGE | REGISTER | SENTENCE COMPLEXITY | DEFAULT RHYTHM | METAPHOR DOMAIN | HUMOR LOGIC | VALUE JUDGMENTS | WHAT THEY NOTICE FIRST | WHAT THEY AVOID NAMING`.

Voice is therefore not a bag of catchphrases.

## 4.2 Narrator persona exists even in third person

Hodgins distinguishes the writer’s broad personal voice from the specific narrator persona adopted for one work. A third-person novel still chooses a stance: intimate, amused, formal, breathless, pitying, distant, etc.

### IVDIVO NARRATOR-PERSONA TEST
Ask:

`WHOSE SENSIBILITY ORGANIZES THE SENTENCE EVEN WHEN NO CHARACTER IS SPEAKING?`

If the answer changes accidentally from paragraph to paragraph, prose voice is unstable.

## 4.3 Anti-imitation consequence

Hodgins warns against falling under an admired writer’s voice. This reinforces the IVDIVO copyright/source-distance rule:

Extract mechanisms, not a source author’s lexical/syntactic signature.

# 5. HODGINS — SETTING IS PHYSICAL + SOCIAL + PERCEPTUAL

## 5.1 Setting is not scenery

Hodgins’s questions expand setting beyond visual backdrop to:

- sound, texture, smell and other senses;
- character reaction to environment;
- character action upon environment;
- social milieu;
- peer pressure;
- community standards;
- family values;
- historical period.

### IVDIVO SETTING STACK

`PHYSICAL PLACE | SENSORY STATE | SOCIAL RULES | WHO BELONGS | WHO HAS ACCESS | STATUS SIGNALS | FAMILY/PEER PRESSURE | TIME/PERIOD | CHARACTER RELATION TO PLACE | ACTION AFFORDANCES`.

This directly strengthens the youth-recognition engine: school, gym, bus, party, kitchen and orbital habitat are social systems, not painted backgrounds.

## 5.2 Setting becomes character-revealing through differential perception

The same river, classroom, office or habitat should not be described identically by different people.

Operational chain:

`PLACE → POV SELECTS DETAIL → DETAIL HAS PERSONAL MEANING → EMOTION/ASSUMPTION → ACTION`.

### IVDIVO DIFFERENTIAL-SETTING TEST
Ask what three different characters notice first in the same room and why. If answers are interchangeable, characterization and POV are underdeveloped.

# 6. LE GUIN — PROSE RHYTHM IS STRUCTURAL MOTION

## 6.1 Stress and syntactic "bars"

In *The Wave in the Mind*, Le Guin proposes two interacting prose-rhythm layers:

- syllabic stress;
- syntactic/semantic phrase groups shaped by syntax, punctuation, sense and breath, which she calls "bars".

The operational value is auditory, not metrical dogma.

### IVDIVO BAR-RHYTHM PASS
Read the prose aloud and mark natural phrase groups:

`| phrase | phrase | phrase |`

Check:
- where breath naturally falls;
- whether important words receive emphasis;
- whether syntax fights intended emotion;
- whether every sentence falls into the same beat;
- whether paragraph endings earn their emphasis.

## 6.2 Good prose rhythm changes

Le Guin argues that prose is not a metronome. Its beat should be subtle, changing and responsive to thought/action. She also connects rhythm beyond the sentence to syntax, paragraphing, events and thematic recurrence.

### IVDIVO MULTISCALE RHYTHM MAP
Track rhythm at four levels:

`SENTENCE LENGTH/CLAUSE → PARAGRAPH SHAPE → SCENE PRESSURE → CHAPTER/SEQUENCE RECURRENCE`.

This prevents “style” from being reduced to sentence length alone.

## 6.3 Short sentences gain power through contrast

*Steering the Craft* explicitly rejects the idea that short = automatically strong. Continuous short/simple syntax becomes choppy and monotonous; complex syntax can carry a reader fluidly when well built.

### IVDIVO CADENCE CONTRAST RULE
Short sentence = emphasis resource, not default house style.

Audit for repeated pattern:

`short statement → blank line → short statement → fragment → blank line`.

When repeated mechanically, consolidate, vary clause architecture, or allow the thought to develop across a fuller sentence/paragraph.

## 6.4 Narrative sentences should lead forward

Le Guin’s larger point: a beautiful sentence that stops the story merely to be admired can fail as narrative prose. Sentence beauty must participate in the whole.

### IVDIVO SENTENCE-FORWARD TEST
For a sentence/paragraph ask:

`WHAT DOES IT MAKE THE READER EXPECT / FEEL / SEE / UNDERSTAND NEXT?`

If it performs no forward, deepening, rhythmic or emotional function, its ornament is not enough.

# 7. HODGINS — PARAGRAPH AND REVISION INTEGRITY

## 7.1 Paragraph coherence is designed

Hodgins treats a paragraph as an organized unit whose sentence order may be chronological, spatial, causal, logical or idea-driven. Coherence can be created through point-of-view consistency, syntax, repeated key concepts and transition.

### IVDIVO PARAGRAPH-JOB CARD

`PARAGRAPH CENTER | ORDER PRINCIPLE | ENTRY | DEVELOPMENT | EMPHASIS/EXIT | LINK TO NEXT`.

Not every sentence needs its own paragraph.

## 7.2 Style controls reader movement

Hodgins explicitly asks whether sentences speed the reader up, slow them down, rock, stop or force jumps. Style is therefore partly **attention/tempo engineering**.

### IVDIVO TEMPO INTENTION
For important passages assign intended reading motion:

`GLIDE | PRESS | STUMBLE | PAUSE | ACCELERATE | HOLD | SNAP | RELEASE`.

Then test syntax/paragraphing aloud against that intention.

## 7.3 Revision seeks interdependent whole

Hodgins’s revision checklist converges strongly with Aristotle and the founder correction: plot events should arise from character/causality, scenes should do several jobs, and removable material that changes nothing is suspect.

### IVDIVO INTEGRITY AUDIT
For every scene/passage ask:

`PLOT MOVEMENT | CHARACTER | RELATIONSHIP | SETTING/WORLD | INFORMATION | EMOTION | MOTIF/THEME | RHYTHM/PACING`.

It need not score in all categories, but a substantial scene should usually contribute more than one. If removal changes almost nothing, merge/cut/rebuild.

# 8. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

## 8.1 Character experience is a routed system

Rosenfeld + Hodgins + Le Guin produce a more precise chain:

`WORLD EVENT`
→ `POV ACCESS LIMIT`
→ `ATTENTION SELECTION`
→ `SENSORY DETAIL`
→ `PERSONAL INTERPRETATION`
→ `SURFACE/SUBSET EMOTION`
→ `THOUGHT RHYTHM`
→ `SENTENCE RHYTHM`
→ `ACTION/CHOICE`
→ `NEW WORLD STATE`.

This means POV, character, setting, style and plot cannot be edited as fully independent departments.

## 8.2 Voice is selection + syntax + attitude, not quirks

A distinctive voice emerges from:

`WHAT IS NOTICED + WHAT IT MEANS + WORD CHOICE + SENTENCE SHAPE + RHYTHM + VALUE JUDGMENT`.

This is a stronger operational model than adding slang or signature phrases.

## 8.3 Emotional prose should not halt the external story by default

Rosenfeld’s active cues and thought/action braid plus Le Guin’s forward-moving sentence principle imply:

`FEELING` should usually transform `ATTENTION / SPEECH / BODY / CHOICE / TACTIC`, not exist as an isolated author explanation.

## 8.4 Setting is one of the engines of youth recognition

Hodgins’s social setting clarifies why ordinary-life scenes matter. A school is also peer hierarchy, schedule, reputation, teacher authority, transport, money, parental expectations and access. The environment itself generates pressure and choices.

# 9. NEW PRODUCTION GATES — CJ–CW

## GATE CJ — POV INTIMACY CONTRACT
POV specifies desired reader distance and experiential access, not only person/tense.

## GATE CK — KNOWLEDGE FILTER
Every close-POV fact must be observed, remembered, inferred, told, trained, felt or explicitly imagined/suspected.

## GATE CL — ATTENTION FILTER
Description must reflect what this character notices and why.

## GATE CM — EMOTION EVIDENCE
Important emotion should have observable/sensory/verbal/cognitive evidence, not only a label.

## GATE CN — EMOTIONAL LAYERING
Major emotional scenes distinguish surface feeling from additional underlying/contradictory feelings.

## GATE CO — THOUGHT/ACTION BRAID
Interior reflection is interwoven with physical action, setting or active perception unless deliberate stillness is the scene’s function.

## GATE CP — POV ADMISSION
Each additional POV must provide unique knowledge, experience, social position, voice or causal function.

## GATE CQ — VOICE FINGERPRINT
Major POVs differ through attention, vocabulary, syntax, rhythm, metaphor field and value judgments—not catchphrases alone.

## GATE CR — SETTING RELATION
Setting reveals character through what is noticed, desired, feared, used, avoided and changed.

## GATE CS — SOCIAL SETTING
Ordinary locations include peer/family/institutional rules, status and access pressures, not only architecture.

## GATE CT — BAR RHYTHM
Important prose is audited aloud for stress, breath, syntactic grouping and unintended repeated beat.

## GATE CU — CADENCE CONTRAST
Short/simple syntax is used through contrast; mechanical short-paragraph repetition is flagged.

## GATE CV — PARAGRAPH COHERENCE
Paragraph boundaries follow a thought/action/perception unit and an identifiable order principle rather than arbitrary dramatic chopping.

## GATE CW — WHOLE-INTEGRITY REVISION
Scenes/passages are assessed for causal necessity and multifunction contribution before line polish.

# 10. MANUSCRIPT VALIDATION — LESSON ZERO BOOK 1 v0.5

**Scope:** narrow diagnostic sample only. This is not a whole-book verdict.

## 10.1 Cadence finding

A Book 1 sample around Ethan at the school showcase contains a recurrent emphatic pattern of very short standalone paragraphs, including a sequence structurally equivalent to:

`short negation → short negation → single-word/short judgment`.

Later paragraphs again isolate many short units. Locally this can create pressure, shame and clipped self-correction. Across a long manuscript, however, repeated use risks the exact monotony Le Guin warns about: the emphasis loses contrast because nearly everything receives emphasis.

### Diagnosis
The problem is **not** “short sentences are bad.”

The problem is:

`EMPHASIS TECHNIQUE USED AS DEFAULT RHYTHM → CONTRAST COLLAPSES → PROSE SOUNDS MANUFACTURED`.

### Required Book 1 v0.6 rhythm audit
For representative pages from every chapter measure qualitatively:

`SHORT / MEDIUM / LONG SENTENCE MIX | CLAUSE COMPLEXITY | PARAGRAPH LENGTH MIX | FRAGMENT RATE | REPEATED RHETORICAL TEMPLATE | READ-ALOUD BEAT | WHERE SHORT LINE IS EARNED`.

Do not flatten all prose into long paragraphs. Preserve genuinely sharp turns; restore contrast around them.

## 10.2 POV-strength finding

Another Book 1 sample around Maya/Aoife/Taal shows a useful close-POV mechanism: visual translator outputs are not treated as neutral exposition; Aoife’s prior mistake changes what she notices, she compares current signals with memory, notices missing environmental carrier information, and then changes the group’s interpretation/action.

This aligns well with the new chain:

`PRIOR EXPERIENCE → ATTENTION BIAS → OBSERVABLE CUE → INTERPRETATION → EMOTION → DECISION`.

### Preserve
During future line editing, do not “streamline” away the causal perceptual chain merely because it takes space. It is doing character, worldbuilding, mystery and decision simultaneously.

## 10.3 Revision consequence

Book 1 should therefore be edited in this order:

1. ONE MAIN STORY / causal spine audit;
2. scene necessity and pressure-transfer;
3. POV knowledge/intimacy consistency;
4. character attention/emotion evidence;
5. paragraph/rhythm architecture;
6. line-level word choice.

A prose pass before the structural passes risks polishing passages that later need removal or reconstruction.

These are routed passes. They are not all to be consciously held in mind while generating every first-draft sentence.

# 12. LIMITS / FAILURE MODES

1. Rosenfeld’s surface/subset model is a useful drafting heuristic, not a scientific taxonomy of emotion.
2. Close POV is not automatically superior to omniscient or distant narration.
3. Sensory detail can become clutter if every sense is mechanically represented.
4. Distinctive voice can be overdesigned into caricature.
5. Le Guin’s rhythm analysis is explicitly exploratory; `bars` are an auditory tool, not a fixed metrical system.
6. Long sentences are not inherently literary; they must remain intelligible and functional.
7. Social setting should emerge through scene pressure and behavior, not encyclopedic sociology paragraphs.
8. The Book 1 cadence observation is sample-based; whole-manuscript measurement/audit remains required before claiming global frequency.
