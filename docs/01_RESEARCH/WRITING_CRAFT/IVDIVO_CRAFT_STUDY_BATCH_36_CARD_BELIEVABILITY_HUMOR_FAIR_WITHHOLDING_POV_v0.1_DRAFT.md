# IVDIVO CRAFT STUDY — BATCH 36
## CARD: BELIEVABILITY · COMIC DISTANCE · FAIR WITHHOLDING · POV ARCHITECTURE

Date: 2026-08-16
Status: WORKING / SOURCE-GROUNDED / STRICT FULL READ STILL OPEN
Source: Orson Scott Card — *Characters & Viewpoint*

## 1. Purpose
This batch captures new mechanisms from the Pass-18 continuation and converts them into production gates without falsely promoting the source to FULL READ COMPLETE.

## 2. Core synthesis
A believable fictional character is not made by copying reality or by piling up detail. Believability emerges when:

`ACTION + MOTIVE + ATTITUDE + IMPLIED PAST + SOCIAL NETWORK + PROPORTIONATE JUSTIFICATION → PLAUSIBLE HUMAN CAUSALITY`.

A viewpoint then decides how much of that causal system the reader can access, when, and with what interpretive bias.

## 3. New production gates

### B36-G01 — REALITY IS NOT A DEFENSE
If a behavior/event feels false, `this happened in real life` is not sufficient. Add causal preparation or change the event.

### B36-G02 — ORDINARY-WORLD VALUE
Before inventing another spectacular technology/worldbuilding beat, test whether ordinary life in the current setting can produce the scene more originally.

### B36-G03 — IDEA-SERVANT REJECTION
A named person who only explains a philosophy, school or technology fails character gate. They need an independent want, relationship pressure and action consequence.

### B36-G04 — MOTIVE REVISION TEST
A new motive reveal is valuable when it changes what prior actions mean. Biography that does not alter interpretation is lower-value exposition.

### B36-G05 — ATTITUDE-BEARING DESCRIPTION
In close POV, setting/object description should normally reveal the viewpoint character's attitude, concern, expertise or blind spot as well as the world.

### B36-G06 — IMPLIED-PAST PREFERENCE
Prefer habit, expectation, social recognition, reflex and shared-history implication over explanatory flashback when the past is not itself the dramatic event.

### B36-G07 — OFFSTAGE-LIFE NETWORK
Recurring major/minor characters must show at least some evidence of obligations or relationships outside the protagonist's immediate need.

### B36-G08 — PROPORTIONAL JUSTIFICATION
The more unusual and climax-critical a behavior is, the more setup it requires. Do not spend pages justifying trivialities while leaving climax choices unprepared.

### B36-G09 — CONTROLLED COMIC DISTANCE
Humor may create distance from discomfort, but it must not cancel beats whose reader effect depends on injury, consent, betrayal, grief or moral cost remaining real.

### B36-G10 — PAIN SATURATION
Before escalating pain/danger, ask whether previous suffering has already saturated the reader. Increase choice, meaning or consequence before gore/intensity.

### B36-G11 — CHOSEN-COST CHARACTER TEST
When possible, reveal character through what cost they voluntarily accept for another value rather than through random damage alone.

### B36-G12 — FIRST-PERSON PURPOSE
Before approving first person: `why tell this? to whom? why now? what outcome does the telling seek?`

### B36-G13 — FIRST-PERSON INSIDE-OUT
A first-person scene must contain character-specific perception/attitude/motive. Camera-style self-description without meaning is a form failure.

### B36-G14 — FAIR WITHHOLDING
Do not hide information the viewpoint character already possesses merely to manufacture suspense unless the narrating situation explicitly earns that concealment.

### B36-G15 — UNRELIABILITY LEGIBILITY
If narrator/POV interpretation is unreliable, establish motive/pattern/corroboration sufficient for readers to recognize uncertainty as designed rather than author cheating.

### B36-G16 — LIMITED-THIRD SWITCH COST
Switch limited POV only at a clear structural break and only when the new viewpoint changes reader knowledge, irony, stakes or emotional access materially.

### B36-G17 — SWITCH RE-ORIENTATION
Immediately after a POV switch, establish new viewpoint owner and enough time/place context to prevent reader re-parsing.

### B36-G18 — POV HEAT
Deep/light/cinematic distance is a scene-function control, not a permanent house style. Modulate consciously.

### B36-G19 — NO DIALOGUE-AS-WORLD-BIBLE
When dialogue is carrying history, relationship, attitude and setting facts that POV/action could carry more naturally, rebuild the scene rather than polishing exposition lines.

### B36-G20 — TRANSFORMATION SCALE
The greater the character change, the greater the causal evidence owed. If cause is intentionally hidden, the story must mark the inconsistency as a question.

## 4. IVDIVO-specific consequences

### Orbital Youth
Post-money daily life is not a void between plot events; it is a source of specificity. The writers' room should mine ordinary behaviors that only exist because money, conventional retail scarcity and routine manufacturing no longer organize life.

Strong everyday material may include:
- how adolescents signal status when consumer price loses force;
- how access to a team/project is socially granted or refused;
- etiquette between human adolescents and conscious robotic persons;
- privacy expectations in highly instrumented habitats;
- family expectations around voluntary contribution;
- embarrassment about competence, reputation, lineage or social standing rather than material wealth;
- ordinary attitudes toward specialist consciousness schools that most people do not practice.

These are story ingredients only when tied to the main causal problem.

### Conscious robotic civilization
A robot person must not function as a human exposition terminal. Require:
`OWN WANT + OWN NETWORK + OWN ATTITUDE + RIGHT TO REFUSE + CONSEQUENCE OF CHOICE`.

### Specialist consciousness schools
Avoid an instructor who exists only to state metaphysical lore. Show lineage, daily practice, disagreements, limits of reproducibility, student/instructor motives and what the practice costs in time/attention/social life.

## 5. Failure modes for LESSON ZERO / future books
- opening a chapter with neutral infrastructure inventory instead of character-filtered observation;
- switching POV to give the reader convenient information rather than because another mind owns the scene;
- making every teenage character equally witty;
- immediately joking after a violation/danger beat that needs residue;
- using a sympathetic adult/robot only as obstacle or lecture device;
- repeated physical danger as substitute for escalation of responsibility;
- hidden clue known by POV character but not told to reader until convenient reveal;
- backstory inserted because it is interesting rather than because current action needs it.
