## Central correction

Collins offers a useful idea — prose rhythm can influence felt scene rhythm — but overstates the mapping from action/suspense to short/choppy syntax.

IVDIVO production law:

`READER EFFECT -> DURATION / CONTINUITY -> PARAGRAPH INTENT -> SENTENCE SHAPE / LENGTH -> CONTRAST`

Not:

`ACTION / SUSPENSE -> SHORT SENTENCES`.

## Production gates

### B153-G01 — LIVE CHARACTER / TECHNIQUE WATCHER

Collins' actor analogy usefully separates:

- inhabiting the character and scene;
- maintaining enough technical awareness to make the material legible.

But do not activate the entire craft system while generating every line.

`GENERATE LIVE MATERIAL -> DIAGNOSE ACTUAL DEFECT -> APPLY RELEVANT TECHNIQUE -> RETEST`.

### B153-G02 — RESTRAINT = FUNCTIONAL SELECTION

Restraint is not a raw word-count target.

`REMOVE NONFUNCTIONAL / REDUNDANT MATERIAL -> PRESERVE LOAD-BEARING DETAIL -> MAINTAIN OR IMPROVE READER MODEL`.

### B153-G03 — SENTENCE RHYTHM BY EFFECT, NOT GENRE LABEL

Do not infer syntax from labels such as `action`, `thriller`, `suspense`, `romance` or `quiet scene`.

Determine:

- what the reader must notice;
- whether subjective time should expand or contract;
- whether continuity or interruption is the desired effect;
- where contrast should land.

Then choose sentence structure.

### B153-G04 — PERIODS ARE NOT FREE SPEED

A run of short sentences may accelerate perceived action, but repeated full stops can also create stop-start reading and therefore slow or flatten flow.

Judge the sequence in context, not by average sentence length.

### B153-G05 — COMPRESSION ≠ CHOPPING

Provisional translation from confirmed later Collins material:

`REMOVE REPEATED MEANING / PROCESS -> MERGE MICROBEATS -> SUMMARY OR INDIRECT WHEN APPROPRIATE -> KEEP SPECIFIC LOAD-BEARING LANGUAGE`.

Do not turn one coherent paragraph into six fragments and call that compression.

### B153-G06 — SPECIFICITY MUST BE POV-LEGIBLE

A more specific term is not automatically better if the POV character would not naturally perceive/name it or the reader cannot recover its meaning.

### B153-G07 — VIVID VERB EVIDENCE FIREWALL

Vivid verbs often contain interpretation.

In mystery / forensic prose:

`RAW TRACE -> NEUTRAL OBSERVATION -> POV INTERPRETIVE WORDING -> CORROBORATED CLAIM`.

Do not let lexical force silently raise evidence status.

### B153-G08 — LEXICAL HIERARCHY

Not every word should be vivid. If every noun, verb and modifier demands attention, nothing has emphasis.

Use lexical intensity selectively around load-bearing perception/action.

### B153-G09 — TECHNICAL SCENE COMPRESSION

For technical process:

`SUMMARY SETUP -> CONSEQUENTIAL TEST / CONSENT / EVIDENCE MOMENT IN REAL TIME -> SUMMARY / CHANGED STATE`.

### B153-G10 — INSTITUTIONAL LANGUAGE MUST NAME THE MECHANISM

In post-money settings, concise words such as `allocation`, `priority`, `access`, `contribution`, `qualification` are acceptable only when they refer to a real causal mechanism rather than disguising money or purchasing power.

## Cross-source comparison

### Collins × Le Guin

Le Guin explicitly rejects an optimum sentence length and warns that sustained short/simple syntax becomes monotonous and choppy. Her rule of functional variety has priority over Collins' broader suspense-shortening recommendation.

### Collins × Roy Peter Clark

Clark demonstrates that periods may behave like stop signs; short sentences do not automatically speed reading. Both short and long structures can carry violence and urgency depending on sequence and contrast.

### Collins × Williams/Bizup

Grammatical complexity and perceived difficulty are not the same thing. A longer sentence can be clearer than a shorter abstract one.

### Collins × Zinsser

Concision means removing clutter, not flattening cadence. Clarity and continuity remain reader-facing goals.

### Collins × LaPlante / Lamott

Technique-watching belongs primarily to diagnosis/revision; discovery must retain enough freedom to produce living material.

## Failure modes

1. **ACTION-SHORT-SENTENCE DOGMA** — action label determines syntax before reader effect is known.
2. **CADENCE-MIMESIS FALLACY** — prose rhythm mechanically imitates heartbeat or physical movement.
3. **COMPRESSION-AS-CHOPPING** — shorter sentences mistaken for tighter prose.
4. **VIVIDNESS SATURATION** — every word intensified until emphasis collapses.
5. **THESAURUS INTENSIFICATION** — generic verb replaced by a showy but inaccurate verb.
6. **SEMANTIC OVERCOMPRESSION** — brevity deletes necessary causal/evidence/relationship information.
7. **EVIDENCE-LOADED VERB** — interpretation smuggled into supposedly objective trace description.
8. **CRAFT-WATCHER FREEZE** — technical monitoring destroys discovery flow.
9. **TECHNICAL DOCUMENTATION PROSE** — precise terminology without POV or story function.
10. **SHORT-SENTENCE KPI** — cadence judged by percentage rather than function.

## IVDIVO application

### LESSON ZERO

No global rewrite. Apply only if a current passage shows actual choppiness, redundant wording, evidence-loaded diction or broken reader-state continuity.

### Orbital Youth

Use sentence rhythm to express character-specific cognition and situational effect, not to give every danger scene the same clipped register.

### ROOM 917

Forensic passages must preserve observation vs interpretation. Compression should remove repeated recorder/setup procedure while preserving exact clue language and evidence boundaries.

### B07 / personhood

Do not use loaded descriptors (`erratic`, `obedient`, `aggressive`, `helpful`) as evidence of a nonhuman person's motive. Separate observed behaviour from human interpretation.
