# 1. LAPLANTE — THE MAKING OF A STORY

## 1.1 Structure observed

The book is deliberately hybrid: process + craft + exercises + close reading of published works. Relevant fiction-craft areas identified in this pass include:

- writing that is surprising yet convincing;
- avoiding a generic “writerly” voice;
- discovery / not knowing too early;
- concrete detail and imagery;
- metaphor/symbol caution;
- point of view and psychic distance;
- transference of emotion onto sensory events;
- developmental revision and “hot spots.”

### IVDIVO USE
Do not reduce craft study to rules. Pair every abstract mechanism with controlled textual examples and manuscript tests.

---

## 1.2 Surprising yet convincing

LaPlante places value on writing that can surprise the reader while remaining credible inside the work. The useful synthesis for IVDIVO is that novelty without causal or psychological support reads arbitrary, while pure predictability loses attention.

### IVDIVO GATE AK — SURPRISE / CREDIBILITY BALANCE
For every major surprise ask:

`WHAT DID THE READER EXPECT? → WHAT CHANGED? → WHAT PRIOR MATERIAL MAKES THE NEW RESULT BELIEVABLE IN RETROSPECT?`

A reveal/turn is weak if it is only unpredictable. It must also become intelligible after the fact.

---

## 1.3 Concrete detail is not inventory

LaPlante’s detail chapter treats concrete details as basic building blocks and emphasizes “thinking small,” multi-sensory observation, and imagery that can work at more than one level. The practical lesson is not “add more description.” It is to choose details that carry perceptual and narrative weight.

### IVDIVO DETAIL CARD
`DETAIL | SENSE CHANNEL | POV RELEVANCE | STORY FUNCTION | CHARACTER FUNCTION | EMOTIONAL/ASSOCIATIVE LOAD | KEEP/CUT`

### GATE AL — DETAIL EARNS SPACE
A concrete detail should normally do at least one strong job and preferably more than one:
- orient;
- characterize;
- alter mood;
- foreshadow;
- embody relationship history;
- carry world information;
- create physical causality;
- become later payoff.

A list of visually specific but dramatically inert objects is still inert prose.

---

## 1.4 Imagery and symbol should emerge, not announce themselves

LaPlante’s chapter architecture explicitly warns against consciously forcing symbols and against abusing metaphor. The transfer principle is that symbolic meaning is strongest when it emerges from repeated lived interaction, image, object, setting or action rather than an authorial declaration that something “represents” an idea.

### GATE AM — SYMBOL EMERGENCE
For a recurring object/image:

`ORDINARY FUNCTION → CHARACTER CONTACT → RECURRING CONTEXT → CHANGED ASSOCIATION → CLIMACTIC/PAYOFF USE`

If the symbol disappears without consequence, it is decoration. If the narration repeatedly explains its meaning, it is probably over-signaled.

---

## 1.5 Psychic distance must be controlled

LaPlante discusses third-person intimacy as capable of moving closer to and farther from a character while warning that abrupt shifts can jolt the reader out of the narrative experience. Her discussion explicitly draws on Gardner’s psychic-distance scale.

### IVDIVO POV DISTANCE CARD
For each scene or passage:

`BASE POV | TARGET DISTANCE | REASON TO MOVE CLOSER/FARTHER | TRANSITION CUE | KNOWLEDGE LIMIT | EMOTIONAL EFFECT`

### GATE AN — CONTROLLED DISTANCE
Distance may change, but the change must have a reason:
- closer for bodily sensation, fear, private judgment, shame, desire;
- farther for spatial orientation, institutional view, temporal compression, or wider social context.

Unmotivated oscillation produces unstable voice.

---

## 1.6 Revision is discovery, not cosmetic cleanup

LaPlante’s revision chapter distinguishes developmental work from mere sentence polishing and includes “hot spots,” constraints and exercise-based deep revision. The practical point: an early draft contains both dead material and unexpectedly alive material; revision must identify where the work is generating energy and where it is merely explaining itself.

### IVDIVO HOT-SPOT PASS
Mark passages that show unusually strong:
- conflict;
- image;
- voice;
- relationship charge;
- sensory presence;
- moral pressure;
- mystery;
- unexpected character behavior.

Then ask whether the surrounding architecture supports these hot spots or buries them.

### GATE AO — DEVELOPMENT BEFORE LINE POLISH
Do not preserve a weak scene because sentences were already polished. Developmental revision may require moving, combining, deleting or rebuilding whole units.

# 2. RIBÓ — PROSE FICTION

## 2.1 Semiotic model separates story and discourse

Ribó’s introductory model distinguishes:

- **story** — events, environments, characters;
- **discourse** — how a narrator presents that story to a narratee/reader.

This is operationally important for IVDIVO because a weak manuscript can have an adequate underlying event chain but poor disclosure/order/POV, or vice versa.

### IVDIVO TWO-LAYER DIAGNOSIS
For a problem ask first:

`IS THE DEFECT IN STORY OR IN DISCOURSE?`

**Story defects:** causality, missing motive, weak objective, absent opposition, impossible chronology.  
**Discourse defects:** wrong reveal order, over-explanation, poor focalization, misplaced flashback, bad compression, weak emphasis.

### GATE AP — DO NOT REWRITE THE WRONG LAYER
Do not alter canon/events to fix a presentation problem. Do not line-edit presentation when the event chain itself is broken.

---

## 2.2 Plot is emplotment, not merely chronology

Ribó explicitly contrasts temporal sequence with plot: the narrator can stress causal relations, rearrange order, alter duration and create a different reader experience from the same story events.

### IVDIVO EMPLOTMENT MAP
For major events maintain two views:

**FABULA / STORY ORDER:** what actually happened.  
**DISCOURSE ORDER:** when/how the reader learns it.

For mysteries add:

`EVENT TIME | EVIDENCE APPEARANCE | CHARACTER INTERPRETATION | READER INFERENCE | TRUE EXPLANATION | REVEAL TIME`.

This prevents clue chronology and revelation chronology from being confused.

---

## 2.3 Setting, character, narration, language and theme are separate but interacting systems

Ribó’s structure is useful because it prevents “story” from becoming one undifferentiated concept. The book separately treats plot, setting, characterization, narration/focalization, language and theme.

### IVDIVO LAYER AUDIT
A scene can succeed in one layer and fail in another. Audit separately:

`PLOT | SETTING | CHARACTER | NARRATION/POV | LANGUAGE | THEME`

Do not call a scene good merely because one layer is strong.

---

## 2.4 Setting contributes verisimilitude and atmosphere

Ribó treats setting as the narrative world rather than background wallpaper, including topography, atmosphere, description and verisimilitude.

### GATE AQ — SETTING PARTICIPATION
A key setting should affect at least one of:
- possible action;
- access;
- risk;
- timing;
- social behavior;
- sensory experience;
- cultural expectation;
- reader belief in the storyworld.

A futuristic location that could be replaced by an empty room without changing decisions is underused.

---

## 2.5 Focalization is not identical to narrator

Ribó’s narration chapter separates narrator/narratee from focalization. This reinforces the existing POV-room rule: **who tells** and **who perceives** are related but not identical questions.

### IVDIVO FOCALIZATION CHECK
For every scene:

`WHO SPEAKS/NARRATES? | WHO PERCEIVES? | WHO KNOWS? | WHO INTERPRETS? | WHO IS DENIED ACCESS?`

This is especially important for institutional and mystery scenes where a narrator can state facts that the focal character could not know.

---

## 2.6 Language is part of narrative design

Ribó treats style, foregrounding, figures of speech and symbolism as narrative functions rather than decorative afterthoughts.

### GATE AR — FOREGROUNDING HAS A COST
Any conspicuously unusual word, syntax pattern, metaphor or repeated image attracts attention. Ask whether that attention is intentionally spent.

If prose becomes conspicuous at a moment when the reader should be following danger, clue logic or dialogue subtext, style may be competing with story function.

# 3. HODGINS — A PASSION FOR NARRATIVE

## 3.1 Structure observed

Hodgins organizes fiction craft around:

1. finding stories;
2. sentence craft;
3. setting;
4. character;
5. plot as a causal chain;
6. structure as architecture;
7. POV and voice;
8. metaphors/symbols/allusions;
9. revision.

This architecture itself supports IVDIVO’s principle that prose quality cannot compensate for missing causality, while also refusing to reduce fiction to plot alone.

---

## 3.2 Plot as causal chain — but conflict is not the only possible organizing metaphor

Hodgins’ plot chapter is explicitly titled “A Causal Chain,” supporting the causality emphasis already found in Aristotle/Burroway/Truby/McKee. At the same time, the book discusses alternatives to treating every story as war between enemies: stories may contain desired outcomes, struggle and consequence without a single villain.

### IVDIVO SYNTHESIS
Preserve the Founder’s ONE MAIN STORY rule while separating it from “must have a villain.”

A main story needs:
`DESIRED CHANGE → PRESSURE/OBSTACLE → CHARACTER RESPONSE → CONSEQUENCE → CHANGED CONDITIONS → OUTCOME`

Opposition can be produced by:
- person;
- institution;
- environment;
- incompatible duties;
- scarcity;
- social structure;
- misunderstanding;
- body limitation;
- time;
- character contradiction.

### GATE AS — OPPOSITION WITHOUT CARTOON VILLAIN
If a story has no villain, identify exactly what prevents the desired state and how that pressure changes action. “Quiet/reflective” is not permission for causal vagueness.

---

## 3.3 Structure is architecture, not formula

Hodgins distinguishes plot from structure: causal material still needs arrangement into a shaped whole. This complements Ribó’s story/discourse distinction and IVDIVO’s specification-first method.

### IVDIVO STRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE MAP
For each novel maintain:

`MAIN ACTION SPINE | MAJOR SEQUENCES | RELATIONSHIP TURNS | REVEAL TURNS | ORDINARY-LIFE COLLISIONS | MIDPOINT RECLASSIFICATION | CRISIS | CLIMAX | AFTERMATH`

The map is a design object, not a rigid beat template.

---

## 3.4 Metaphors and symbols require integration

Hodgins’ “Making Connections” chapter discusses metaphor, symbolism and allusion as networks that can emerge through revision. In the material examined, he transmits a useful Burroway warning: symbolic material fails if it is insufficiently integrated, inadequate to the emotion, or pushed too heavily.

### IVDIVO SYMBOL INTEGRATION TEST
A symbolic object must pass:

`EXISTS NATURALLY IN CHARACTER/WORLD? | HAS ORDINARY USE? | EARNS EMOTIONAL ASSOCIATION? | RETURNS UNDER CHANGED CONDITIONS? | CHANGES/PAYS OFF? | NARRATOR OVER-EXPLAINS?`

This converges with LaPlante’s anti-forced-symbol approach.

---

## 3.5 “Secret life” of draft material

Hodgins describes draft elements that initially seem accidental but may reveal relationships through later revision. Useful implication: early drafting can generate motifs before the writer fully understands them, but later revision must decide whether they earn their place.

### IVDIVO MOTIF AUDIT
For recurring images/objects/actions found accidentally:

`REPETITION FOUND → POSSIBLE CONNECTION → CHARACTER/THEME LINK → CAUSAL OR EMOTIONAL PAYOFF → KEEP/DEVELOP/REMOVE`

Do not retroactively force every accidental repetition into symbolism.

# 4. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

## 4.1 Story layer vs discourse layer

Ribó + LaPlante + Hodgins:

`WHAT HAPPENS` and `HOW THE READER EXPERIENCES WHAT HAPPENS` are separate design problems.

This becomes a standing diagnostic rule for IVDIVO.

---

## 4.2 Specific detail should carry perspective

LaPlante + Ribó setting/focalization + Hodgins setting/voice:

A detail is strongest when it is simultaneously:

`PHYSICALLY TRUE TO WORLD + SELECTED BY THIS POV + RELEVANT TO CURRENT PRESSURE`.

This prevents neutral catalog description.

---

## 4.3 Symbol should be discovered in action, then disciplined in revision

LaPlante + Hodgins converge:

`ORDINARY OBJECT/IMAGE → REPEATED CHARACTER CONTACT → ASSOCIATION → CHANGED CONTEXT → PAYOFF`.

Meaning grows through use. Explanation is secondary and often unnecessary.

---

## 4.4 POV is a controlled information-and-experience system

LaPlante psychic distance + Ribó focalization:

`NARRATIVE ACCESS = WHO PERCEIVES + HOW CLOSE + WHAT THEY KNOW + WHAT THEY MISREAD + WHAT REMAINS OUTSIDE`.

This should be designed per scene, especially for mystery and youth ensemble work.

---

## 4.5 Deep revision must preserve discovered strengths while repairing architecture

LaPlante hot spots + Hodgins revision/connection-making:

Revision is not simply cutting or polishing. It must identify the manuscript’s strongest emergent material and rebuild weak architecture around what deserves to survive.

### IVDIVO DEEP-REVISION SEQUENCE
`MAIN STORY VALIDATION → HOT-SPOT MAP → DEAD-ZONE MAP → STORY/DISCOURSE DIAGNOSIS → REBUILD → POV/DISTANCE → DETAIL/MOTIF → PROSE/RHYTHM → LINE EDIT`.

# 7. LIMITS / FAILURE MODES

1. LaPlante is deliberately anti-dogmatic; turning her discussion into rigid numerical rules would contradict the source’s method.
2. Ribó is introductory narratology, not a complete empirical theory of reader psychology; use it for conceptual distinctions and diagnostics.
3. Hodgins includes and discusses alternative schools; quoted positions from Burroway/others must not be misattributed as original Hodgins doctrine.
4. Symbol/motif work is especially vulnerable to over-interpretation after the fact. The existence of repetition alone does not prove thematic necessity.
5. Moving psychic distance is not inherently an error; abrupt or unmotivated movement is the defect.
6. Non-villain story structures still require pressure, causal change and a concrete outcome if they are to satisfy the IVDIVO story-first production contract.
