# IVDIVO CRAFT STUDY — BATCH 47
## McKee: Act/Scene Design · Information Control · Antagonism · Exposition · Text · Method

Date: 2026-08-17
Status: WORKING / OPERATIONALIZED
Source: Robert McKee, *Story*, Chapters 9–19.

## PURPOSE
Extract the late-book mechanisms that materially improve IVDIVO production while separating film-specific prescriptions from transferable story craft.

## 1. MACRO PROGRESSION
Use major turns relative to smaller turns. Do not make every chapter an "act climax."
Gate: `major change must alter the book-scale strategy/state, not merely raise volume`.

## 2. SUBPLOTS
A subplot must return value to ONE MAIN STORY through causality, relationship pressure, theme-counterpoint, evidence, delay or final choice.
Failure mode: five interesting parallel lives that never alter the central engine.

## 3. SCENE DESIGN
Diagnostic:
`owner → immediate want → tactic → reaction → tactic shift → turning/change`.
Changed state may be action, relation, information, access, commitment, consent, status or safety.

## 4. SETUP / PAYOFF
A payoff should do more than reward recognition:
`setup → delayed relevance → payoff → model/action changes`.
Mystery must remain retrospectively fair.

## 5. SCENE ANALYSIS / SUBTEXT
Separate text from what speakers are doing through text.
Use tactical labels in revision only. If all five characters merely state positions, rebuild the scene around incompatible immediate objectives.

## 6. RHYTHM / COMPOSITION
Climax/suspense may slow down.
`acceleration before decisive moment → earned pause → precise perception/choice → consequence`.
This directly rejects "suspense = short sentences."

## 7. QUALITATIVE ANTAGONISM
Escalation by kind:
`simple obstacle → dependency → value conflict → system-level contradiction`.
No darkness inflation required.

## 8. EXPOSITION
`information → who needs it → why now → what it changes`.
If both speakers know it, do not make them explain it for the reader.

## 9. INFORMATION MODES
Track mystery/suspense/dramatic irony explicitly:
- reader knows less;
- reader and protagonist know roughly the same;
- reader knows more.
False mystery is prohibited.

## 10. COINCIDENCE
Chance may create/complicate. It may not resolve the local climax.

## 11. CHARACTER
Pressure exposes decision models, but:
- no mandatory hidden opposite self;
- no protagonist monopoly on dimensionality;
- supporting characters and conscious robots remain persons.

## 12. DIALOGUE
Transferable rule: dialogue is action/reaction with subtext.
Non-transferable screen rule: all lines must be short/simple.
Novel/audio dialogue may be long when reactions/tactics/state evolve.

## 13. DESCRIPTION / IMAGE SYSTEM
Description selects expressive consequential details.
Motifs should repeat with variation and changed meaning. If the reader can point and say "symbol" on cue, it is probably overdesigned.

## 14. WRITER'S METHOD
Strong transferable principle:
`prove story architecture cheaply before investing in expensive prose`.
Step-outline/treatment are optional implementations.
When a scene fails causally, repair setup/payoff rather than polishing its dialogue.

## B47 FAILURE MODES
- Act-climax inflation
- Subplot parallelism
- Beat-label prose
- Emotional polarity bookkeeping
- False mystery
- Exposition ping-pong
- Coincidence rescue
- Dilemma fetish
- Climax loudness bias
- Supporting-character servitude
- Screen-brevity contamination of prose
- Symbol labeling
- Favorite-scene hostage
- Dialogue-first structural blindness
- Reader-won't-notice hole patch
