## CORE SYNTHESIS

The useful middle principle is not `make things worse every chapter`.

It is:

`ENGAGEMENT → RESISTANCE → RESPONSE → STRATEGY/MODEL CHANGE → CONSEQUENCE → RENEWED ENGAGEMENT`

A middle stays alive when the kind of pressure, the character's strategy, the state of relationships/evidence, and the available choices continue to change.

## B84 PRODUCTION GATES

### B84-G01 — Engagement Adhesive
Every major character/agent remaining in the main engine needs a causal reason to remain engaged.

### B84-G02 — No Artificial Trap
Do not add death, debt, kidnapping, poverty, secret villainy, career destruction or confinement merely to prevent a character from walking away.

### B84-G03 — Meaningful-Loss Stakes
Define the actual thing at risk and why it matters to this person. Death is optional.

### B84-G04 — Stakes Change Choice
A higher stake that changes no strategy, relationship, obligation or decision is mostly decorative.

### B84-G05 — Pressure-Type Rotation
Rotate technical / relational / evidentiary / institutional / consent / social / operational / responsibility pressure before simply increasing physical scale.

### B84-G06 — ARM Expanded
`ACTION/ATTEMPT → RESPONSE → PROCESSING → STRATEGY CHANGE → NEXT ACTION`.
Reaction may be emotional, relational or epistemic rather than physically active.

### B84-G07 — No Mandatory Setback
Success is allowed. A successful action may create larger responsibility, dependency, confidence, trust or later cost.

### B84-G08 — Societal Causality
Societal stakes earn space only when a social rule/public condition changes local options and choices.

### B84-G09 — Wall Diagnosis
Before altering story architecture, distinguish writer fatigue from story failure.

### B84-G10 — Complication Necessity
A new complication must alter the existing causal engine, not merely occupy pages.

### B84-G11 — New Character Personhood
Never inject a character solely as midbook trouble. They need an independent want, relationships, evidence/model and consequences.

### B84-G12 — Organic Subplot
A subplot must feed back into the A-story through decision, evidence, resource, relationship, constraint or payoff.

### B84-G13 — Middle Meeting Loop
Repeated `meeting → explanation → review → another meeting` requires mode change, compression or a genuine state-changing confrontation.

### B84-G14 — Overweight-Middle Triage
Cut/merge structural redundancy before line editing.

### B84-G15 — Redundant Ally Merge
If two supporting characters repeatedly provide the same knowledge, emotional support, opposition or solution function, test combination or differentiation.

### B84-G16 — Reader Reward Balance
Do not let escalation become misery monoculture. Earned competence, intimacy, discovery, humour, repair or partial resolution can carry middle momentum.

### B84-G17 — Post-Money Stakes Firewall
Role/responsibility/qualification/trust are not euphemisms for salary, rent or credits.

### B84-G18 — Robot Engagement Ownership
A conscious robot needs its own reason to remain in the causal engine; human need for its functionality is insufficient.

## SCHOOL COMPARISON

### Bell vs Hodgins
Both treat the middle as development rather than static repetition. Hodgins is the stronger corrective against merely increasing volume.

### Bell vs Rosenfeld
Bell emphasizes confrontation and stakes; Rosenfeld clarifies that scene intention must arise from prior consequence and that repeated obstruction can stall rather than intensify.

### Bell vs Truby / McKee
All value pressure and consequence. IVDIVO rejects fixed act percentages, mandatory defeat and mandatory death.

### Bell vs LaPlante / Ribó
Their quieter causal models expand Bell: knowledge, emotion and relationships can move the story without immediate outward battle.

### Bell vs Lamott
Bell's `Wall` is especially valuable because it distinguishes a process problem from a story problem. Never inflate the plot simply because the writer is tired.

## MIDDLE AUDIT MATRIX

For each middle chapter/sequence, record:

`OWNER | PRESENT WANT | WHY STILL ENGAGED | PRESSURE TYPE | TACTIC | RESULT | EVIDENCE/RELATION CHANGE | NEXT STRATEGY | READER REWARD/COST`

Flag any three adjacent units with the same pressure type + same tactic + same result shape.

## ANTI-OBRUBKI CONSEQUENCE

When the middle drags, do not first shorten prose or add cliffhangers.

Use:

`CAUSAL ENGINE → REDUNDANCY → SCENE NECESSITY → MODE ROUTING → PARAGRAPH INTENT → RHYTHM → WORDING`.

A whole repetitive scene may need compression; sentence-level energy cannot repair structural repetition.
