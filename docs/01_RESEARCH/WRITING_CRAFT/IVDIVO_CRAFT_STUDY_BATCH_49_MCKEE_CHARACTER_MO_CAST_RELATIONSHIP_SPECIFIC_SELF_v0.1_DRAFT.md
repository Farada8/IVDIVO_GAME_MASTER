# IVDIVO CRAFT STUDY — BATCH 49
## McKee Character: MO · Cast Counterpoint · Relationship-Specific Self · Character Evidence

Date: 2026-08-17
Source: Robert McKee — *Character* (strict lifecycle COMPLETE)
Status: PRODUCTION SYNTHESIS / ADDITIVE

## Core finding
The high-value unit is not a trait list and not an allegedly final hidden essence. It is a **recognizable way of acting**:

`CONTEXT → WANT → APPRAISAL → MODUS OPERANDI → TACTIC → COUNTERACTION → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE → STRATEGY CHANGE`.

This makes character testable in scenes.

## Mechanism 1 — Modus Operandi
A major character should have a characteristic way to get what they want and avoid what they fear. Under pressure they may change tactics, but the shift should still feel like this person responding to new evidence.

### Diagnostic
Put two major characters in the same situation. If both choose the same first tactic, same backup tactic, same conversational strategy and same risk threshold, differentiation is probably cosmetic.

## Mechanism 2 — Relationship-specific self
The same person should naturally vary around:
- friend;
- parent/family;
- authority/instructor;
- rival;
- intimate partner;
- dependent/subordinate;
- public audience;
- conscious robot/nonhuman person.

The variation is not a fake personality switch. Different relationships expose different interests, risks, trust levels, habits and power balances.

## Mechanism 3 — Character evidence ladder
Do not convert one dramatic moment into an absolute diagnosis.

`SELF-REPORT → REPUTATION → LOW-COST CHOICE → REPEATED CHOICE → COSTLY CHOICE → AFTERMATH`.

The later evidence can reinterpret the earlier evidence. This is especially important for mystery and close POV.

## Mechanism 4 — Cast counterpoint
A cast becomes useful when members do not merely possess different adjectives but generate different causal responses to the same problem.

`SAME PROBLEM → DIFFERENT MODEL → DIFFERENT TACTIC → COLLISION → NEW INFORMATION / RELATIONSHIP CHANGE`.

This is a strong solution to ensemble fog.

## Mechanism 5 — Value shadow
Do not invent a mandatory flaw. Test whether a useful value produces a cost under some conditions:
- responsibility → reliability / overcontrol;
- autonomy → initiative / coordination refusal;
- care → support / paternalism;
- transparency → accountability / coercive exposure;
- competence → efficacy / overconfidence;
- skepticism → caution / paralysis.

## Mechanism 6 — Independent life
For every named conscious supporting person:
1. What do they want when the protagonist is absent?
2. Who else matters to them?
3. What can they refuse?
4. What price do they personally pay?
5. What would they do if the protagonist disappeared from the book?

If these answers collapse, the character remains a service function.

## Mechanism 7 — Long-form renewal
A sequel must not reset a familiar character to replay the same lesson. Give them a new context that stresses a previously unused side of their model.

`PRIOR CONSEQUENCE → NEW RESPONSIBILITY/RELATIONSHIP → OLD STRENGTH UNDER NEW CONDITIONS → NEW COST → STRATEGY UPDATE`.

## Rejections / firewalls
- no “true self” as IVDIVO metaphysical ontology;
- no mandatory maximal crisis to reveal character;
- no suffering/trauma as price of depth;
- no hidden subconscious counterdesire required;
- no mandatory underdog design;
- no requirement that the climax exhaust a person's total humanity;
- no archetype treated as psychological/scientific fact;
- no screen-economy rule applied automatically to prose;
- no modern money/job/rent motivation imported into mature orbital city life;
- no supporting conscious robot treated as Helper/Tool/Service only.

## Writers' Room use
When a scene feels generic, do not first add a joke, backstory paragraph or distinctive costume. Ask:

**Why does this person interpret this exact situation differently, and what tactic follows from that interpretation?**

That question should precede line editing.
