## Mechanisms added / strengthened

### 1. Emotional dimensionality

`VALUE / CONCERN + RELATIONSHIP + CIRCUMSTANCE + CURRENT COST → EMOTIONAL MIX → TACTIC → CONSEQUENCE`.

A broad label such as love, fear, grief, loyalty, anger, courage, shame, or responsibility is not a playable scene state by itself.

### 2. Trait stability ≠ behavioral uniformity

The same core concern can generate different surface behavior under different circumstances.

Example pattern:
`CARE → ordinary patience` but under acute threat `CARE → fierce interruption / anger / refusal`.

The apparent contradiction is legitimate only when both behaviors remain causally rooted in the same person, relationship, and situation.

### 3. Emotional contradiction ≠ random contradiction

Do not add an opposite trait merely to make a character look complex. A contradictory color needs a causal bridge through value, relationship, circumstance, knowledge, cost, or competing priority.

### 4. Growth needs seeds

`EARLY CAPACITY / SMALL CHOICE → PRESSURE → PARTIAL VARIATION → CONSEQUENCE → LATER HIGH-COST CHOICE`.

No mandatory transformation is implied. Stable, partial, negative, tactical, or deferred series change remain valid.

### 5. Surface behavior is evidence, not motive proof

`OBSERVED EMOTION / GESTURE / INFLECTION → POV INTERPRETATION → ALTERNATIVE MODEL → CORROBORATION`.

Strong emotion does not prove motive, guilt, personhood, consent, or ontology.

### 6. Emotional writing is not microgesture accumulation

Do not render emotional dimensionality as:
`line → glance → hand movement → one-line thought → line → facial expression`.

Prefer scene-level action/tactic, selective embodiment cues, paragraph-level continuity, and sentence rhythm appropriate to reader effect.

## Cross-source comparison

- McKee `Character`: dimensionality and relationship-specific contradiction are useful, but Collins provides a more circumstance-driven route from broad passion to component feelings.
- Corbett: surprising behavior must remain retrospectively character-true; Collins's opposite-color mechanism is valid only under that constraint.
- Card / Kress: relationship and present pressure alter behavior; one fixed trait label is insufficient.
- Oatley: emotion is tied to goals/concerns and changes when those concerns change.
- Zunshine / le Carré / Fludernik: observed behavior and emotional display remain evidence to interpret, not automatic world fact.
- Lamott / LaPlante / Swain: analytic decomposition belongs especially to troubleshooting/revision and must not freeze live generation.

## IVDIVO operationalization

### ONE MAIN STORY
Emotional variation pays into the main engine only when it changes tactic, relationship terms, evidence handling, consent, responsibility, authority/access, commitment, or future option space.

### YA
Do not force every young character through a healing arc. Growth may be uneven, partial, tactical, relational, or a tested stability. A competent teenager may retain core values while learning a different strategy or bearing a larger responsibility.

### Relationships
Do not treat love/trust as one emotional color. Relationship state should be rendered through context-specific mixtures: attraction + embarrassment; care + anger; trust + doubt; loyalty + resentment; intimacy + boundary protection.

### Conscious robots / nonhuman persons
Do not map human affect labels or facial cues by default. Use the entity's embodiment, priorities, relationships, knowledge limits, consent/refusal, and action history. Apparent contradiction may arise from competing priorities without implying 'becoming human.'

### Mystery / evidence
`EMOTIONAL DISPLAY → POV MODEL`, not `EMOTIONAL DISPLAY → FACT`.
Trust, fear, attraction, grief, anger, silence, or confidence may alter investigative behavior; they do not change what a trace proves.

### Choppy prose
Emotional complexity must not become microgesture confetti. Preserve paragraph continuity when several beats form one perceptual/decision unit. Sentence length follows reader effect, not emotion intensity alone.
