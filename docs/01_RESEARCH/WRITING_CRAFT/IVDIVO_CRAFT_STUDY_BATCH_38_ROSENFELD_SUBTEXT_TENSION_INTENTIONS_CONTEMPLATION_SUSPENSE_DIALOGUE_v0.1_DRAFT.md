# 1. CORE SYNTHESIS

A scene should be tested on at least four simultaneous layers:

`SURFACE ACTION`  
`CHARACTER INTENTION`  
`PRESSURE / SUPPORT / UNCERTAINTY`  
`SUBTEXT / RELATIONSHIP / THEMATIC MEANING`

A scene can be quiet and still be causally alive if something changes in decision, relationship, knowledge, emotional readiness or future strategy.

---

# 2. SUBTEXT ENGINE

Useful abstraction:

`WHAT IS SAID/DONE → WHAT EACH PERSON WANTS → WHAT CANNOT BE SAID DIRECTLY → WHAT THE READER CAN INFER → HOW THAT INFERENCE CHANGES THE SCENE`

Strong carriers:
- involuntary behavior;
- body language;
- repeated but evolving imagery;
- meaningful objects;
- foreground/background counterpoint;
- innuendo;
- avoided topic;
- social asymmetry;
- setting detail that echoes or contradicts the foreground action.

## Production rule

Do not label subtext after creating it. If the scene shows Ethan arranging a schedule because he fears the group is drifting, prose should not then explain that he fears the group is drifting unless new information is added.

## Failure gate

**SYMBOL ANNOUNCEMENT:** image/object is followed by an explanation of what it symbolizes.

**SUBTEXT DECORATION:** symbolism is aesthetically related to theme but does not affect character perception, emotion, choice or reader model.

---

# 3. TENSION ENGINE

Rosenfeld is most useful when tension is separated from overt conflict.

Transferable model:

`VALUED OUTCOME → UNCERTAINTY → RESISTANCE / POWER SHIFT / EXPECTATION GAP → DELAY OR DECISION → CONSEQUENCE`

Tension sources:
- a goal may not be achieved;
- another person has leverage;
- a normal pattern changes without explanation;
- character and reader know different amounts;
- an expected response does not occur;
- environment/body supplies ambiguous warning;
- a technical interpretation competes with another plausible interpretation.

## Pace correction

Suspense does **not** imply short sentences.

Use:
- slower sensory attention when anticipation is the pressure;
- faster syntax when action itself is the pressure;
- summary when routine process delays the actual uncertainty without adding value.

Production gate:

`DESIRED READER EFFECT → PACE → SENTENCE RHYTHM`

not

`SUSPENSE → SHORT SENTENCES`.

---

# 4. INTENTION ENGINE

Distinguish:

**MOTIVATION** = why this matters to the character.  
**PLOT INTENTION** = what larger outcome the character is pursuing.  
**SCENE INTENTION** = what the character plans to do now.

Operational sequence:

`PRIOR CONSEQUENCE → IMMEDIATE INTENTION → ACTION → OPPOSITION/SUPPORT → RESULT → NEW INTENTION`

A scene fails when the intention can be replaced by any arbitrary errand without changing the book.

Opposition need not equal failure. Valid result modes:
- success;
- partial success;
- costly success;
- blocked attempt;
- new information changes the goal;
- another person helps;
- character correctly decides not to act.

---

# 5. CONTEMPLATIVE SCENE ENGINE

A contemplative scene earns its place when it performs transformation of meaning rather than restatement.

`EVENT / PRESSURE → PROCESSING → REINTERPRETATION → DECISION OR CHANGED READINESS → NEXT ACTION`

Good uses:
- digest consequence after an intense scene;
- make a decision the reader needs to understand from inside;
- reveal a changed relationship to prior facts;
- allow emotional aftershock before a new strategy.

Bad uses:
- repeating the lesson already demonstrated;
- philosophical speech unrelated to an actual consequence;
- consecutive contemplation with no new state change.

Setting should give the mind something to react against rather than leaving pages of abstract thought in a vacuum.

---

# 6. SUSPENSE ENGINE

Suspense is best treated as **meaningful uncertainty**, not merely danger.

Useful sequence:

`SIGNAL → PLAUSIBLE EXPLANATION A → SECOND SIGNAL → PLAUSIBLE EXPLANATION B → NARROWING OPTIONS → CONSEQUENCE`

This is especially useful for IVDIVO technical / institutional / mystery lines because tension can arise while all adults remain competent.

Fair-play constraint:
- do not suppress facts a POV character already knows merely to keep the reader anxious;
- do not convert an inference into a fact without an evidence step;
- delay must come from uncertainty, access, timing, consent, incomplete data, competing explanations or real procedural constraint.

# 7. DIALOGUE ENGINE

Before writing direct dialogue, identify the tactical substructure.

`PERSON A WANTS X`  
`PERSON B WANTS Y`  
`EXACT WORDS ARE THEIR TACTICS`

Rosenfeld’s simple `Please / No` abstraction is useful even when literal dialogue is sophisticated.

Direct dialogue earns scene space when exact wording changes:
- power;
- trust;
- consent;
- evidence status;
- relationship;
- decision;
- emotional state;
- public interpretation.

Routine reporting, repeated facts, logistics and known procedure are candidates for indirect or summary compression.

## Dialogue subtext

Best sources:
- the thing neither person names;
- old history activated by current wording;
- unequal standing;
- body/setting contradicting speech;
- one speaker answering a safer question than the one actually asked.

## Debate-dialogue gate

If every participant can be paraphrased as a clean thesis statement, the scene is probably a panel discussion rather than drama.

Require:
- local wants;
- interruptions;
- social standing;
- incomplete knowledge;
- personal cost;
- tactical language;
- unequal willingness to disclose.

---

# 8. PROLOGUE / FIRST-SCENE CONTROL

Prologue must perform a function that is substantially better outside normal chronology/POV.

Reject prologue if it only:
- compensates for a weak Chapter 1;
- previews generic danger;
- explains backstory that present action could reveal;
- exists because the author wants a cinematic teaser.

First-scene requirement is not `start with explosion`.

Use:
`PERSON + DISTURBANCE/PROBLEM + IMMEDIATE WANT + ORIENTATION + OPEN CONSEQUENCE`.

Worldbuilding should enter through what the protagonist is doing, noticing or needing.

# 9. NEW PROVISIONAL GATES — B38

These remain provisional until Rosenfeld strict lifecycle closes.

**B38-G01 — Surface/Subtext Separation**  
Scene must have a visible action layer and, when subtext is intended, an inferable second layer that is not merely explained afterward.

**B38-G02 — Symbol Silence**  
Do not verbally decode a symbol/image that already communicates its function.

**B38-G03 — Tension ≠ Argument**  
Tension may arise from uncertainty, expectation gap, power, silence, sensory warning or competing models.

**B38-G04 — Suspense Pace Choice**  
Choose slow/fast rhythm from reader effect, not genre label.

**B38-G05 — Intention Lineage**  
Current scene intention must be traceable to prior consequence and larger story pressure.

**B38-G06 — Support Is Causal**  
Allies/help may move story; do not force failure merely to preserve tension.

**B38-G07 — Contemplation Changes Readiness**  
Reflection survives only if it changes interpretation, decision, relationship or next strategy.

**B38-G08 — No Contemplation Echo**  
Do not repeat the same internal conclusion in consecutive scenes.

**B38-G09 — Fair Suspense Delay**  
Delayed answer must be caused in-world, not hidden by authorial manipulation.

**B38-G10 — Exact-Words Test**  
Use direct dialogue only when exact wording matters enough to justify scene time.

**B38-G11 — Dialogue Tactical Ownership**  
Every major speaker needs a local objective, not merely a thematic position.

**B38-G12 — Reveal Aftermath**  
A reveal must alter behavior, relation, evidence status or decision.

**B38-G13 — Prologue Necessity**  
Prologue must perform a nonlinear/alternate-POV/high-value function that Chapter 1 cannot perform as well.

**B38-G14 — First-Scene Causal Curiosity**  
Opening must create a concrete question/outcome the reader can care about without encyclopedic setup.
