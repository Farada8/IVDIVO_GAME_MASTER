## Primary professional role

Integration-oriented fiction craft source emphasizing:
- scene vs summary;
- setting as physical + social system;
- character through action and environment;
- plot/obstacle/stakes;
- dialogue as character and plot;
- POV/voice authority;
- revision by re-imagination;
- whole-manuscript unity/integrity.

## Strongest contributions extracted so far

1. **Scene proximity selection:** dramatize what must be witnessed directly; summarize what only needs causal/temporal transmission.
2. **Obstacle differentiation:** escalating obstacles should reveal different dimensions/tactics, not repeat the same response.
3. **Non-villain plot pressure:** internal conflict, institutions, places and value systems can provide meaningful opposition.
4. **Setting beyond scenery:** peer pressure, community standards, family values and historical time are part of setting.
5. **POV as authority:** a POV change changes narrative authority/voice, not just pronouns.
6. **Close-third double vision:** reader can share a character's perception while recognizing distortion/irony.
7. **Dialogue multifunction:** what characters say, how, and avoid saying carries character/plot/information.
8. **Revision as re-seeing:** deep POV/voice/staging defects may require rewriting from a new imaginative position.
9. **Whole-integrity audit:** character, POV, voice, plot, pace, dialogue, style, theme and unity must reinforce one another.

## Limits / cautions

- The book predates contemporary YA, platform/serial and digital-reading contexts; transfer must be medium-aware.
- Some craft language reflects its era and should not be elevated into universal law.
- The scene 'competition' model is useful but should not become compulsory hostility; Hodgins himself discusses alternatives to conflict-dominated plot.
- His revision advice can be over-applied: full rewrite is warranted for foundational imaginative defects, not every imperfect paragraph.
- Examples are pedagogical rather than empirical evidence of universal reader response.

## Cross-source position

Agrees strongly with:
- McKee: purposeful scene change and action;
- Rosenfeld: scene intention/exchange/consequence;
- LaPlante: deep revision before cosmetic polish when foundations are wrong;
- Le Guin/Burroway: resistance to treating 'story as war' as the only possible narrative model;
- Chatman: narrative function must be distinguished from mere event chronology.

Useful tension with rigid beat systems: Hodgins is more pluralistic and integration-driven.
