## Source passport

**Primary problem addressed:** how to move from completed draft to structurally stronger, deeper and cleaner manuscript without confusing developmental revision with sentence polishing.  
**School/tradition:** pragmatic commercial-fiction craft / staged self-editing.  
**Strongest IVDIVO contribution:** explicit separation of drafting and revision plus whole-manuscript `large → small` diagnostic order.  
**Best IVDIVO use:** revision routing, developmental triage, dedicated passes, scene-level checks after story architecture is stable.  
**Weakness if over-applied:** checklist thinking can produce generic commercial smoothness; some scene advice favors worsening outcomes too strongly; exact cooling/waiting prescriptions are workflow suggestions, not universal laws.  
**Not sufficient for:** literary aesthetics, narratology, YA psychology, fair-play mystery, prose music, social realism or source ethics by itself.

## High-value source mechanisms currently extracted

1. **Draft fast / revise slow and cool.** Drafting and revision require different cognitive modes.
2. **Do not repeatedly rebuild the unfinished manuscript from page one.** Record structural concerns and keep enough forward momentum to discover the whole story.
3. **Create distance before deep revision.** The useful mechanism is perceptual distance, not an exact mandatory waiting period.
4. **First read-through before local repair.** Read for the experience of the whole rather than immediately editing sentences.
5. **Revision goes large → small.** Story/structure/character/scene problems precede style and copy polish.
6. **Dedicated-pass logic.** Different defects require different reading lenses; one overloaded pass misses both macro and micro problems.
7. **Scene function test.** Bell’s scene diagnostics include objective, obstacle/pressure, outcome, reaction and contribution to the whole.
8. **Deepening follows structural viability.** Additional emotion, sensory detail, voice and resonance cannot rescue a scene whose function is wrong.

## Cross-source correction

Bell’s useful `Objective → Obstacle → Outcome` model is retained but bounded by Scofield, Rosenfeld, McKee, Swain, Hodgins and IVDIVO manuscript tests:

`OBJECTIVE → PRESSURE / OBSTACLE / SUPPORT → TURN / BULL'S-EYE → OUTCOME → CHANGED NEXT INTENTION`.

A scene does **not** need to make the protagonist worse off every time. A success, restraint, discovery, refusal, partial answer or relationship gain can create the harder next problem.
