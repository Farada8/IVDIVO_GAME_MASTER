## Source passport

### Problem addressed
How to build, select, connect and revise scenes so they create sustained character/plot movement instead of disconnected dramatized moments.

### Strongest contribution
Practical scene-level routing: intentions, complications, scene types, endings, transitions and revision assessment.

### Useful IVDIVO domains

- scene cards;
- chapter engineering;
- action vs vignette diagnosis;
- scene ending design;
- contemplative / aftermath placement;
- transition compression;
- first-scene audit;
- scene-to-scene causal chain;
- line-level anti-fragmentation by narrative selection.

### Limits

- checklist-heavy advice can become formulaic if treated as compulsory surface structure;
- not every literary scene requires overt antagonist/conflict;
- “intention” must include relational, epistemic and contemplative decisions, not only external tasks;
- not a specialist mystery/fair-play authority;
- not a prose-style or voice authority by itself;
- examples are craft illustrations, not empirical evidence.

## Extracted core mechanisms

### M1 — Scene Intention Recursion
`PRIOR CONSEQUENCE → IMMEDIATE INTENTION → COMPLICATION → RESULT → NEXT INTENTION`

### M2 — Plot/Scene Intention Split
Durable book-level goal produces changing present-moment actions rather than repeated versions of one scene.

### M3 — Opposition / Support Variation
Intentions may be blocked, complicated, partially achieved or supported. Permanent thwarting is not required.

### M4 — Ending Operator
Scene ending can move inward toward character/emotion/decision or outward toward larger consequence/question.

### M5 — Contemplative Recovery
Reflection earns scene-time when it digests prior pressure and produces changed action or interpretation.

### M6 — Transition Economy
Do not fully dramatize time/place/process where nothing load-bearing changes.

### M7 — First-Scene Burden
Open with protagonist + current pressure + ordinary world + curiosity, not encyclopedia explanation.

### M8 — Adjacent Scene Integrity
Assess a scene both on its own and as a consequence of the previous scene / cause of the next.

## Cross-source map

**Agrees with:**

**Differs in scale/terminology:**
- Rosenfeld is more operational/checklist-driven than McKee;
- less formal causal theory than Swain;
- more scene-type taxonomy than Hodgins.
