## Main problem addressed

How to learn and revise fiction by practicing isolated craft moves rather than relying only on abstract advice.

## Method

Short controlled exercises aimed at:
- imagination;
- writerly perception/sensibility;
- specific craft techniques;
- revision through variable testing.

The book is intentionally non-linear. Exercises are selected according to need rather than treated as a universal sequence.

## High-value IVDIVO contributions extracted so far

1. **Five-fork continuation lab** for stalled stories: generate multiple continuations before committing.
2. **Opening set-in-motion question:** identify what a strong beginning actually starts.
3. **Revision as re-envisioning:** early drafts may change POV, tense, scene order, character presence, imagery, body language and scene length.
4. **Preserve living first-draft material:** revise around what already works rather than reflexively restarting.
5. **Dialogue compression:** habitual/repeated conversation can be summarized before exact direct dialogue enters at the consequential moment.
6. **Constraint as diagnostic:** short formal exercises reveal padding/fuzziness but do not prescribe final novel lengths.
7. **Practice without failure stigma:** weak sentences/characters can be recast rather than defended because of sunk cost.

## Limits / failure modes

- Exercise success does not equal story success.
- Artificial prompts may produce interesting but non-organic material.
- Beginner-focused constraints can become formulaic if imported directly into production.
- The book contains many examples/exercises; not every exercise deserves equal production weight.
- Its character-driven preference should be compared with plot/system-based approaches rather than accepted as an exclusive theory.

## Cross-source relation
