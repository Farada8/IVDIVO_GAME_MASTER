## Source passport

**Primary problem addressed:** making characters memorable through desire, action, contradiction, emotion and distinctive behavior rather than biography alone.  
**School/tradition:** pragmatic commercial-fiction character craft.  
**Strongest contribution:** character memorability as a bundle of active pressures — will, yearning/burn, inner conflict, resourcefulness, unexpected-but-credible behavior and relational conduct.  
**Best IVDIVO use:** ensemble differentiation, YA agency, ordinary-life character revelation, non-plot-device supporting characters, series continuity.  
**Weakness if over-applied:** Bell's “death” framing can inflate stakes; wound may be mistaken for mandatory trauma; unpredictability may become random quirk; wit/decency can become checklist likability; “strength of will” may undervalue quieter forms of agency.  
**Not sufficient for:** developmental psychology, sociology, cultural specificity, trauma research, sociolinguistics or relationship realism by itself.

## Current bounded conclusion

Adopt Bell's active-character emphasis, but translate it into IVDIVO terms:

`PRIVATE WANT / BURN → CHOICE UNDER PRESSURE → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC ACTION → RELATIONSHIP COST → CONSEQUENCE → CHANGED STRATEGY`.

A character is not memorable because the dossier is detailed. A character becomes memorable because the reader repeatedly sees **what this particular person notices, wants, refuses, risks, gets wrong, protects and pays for**.
