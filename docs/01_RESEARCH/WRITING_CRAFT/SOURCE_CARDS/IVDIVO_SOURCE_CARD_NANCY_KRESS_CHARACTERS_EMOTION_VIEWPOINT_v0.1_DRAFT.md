## Preliminary source claims under test

### KRESS-C01 — Motivation is operational, not decorative
A character becomes story-active because they want something. Backstory matters only insofar as it makes present motivation, behavior, emotion, and choice intelligible.

### KRESS-C02 — Reader-assumption controls backstory burden
Common motivations need little explanation; unusual motivations require more causal support. This is context-dependent: what feels obvious in one culture or century may be strange in another.

### KRESS-C03 — Complex characters can want incompatible things
Conflicting values/motivations generate internal pressure and become character-revealing when the person must choose between them.

### KRESS-C04 — Emotion derives from concern under pressure
The useful chain is approximately:
`BACKSTORY → PERSONALITY / VALUES → PRESENT WANT → EMOTION → BEHAVIOR`.
Emotion should not float free of what the character cares about.

### KRESS-C05 — Frustration is a story engine
Plot grows because a character cannot obtain a wanted result cleanly. The important craft variable is not raw intensity but how THIS character responds and whether their response strategy changes.

### KRESS-C06 — Breaking points require stored pressure
An emotional rupture works only if earlier scenes establish repeated pressure and prior restraint. Without preparation it reads as melodrama or author manipulation.

### KRESS-C07 — Emotion should alter action
Thought, dialogue, bodily response, gesture, sensory selection and action can render emotion; abstract labeling alone is weak. The strongest emotional material changes what the character does next.

### KRESS-C08 — POV distance is a continuum
Close / middle / distant third are relative operating distances, not rigid boxes. Sudden repeated jumps from deep interiority to distant overview can create narrative vertigo.

### KRESS-C09 — First-person filtering can create needless distance
Constructions such as “I wondered / I feared / I reminded myself” may weaken immediacy when the actual thought can be rendered directly in the character’s language.

### KRESS-C10 — Character change is not mandatory in every form
Some protagonists change; some remain stable and reveal a world, expose a pattern, or sustain a series identity. The decision must be intentional rather than an accidental arc reset.

### KRESS-C11 — Drafting and criticism are different cognitive jobs
Kress’s “fourth persona” adds critic after character / writer / reader, but warns against activating the critic too early during generative work.

## Failure modes extracted so far

1. BACKSTORY BEFORE NEED — history arrives before present action creates a question.
2. MOTIVATION WITHOUT CULTURAL SUPPORT — author assumes a motive is obvious when the storyworld makes it unusual.
3. GENERIC EMOTION — interchangeable clenched fists, tears, racing hearts, etc. that do not belong to a specific character.
4. EMOTION LABEL SUBSTITUTE — narrator names feeling instead of dramatizing concern + response.
5. CONSTANT BLOCKAGE — “frustration” misread as making every attempt fail, producing monotony rather than causality.
6. UNPREPARED BREAKING POINT — explosive scene without accumulated pressure / prior restraint.
7. CONTRADICTION FOR DEPTH — incompatible traits or motives added because “complex characters are contradictory,” but behavior has no plausible causal bridge.
8. POV DISTANCE WHIPLASH — close interior → aerial authorial summary → close interior repeatedly inside one scene without control.
9. FILTER-WORD FOG — first/close-third experience repeatedly mediated through “she saw / he felt / I thought” when direct perception would be stronger.
10. DIALECT / SLANG COSTUME — voice simulated through surface markers rather than perception, values and syntax.
11. SERIES RESET — protagonist learns something, then next book restores the old behavior merely because it is familiar.
12. CRITIC TOO EARLY — line-level self-policing blocks discovery before the scene exists.

## Cross-source comparisons (preliminary)

### With Bell
Bell often frames story pressure around desire, opposition and choice. Kress adds a useful diagnostic layer: the SAME obstruction should not produce identical reactions across characters; response to frustration is characterization.

### With Rosenfeld
Rosenfeld’s scene-intention model and Kress’s motivation/frustration model converge on causal scenes. Kress adds emotional causation and breaking-point preparation; Rosenfeld is stronger on scene architecture and transition.

### With Oatley / Herman
Oatley connects goal/plan to reader simulation; Herman distinguishes behavior from reason. Kress operationalizes the writing side: author must know the motive/backstory relation, but need expose only what the reader requires for the behavior to remain intelligible.

### With Le Guin / Bell Voice
Kress’s POV-distance continuum supports the existing IVDIVO rule that closeness is produced by selection, syntax, interior access and perception—not by pronoun choice alone.

## Canon firewall

Craft principles may be used; example-world assumptions must not be imported.
Particularly:
- contemporary wage/rent motivations are NOT default Orbital Youth motivations;
- conscious robots are persons, not owned appliances;
- ordinary Orbital Youth does NOT possess human consciousness/soul-transfer technology;
- dimensional/subtle-body knowledge is specialist and underdeveloped relative to external technology.
