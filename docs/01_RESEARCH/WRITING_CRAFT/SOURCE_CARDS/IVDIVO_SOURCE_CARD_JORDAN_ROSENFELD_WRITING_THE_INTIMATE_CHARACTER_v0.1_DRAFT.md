## Source passport

**Primary problem addressed:** how to create the experience of living through a character by controlling narrative distance, information access, embodied cues and emotional perception.

**School/tradition:** contemporary practical fiction craft with strong close-POV and sensory/character-cue orientation.

**Strongest IVDIVO contributions:**
- POV as information + worldview filter, not pronoun choice;
- third-person intimate as a versatile balance of intimacy and control;
- embodied perception as character truth;
- setting experienced through body and attitude;
- surface/subset emotion contradiction;
- thoughts must be interwoven with action/setting to preserve forward motion;
- multiple POV requires real differentiation and plot function;
- technology can be a storytelling device rather than exposition topic.

**Weakness if over-applied:**
- language around universal need for wounds/flaws/transformation can become formulaic;
- “three emotion cues” style exercises must not become quota writing;
- extremely intimate POV can create over-internalized, slow scenes;
- sensory detail can become decorative inventory;
- multiple POV advice needs stricter IVDIVO economy because ensemble books can fragment easily;
- not every story requires large internal transformation.

**Not sufficient for:** macro story selection, mystery fair play, institutional ethics, science plausibility, orbital economics, dialogue subtext, market positioning.

## Cross-source positioning

### Rosenfeld ↔ Alicia Rasley
Rasley is stronger on information control, narrator reliability and fair access; Rosenfeld is stronger on embodied/emotional intimacy. Use together.

### Rosenfeld ↔ Bell Voice
Bell asks how character background/attitude creates language; Rosenfeld asks what the character is physically/mentally able to experience. Together: `ACCESS → PERCEPTION → ATTITUDE → LANGUAGE`.

### Rosenfeld ↔ Oatley
Oatley explains reader identification as simulation of goals/plans/emotions; Rosenfeld supplies page-level sensory and POV cues that enable that simulation.

### Rosenfeld ↔ Zunshine
Rosenfeld intensifies mind access; Zunshine warns that beliefs/inferences need source-monitoring. Close POV cannot turn subjective interpretation into objective truth.

### Rosenfeld ↔ Hodgins
Both treat POV/voice as structural, not cosmetic. Hodgins is stronger on whole-scene re-imagination and narrative authority.
