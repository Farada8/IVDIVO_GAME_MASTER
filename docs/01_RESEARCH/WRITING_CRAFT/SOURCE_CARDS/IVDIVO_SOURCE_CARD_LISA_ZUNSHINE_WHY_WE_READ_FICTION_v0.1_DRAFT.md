## Primary contribution

Zunshine sharpens two craft problems:

1. readers continually infer states of mind from behavior;
2. readers also track **who/what supplied a representation** and may store it provisionally rather than as direct truth.

For IVDIVO this makes source provenance a first-class mystery variable.

## High-value mechanisms extracted

- behavioral cue → mental-state inference;
- metarepresentation / information stored under advisement;
- source-monitoring and reliability shifts;
- suspicion redirecting attention toward the source itself;
- graded confidence rather than binary truth/lie;
- cognitive cost of multiple deceptive actors;
- physical clues as tools for reconstructing action/mind, not independent truth objects;
- strategic control of reader access to investigator minds;
- no automatic role immunity from misreading/concealment.

## Limits / failure modes

1. Theory-of-Mind account is not a total explanation of why fiction works.
2. Cognitive/evolutionary claims in this 2006 book are explicitly framed by the author as developing/speculative and should not be turned into hard universal laws.
3. Source-monitoring does not imply every narrator or institution must be unreliable.
4. `suspect everybody` is genre-specific pressure, not a mandate for arbitrary betrayal.
5. Mental-state inference remains fallible and culturally/contextually shaped.
6. Detective-fiction observations must be historicized; formal conventions change.
