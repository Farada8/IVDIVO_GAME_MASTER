## CONTINUOUS FULL READ LOG
### Foreword — COMPLETE
Swain frames the book explicitly as a toolbox for fiction creation rather than literary-quality law. He emphasizes devices that have proved useful in commercial storytelling and says no writer uses all techniques.

### Chapter 1 — FICTION AND YOU — STRICT COMPLETE
Core architecture introduced:
1. group words into motivation-reaction units;
2. group M/R units into scenes and sequels;
3. group scenes/sequels into story pattern;
4. create characters that give story life.

Eight traps:
1. unrealistic view;
2. hunt for magic secrets;
3. try to learn the hard way;
4. refuse to follow feeling;
5. attempt to write by rules;
6. do not want to be wrong;
7. bow down to the objective;
8. fail to master technique.

Most valuable craft distinction: Swain separates creation from criticism. Feeling/excitement drives the generative state; principles and rules-of-thumb are used later in reappraisal. He explicitly describes rules as checklist rather than blueprint.

Chapter 1 closes by arguing that skill is learned through writing live stories, accepting error, examining process mistakes, and gradually building a personal tool kit. Direct transition to Ch2 verified.

### Chapter 2 — THE WORDS YOU WRITE — STRICT COMPLETE
Continuous coverage is now verified from chapter opening through the direct transition into Chapter 3.

Confirmed architecture and claims:
- star writers often work largely by ear;
- clarity, accuracy and specificity matter more than grammatical prestige;
- words can help, hinder or clutter;
- three immediate goals: choose right words, make copy vivid, keep meaning clear;
- three working jobs: Selection / Arrangement / Description;
- selection determines what slice of life the reader receives: who, when, where, which action, which noticed detail, and why it matters;
- selection is inseparable from viewpoint/attention: the usual vantage is inside a particular person's experience, unless an objective/other mode is deliberately established;
- arrangement changes meaning: identical facts in a different order produce different reader emphasis/inference;
- description exists to make selected material experientially available;
- Swain explicitly rejects simplistic equivalence between good prose and short words/sentences/paragraphs;
- simplicity and brevity are conditional virtues; in the wrong place brevity can damage the effect;
- his primary stated criterion here is vividness;
- vividness is built from sensory-verifiable particulars, action/movement, and concrete nouns/verbs;
- greater technical specificity only helps when the intended reader can understand/infer the term; otherwise precision becomes opacity;
- active verbs are advocated to clarify happening/agency, but Swain's blanket hostility to `to be` must be treated as a period-specific overgeneralization rather than a law;
- past-perfect `had` is treated as a warning sign for backward drift, yet Swain himself allows legitimate past-perfect transitions and other necessary uses;
- pronouns require clear antecedents: referent confusion damages basic reader orientation;
- modifiers should add precision or emotional/character meaning rather than redundantly intensify what a noun/verb already supplies;
- Swain explicitly states that vividness can outrank brevity;
- simile/metaphor can connect new experience to known experience when they sharpen rather than decorate;
- denotation and connotation both matter: wording carries emotional/social evaluation beyond dictionary meaning;
- he identifies common language problems including monotonous sentence structure, subject/verb separation, misplaced modifiers, accidental repetition, grammar fetishism, and unclear meaning;
- sentence variation should serve effect; syntactic acrobatics for their own sake distract;
- the problem in an overloaded sentence is often number/organization of logical jobs, not sheer word count;
- accidental repetition is noise, intentional patterned repetition can create emphasis/rhythm;
- fragments, nonstandard punctuation, stream-of-consciousness and one-word paragraphs are valid when used deliberately for reader effect and remain intelligible;
- chapter ends on the demand that basic meaning/orientation be immediately graspable, then transitions directly to Chapter 3.

### Chapter 3 — PLAIN FACTS ABOUT FEELINGS — STRICT READ STARTED, IN PROGRESS
Direct transition from Chapter 2 verified.

Opening architecture lists nine steps:
1. decide what is good/bad;
2. give the reader a character as compass;
3. create a story world;
4. inject change;
5. draw motive power from cause/effect;
6. pin development to motivation/reaction;
7. make M-R units shape emotion;
8. relate copy length to tension;
9. write in M-R units.

Strict reading has advanced through the opening `How to tell good from bad` argument:
- evaluation requires a specific instance plus a yardstick;
- events do not carry stable narrative meaning in isolation;
- significance is relational: a rainstorm can be good or bad depending on whose interests, values and circumstances define the yardstick;
- Swain's central move here is `significant to somebody`, not significance-in-itself.

## PROVISIONAL MECHANISMS

### S09-M01 — CREATION / CRITIQUE SEPARATION
`GENERATIVE FEELING / DISCOVERY -> LIVE MATERIAL -> REAPPRAISAL -> PROCESS DIAGNOSIS -> TOOL -> REVISION`.
Do not run all IVDIVO production gates simultaneously while a scene is being discovered.

### S09-M02 — RULES AS CHECKLIST, NOT BLUEPRINT
Craft principles diagnose and expand options. They do not outrank current canon, character truth, causality, personhood or reader evidence.

### S09-M03 — FEELING -> TECHNIQUE
Feeling identifies what the writer is trying to transmit; technique shapes the transmission.
IVDIVO firewall: subjective conviction is not evidence about world ontology, science, motive or another person's consent.

### S09-M04 — ERROR-BASED LEARNING LOOP
`WRITE -> OBSERVE FAILURE / READER EFFECT -> LOCATE PROCESS GAP -> APPLY TOOL -> RETEST`.

### S09-M05 — PROCESS, NOT MAGIC SECRET
Swain's value is decomposition of complex writing effects into learnable operations. Reject conversion of those operations into deterministic formulas.

### S09-M06 — SELECTION / ARRANGEMENT / DESCRIPTION ROUTER
`WHAT EXISTS IN STORYWORLD -> WHAT THIS POV/SCENE SELECTS -> IN WHAT ORDER THE READER RECEIVES IT -> HOW IT IS RENDERED`.
Selection and arrangement are meaning-making operations, not neutral transcription.

### S09-M07 — ATTENTION IS CHARACTERIZATION
`POV CAPACITY + PRESENT WANT + RELATIONSHIP + KNOWLEDGE -> NOTICED DETAIL -> READER INFERENCE`.
A detail belongs because this observer notices it now and it performs story/character/world work, not because the world bible contains it.

### S09-M08 — ORDER CHANGES INFERENCE
`SAME FACTS + DIFFERENT DISCOURSE ORDER -> DIFFERENT READER MODEL`.
For mystery, source/evidence status must remain fair even when order is used for suspense.

### S09-M09 — VIVIDNESS OVER BREVITY WORSHIP
`READER EFFECT -> REQUIRED DURATION/RHYTHM -> SPECIFIC RENDERING`.
Shorter is not automatically stronger. Brevity can destroy continuity, accumulation, perception or emotional pressure when imposed mechanically.

### S09-M10 — LEGIBLE SPECIFICITY
`MORE PRECISE TERM -> POV AUTHENTIC? -> READER CAN UNDERSTAND/INFER? -> CAUSAL/CHARACTER PAYOFF?`.
Technical specificity that the reader cannot place is not vividness; it is opacity.

### S09-M11 — CONCRETE NOUN / ACTIVE EVENT PAYOFF
Prefer concrete, definite naming and action where they make the experience more exact. Do not turn this into a ban on abstraction, being verbs, long sentences or specialist vocabulary.

### S09-M12 — ACTIVE EVENT / STATIC DRIFT DIAGNOSTIC
`WHAT IS THE READER SUPPOSED TO TRACK? -> WHO/WHAT ACTS? -> DOES THE VERB CLARIFY THAT ACTION/STATE?`
Use active verbs when they sharpen agency, causality or movement. Do not ban passive voice or `to be`; both can be correct when receiver/state/identity is the point.

### S09-M13 — TEMPORAL FORWARD PRESSURE
Past material earns space when it changes present interpretation, relationship, evidence or action. Past perfect is a temporal-routing tool, not a defect category.

### S09-M14 — PRONOUN PROVENANCE / REFERENT CLARITY
`PRONOUN -> UNAMBIGUOUS REFERENT -> SOURCE/AGENT TRACKING -> READER MODEL`.
Particularly important in ensemble, mystery and institutional prose where a vague `they/it/this` can conceal who knew, decided or acted.

### S09-M15 — MODIFIER FUNCTION TEST
An adjective/adverb earns its place when it changes meaning, POV attitude, relationship, rhythm or precision. If it merely repeats the noun/verb's existing meaning, test deletion or replacement by action.

### S09-M16 — CONNOTATION / LOADED-LANGUAGE GATE
`DENOTATION -> CONNOTATION -> WHO USES THIS WORD? -> WHAT EVALUATION DOES IT SMUGGLE IN?`
Narrator, institution, school and character labels must not silently become neutral world facts.

### S09-M17 — SYNTACTIC VARIATION BY EFFECT
Vary length/form/order when reader effect requires it. Reject both monotonous same-shape prose and conspicuous syntactic acrobatics with no story function.

### S09-M18 — CONTENT LOAD > SENTENCE LENGTH
If a sentence is hard to follow, diagnose competing logical jobs, subject/verb separation and reader-state transitions before using arbitrary word-count rules.

### S09-M19 — INTENTIONAL REPETITION VS ACCIDENT
`REPETITION -> NEW EMPHASIS/MEANING/RHYTHM? -> KEEP; OTHERWISE NOISE`.
Developing repetition is distinct from inadvertent lexical echo.

### S09-M20 — RULE-BREAKING INTENT / FRAGMENT GATE
Fragments, one-word paragraphs and nonstandard syntax are legitimate when context makes meaning clear and the break creates a specific effect. Repeated accidental fragmentation without contrast/function remains a defect.

### S09-M21 — BASIC ORIENTATION CLARITY
Mystery may withhold answers; prose should not accidentally withhold basic syntax, referents or causal orientation. Reader rereading caused by deliberate ambiguity differs from rereading caused by unclear construction.

### S09-M22 — EVALUATION IS RELATIONAL
`SPECIFIC EVENT/STATE -> PARTICULAR PERSON/AGENT -> YARDSTICK/VALUES/GOAL -> MEANING -> RESPONSE`.
A technology, rule, event or institution is not narratively `good/bad` in the abstract. Show who benefits, who pays, who judges, and by what standard.

## CROSS-SOURCE NOTES
- **Lamott / LaPlante:** strong convergence on separating generation from criticism/revision.
- **Bell:** Bell inherits Swain's practical-tool orientation; Swain himself repeatedly frames craft as usable technique rather than immutable law.
- **Bernays/Painter / LaPlante:** correct Swain's sweeping dismissal of exercises; targeted exercises can be controlled diagnostic/generative probes.
- **Francine Prose / Le Guin / Lodge / Zinsser / Clark / Pushman:** strong convergence against sentence-length dogma and against banning fragments as a class. Swain explicitly says simplicity/brevity are conditional and that vividness can outrank brevity.
- **Le Guin / Clark / Williams-Bizup / Pinker:** correct Swain's strongest prescriptions against `to be`, passive voice, past perfect and modifiers. Synthesis is function/clarity/agency, not grammatical purge.
- **Zinsser:** useful productive tension. Zinsser attacks clutter; Swain rejects primer-level simplicity. Synthesis: remove nonfunctional material, not complexity required for exact experience.
- **Fludernik / Chatman:** Swain's arrangement layer maps to discourse order: changing order changes reader interpretation without changing world facts.
- **Zunshine / Herman:** necessary epistemic correction: discourse order, connotation and plausible interpretation must not silently upgrade hypothesis/judgment into fact.
- **Wolf:** world database may be huge; Selection determines the much smaller reader-facing subset.
- **Roy Peter Clark / Le Guin:** deliberate repetition can create rhythm/structure; accidental repetition is noise.

## IVDIVO PROVISIONAL APPLICATION
### 100-SPECIALIST WRITERS' ROOM
Use staged passes rather than 100 simultaneous blockers:
`DISCOVERY -> STORY/CAUSALITY -> CHARACTER/PERSONHOOD -> SCENE -> POV/EVIDENCE -> DIALOGUE -> PROSE/RHYTHM -> READER TEST`.

### ANTI-FRAGMENTATION / LINE EDIT
Current router is reinforced:
`ONE MAIN STORY / CAUSALITY -> SCENE NECESSITY -> ORDER/DURATION/FREQUENCY -> SHOW/TELL -> POV/FOCALIZATION -> DIALOGUE MODE -> PARAGRAPH INTENT -> READER-STATE CONTINUITY -> SENTENCE RHYTHM -> WORDING`.
Do not optimize short-sentence percentage. A fragment/one-line paragraph is valid only when it earns contrast, emphasis, voice, timing, fracture, revelation or pause. A long sentence/paragraph may be the correct carrier for accumulation, continuous perception, technical reasoning or emotional pressure.

### INSTITUTIONAL LANGUAGE / POST-MONEY WORLD
For words such as `allocation`, `priority`, `assignment`, `qualification`, `contribution`, `access`, `service`:
`DENOTATION -> REAL MECHANISM -> WHO DECIDES -> WHO CAN REFUSE/APPEAL -> WHO BEARS COST -> CONNOTATION`.
Do not let neutral-looking vocabulary smuggle salary/rent/credits or purchasing hierarchy back into post-money settings.

### LESSON ZERO BOOK ONE
No reason to reopen development lock from Swain Ch2. New language mechanisms apply only if a concrete current prose defect is found.

### ORBITAL / POST-MONEY WORLD
Show post-money normality through selected lived details, not explanatory lectures. The same city rule can be beneficial to one person and burdensome to another; narratively evaluate it through specific people/yardsticks rather than abstract author verdicts.

### CONSCIOUS ROBOTS
Robot POV selection must follow the robot's embodiment, knowledge, relationships and present want. Human labels such as `efficient`, `obedient`, `erratic`, `safe` or `helpful` are connotative evaluations, not proof of robot motive or personhood. Robot evaluation of the same event may use a different yardstick.

### CONSCIOUSNESS SCHOOLS
Specialist vocabulary can be precise within a school without becoming world ontology. Preserve:
`PRACTITIONER OBSERVATION -> REPRODUCIBLE EFFECT -> SCHOOL INTERPRETATION -> COMPETING INTERPRETATION -> POSSIBLE ONTOLOGICAL CLAIM`.
Loaded labels such as `higher`, `blocked`, `pure`, `lower`, `advanced` must be attributed to a speaker/school unless independently established.

### MYSTERY / EVIDENCE
Because arrangement and connotation change inference, every reordered clue/reveal should preserve provenance:
`WORLD FACT -> TRACE/SOURCE -> WORDING/FRAME -> POV INTERPRETATION -> DISCOURSE ORDER -> READER MODEL`.
Suspense may change when/how a fact is revealed; it may not change what the fact actually proves.
