## PURPOSE

## CORE SYNTHESIS

### 1. Dialogue is behavior
A line is not justified by realism or wit. Ask what the speaker is trying to cause.

`WANT → TACTIC → WORDING/BEHAVIOR → REACTION → TACTIC CHANGE → STATE CHANGE`.

### 2. The exact-words threshold
Direct dialogue is expensive. Exact words earn space when wording itself changes:
- consent;
- trust;
- status;
- power;
- evidence interpretation;
- relationship;
- public reputation;
- decision;
- comic timing;
- emotional state.

Routine/procedural/repeated exchanges are candidates for indirect dialogue or summary.

### 3. Repetition is functional, not lexical
Changing synonyms does not create a new beat. Progress requires changed tactic, evidence, leverage, relationship, emotion, or objective.

### 4. Quiet scenes are real scenes
A scene can progress through confession, evasion, invitation, mirroring, silence, touch, joke, restraint, or acceptance. Volume is not causality.

### 5. Character voice is relational
Do not assign one permanent speech costume. A person speaks differently with a friend, parent, mentor, subordinate, romantic interest, public authority, stranger, or conscious robot.

### 6. Third-things create indirect pressure
Characters often speak through a task, object, institution, cultural reference, food, technology, sport, music, route or shared artifact. This can carry romance and ideological pressure without explicit declaration.

### 7. Silence has a cost
Pauses and silence lose effect if overused. They must perform action: refuse, absorb, invite, intimidate, protect, reconsider, signal consent/non-consent, or shift attention.

### 8. Sentence design is an effect tool
McKee's suspense/cumulative/balanced distinction is useful, but permanent suspense sentences become artificial. Use syntax by reader effect.

### 9. Dialogue repair starts below language
If a scene is false, first test:
`scene purpose → motive → relationship → obstacle → evidence → tactic → changed state`.
Only then rewrite phrasing.

## IVDIVO FAILURE MODES
- five-person position panel;
- all teens share one sarcastic voice;
- smart robot speaks like interface help text;
- specialist school members all speak as omniscient world bible;
- exposition characters already know;
- exact quotes for routine logistics;
- pause/ellipsis on every meaningful line;
- short sentence = suspense assumption;
- narrator explains subtext after reader already understood;
- repeated arguing with altered wording;
- hidden trauma invented solely to create subtext;
- scene repaired by paraphrase instead of causal redesign.

## NEW PRODUCTION GATES B48

**B48-G01 — VERBAL ACTION:** What effect is this speaker trying to produce now?

**B48-G02 — LISTENER MODEL:** What does the speaker believe the listener knows/wants/fears?

**B48-G03 — REACTION CAUSALITY:** The reaction must cause the next tactic or end the exchange.

**B48-G04 — EXACT-WORDS THRESHOLD:** If wording does not matter, summarize/indirect.

**B48-G05 — TACTIC ROTATION:** Same tactic repeated with new wording = repetition.

**B48-G06 — QUIET STATE CHANGE:** Quiet scene passes if relation/knowledge/consent/plan/emotion changes.

**B48-G07 — RELATIONAL VOICE:** Voice varies by relationship without losing identity.

**B48-G08 — NO UNIVERSAL WIT:** Cleverness must belong to a specific character mechanism.

**B48-G09 — THIRD-THING INFERENCE:** Indirection must remain readable and causally useful.

**B48-G10 — SILENCE ACTION:** Pause/silence needs a function.

**B48-G11 — SOURCE ATTRIBUTION:** Spoken claim remains a claim until verified.

**B48-G12 — ROBOT PERSONHOOD:** Conscious robot has independent goal/tactic/relationship/right to refuse.

**B48-G13 — POST-MONEY REGISTER:** No casual wages/rent/credits as ordinary orbital motivation.

**B48-G14 — SPECIALIST CONFIDENCE:** Separate observation/practice result/interpretation/lineage claim.

**B48-G15 — STRUCTURE FIRST:** Do not line-edit a structurally dead dialogue scene.

**B48-G16 — RHYTHM RANGE:** Fragments, simple lines, long reflective sentences and silence are contrast tools, not quotas.

## PRODUCTION CONSEQUENCE
For dialogue-heavy chapters, do not begin with dialogue polish. Build a compact scene ledger:

`SCENE OWNER | WANT | LISTENER | LISTENER MODEL | TACTIC 1 | REACTION | TACTIC 2 | EVIDENCE/LEVERAGE SHIFT | STATE CHANGE | DIRECT/INDIRECT ROUTING`.

Only after this passes should line-level voice/rhythm work begin.
