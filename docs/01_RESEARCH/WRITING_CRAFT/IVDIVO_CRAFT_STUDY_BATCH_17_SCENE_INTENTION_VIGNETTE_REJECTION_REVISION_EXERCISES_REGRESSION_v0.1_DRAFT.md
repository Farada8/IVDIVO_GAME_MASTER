## Mechanism A — scene intention is downstream of story causality

Rosenfeld separates motivation from intention. Motivation explains why a character wants to act; intention is the immediate planned action in the scene. Crucially, scene intentions should arise from the inciting situation and consequences of previous scenes rather than being invented locally.

IVDIVO transfer:

`PRIOR CONSEQUENCE → CHARACTER STATE/MOTIVATION → IMMEDIATE INTENTION → RESISTANCE/COMPLICATION → RESULT → NEW CONSEQUENCE → NEXT INTENTION`

This is stronger than the weak template `GOAL → CONFLICT → DISASTER` because the emphasis is not on manufacturing disaster; it is on causal inheritance and state change.

### Failure modes

1. `SCENE HAS ACTIVITY BUT NO INTENTION` → pleasant or atmospheric vignette.
2. `INTENTION HAS NO PRIOR CAUSE` → chapter feels episodic/arbitrary.
3. `INTENTION SUCCEEDS TOO EASILY` → low dramatic pressure.
4. `INTENTION IS BLOCKED FOREVER` → authorial stalling.
5. `SCENE RESULT DOES NOT CREATE A NEW STATE` → story does not accumulate.

## Mechanism B — vignettes can be well written and still be structurally dead

Rosenfeld's revision section explicitly warns against scenes that are competent on the page but do not contribute to plot. A scene must be assessed on three scales:

`LOCAL EFFECTIVENESS`
`+ CONTRIBUTION TO ADJACENT SCENES`
`+ CONTRIBUTION TO WHOLE STORYLINE`.

IVDIVO diagnostic:

`IF THIS SCENE VANISHES, WHAT FUTURE DECISION/EVENT/RELATIONSHIP/KNOWLEDGE STATE BECOMES IMPOSSIBLE OR UNJUSTIFIED?`

If answer = only “we lose some flavor/information,” scene is a compression/merge candidate unless the flavor itself is an intentional attachment or tone function.

## Mechanism C — not every scene needs the same ingredient ratio

Rosenfeld treats momentum, plot information, tension, setting, POV, imagery, dialogue and narrative summary as variable ingredients. Some scenes can be quiet or contemplative; the scene test is purposeful movement, not mandatory visible action.

IVDIVO consequence: no universal quota for dialogue, action, sensory detail or sentence length.

## Mechanism D — line symptom may indicate deeper rendering problem

LaPlante's revision diagnostics include highlighting weak verb constructions, abstractions/generalities, imagery, metaphors/similes and reading aloud. Important operational point: a clumsy sentence is sometimes evidence of an imprecisely conceived image/action, not merely bad wording.

IVDIVO rule:

`LINE PROBLEM → ASK WHETHER IMAGE/ACTION/POV/EMOTION IS ACTUALLY UNDER-SPECIFIED → RECONCEIVE IF NECESSARY → THEN REWRITE LINE`.

This blocks cosmetic “AI polish” that preserves vague thinking.

## Mechanism E — revision by transformation, not only correction

LaPlante recommends controlled experiments: change POV, tense, setting, narrator, distance; rewrite scene as narrative or narrative as scene; describe what will NOT happen; expand physical reality; research missing real-world facts.

The purpose is diagnostic discovery, not automatically keeping the transformed version.

IVDIVO REVISION LAB:

For a scene that repeatedly fails after normal edits, run 2–3 temporary transformations:

- POV swap;
- scene ↔ summary swap;
- time-distance change;
- setting change;
- antagonist/secondary-character retelling;
- concrete-research enrichment.

Then extract what the experiment reveals and rewrite the canonical version from that knowledge.

### Failure mode

Treating exercise output as production prose without reevaluating story function.

# 3. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

Rosenfeld + LaPlante converge on a stronger revision hierarchy:

`WHOSE STORY STATE?`
→ `WHAT DOES THE CHARACTER NOW INTEND?`
→ `WHY NOW?`
→ `WHAT RESISTS/COMPLICATES?`
→ `WHAT CHANGES?`
→ `WHAT MUST HAPPEN NEXT BECAUSE OF IT?`
→ only then `POV / SCENE-SUMMARY / DETAIL / DIALOGUE / LINE / RHYTHM`.

This directly reinforces the IVDIVO rule that prose polish cannot rescue a scene whose causal function is wrong.

# 5. NEW PRODUCTION GATES

## Gate FD — Prior-Consequence Intention
Every major scene intention must have an identifiable cause in prior story state, character history or current pressure.

## Gate FE — Scene Result State Change
A scene must leave at least one consequential state changed: action option, knowledge, relationship, authority, cost, risk, desire or plan.

## Gate FF — Vignette Rejection
A scene cannot survive solely because it is vivid, funny, atmospheric or well written. It needs a whole-story function or a deliberate attachment/tone function that cannot be compressed without loss.

## Gate FG — Adjacent-Scene Coupling
Scene assessment must test backward inheritance and forward consequence, not local quality alone.

## Gate FH — Ingredient Variability
No mandatory fixed ratio of dialogue/action/summary/detail. Function determines mix.

## Gate FI — Line-Symptom Escalation
When a sentence repeatedly resists repair, test whether the underlying image/action/emotion/POV is vague or misconceived before further wording edits.

## Gate FJ — Transformational Revision Lab
For stubborn scenes, temporary POV/tense/scene-summary/setting transformations may be used diagnostically; exercise output is not automatically production text.

## Gate FK — Reveal Job Ownership
Each planned reveal has a chapter/scene owner. Earlier scenes may seed or suspect it but may not spend the owned reveal unless architecture is deliberately revised.

## Gate FL — Suspicion/Recognition Separation
`I notice a feature` ≠ `I recognize a historical match` ≠ `I establish structural similarity` ≠ `I establish lineage`.

## Gate FM — Regression Against Accepted Patch
Every newly assembled master must be checked against previously accepted structural patches; later assembly cannot silently reintroduce a known defect.
