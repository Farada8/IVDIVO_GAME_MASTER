## 1. Jordan Rosenfeld — scene architecture
The revised *Make a Scene* treats scene construction as a system rather than a single conflict formula. A scene combines protagonist/allies/opposition, POV, momentum, new plot information, tension, setting, imagery, and selective summary. Crucially, Rosenfeld emphasizes that scenes must weave together: new information and decisions are usually consequences of prior scenes, and scene intentions create purposeful action.

### Mechanism A — Scene Handoff
A strong scene ending should not merely stop at a clever line. It should change the state from which the next scene begins.

`SCENE INTENTION → COMPLICATION → RESULT → NEW INFORMATION / COST / QUESTION → NEXT INTENTION`

This means a chapter-ending hook is strongest when it is also causal infrastructure.

### Failure modes
- `punchline ending with no state change`;
- `mystery tease unrelated to next action`;
- `chapter transition that resets desire instead of carrying consequence`;
- `every scene ending as cliffhanger`, which flattens rhythm and makes quieter resolutions impossible.

## 2. James Scott Bell — ending resonance and payoff
Bell's useful contribution is not a compulsory outline formula but a payoff discipline: readers judge an ending by whether the final state feels earned by what came before. Twists or mysteries that are easy to invent but cannot be justified later create debt rather than suspense. He also contrasts discovery-first and planned-ending methods and argues for at least an ending direction that can guide setup and revision.

### Mechanism B — Backward Resonance
For each major ending/reveal:

`FINAL EFFECT → REQUIRED PRIOR CONDITIONS → EARLIER PLANTS → INTERMEDIATE REINTERPRETATIONS → CLIMACTIC CHOICE → AFTERMATH`

The final page should change the meaning of earlier material without invalidating it.

### Failure modes
- deus-ex-machina rescue;
- oral answer-key ending;
- surprise without prior support;
- serial hook that reopens the book's already closed main conflict;
- final revelation larger than the evidentiary ceiling created by the novel.

## 3. Cross-source synthesis — Reveal Ownership
A story with layered mystery needs explicit ownership of each reveal. If Chapter 7 is supposed to perform `first controlled historical comparison`, Chapter 3 may plant the anomaly but must not perform the comparison already.

### Reveal Ownership Record
`REVEAL ID | OWNER CHAPTER | PRE-OWNER PLANTS ALLOWED | EXACT CLAIM ALLOWED | CLAIMS FORBIDDEN | ACTION CAUSED | LATER PAYOFF`

This is stricter than a clue ledger. A clue ledger tracks information; reveal ownership tracks **dramatic work allocation**.

## 4. New production gates

### Gate FW — Scene Handoff Causality
Every major scene/chapter ending must leave a changed state that materially shapes the next intention, unless deliberate closure/rest is the function.

### Gate FX — Hook Must Carry Story
A hook cannot exist only to make the reader turn the page; it must also alter knowledge, desire, danger, relationship, cost, or available action.

### Gate FY — Reveal Ownership
Each major reveal has one primary owner scene/chapter. Earlier scenes may plant or suspect but may not spend the owned proof-level.

### Gate FZ — Pre-Reveal Vocabulary Ceiling
Before a reveal's owner chapter, dialogue/narration must not use language that effectively states the later conclusion in softer wording.

### Gate GA — Consequence Bridge
If Chapter N ends with new authority, evidence or risk, Chapter N+1 must begin from the changed practical/emotional state rather than unrelated setup.

### Gate GB — Resonant Return
Repeated image, phrase, object or decision pattern earns late return only if its meaning has changed through character/plot development.

### Gate GC — Ending Evidence Ceiling
Final claims cannot exceed what the accumulated evidence supports.

### Gate GD — No Answer-Key Ending
Do not resolve complex mystery primarily through one character explaining the entire hidden truth.

### Gate GE — Serial Hook Separation
The sequel hook may open a new/higher-order question only after the book's central action has reached a local result.

### Gate GF — Quiet Ending Permission
Not every chapter must end in danger or shock. A resolved emotional beat, altered relationship, earned uncertainty, or changed permission can be a valid handoff.

1. Ethan uses bounded authority correctly and feels its weight;
