## 1. Source role

Hodgins is most useful to IVDIVO as an integration-oriented craft source. His strongest contribution is not a proprietary beat sheet but a set of questions that force scene, character, setting, POV, voice, pace, dialogue, style, theme and unity to reinforce one another.

The source is therefore best routed to:
- scene selection;
- physical + social setting;
- character-through-environment;
- dialogue multifunction;
- limited/close third POV diagnostics;
- deep revision and re-imagination;
- whole-chapter integrity audits.

## 2. Scene vs summary selection

Hodgins distinguishes full scene from summary/half-scene according to what the reader must experience directly. A full scene is justified when an encounter matters enough that we need proximity: character is revealed, plot changes or advances, and power/desire shifts among participants. Summary is appropriate when events over time matter but their full moment-by-moment enactment does not.

### IVDIVO mechanism

`EVENT MATERIAL → DOES EXACT BEHAVIOR / WORDING / POWER SHIFT MATTER?`

If YES:
`SCENE → competing wants → exchange → shift → consequence`.

If NO:
`SUMMARY / HALF-SCENE → retain causal facts + character pressure → leap to next live decision`.

### Failure modes

1. **Camera addiction:** dramatizing every arrival, briefing, walk, log review and routine exchange.
2. **Summary starvation:** compressing the very confrontation in which consent, authority, interpretation or relationship actually changes.
3. **False intensity:** calling a scene dramatic merely because voices are raised; the meaningful test is whether the state changes.

## 3. Plot as obstacle + character revelation, not merely event escalation

Hodgins asks whether deterrents become more difficult, whether stakes increase, and—critically—whether each obstacle provokes a response that reveals something new about the protagonist rather than eliciting the same response repeatedly.

### IVDIVO obstacle-differentiation test

For every major obstruction:

`OBSTACLE → WHAT NEW PART OF CHARACTER DOES THIS FORCE? → DIFFERENT TACTIC? → DIFFERENT COST? → DIFFERENT RELATIONSHIP EFFECT?`

If five successive obstacles all produce `Ethan organizes harder`, the plot is moving while the character is static.

For Book Two the target progression is instead:

`authority offered → restraint`;
`system works → authorization`;
`burden discovered → doubt`;
`privacy blocks certainty → negotiated evidence`;
`fallback cost appears → bounded trade-off`;
`deadline → accountable decision`.

## 4. Non-villain opposition and internal struggle

Hodgins explicitly asks whether sufficient tension can exist without spectacular external events and whether a person/place/institution can embody the values of protagonist or antagonist positions.

This supports an IVDIVO rule already emerging from the manuscript: **opposition does not require a villain.** Institutions, procedures, time, incompatible legitimate duties, evidence standards and the protagonist's own value hierarchy can provide strong resistance.

### Book Two application

Dr Leena Voss should remain a strong good-faith opponent. Her strongest argument is not deception but that Lattice measurably prevents harm, public infrastructure necessarily distributes inconvenience, and shutting it down also harms people. The current architecture already protects this. The novel becomes weaker if Voss is converted into a secret manipulator merely to make Ethan's climax easier.

## 5. Setting as action system

Hodgins' setting questions explicitly include more than physical description: senses, how the character reacts to or acts upon the environment, peer groups and pressures, community standards, family values, historical period, and the essential narrative role of place.

### IVDIVO Setting Action Matrix

For every major arena:

`PHYSICAL CONSTRAINTS`
`+ SENSORY SIGNATURE`
`+ SOCIAL RULES`
`+ STATUS / ACCESS`
`+ PEER PRESSURE`
`+ FAMILY / COMMUNITY VALUES`
`+ TIME / TECHNOLOGY PERIOD`
`+ WHO CAN ACT WHERE`
`+ WHAT THE POV CHARACTER NOTICES`
`= STORY-PRODUCING SETTING`.

### Failure mode

Future architecture that is merely decorative produces travel-brochure SF. A location earns page space when it changes possible action, authority, information, cost or relationship.

## 6. POV and voice as authority allocation

Hodgins treats POV change as more than pronoun substitution. Changing first/third or narrative distance changes where narrative authority resides, how much vernacular belongs in the prose, what can be known, and how the reader judges the character.

He notes the value of close third's 'double vision': the narrative can stay near enough to share a character's perception while standing back just far enough for the reader to detect distortion, irony or humor.

### IVDIVO close-third double-vision model

`CHARACTER PERCEPTION`
`+ NARRATORIAL DISTANCE CONTROL`
`→ reader can inhabit the character without being forced to accept every interpretation as fact`.

This is highly relevant to Ethan. His prose should let readers enjoy his system-mindedness and competence while still noticing when he turns overview into entitlement, or later when he turns responsibility into a desire for a perfect decision.

### Failure mode

Authorial correction after every flawed Ethan interpretation destroys double vision. The reader should often infer the gap from behavior, consequence, another person's reply or the environment itself.

## 7. Dialogue as multifunction action

Hodgins frames dialogue as character evidence: what people say, how they say it, and what they avoid saying become information the reader stores. Dialogue can advance plot, reveal traits/emotion and transmit new information—but the strongest dialogue often does several of these simultaneously.

### IVDIVO multifunction dialogue gate

A significant dialogue exchange should ideally perform at least two of:
- pursue a want;
- change leverage;
- reveal character;
- alter relationship;
- introduce/reframe evidence;
- expose avoidance/subtext;
- trigger a decision;
- sharpen setting/status.

Routine dialogue that performs only logistics should be considered for indirect speech or summary.

## 8. Revision as re-imagination, not cosmetic correction

Hodgins' revision discussion distinguishes fixing lines from fully re-seeing a scene. His Faulkner example is used to argue that a POV/voice problem may require rewriting from a new imaginative position rather than mechanically editing pronouns and sentences.

### IVDIVO revision routing

`LINE DEFECT? → line edit`
`SCENE PURPOSE / POV / POWER / STAGING DEFECT? → re-imagine scene before prose`
`CHAPTER CAUSAL DEFECT? → rebuild sequence`
`BOOK ENGINE DEFECT? → development pass, no polishing first`.

### Failure mode

Using line editing to preserve a scene whose central imaginative decision is wrong.

## 9. Whole-integrity revision checklist

Hodgins' late revision checklist is unusually valuable because it treats elements as interdependent. For IVDIVO the adapted whole-integrity audit is:

### Character
Does the reader know enough to understand behavior at the decisive moment? Does behavior arise from this character rather than plot convenience?

### POV
Is the knowledge budget consistent? Is the selected distance doing work? Would another POV materially improve the causal/emotional problem—or merely add coverage?

### Voice
Does syntax, rhythm, vocabulary and attention pattern match the POV consciousness/narrative persona?

### Plot
Is there a causal relation among events? Does each event arise naturally from character + situation?

### Pace
Does the prose and scene allocation speed up/slow down according to function rather than default cadence?

### Dialogue
Do speakers sound distinct? Does important dialogue do multiple jobs?

### Style
Do sentence and paragraph decisions control reader movement intentionally? Are abstractions anchored in concrete perception/action where needed?

### Theme
Does the manuscript illuminate the concern through action and recurrence, or merely state it?

### Unity
Would removing a scene/passage make little difference? If yes, why is it still here? Does each scene perform multiple relevant functions?

### Integrity
Do all layers support a single coherent whole rather than feeling like separate craft exercises?

## 10. Cross-source comparison

### Hodgins + McKee
Converge on scene as change through competing desires/actions. Hodgins is less rigidly value-turn terminology driven and more tolerant of varied forms.

### Hodgins + Le Guin / Burroway
Important correction: conflict/war is not the only possible plot metaphor. Growth, illumination, connection and consensus can generate structure. For commercial YA/SF IVDIVO still needs strong forward pressure, but that pressure need not be villain-centered.

### Hodgins + Rosenfeld
Converge on scene intention, exchange and result. Rosenfeld offers tighter scene-type tools; Hodgins contributes stronger whole-story integration and setting/social milieu.

### Hodgins + LaPlante
Converge on deep revision as re-seeing, not simply polishing. Both warn indirectly against protecting wrong material because it is already well written.

### Hodgins + Chatman
Chatman's kernel/satellite distinction helps bound Hodgins' 'every scene contributes several things' principle: not every element must be causally indispensable, but even satellites need a justified experiential/character/thematic function.

## 11. New IVDIVO production gates

### JU — Scene Proximity Gate
Use full scene only when exact behavior, wording, perception or power shift deserves reader proximity.

### JV — Obstacle Differentiation Gate
Successive obstacles must force meaningfully different responses, tactics, costs or revelations—not repeat the protagonist's default move.

### JW — Non-Villain Opposition Gate
Do not invent a villain when incompatible legitimate goals, procedures, institutions, time or uncertainty can carry stronger opposition.

### JX — Setting Action Gate
A major setting must alter possible action, access, status, information, sensory experience or social pressure.

### JY — Social Milieu Gate
For YA, setting audit must include peers, family/community values, status rules and institutional norms, not only architecture.

### JZ — POV Authority Gate
Any POV change must specify what authority/knowledge/voice shifts with it; pronoun replacement is not a POV redesign.

### KA — Double-Vision Gate
Close-third prose should allow the reader to inhabit a character's interpretation while retaining evidence from which that interpretation can be judged.

### KB — Multifunction Dialogue Gate
Important dialogue should perform multiple story functions; routine single-function exchange is a compression candidate.

### KC — Re-Imagination Gate
When purpose/POV/staging is wrong, rewrite the scene from its imaginative foundation before line editing.

### KD — Whole-Integrity Gate
Final revision must test character, POV, voice, plot, pace, dialogue, style, theme, unity and interdependence as one system.

## 12. Manuscript validation — Book Two

### Finding 1 — Rivergate already passes physical setting, but social setting needs explicit expansion

Current Chapter 2 gives useful physical-functional detail: service entrance vs public glass, access credentials, variable-height handrails, refuge rooms, translation nodes and route-width changes. These are not random description; they express who the infrastructure assumes a person can be.

However, the 35-chapter architecture can gain more YA/social pressure if Rivergate also becomes a status arena:
- who is impressed by the Youth Cohort badge;
- who thinks the five are publicity mascots;
- what ordinary school peers think of their access;
- whether families trust or distrust predictive routing;
- whether youth participants are treated as real reviewers or decorative consultation;
- how adults behave differently when cameras/public comment are present.

This is not a request for extra subplots. It is a request to let the existing setting produce pressure.

### Finding 2 — Lattice conflict is strongest when redistribution replaces simple victory

The current training-view change from `CONFLICT RESOLVED` to `CONFLICT REDISTRIBUTED` is excellent. It expresses the book's actual system: the problem is not whether a conflict disappears but who absorbs cost. Protect this line/mechanism.

### Finding 3 — Voss should remain good-faith

The architecture explicitly states that Voss is not a villain and is often right. This aligns with the non-villain opposition model and should be protected across later drafting.

### Finding 4 — Ethan's close third has useful double vision

Chapter 2's `I like infrastructure` / STAFF ONLY exchange and his attraction to access he has not been offered lets the reader understand both the competence and the appetite for authority without narrator condemnation. Protect this mode. Do not repeatedly add authorial lines explaining that Ethan's impulse is problematic.

### Finding 5 — Chapter 3 carries a reveal-boundary risk

The current Chapter 3 material gives the reader actor-order / inferred-relation logic and enough memory pressure for Samir to recognize familiarity. But the formal Chapter 7 job is to establish the controlled historical resemblance. Therefore Ch3 must remain at `familiarity / anomaly / request lineage`, not become `side-by-side R-14 comparison / provenance discussion`.

This is both a mystery-ladder issue and a scene-function issue: Chapter 3's live dramatic job is **what Lattice thinks a person/relation is**, not historical proof.
