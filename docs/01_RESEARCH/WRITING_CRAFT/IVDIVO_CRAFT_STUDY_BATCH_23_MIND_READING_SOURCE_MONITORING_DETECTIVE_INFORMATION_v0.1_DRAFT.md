# 1. SOURCE PASSPORT

Zunshine asks how fiction engages our habitual attempts to infer minds from behavior and how readers keep track of the **source and reliability** of representations.

# 2. MIND-READING — BEHAVIOR INVITES MENTAL-STATE INFERENCE

Zunshine's starting point is that readers routinely interpret observable behavior as evidence of thoughts, feelings, beliefs, desires and intentions. This often feels automatic, but the inference remains an inference.

## IVDIVO translation

For important human evidence, separate:

`OBSERVED BEHAVIOR`
→ `INFERRED MENTAL STATE`
→ `SOURCE OF INFERENCE`
→ `ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION`
→ `CONFIDENCE`
→ `WHAT NEW EVIDENCE COULD REVISE IT`.

### Failure mode

A manuscript becomes cognitively flat when narration collapses behavior and motive into one unquestionable fact.

Example of weak version:

`Maya crossed her arms because she distrusted Voss.`

Stronger dramatic possibility:

`Maya crossed her arms.`
Other characters/readers infer distrust, defensiveness, impatience or simple discomfort from context, unless POV has legitimate access to more.

# 3. METAREPRESENTATION — INFORMATION STORED UNDER ADVISEMENT

A high-value Zunshine concept is metarepresentation: information can be held with an implicit source tag and provisional status rather than stored as raw truth.

Example structure:

`"THE INSPECTOR THINKS X"`

is not equivalent to

`X IS TRUE`.

The former can be revised when new evidence arrives.

## IVDIVO Source-Tag Model

Every important mystery datum should be representable as:

`CONTENT`
`SOURCE`
`ACCESS PATH`
`OBSERVATION / REPORT / INFERENCE / MODEL OUTPUT / MEMORY`
`CONFIDENCE`
`BIAS / INCENTIVE`
`CORROBORATION`
`CURRENT STATUS`.

### Production consequence

Do not write a clue ledger that only records *what the clue says*. Record **who/what produced it and at which epistemic level**.

# 4. READER DOUBT REDIRECTS ATTENTION TO SOURCE

Once a reader receives a good reason to distrust a representation, attention shifts from only `WHAT HAPPENED?` to:

`WHO IS TELLING / SHOWING ME THIS?`
`WHY THIS VERSION?`
`WHAT IS OMITTED?`
`WHAT ELSE DOES THIS SOURCE CONTROL?`

This creates a powerful rereading engine.

## IVDIVO Suspicion Pivot

A source should become suspect through **specific contradiction, omission, incentive or provenance problem**, not because the author arbitrarily announces unreliability.

After the pivot, previously planted details should support reinspection.

`SOURCE TRUST`
→ `DISCREPANCY`
→ `SOURCE BECOMES OBJECT OF INQUIRY`
→ `OLD CLUES RE-READ`
→ `NEW MODEL`.

# 5. DETECTIVE INFORMATION — STRONG VS WEAK SOURCE TAGS

Zunshine notes that detective fiction encourages unusually strong provisional tagging: almost every attribution about motive or truth may need to remain revisable.

This should not be interpreted as `trust nothing`.

## IVDIVO Confidence Ladder

Use graded states rather than binary true/false:

`OBSERVED`
`CORROBORATED`
`LIKELY`
`PLAUSIBLE`
`CONTESTED`
`SOURCE-DEPENDENT`
`DISPROVED`
`UNKNOWN`.

### Failure mode

If every line is equally uncertain, uncertainty itself stops being informative and readers lose a stable platform for reasoning.

---

# 6. ONE LIAR IS EXPENSIVE — DECEPTION HAS COGNITIVE COST

Zunshine's detective discussion treats proliferating possible liars as cognitively demanding because readers must maintain multiple source-tagged models simultaneously.

## IVDIVO Deception Budget

Do not assume that making **more people secretly deceptive** automatically creates more mystery.

For every active deceptive source:

`WHAT FALSE / PARTIAL MODEL MUST READER TRACK?`
`WHAT TRUE MODEL EXISTS UNDER IT?`
`WHAT OTHER SOURCES INTERACT WITH BOTH?`
`WHAT PAYOFF JUSTIFIES THE LOAD?`

### Production gate

If removing one liar/deceptive layer preserves the mystery while reducing tracking burden, removal may strengthen the book.

This is especially relevant to YA ensemble fiction, where relationship tracking already consumes reader bandwidth.

# 7. MATERIAL CLUES ARE NOT INDEPENDENT OF MIND-READING

Zunshine argues that physical clues matter in detective fiction because they are used to reconstruct intentions, plans, beliefs and actions. A clue is not narratively meaningful simply because it is an object or trace.

## IVDIVO Clue Interpretation Chain

`MATERIAL / DIGITAL TRACE`
→ `WHO COULD HAVE PRODUCED IT`
→ `UNDER WHAT CONDITIONS`
→ `WHAT ACTION IT IMPLIES`
→ `WHAT MENTAL / OPERATIONAL MODEL WOULD MAKE THAT ACTION LIKELY`
→ `ALTERNATIVE MODEL`
→ `TEST`.

This reinforces the existing rule:

`CLUE ≠ INTERPRETATION ≠ PROOF`.

### Book Two application

An actor-order transition in Lattice is not itself proof of common origin. It becomes a clue only through comparison, provenance, alternative implementations and later counterevidence.

---

# 8. MIND-READING IS EQUAL-OPPORTUNITY

A useful detective-fiction lesson is that no role should receive automatic immunity from concealment, misreading or mistaken inference merely because of narrative convention.

For IVDIVO, however, this is **not permission for arbitrary betrayal twists**.

## Bounded application

- protagonist can misread;
- mentor can misread;
- institution can correctly report facts while misinterpreting them;
- antagonist can infer protagonist accurately;
- model can predict behavior accurately while misclassifying reason/entitlement.

### Failure mode

If every trusted role is secretly false, relationship investment becomes strategically irrational for the reader.

---

# 9. DETECTIVE POV — CLOSING THE MIND REQUIRES FAIRNESS

Zunshine notes a recurring detective strategy: writers may allow readers substantial access to the investigator's mind, then strategically close access at certain moments to preserve surprise.

## IVDIVO Fair Access Rule

If a POV character discovers a decisive clue while the reader is inside that POV, withholding the **existence** of the discovery merely to preserve a twist is dangerous.

Acceptable forms:
- character sees something but does not yet know its significance;
- character forms a suspicion but refuses to state it before testing;
- scene ends before analysis if the transition is natural;
- reader is told the character has noticed something but not given a false claim that nothing was noticed.

Unfair form:
- interior POV knows the answer and narration simply pretends the thought did not occur.

# 10. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

## Zunshine + Oatley
Oatley: readers simulate character goals/plans and infer states beneath behavior.  
Zunshine: readers additionally track **whose representation** of those states they are using and with what reliability.

**Synthesis:**

`CHARACTER MODEL`
needs both
`MENTAL-STATE INFERENCE`
and
`SOURCE TAG`.

## Zunshine + Herman
Herman: action understanding requires reason attribution but reason attribution is provisional and person-level.  
Zunshine: source-monitoring prevents inferred reasons from becoming untagged truth.

**Synthesis:** every major reason claim should preserve provenance and revisability.

## Zunshine + McKee / mystery craft
McKee/Bell: revelations must change action and be causally earned.  
Zunshine: a revelation often works by changing the **status/source interpretation** of information already present.

**Synthesis:** high-value reveal can be `same evidence, new source model`, not only `new secret fact`.

## Zunshine + Brody YA mystery
Brody: YA investigator must be personally connected and have something at stake.  
Zunshine: detective engagement depends on active source/mental-state tracking.

**Synthesis:** the YA investigation should alter relationships and trust, not remain an abstract puzzle.

# 11. MANUSCRIPT VALIDATION — BOOK TWO / LATTICE

## Supported A — declared vs inferred relation is already a source-tag problem

Chapter 3 distinguishes declared companion relations from inferred `probable companion continuity`. This is exactly the kind of epistemic tagging the novel should continue to foreground.

The key thematic error would be:

`MODEL INFERS RELATION`
→ `SYSTEM / PEOPLE TALK AS IF RELATION WAS DECLARED FACT`.

## Supported B — Voss is stronger as a mostly reliable source

Do **not** turn Voss into a conventional liar merely to make the mystery twistier. Her professional openness creates a more interesting problem: good-faith competent people can inherit a design assumption they do not fully understand.

That preserves cognitive room for provenance mystery without multiplying unnecessary deceptive actors.

## Supported C — Aoife is central to source-monitoring

Her function should extend beyond `records things`. She should repeatedly distinguish:

- raw footage;
- edited clip;
- caption/context;
- participant account;
- institutional summary;
- model inference;
- public interpretation.

This can make her plot-essential rather than merely observational.

## Defect / risk D — source levels can blur in future provenance discussion

The Book Two mystery must not allow dialogue such as:

`"R-14 has the same structure, so someone copied it."`

without intermediate tags.

Required chain:

`Samir notices resemblance`
→ `controlled structural comparison`
→ `specific rare operator match`
→ `documented lineage checked`
→ `alternative convergence considered`
→ `second independent occurrence`
→ `only then stronger origin hypothesis`.

## New application artifact

Create/maintain a Book Two **Source-Monitoring & Inference Ledger** for major clues, with source, epistemic class, confidence, alternative explanation and action consequence.

# 12. NEW PRODUCTION GATES

### Gate JG — Source Tag Survival
Every major clue/claim must retain who or what generated it and whether it is observation, report, inference, model output, memory or interpretation.

### Gate JH — Inference Is Not Observation
Narration and dialogue must not silently upgrade a character inference into an observed fact.

### Gate JI — Suspicion Pivot
A source becomes suspect only through concrete discrepancy/incentive/provenance evidence, after which earlier cues should support rereading.

### Gate JJ — Uncertainty Has Levels
Use graded evidence states; do not make every claim equally dubious.

### Gate JK — Deception Budget
Every active liar or strategic concealment layer must justify its reader-tracking cost with structural payoff.

### Gate JL — Stable Reality Floor
Even mystery-heavy narratives need enough trusted observations for the reader to reason rather than merely wait for authorial correction.

### Gate JM — Material Clue Interpretation
Physical/digital evidence becomes plot-relevant through competing models of who produced it, why and under what conditions.

### Gate JN — Mental-State Alternative
Important behavioral evidence should support at least one plausible alternative motive until evidence rules it out.

### Gate JO — No Role Immunity
Protagonist, mentor, institution and antagonist may all misinfer, but concealment must remain character/plot-caused rather than twist-caused.

### Gate JP — POV Discovery Fairness
Do not hide a decisive discovery from the reader through artificial suppression inside the discovering POV.

### Gate JQ — Same Evidence / New Source Reveal
Test whether a reveal can deepen by changing provenance/reliability/meaning of known evidence rather than adding another unrelated secret.

### Gate JR — Provenance Before Origin Claim
Similarity cannot be upgraded to common origin until lineage, convergence and independent-occurrence alternatives are tested.

### Gate JS — Context Is Evidence
For recordings, quotes, model outputs and reports, missing context must be treated as an epistemic variable, not decoration.

### Gate JT — Relationship Consequence of Suspicion
In YA ensemble mystery, suspicion should change trust, disclosure, alliance or action; otherwise it risks becoming puzzle-only cognitive load.
