# 1. BELL — PLOT & STRUCTURE

## 1.1 Plot must work for the reader

Bell frames plot as the mechanism that carries the reader through the story. Whatever planning method a writer uses, the finished manuscript must answer reader-facing questions:

`WHAT IS THIS STORY ABOUT? | IS SOMETHING HAPPENING? | WHY KEEP READING? | WHY CARE?`

### IVDIVO TEST — READER-FACING PLOT
At every 10–15% of the novel, a cold reader should be able to identify:
- current main objective;
- current obstacle;
- current stakes;
- open forward question.

If only lore has accumulated, the story engine is weak.

## 1.2 Plot and character are mutually dependent

Bell explicitly rejects a plot filled with cardboard people. His useful “bond” concept separates several reader connections: identification/empathy, sympathy, likability, inner conflict.

### IVDIVO READER-BOND TEST
The protagonist need not be nice, but early material should give at least two routes of investment:
- recognizable human pressure;
- vulnerability;
- competence/resourcefulness;
- unfair jeopardy;
- moral line;
- difficult inner conflict;
- relationship need.

Do not mechanically stack sympathy tricks; manipulation is a failure mode.

## 1.3 A teaser is a retention tool, not a substitute for causality

Bell allows a later high-charge scene to be previewed before returning to chronology. Its function is curiosity, not story repair.

### IVDIVO LIMIT
A teaser is allowed only when:
- it creates a meaningful question;
- later chronology pays it off;
- it does not hide the fact that the actual opening lacks a story disturbance;
- it does not reveal information the later mystery requires to remain unknown.

# 2. BELL — CONFLICT & SUSPENSE

## 2.1 Conflict is not merely fighting

Bell’s book spans conflict at multiple levels: overall story structure, POV, openings, middle movement, subplot/backstory, inner conflict, dialogue, theme, prose style, revision.

Operational synthesis:

`CHARACTER WANTS CHANGE + ANOTHER FORCE PREVENTS/ALTERS IT = CONFLICT`

The opposing force may be:
- person;
- institution;
- environment;
- law;
- time;
- scarce resource;
- relationship;
- self-contradiction;
- incompatible values.

## 2.2 Conflict must remain alive in the middle

The middle dies when the same obstacle repeats without changing the problem.

### IVDIVO MIDDLE-PRESSURE LADDER
Each major complication should alter at least one variable:
`ACCESS | TIME | COST | KNOWLEDGE | TRUST | STATUS | LEGALITY | TARGET | METHOD | MORAL ACCEPTABILITY`.

Escalation is therefore not “more danger” alone; it is narrowing or transforming the protagonist’s solution space.

## 2.3 Inner conflict becomes dramatic only when it changes behavior

An internal dilemma that remains private commentary does not automatically create plot.

### IVDIVO INNER-CONFLICT TEST
`COMPETING VALUES/WANTS → DECISION DELAY OR TACTIC CHANGE → EXTERNAL CONSEQUENCE`.

If inner conflict never changes action, it is characterization material, not yet dramatic machinery.

## 2.4 Suspense = future-oriented uncertainty plus consequence

Bell’s suspense section repeatedly centers the reader’s “What happens next?” state.

IVDIVO synthesis:

`VALUED OUTCOME + UNCERTAINTY + ANTICIPATED CONSEQUENCE + DELAY/OBSTACLE = SUSPENSE`

Mystery asks primarily **what/why happened**; suspense asks **what will happen and can it be prevented/achieved**. A novel can run both simultaneously.

### IVDIVO SUSPENSE CARD
`DESIRED/FEARED OUTCOME | PROBABILITY AS READER PERCEIVES IT | CLOCK/PRESSURE | WHAT CAN GO WRONG | INFORMATION READER HAS | DELAY MECHANISM | PAYOFF`.

## 2.5 Cliffhanger must arise from current causal pressure

A chapter ending is stronger when the new question is caused by what just happened, rather than by a random intrusion.

### IVDIVO CHAPTER-EXIT TEST
Preferred exits:
- irreversible decision;
- new threat caused by action;
- evidence that reclassifies the problem;
- relationship rupture;
- deadline change;
- discovery that forces immediate next action.

Random “and then someone screamed” endings are low-value unless causally earned.

## 2.6 Stretching tension requires controlled delay, not filler

Suspense can be increased by delaying an expected outcome, but every delay beat must add pressure, information, cost, or complication.

### IVDIVO DELAY TEST
A suspense delay is valid only if it changes at least one of:
`EXPECTATION | RISK | INFORMATION | CHARACTER STATE | TIME LEFT | AVAILABLE OPTION`.

Pure waiting is not tension.

# 3. KRESS — CHARACTERS, EMOTION & VIEWPOINT

## 3.1 Frustration is a plot-producing emotion

Kress makes an especially useful argument: desire begins motion, but **frustration**—not getting what one wants—generates plot because it demands a response.

Operational chain:

`WANT → BLOCK → FRUSTRATION → CHARACTER-SPECIFIC RESPONSE → ACTION → CONSEQUENCE → NEW BLOCK`.

### IVDIVO CHARACTER-CAUSALITY TEST
When a character is blocked, do not ask only “What happens next?” Ask:
`HOW DOES THIS PARTICULAR PERSON RESPOND TO FRUSTRATION?`

Their response should materially shape the next plot event.

## 3.2 Different responses to the same frustration create different plots

Kress demonstrates that revenge, resignation, persistence, self-blame, fear, bitterness, or strategic recalculation would produce different stories.

### IVDIVO STRESS PROFILE — ADD
For each core character define:
`DEFAULT FRUSTRATION RESPONSE | SECONDARY RESPONSE | BREAKING-POINT RESPONSE | ABILITY TO ADAPT RESPONSE`.

This prevents characters from becoming interchangeable functions.

## 3.3 Emotion must be dramatized for this character, not assumed universal

The writer’s emotional response is not automatically the reader’s or character’s response. The text must supply enough behavior/context for readers to understand why this person reacts this way.

### IVDIVO EMOTION-EVIDENCE TEST
For a major emotional turn show at least two of:
- pressure source;
- prior restraint/history;
- bodily response;
- perception change;
- speech/action change;
- decision consequence.

Do not rely only on emotion labels.

## 3.4 Breaking point requires accumulated pressure + prior control

Kress’s “breaking point” is strong because contrast gives release force. Continuous maximum emotion loses impact.

### IVDIVO EMOTIONAL-ACCUMULATION LEDGER
For a planned break:
`PRESSURE 1 → CONTROL | PRESSURE 2 → CONTROL COST | PRESSURE 3 → CRACK | FINAL TRIGGER → BREAK → ACTION CONSEQUENCE`.

A breakdown/release without accumulation reads as melodrama.

## 3.5 Emotional climax should propel action

The important point in Kress is that the breaking point is not merely expressive. It is especially dramatic when it causes action.

### IVDIVO RULE
`EMOTIONAL CHANGE → NEW ACTION` is stronger than `EMOTIONAL MONOLOGUE → STORY CONTINUES UNCHANGED`.

## 3.6 POV controls whose emotion the reader shares

Kress reinforces the POV findings already extracted from Rasley/Card: first person offers immediacy and individual language but constrains range; third person can vary distance; multiple POV increases scope but risks fragmentation; omniscient gains breadth at cost of some intimacy.

### IVDIVO SYNTHESIS
POV is an emotional allocation system:
`WHO GETS INTERIOR ACCESS → WHO RECEIVES READER EMPATHY → WHAT REMAINS EXTERNAL/AMBIGUOUS`.

# 4. PROVOST — MAKE YOUR WORDS WORK

## 4.1 Judge prose by function, not by decorative correctness

Provost’s central standard is pragmatic: writing “works” when it performs its intended job for the reader. Grammar and technique are tools, not ends.

### IVDIVO LINE TEST
For any sentence/paragraph ask:
`WHAT JOB IS THIS UNIT DOING?`
Possible jobs:
- orient;
- characterize;
- create image;
- accelerate;
- delay;
- imply;
- reveal evidence;
- produce emotion;
- establish voice;
- transition;
- pay off setup.

If the only answer is “sounds literary,” inspect for deletion or reassignment.

## 4.2 One concrete detail can perform multiple functions

Provost’s revolver example shows a detail can simultaneously be description, diction/style, and characterization.

### IVDIVO MULTIFUNCTION DETAIL TEST
Prefer details that do 2+ jobs:
`SENSORY REALITY + CHARACTER + TENSION + WORLD RULE + FORESHADOWING`.

Do not force multifunctionality on every noun; use it to reduce inert descriptive inventory.

## 4.3 Craft categories overlap

Style, dialogue, description, characterization, pace, viewpoint, tension, and credibility are not independent modules. A lexical choice can alter several simultaneously.

### Production implication
Specialist passes must not “optimize” one layer while damaging another. Any line-level change affecting factual meaning, character knowledge, clue strength, voice, or pacing needs regression against those layers.

## 4.4 Music is sentence architecture, not ornament

Provost’s separate chapter on “Music” supports Rosenfeld/Clark: sentence length, stress, sound, repetition, syntax, and variation alter reader experience.

### IVDIVO RHYTHM FUNCTION MAP
For a paragraph specify intended motion:
`STEADY | ACCELERATING | HALTING | ACCUMULATIVE | LYRICAL | BLUNT | DISORIENTED`.
Then inspect sentence architecture for whether it produces that effect.

## 4.5 Unity means one governing purpose

Provost’s “unity” category reinforces the founder’s main-story rule at smaller scale. A paragraph, scene, chapter, and book should not pull toward unrelated centers without hierarchy.

### IVDIVO UNITY TEST
For each unit state its governing purpose in one sentence. Secondary functions may exist, but must support or productively counterpoint the main one.

# 5. CLARK — WRITING TOOLS / HOW TO WRITE SHORT

## 5.1 Tools, not rules

Clark explicitly frames craft as cause/effect rather than right/wrong. This matches IVDIVO’s cross-school method: a tool has conditions and effects; exceptions are not automatically errors.

### IVDIVO TOOL RECORD
Every prose rule should be stored as:
`TOOL | EFFECT | WHEN USEFUL | OVERUSE FAILURE | COUNTER-EXAMPLE/EXCEPTION`.

## 5.2 Close reading should expose invisible machinery

Clark’s method parallels Pushman: find a passage that creates an effect, inspect syntax/rhetoric/structure, name the device, then abstract it into a tool.

This strengthens CRAFT-007’s reverse-engineering card.

## 5.3 Do not apply every tool while drafting

Clark warns that trying to consciously control dozens of tools simultaneously causes paralysis. Early writing can flow; specific tools are brought in during targeted revision.

### IVDIVO PASS DISCIPLINE
Do not ask a scene writer to simultaneously optimize 100 parameters consciously. Route them into ordered passes:
`STORY/SCENE CAUSALITY → POV/KNOWLEDGE → CHARACTER/RELATIONSHIP → DIALOGUE → DETAIL → RHYTHM → LINE CLARITY`.

The heavy framework stays backstage.

## 5.4 Word order creates emphasis

Clark’s “nuts and bolts” approach treats sentence positions as functional. Strong information can be placed where reader attention naturally lands, especially ends of sentences/paragraphs.

### IVDIVO EMPHASIS TEST
For important lines ask:
- what word/image/decision should resonate last?
- did a weak qualifier or logistical phrase accidentally occupy the strongest position?

## 5.5 Short writing increases the weight of every word

*How to Write Short* argues that compression magnifies stylistic choices. Focus, intelligence/wit, and polish matter more when space is restricted.

### IVDIVO APPLICATION
Use short-form discipline for:
- chapter-ending lines;
- clues;
- interface text;
- messages;
- vows/orders;
- memorable dialogue;
- premise/logline;
- scene exits.

A short line should not merely be short; it must carry disproportionate function.

## 5.6 Compression can add force, but overcompression destroys context

Clark’s broader point is not “shorter is always better.” Good short writing selects and concentrates.

### IVDIVO COMPRESSION TEST
Before cutting, identify what cannot be lost:
`CONTEXT | CAUSAL LINK | CHARACTER MOTIVE | RELATIONSHIP SUBTEXT | ORIENTATION | EMOTIONAL ACCUMULATION`.

Cut redundancy, not necessary meaning.

## 5.7 Contrast in sentence length is a rhetorical resource

Clark’s toolset and Provost’s music chapter converge: a short sentence has force partly because it is surrounded by longer structures. Uniform shortness removes contrast.

### IVDIVO ANTI-GPT CADENCE RULE
Do not produce page after page of:
`short statement. short statement. rhetorical fragment. blank line. short statement.`

Use sentence and paragraph length contrast according to scene function.

# 6. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

## 6.1 Frustration-to-plot engine

Kress + Bell + Storr/Egri from prior batches:

`WANT`
`→ BLOCK`
`→ FRUSTRATION`
`→ CHARACTER-SPECIFIC INTERPRETATION`
`→ TACTIC`
`→ OPPOSITION`
`→ CONSEQUENCE`
`→ STRONGER/CHANGED FRUSTRATION`
`→ ADAPT OR BREAK`

This gives character and plot one engine instead of two parallel tracks.

## 6.2 Suspense is managed expectation

Bell + Storr + Truby mystery work:

`VALUED FUTURE OUTCOME`
`+ READER CAN IMAGINE IT`
`+ READER DOES NOT KNOW WHICH WAY IT WILL GO`
`+ CONSEQUENCE MATTERS`
`+ TIME/OBSTACLE DELAYS CERTAINTY`
`= SUSPENSE`

Mystery information gaps should feed suspense by changing the perceived probability of future outcomes.

## 6.3 Emotional force depends on contrast and consequence

Kress + Provost + Clark:

`ACCUMULATION / RESTRAINT → CONTRAST → RELEASE → ACTION/STATE CHANGE`.

Constant peak emotion or constant short emphatic prose flattens intensity because there is no contrast.

## 6.4 Prose efficiency is functional density

Provost + Clark:

Good compression is not minimum word count. It is increasing the amount of useful work performed per unit of prose without deleting necessary context.

`FUNCTIONAL DENSITY = STORY/CHARACTER/IMAGE/VOICE EFFECTS PER READER ATTENTION COST`.

This is a conceptual production metric, not a literal numerical score.

## 6.5 Revision must route tools by problem

Bell/Provost/Clark support the existing IVDIVO specialist-pass architecture. Do not “make prose better” generically. Diagnose:
- structural boredom;
- weak conflict;
- absent suspense;
- emotional underdevelopment;
- POV distance;
- redundant wording;
- rhythm monotony;
then select the correct tool.

# 7. NEW PRODUCTION GATES FROM BATCH 06

## GATE AA — FRUSTRATION CAUSALITY
A meaningful block must produce a character-specific response that changes action; frustration cannot remain generic complaint.

## GATE AB — MIDDLE VARIABLE CHANGE
Repeated complications fail unless they alter access, cost, knowledge, trust, time, status, legality, target, method, or moral acceptability.

## GATE AC — SUSPENSE FUTURE MODEL
A suspense sequence must identify the feared/desired future outcome, uncertainty, consequence, and delay mechanism.

## GATE AD — EARNED CLIFFHANGER
A chapter-end question should be caused by current action/reveal rather than random interruption.

## GATE AE — EMOTIONAL ACCUMULATION
A breaking point requires prior pressure/restraint and must cause a consequential action or state change.

## GATE AF — FUNCTIONAL PROSE
Every substantial prose unit must have a definable reader/story function; decorative correctness is insufficient.

## GATE AG — MULTIFUNCTION DETAIL
Important descriptive passages should prioritize details that also characterize, imply tension/world/history, or influence action.

## GATE AH — RHYTHMIC CONTRAST
Sentence/paragraph rhythm must vary according to function; uniform short-paragraph cadence is a line-level defect unless deliberately sustained for a bounded effect.

## GATE AI — COMPRESSION WITHOUT AMPUTATION
Cuts cannot remove motive, causal bridge, orientation, necessary context, or earned emotional accumulation merely to achieve speed.

## GATE AJ — TOOL ROUTING
Editing feedback must identify the problem class before prescribing a craft tool; no universal “tighten it” pass.

# 9. LIMITS / CONFLICTS

1. Bell’s strong commercial-plot orientation is useful for retention but should not override literary/reflective forms where pressure operates differently.
2. Kress’s claim that frustration is “the most useful emotion” is best treated as a productive craft lens, not empirical psychological law.
3. Provost/Clark include nonfiction/journalistic craft; only mechanisms that transfer cleanly to fiction should enter fiction production.
4. Compression tools can damage IVDIVO when they erase context, atmosphere, causal explanation, or emotional buildup—the exact failure the Founder previously identified in overly short schematic stories.
5. Emotional “breaking points” become melodramatic when accumulation and character consistency are absent.
