## 1.1 Reader transport as an effect stack

Frey frames immersive fiction as a progression from concrete imagined experience toward emotional involvement. His wording is prescriptive and partly metaphorical rather than modern experimental reader science, so it must not be treated as empirical proof. Still, as a craft diagnostic it yields a useful sequence.

### [OUR SYNTHESIS / DRAFT]

`CONCRETE SITUATION`
→ `LEGIBLE PERSON`
→ `VALUED DESIRE / VULNERABILITY`
→ `READER CONCERN`
→ `UNCERTAIN OUTCOME`
→ `CHARACTER CHOICE`
→ `CAUSAL CONSEQUENCE`
→ `PAYOFF / NEW PRESSURE`

The manuscript implication is crucial: sensory vividness alone does not create transport. Detail without desire and consequence becomes décor.

### Failure mode

`VIVID DETAIL WITHOUT ACTIVE HUMAN PROBLEM = WELL-LIT STASIS`

This corrects a common AI failure in SF: polished description of a future system that produces no immediate reason to care.

## 1.2 Author/Reader Contract

Frey's strongest transferable contribution in this pass is not a rigid genre formula but the concept that the opening makes promises.

A novel implicitly promises:
- what kind of human problem matters;
- what kind of story movement the reader can expect;
- what central question/conflict will be paid off;
- what level of causality and fairness governs the world;
- what emotional/intellectual reward the book is selling.

### IVDIVO Contract Card

`OPENING PROMISE | HERO DESIRE | STORY QUESTION | PRINCIPAL PRESSURE | HUMAN COST | GENRE MODE | MYSTERY PROMISE | RELATIONSHIP PROMISE | WORLD PROMISE | CLIMAX PROMISE | LOCAL ENDING OWED`

### Failure mode

The gravest breach is not simply a weak sentence. It is promising a confrontation/question and then allowing the story to evaporate before a meaningful climax and resolution.

For IVDIVO:

`SERIES HOOK MAY REMAIN OPEN`

but

`BOOK'S MAIN STORY MUST CLOSE`.

## 1.3 Contrivance test

Frey correctly attacks author-gift solutions: a problem disappears because the author supplies an unearned resource, coincidence, power, authority or conveniently compliant person.

### Gate formula

`SOLUTION RESOURCE` must be one of:
- established skill;
- established relationship;
- established tool;
- established rule/permission;
- costly newly acquired knowledge;
- believable external intervention already prepared by the causal world.

If none applies, test for contrivance.

### Important qualification

Coincidence can plausibly **create trouble** more freely than it can **solve trouble**. This is a working dramatic principle, not a metaphysical law.

## 1.4 Pseudo-rule warning

Frey explicitly warns against teaching convenient beginner constraints as universal laws. This reinforces the IVDIVO source policy:

`CRAFT SOURCE → TOOL / MECHANISM / CONDITION`, not `AUTHOR SAID IT → UNIVERSAL LAW`.

## 2.1 Invisible exposition

Le Guin's SF-specific problem is directly relevant to IVDIVO: invented worlds require information the reader cannot already know. The error is not exposition itself. The error is allowing exposition to congeal into a lecture that suspends the story.

### [OUR SYNTHESIS / DRAFT] Exposition Brick

Every nontrivial world-information unit should ideally attach to at least one live function:

`DECISION | DANGER | DESIRE | OBSTACLE | STATUS | ACCESS | RELATIONSHIP | MYSTERY | TOOL USE | COST | HUMOR | WONDER`

If an information block attaches to none, it is a candidate for:
- deletion;
- compression;
- relocation;
- indirect narration;
- later reveal;
- physical demonstration.

### Failure mode

`QUESTION CREATED ONLY SO EXPERT CAN LECTURE`.

Characters should ask because the answer changes what they can do, decide, fear, want or infer.

## 2.2 Crowding and leaping

Crowding = precise, vivid, interconnected, functionally dense writing.

Leaping = omission: skipping what does not deserve narrative time.

This is not the same as “write short.”

### Production rule

`CROWD THE MOMENT THAT CHANGES STATE; LEAP OVER REPEATED PROCESS THAT DOES NOT.`

Especially in technical action:
- no blow-by-blow just because every step is known;
- preserve only steps needed for orientation, causality, suspense, character or payoff.

## 2.3 Story must move

Le Guin's workbook definition is operationally useful: narrative must end somewhere different from where it began, even when the change is small or internal.

### IVDIVO scene-state test

At scene exit, at least one must change:

`KNOWLEDGE | PLAN | RELATIONSHIP | ACCESS | RISK | TIME | RESOURCE | STATUS | BODY | LOCATION | BELIEF | OBLIGATION | QUESTION QUALITY`.

No delta = inspect whether this is really a scene or should be compressed.

# 3. Alice LaPlante — The Making of a Story

## Current status

## 3.1 False completion

LaPlante's revision discussion is especially relevant to large IVDIVO assemblies: a draft can look polished and finished while the actual repair required by one confusing climax or relationship forces wider narrative restructuring.

### Operational consequence

A word count, complete chapter count or polished style does **not** freeze development.

Before line-edit lock:

`CLIMAX CLARITY → RISING ACTION CAUSALITY → CHARACTER DEFINITION → RELATIONSHIP ENGINE → POV → SCENE SELECTION → THEN MICROSTYLE`.

## 3.2 Explode weak spots, don't cosmetically seal them

A weak relationship, confusing climax or thin motive should first be explored by generating alternative scenes/POVs/history/constraints—not merely repaired with an explanatory paragraph.

### Gate

`IF FIX = EXPLANATION`, ask first:

`WHAT DRAMATIC MATERIAL IS MISSING?`

## 4.1 Beginning ends in commitment

Swain's useful commercial diagnostic: curiosity can open a story, but sustained suspense requires the focal character to commit to dealing with the threat/problem in pursuit of a goal.

### [OUR SYNTHESIS / DRAFT]

`DISTURBANCE → DESIRE THREATENED / OPPORTUNITY OPENED → PROTAGONIST DECISION → COMMITMENT → STORY FIGHT`.

For a long novel, the precise page is flexible. What matters is that the protagonist stops merely receiving setup and starts carrying a chosen burden.

### Failure mode

`50 pages of interesting setup before the hero owns a task`.

## 5.1 Scene pulse is double

Scofield's useful diagnostic separates:
- action beats;
- emotional beats;
while still requiring the scene to serve the larger novel pulse.

### IVDIVO scene audit

`PHYSICAL ACTION BEAT`
↔ `POV REACTION / INTERPRETATION`
→ `NEXT ACTION BEAT`
↔ `NEW EMOTIONAL/RELATIONAL STATE`.

A scene with only action becomes mechanical. A scene with only interpretation becomes static.

## 5.2 Precedent / payoff integration

Strong major scenes often feel like multiple earlier elements are “coming to a head.” This is a useful setup/payoff test:

`CLIMACTIC ELEMENT → WHERE WAS ITS PRECEDENT?`

No precedent does not automatically mean failure, but a climax built mostly from newly introduced mechanisms will often feel arbitrary.

# 6. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS — OPENING ENGINE

[OUR SYNTHESIS / DRAFT — NOT CANON]

The first 5–10 chapters of a commercial YA/SF novel should not merely “introduce the world.” They should progressively bind the reader to one fight.

### Opening engine

`ORDINARY-LIFE DESIRE`
→ `DISTURBANCE / OPPORTUNITY`
→ `PERSONAL RELEVANCE`
→ `INITIAL CHOICE`
→ `CONCRETE TASK`
→ `RULES / COSTS DISCOVERED THROUGH ACTION`
→ `FIRST TEST`
→ `RESULT`
→ `COMMITMENT DEEPENS`
→ `MAIN STORY QUESTION BECOMES UNAVOIDABLE`.

### Reader Promise Gate

By early opening movement the reader should be able to answer, without a world bible:

1. Who is the principal hero of this book?
2. What does he want **now**?
3. What concrete task has entered his life?
4. What can go wrong?
5. Why can't he simply leave?
6. Who else matters emotionally?
7. What kind of future/world experience is this book promising?
8. What question makes the next chapter necessary?

# 7. MANUSCRIPT VALIDATION — BOOK TWO v0.2, CHAPTERS 1–6

## 7.1 Chapter 1 — THE BADGE

### Strong / protect

The chapter opens with a human-scale comic object—Ethan's laminated authority—and converts it into actual consequence. The rescue-rover demonstration is not decorative ordinary life: the routing anomaly and rover boundary failure create a real moment where Ethan correctly exercises limited stop authority.

That means the chapter performs several functions at once:

`ORDINARY LIFE + FRIENDSHIP + OLLIE'S INDEPENDENT WORLD + NEW AUTHORITY THEME + LATTICE SEED + ACTION + CONSEQUENCE + RIVERGATE INVITATION`.

This is high functional density and should be protected.

The chapter also gives a clean Book-One continuity payoff: Ethan's instinct to turn facts into instructions is present, but he recognizes his scope before anyone lectures him.

### Risk

Several emphasis devices inherited from older Book One prose remain too available:

- isolated micro-paragraphs;
- `Not X. Not Y. Not Z.` stacks;
- explanatory punch line after the behavior has already demonstrated the point.

These are not prohibited. They must become scarce enough to regain force.

### Patch direction

Do not rewrite the whole opening. Preserve the stop sequence, Taven/Mel anomaly, paper-eye motif and invitation. Smooth only accidental cadence stacking.

## 7.2 Chapter 2 — YOUR CALL

### Strong / protect

The authorization scene satisfies the exposition-brick test better than a conventional briefing because each technical term determines:
- Ethan's legal scope;
- what happens if he acts;
- what happens if he does nothing;
- what Aaron can do that Ethan cannot;
- the fallback cost.

The scene ends with an actual access change: Ethan's credential opens operations doors that used to be closed to him.

### Risk

The hold limitations are delivered in a repeated `Not X / Not Y / Not Z` rhetorical ladder. The content is necessary; the delivery risks becoming a house template.

### Patch direction

Convert most exclusions into one syntactically varied paragraph, retaining only the single most important isolated limitation if emphasis is needed.

## 7.3 Chapter 3 — THE PEOPLE IN THE MODEL

### Strong / protect

The simulation is the correct way to introduce Lattice: a route problem with competing costs comes before abstract doctrine. Voss is credible because she does not pretend software is neutral weather and sometimes agrees with criticism. The opposition therefore becomes intellectual and operational, not villain-coded.

Samir's distinction between a model of a person and the person outside the model is conceptually strong **because it is attached to the immediate question of what inputs Lattice may act on**.

Ollie's fallback question creates a second material story engine and prevents the chapter from becoming pure governance dialogue.

### Risk A — thesis-perfect dialogue

Lines such as `CONFLICT REDISTRIBUTED` are powerful but can feel authored if they exist only to state theme.

**Required payoff:** the label change must alter later audit language, metric choice or decision-making. If it never changes behavior, it becomes a slogan.

### Risk B — chapter ending is thematic more than operational

Current ending gives Ethan an excellent realization—real responsibility contains real people—but the next task remains mostly implicit.

### Patch direction

Before chapter exit, expose the project clock and task in concrete form:

`RIVERGATE PUBLIC OPENING → DATE / COUNTDOWN → AUDIT DELIVERABLE → WHAT ETHAN'S TEAM MUST TEST BEFORE THEN`.

The exact calendar detail must be continuity-checked before insertion; do not invent a date silently.

Resulting exit should be:

`IDEA → OBLIGATION → CLOCK → NEXT TEST`, not `IDEA → THEME → END`.

## 7.4 Chapter 4 — FIRST HOLD

### Strong / protect

This is the correct first test of real authority because Ethan's first meaningful act is **not** to activate the hold. The system logs restraint as a decision rather than treating non-action as absence.

That advances Book Two beyond Book One.

### Risk

The final explanation `Apparently authority became real when he chose not to` is already substantially demonstrated by the logged `NO HOLD INVOKED` and Aaron exchange. Line edit should test whether the explanatory sentence can be reduced without losing the emotional turn.

## 7.5 Chapter 5 — NICO

### Strong / protect

Maya's arc is behavioral rather than lecture-based. The best beat is not her understanding a policy. It is her replacing `OBSERVER FOUND...` with `NICO ALVAREZ FOUND...` and submitting the observation under his name.

This passes character/plot coupling:

`OLD AUTONOMY CONFLICT → NEW RESPONSIBILITY → TEMPTATION TO CONTROL → RULE CHECK → RESTRAINT → NICO ACTS → MAYA CREDITS HIS AGENCY`.

### Risk

Lists of what Maya may/may not do are useful once because the scene immediately tests them. Similar policy lists should not become a repeated chapter template.

## 7.6 Chapter 6 — THE SYSTEM WORKS

### Strong / protect

Excellent strategic choice: the novel demonstrates that Lattice genuinely prevents harm before exposing its systemic moral cost. This protects the book from becoming “smart teens discover obvious evil algorithm.”

### Production requirement

The prevented incident must remain specific, embodied and memorable enough that when characters later consider stopping Lattice, the reader can emotionally recall **what stopping it may cost**.

Do not summarize this chapter into mere evidence. It is part of the later climax's moral pressure.

# 9. NEW PRODUCTION GATES — DJ–DU

### DJ — Reader Contract Gate
The opening must create identifiable promises that the book later pays off.

### DK — Transport Stack Gate
Sensory detail must attach to human desire, uncertainty or consequence; atmosphere alone is not immersion.

### DL — Contrivance Gate
A major solution cannot depend on an unprepared author gift.

### DM — Climax-Owed Gate
If the opening promises a central conflict/question, the novel must stage and resolve it rather than dissolve it into explanation.

### DN — Exposition Brick Gate
World information should alter a live decision, action, status, relationship, risk, mystery or wonder state.

### DO — Expository Lump Gate
If removing an information passage leaves current action unchanged, inspect for compression/relocation.

### DP — Crowding/Leaping Gate
Dramatize densely where state changes; leap over repeated process with no new state delta.

### DQ — Scene Delta Gate
A scene exits with changed knowledge, plan, relationship, access, risk, time, resource, status, body, location, belief, obligation or question quality.

### DR — Commitment Gate
The hero must transition from receiving setup to owning a concrete task.

### DS — Action/Emotion Couple Gate
Scene action beats and POV emotional/interpretive beats must causally affect each other.

### DT — Precedent Gate
A climax-critical tool/rule/relationship should have earlier precedent unless surprise itself is the designed effect and remains fair.

### DU — Clock Visibility Gate
If a deadline drives later suspense, seed the operational horizon early enough that escalation feels cumulative rather than suddenly timed.
