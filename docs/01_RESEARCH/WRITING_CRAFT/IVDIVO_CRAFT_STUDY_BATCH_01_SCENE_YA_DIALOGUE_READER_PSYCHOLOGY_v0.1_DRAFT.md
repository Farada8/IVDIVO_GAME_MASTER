# SOURCE 1 — DWIGHT V. SWAIN, *TECHNIQUES OF THE SELLING WRITER*

**Current study status after this pass:** READ PASS / MECHANISM EXTRACTION — PARTIAL, substantial core extraction.  
**Primary role:** micro-causality, scene/sequel, reader experience, commercial narrative propulsion.

## Source architecture observed
Swain explicitly organizes fiction craft around four nested operations:
1. group words into motivation-reaction units;
2. group MR units into scenes and sequels;
3. group scenes and sequels into story pattern;
4. create characters capable of giving the pattern life.

He also explicitly rejects the idea that an incident is already a story. An incident requires point of view, dynamic characters, complications, climax and resolution before it becomes story material.

## Mechanism S01-A — Motivation-Reaction Continuity
**Input:** external stimulus or new information perceived by the POV character.  
**Process:** stimulus is registered before the character's response; reaction is psychologically ordered rather than authorially scrambled.  
**Output:** the reader experiences events as if they unfold from inside the character's causal field.  
**Failure mode:** reaction appears before its stimulus; physical response, thought and action are ordered in ways that make the prose feel artificial or disembodied.  
**IVDIVO TEST:** in high-pressure scenes, identify every meaningful external stimulus and the POV response it causes. Any response with no visible cause is flagged.

## Mechanism S01-B — Scene / Sequel Alternation
**Scene function:** active attempt against resistance.  
**Sequel function:** absorb consequences, process emotion/problem, make a new decision that generates the next active objective.  
**Failure mode 1:** endless scene-without-processing produces mechanical action and emotional numbness.  
**Failure mode 2:** endless sequel-without-new-decision produces introspective stasis.  
**IVDIVO TEST:** after every major setback, determine whether the manuscript gives enough space for consequence → evaluation → decision. After every reflective unit, identify the new objective it generates.

## Mechanism S01-C — Event Is Not Story
A theft, disappearance, anomaly, arrival, murder or cosmic phenomenon is only material. Story begins when a particular character must do something about it and actions generate further consequences.

**IVDIVO TEST:** every book seed must be rewritten from EVENT language into ACTION language: `When X occurs, Y must accomplish Z despite Q, or R.`

## Mechanism S01-D — Technique Is a Tool, Not a Formula
Swain repeatedly treats craft devices as tools that may be used consciously or unconsciously; no single secret or magic key creates fiction.

**IVDIVO consequence:** never allow Scene/Sequel or MRU terminology to become visible mechanical scaffolding. Use the mechanism for causality; vary surface form freely.

# SOURCE 2 — WILL STORR, *THE SCIENCE OF STORYTELLING*

**Current study status:** READ / EXTRACTION — PARTIAL, major cognitive-story mechanisms extracted.  
**Primary role:** attention, curiosity, control, active protagonist, flawed self, status/belonging, prediction.

## Mechanism S02-A — Change Captures Attention
Storr's argument repeatedly begins from the brain as a model-building/prediction system that notices change. Stable conditions fade into background; unexpected change creates an explanatory problem.

**IVDIVO TEST:** opening chapters must contain an intelligible deviation from expected normality, but the deviation may be social, relational, bodily, institutional or technological — not necessarily supernatural.

## Mechanism S02-B — Information Gap, Not Fog
Curiosity is stimulated when the reader can perceive a gap between what is known and what could be known. Total opacity does not automatically generate curiosity.

**IVDIVO TEST:** for every mystery question record: `KNOWN FACTS | CURRENT HYPOTHESIS | MISSING PIECE | NEXT POSSIBLE TEST`.

## Mechanism S02-C — Protagonist as Causer of Effects
Storr emphasizes goal-seeking control: once disrupted, a protagonist should react, choose and attempt to impose a model/order on chaos. Passive suffering does not create the same dramatic engine.

**IVDIVO TEST:** remove the protagonist's decisions from the plot synopsis. If almost all major events still happen the same way, agency is insufficient.

## Mechanism S02-D — Human Connection as High-Value Content
The book's synthesis of story research repeatedly returns to social information, belonging, status and human connection as central story material.

**IVDIVO consequence:** cosmic scale may raise pressure, but the emotionally legible stake must often remain: who trusts me, who chooses me, who excludes me, whom I may lose, whose judgment matters, where I belong.

## Mechanism S02-E — Prediction and Reclassification
A good reveal should let the reader reconstruct prior evidence under a better model. Surprise without retrospective intelligibility is weaker.

**IVDIVO TEST:** after a major reveal ask: can the reader identify at least two earlier observations that now mean something different?

# SOURCE 3 — ROBERT McKEE, *DIALOGUE: THE ART OF VERBAL ACTION FOR PAGE, STAGE, AND SCREEN*

**Current study status:** READ / MECHANISM EXTRACTION — PARTIAL, dialogue engine substantially extracted.  
**Primary role:** verbal action, subtext, beat progression, character-specific speech, exposition discipline.

## Mechanism S03-A — Dialogue Has Three Core Dramatic Jobs
Dialogue can carry exposition, characterize, and perform action. The strongest lines often do more than one job at once.

**IVDIVO TEST:** tag important lines `ACTION / CHARACTER / EXPOSITION`. Any long passage tagged only EXPOSITION is a rewrite candidate.

## Mechanism S03-B — The Said / Unsaid / Unsayable
Surface text is not the full dramatic event. Characters possess desires and constraints that determine what they say, what they conceal, and what they cannot admit even to themselves.

**IVDIVO TEST:** before key dialogue write for each participant: `WANTS | HIDES | CANNOT ADMIT | TACTIC NOW`.

## Mechanism S03-C — Beat = Action / Reaction in Subtext
McKee's dialogue design treats each beat as a specific action attempting an effect, met by reaction/counteraction. Progression requires beats to top/change one another rather than repeat the same exchange.

**IVDIVO TEST:** underline each dialogue beat and assign an action verb. Three consecutive beats with the same tactic and no value shift = compression/rewrite.

## Mechanism S03-D — Character-Specific Language
Character voice is constrained/formed by vocabulary, locution, culture, education, profession, temperament and the principle of creative limitation.

**IVDIVO TEST:** remove dialogue attribution from a five-line exchange among core characters. If identity becomes impossible to infer in every case, voice differentiation is weak.

## Mechanism S03-E — Scene Turning Point Must Be Behavioral
Dialogue should not merely exchange information; a specific beat should move a scene value into a different charge/state.

**IVDIVO TEST:** identify the exact line/gesture at which the relationship or informational power changes. If no beat changes value, the dialogue scene may be activity rather than action.

## Mechanism S03-F — Common Dialogue Failure Classes
Useful audit categories from the source: empty talk; overly emotive/knowing/perceptive speech; cliché; character-neutral language; on-the-nose writing; monologue fallacy; repetition; splintered/misshapen scenes; paraphrasing.

**IVDIVO Dialogue Red Team:** every final dialogue pass explicitly checks these categories.

# SOURCE 4 — JESSICA BRODY, *SAVE THE CAT! WRITES A YOUNG ADULT NOVEL*

**Current study status:** READ / EXTRACTION — PARTIAL, YA architecture and series functions extracted.  
**Primary role:** YA hero, plot/structure/character coupling, story-type obligations, series planning.

## Mechanism S04-A — YA Is an Age Category, Not a Single Genre
YA can carry mystery, thriller, romance, SF, horror, comedy, dystopia and other genre engines. Therefore 'youth' is not itself enough to define plot.

**IVDIVO TEST:** every youth book declares both `AGE LENS` and `STORY ENGINE / GENRE PROMISE`.

## Mechanism S04-B — Plot / Structure / Character Transformation Must Couple
Brody explicitly describes these as interconnected. A structurally efficient plot that does not alter the hero, or a character arc that does not cause plot action, is incomplete.

**IVDIVO TEST:** for every major beat write two deltas: `EXTERNAL STATE CHANGE` and `CHARACTER MODEL/RELATIONSHIP CHANGE`. At least the major turning points must carry both.

## Mechanism S04-C — Story Type as Transformation Lens
Her ten story categories are not bookstore genres but transformation/theme lenses (rites of passage, buddy love, institutionalized, superhero, golden fleece, fool triumphant, dude with a problem, out of the bottle, whydunit, monster in the house).

**IVDIVO use:** use these only as diagnostic lenses, never as mandatory templates. One IVDIVO novel may combine an SF surface genre with a rites-of-passage human engine and a whydunit investigative engine.

## Mechanism S04-D — Series Planning Must Preserve Book-Level Shape
The presence of a series does not remove the need to structure each volume. The series layer should extend transformation/questions rather than excuse an unfinished local story.

**IVDIVO TEST:** every volume must state its own `OPENING STATE → LOCAL OBJECTIVE → CLIMAX ACTION → NEW STATE`, plus a separate `SERIES DOOR`.

## Mechanism S04-E — Reverse Engineering Successful YA
Brody explicitly advocates dissecting successful YA novels to understand structure and transformation.

**IVDIVO consequence:** YA fiction references in the library are not decorative inspiration. They receive evidence-led reverse-engineering passports.

# SOURCE 5 — IGNASI RIBÓ, *PROSE FICTION: AN INTRODUCTION TO THE SEMIOTICS OF NARRATIVE*

**Current study status:** READ / EXTRACTION — PARTIAL, narratological framework extracted across plot/character/narration/language.  
**Primary role:** analytical vocabulary; distinction between story and discourse; focalisation; emplotment; setting; characterization.

## Mechanism S05-A — Story ≠ Discourse
The storyworld contains events, environments and characters; discourse is the communicative arrangement through narrator/focalisation/language/order.

**IVDIVO TEST:** when a chapter feels weak, diagnose separately: is the underlying event chain weak, or is the event chain strong but badly presented?

## Mechanism S05-B — Emplotment Is Selection and Arrangement
Plot is not raw chronology. Emplotment alters order, duration, frequency, connection and relevance to create a meaningful progression.

**IVDIVO TEST:** chronology ledger and discourse-order ledger remain separate for mystery and multi-POV books.

## Mechanism S05-C — Suspense and Surprise Are Different Operations
Suspense manages anticipation of future events; surprise disrupts expectation. Both depend on how discourse distributes information.

**IVDIVO TEST:** tag every major reveal as primarily `SUSPENSE PAYOFF`, `SURPRISE RECLASSIFICATION`, or `BOTH`; ensure setup differs accordingly.

## Mechanism S05-D — Character Exists at Two Analytical Levels
At discourse/structural level characters can be treated as actants/functions; at storyworld level readers experience them as individuated existents. Fiction fails when functional role is mistaken for sufficient human individuality.

**IVDIVO TEST:** every core character has both a `PLOT FUNCTION` and a `LIFE BEYOND FUNCTION` test. No character bible field is kept unless it can alter perception/action/relationship in scenes.

## Mechanism S05-E — Focalisation Is Not Identical to Narration
Who tells and who perceives can be distinguished. This is essential for controlled information, mystery, unreliable interpretation and psychic distance.

**IVDIVO TEST:** each scene card records `NARRATOR MODE` and `FOCAL CHARACTER / INFORMATION ACCESS` separately when relevant.

# SOURCE 6 — JAMES SCOTT BELL, *REVISION AND SELF-EDITING FOR PUBLICATION*

**Current study status:** READ / EXTRACTION — PARTIAL, revision architecture and action/reaction loop extracted.  
**Primary role:** developmental/self-edit pass; action/reaction movement; revision sequencing.

## Mechanism S06-A — Draft Fast / Revise Cool
Bell separates generative writing from analytical revision. Excessive line-level policing during drafting can inhibit generation; revision requires slower diagnostic attention.

**IVDIVO production consequence:** story architecture is frozen before prose; during approved scene drafting do not continuously reopen global architecture. Structural defects discovered later return through a formal change request rather than silent drift.

## Mechanism S06-B — Action Scene / Reaction Scene Loop
Action scene: objective → obstacle/conflict → outcome. Reaction: emotion → analysis → decision. Decision generates the next objective.

**IVDIVO TEST:** after each major scene/sequence, ask whether the outcome creates either a new objective or a required reaction/decision. If neither, forward causality is weak.

## Mechanism S06-C — Setup Must Be Marbled Into Drama
Setup scenes still need action/reaction architecture. Background information should be distributed inside a live dramatic situation rather than placed as inert preface.

**IVDIVO TEST:** any scene whose primary purpose is 'setup' must also alter a relationship, objective, risk, decision or actionable knowledge.

## Mechanism S06-D — First Read-Through Before Micro-Editing
The source's revision structure separates the global first read/deepening/checklist stages from sentence polishing.

**IVDIVO consequence:** developmental regression precedes line edit. A 100k-word manuscript with a broken main story is not sent straight to cadence/polish.

# CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS — BATCH 01

## C01 — CAUSAL SCENE LOOP
Support: Swain + Bell + McKee + Ribó.

`OBJECTIVE → ACTION/TACTIC → RESISTANCE → TURN/OUTCOME → EMOTION/INTERPRETATION → DECISION → NEXT OBJECTIVE`

This is now a core IVDIVO scene/sequence diagnostic. It is **not** a mandatory visible scene shape; steps may be compressed, displaced or embedded, but causal equivalents must exist when the scene is plot-bearing.

## C02 — READER QUESTION LOOP
Support: Storr + Ribó + Brody.

`CHANGE → QUESTION → PARTIAL MODEL → TEST/ACTION → NEW EVIDENCE → MODEL REVISION → PAYOFF/NEW QUESTION`

Use for mystery, science-fiction discovery and gradual ontology reveal.

## C03 — DIALOGUE ACTION LOOP
Support: McKee + Swain causality + character-action framework.

`WANT FROM OTHER → VERBAL TACTIC → COUNTERACTION → TACTIC CHANGE → BEAT TURN → RELATIONSHIP/INFO VALUE DELTA`

Dialogue that lacks this loop may still work as atmosphere/comedy/texture, but must justify its page-time separately.

## C04 — YA DOUBLE DELTA
Support: Brody + Storr social psychology.

Every major YA turn should be tested for both:
- **external consequence**, and
- **identity/belonging/relationship consequence**.

Fantastic events that do not enter the young person's social/relational future remain emotionally distant.

## C05 — STORY VS PRESENTATION DIAGNOSIS
Support: Ribó + Swain + Bell.

Before rewriting prose, identify whether the defect is:
1. STORY/FABULA — weak objective, cause, consequence, climax;
2. DISCOURSE/COMPOSITION — wrong order, duration, POV, reveal timing;
3. PROSE/PERFORMANCE — weak sentence, image, rhythm, diction.

Never use line editing to conceal a level-1 structural defect.

# NEW PRODUCTION TESTS CREATED BY THIS BATCH

### Scene Causality Gate
For every plot-bearing scene:
- What does POV want now?
- What action/tactic attempts change?
- What resists?
- What exact beat/incident turns the scene?
- What is objectively different afterward?
- What emotional/interpretive consequence follows?
- What new decision/objective is caused?

### Dialogue Action Gate
For each key exchange:
- What does A want from B?
- What does B want from A?
- What is said / unsaid / unsayable?
- What tactic does each beat use?
- Where does tactic change?
- Where does power/trust/information change?
- Is exposition carried as ammunition rather than lecture?

### YA Human-Stakes Gate
For each major fantastic/technological event:
- Which ordinary youth problem does it alter?
- What does it do to belonging/status/friendship/family/love/body/future?
- Which decision must the young character make because of it?

### Mystery Fairness Gate
- What does the reader know?
- What hypothesis can the reader reasonably form?
- What is withheld, and why is withholding fair?
- Which earlier evidence gains new meaning at reveal?
