## 1. CENTRAL PRODUCTION ROUTER
Swain's strongest late-book contribution is process separation:

`GENERATE / DISCOVER → RESEARCH ENOUGH → FLEXIBLE PLAN → DRAFT → STRUCTURAL REVISION → POLISHING / LINE EDIT → PRODUCTION CHECK`.

Do not collapse these into one simultaneous operation.

## 2. GENERATOR / CRITIC SEPARATION
Swain explicitly distinguishes the creative impulse from critical judgment.

IVDIVO:
`CREATE HOT → CRITIQUE COOL`.

### Gate B105-G01 — NO 100-AGENT PARALYSIS
The Writers' Room may contain many specialist functions, but they should run in staged passes. Do not make every desk veto every sentence during first-generation prose.

Use:
1. live scene generation;
2. canon/causality pass;
3. character/personhood pass;
4. scene/POV/evidence pass;
5. dialogue/subtext pass;
6. paragraph/rhythm pass;
7. wording polish;
8. reader test.

## 3. RESEARCH BALANCE
Too little research may break plausibility; too much research may prevent production.

### Gate B105-G02 — RESEARCH QUESTION BEFORE RESEARCH
Before researching, name the story decision the research must unlock.

Possible sources:
- reference/text source;
- current primary technical source;
- specialist/expert;
- field observation / lived process;
- controlled experiment or comparison.

### Gate B105-G03 — STOP WHEN STORY DECISION IS SUPPORTED
When sufficient evidence exists to make the bounded story decision, stop expanding research merely because more information exists.

## 4. HISTORICAL SOURCE AUTHORITY
Swain's 1965/1981 publishing advice is historical evidence, not current market guidance.

Do not operationalize as current instructions:
- postal manuscript submission;
- paper weight/ribbons/carbons;
- historical magazine markets;
- dated agent/editor assumptions;
- period-specific sex/gender/audience rules.

Current market claims require current sources.

## 5. FLEXIBLE PLANNING
A plan is a working hypothesis, not canon.

`PLAN → DRAFT → LIVE MATERIAL → DIAGNOSE → REPLAN IF CAUSAL/TRUTH GAIN IS REAL`.

### Gate B105-G04 — OUTLINE SERVES STORY
Do not preserve a scene merely because it appears in the outline. Do not discard a stronger live discovery merely because it was not predicted.

Any new discovery still passes:
`CANON + PERSONHOOD + CAUSALITY + ONE MAIN STORY + DOWNSTREAM PAYOFF`.

## 6. REVISION != POLISHING
### Revision
Changes structure, scene function, order, causality, character behavior, evidence architecture, emphasis or inclusion.

### Polishing
Improves clarity, rhythm, diction, local sequence, clutter, referents and line-level impact after structure is stable.

### Gate B105-G05 — MACRO BEFORE MICRO
Never line-polish a scene whose function, ownership, causal role or POV/evidence status is still unstable.

## 7. CUTTING HIERARCHY
Small problem → word/phrase/sentence cut.
Large problem → paragraph/scene/character/strand cut or merge.

### Gate B105-G06 — CUT AT DEFECT LEVEL
Do not solve 2,000 unnecessary words by replacing 200 adjectives. Remove the unit causing the redundancy.

## 8. CLARITY / READER-STATE CONTINUITY
Line edit must preserve causal and referential continuity.

Track:
`ACTIVE SUBJECT → TIME → PLACE → KNOWLEDGE → CAUSE → RESPONSE → EMPHASIS`.

Ambiguity may be productive in mystery, but the reader should know **what is uncertain** and why.

## 9. CHOPPINESS / SENTENCE LENGTH
Swain explicitly warns that an excess of short sentences can sound choppy unless that is the intended effect.

### Gate B105-G07 — NO SHORT-SENTENCE KPI
`INTENSITY != SHORT-SENTENCE DENSITY`.

Choose sentence architecture for:
- speed;
- accumulation;
- subjective time expansion;
- abrupt decision;
- revelation;
- breath;
- humor timing;
- technical reasoning;
- emotional continuity.

A short fragment is valid when its function is specific. A row of fragments performing one continuous thought should first be tested for paragraph/sentence integration.

## 10. PRIVATE ERROR CHECKLIST
Swain recommends building a personal checklist from recurring errors rather than relying only on generic maxims.

IVDIVO adaptation:
Maintain per-project regression list such as:
- stale money/rent/credits returning to orbital canon;
- human consciousness-transfer creeping into ordinary technology;
- POV knowledge leak;
- robot-as-tool personhood regression;
- scene-end thematic restatement;
- short-paragraph confetti;
- meeting/model/test repetition;
- premature mystery reveal;
- school interpretation upgraded to ontology.

## 11. WRITER STALL DIAGNOSIS
A stall may mean:
- missing research;
- missing cause;
- unclear want;
- wrong POV;
- exhausted tactic;
- duplicated scene function;
- insufficient consequence;
- author fatigue.

### Gate B105-G08 — DIAGNOSE BEFORE ADDING PLOT
Do not add villain/anomaly/accident/romance/technology as the default cure for a stalled middle.

## 12. PRODUCTION SUSTAINABILITY
Output targets and routines are tools, not moral tests.

Use sustainable cadence that preserves quality and completion. Swain's personal numerical output targets are not universal IVDIVO requirements.

## 13. CROSS-SOURCE CONVERGENCE
### Lamott / LaPlante
Strong convergence on discovery first, critical/revision pass later.

### Bell Plot & Structure / Rosenfeld
Strong convergence on macro diagnosis before prose polish and adaptive planning.

### Zinsser / Prose / Lodge / Le Guin
Strong convergence that sentence length is functional, not a quality metric.

### Wolf
World database can exceed book narrative; research volume does not justify exposition volume.

### Herman / Zunshine
Research and explanation need evidence status; plausible narrative explanation is not verified cause.

## 15. FINAL PRODUCTION ORDER
`DISCOVERY → CANON → ONE MAIN STORY → CAUSALITY → CHARACTER/PERSONHOOD → CHAPTER/SCENE FUNCTION → POV/EVIDENCE → DIALOGUE/SUBTEXT → PARAGRAPH/READER-STATE → RHYTHM → WORDING → READER TEST`.

This is the Swain contribution after filtering the dated commercial, moral and psychological assumptions.
