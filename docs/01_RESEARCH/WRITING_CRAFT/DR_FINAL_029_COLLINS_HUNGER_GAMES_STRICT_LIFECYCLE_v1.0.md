## STRUCTURE MAP

### 1. Ordinary-life baseline creates measurable stakes
District 12 establishes family bonds, food acquisition, illegal competence, class differences, local distrust and Katniss's protective identity before the central rupture.

### 2. Volunteering converts private responsibility into institutional capture
Katniss's protective action for Prim becomes a public role. The institution immediately reframes personhood into spectacle, representation and competition.

### 3. Capitol preparation teaches a second survival system
Training, styling, interviews, scores and sponsors create a public-performance economy alongside physical survival. Katniss and Peeta must manage how observers model them.

### 4. Arena entry converts world knowledge into asymmetric competence
Hunting, plants, camouflage, strength, social inference and spatial judgment have different values. Competence is specific rather than generic.

### 5. Alliances and evidence revise the initial enemy model
Rue, Peeta, Thresh, Foxface and the Careers become differentiated agents rather than one enemy category. The imposed competition does not erase local ethics or cross-district bonds.

### 6. Institutional rules become endogenous to spectacle
Gamemakers alter water, fire, supplies, pair-survival rules and endgame conditions because audience interest is part of the operating system. Rules are not neutral natural law.

### 7. Public romance becomes both survival tactic and real relationship pressure
The love narrative begins partly as strategic performance, changes sponsor behavior, alters rescue decisions, and gradually produces genuine but ambiguous emotional consequences.

### 8. The berry move attacks the institution's required output
Katniss and Peeta exploit a structural dependency: the Games require a victor. Their refusal of the imposed one-winner frame forces the institution to change its immediate decision.

### 9. Victory does not end coercion
Post-arena styling, highlight editing and interviews reveal that survival creates a new public-role problem. Katniss must manage an interpretation of her action to protect herself and others.

### 10. Local victory closes while relationship and political costs remain
Both survive, but Katniss/Peeta trust and meaning are unsettled; Capitol retaliation risk remains; public identity cannot simply be discarded after the arena.

## HIGH-VALUE MECHANISMS

### M1 — Specific ordinary life before rupture
`SPECIFIC ROUTINE → RELATIONSHIPS / VALUES / SKILLS → RUPTURE → READER CAN MEASURE WHAT IS LOST`.

Use for Orbital Youth: ordinary life is not filler if it establishes nonfungible desires, relationships, competence and normal social rules later put under pressure.

### M2 — Formal equality may hide unequal exposure
A universal rule can still distribute risk unequally because participants enter it with different material conditions, histories or incentives.

Gate:
`SAME RULE ≠ SAME BURDEN`.

For IVDIVO governance, always ask who has more reason or pressure to opt into a formally equal system and who bears repeated cost.

### M3 — Public/private identity split
`PRIVATE STATE → PUBLIC PERFORMANCE → AUDIENCE MODEL → MATERIAL / INSTITUTIONAL CONSEQUENCE → PRIVATE STATE CHANGES`.

Performance is not merely disguise; once institutions and audiences act on it, the performance becomes causally real.

### M4 — Real audience of action
Characters often perform for observers rather than the named interlocutor.

`ACTION / SPEECH → VISIBLE TARGET → REAL AUDIENCE → AUDIENCE INFERENCE → RESOURCE / RULE / REPUTATION CHANGE`.

Use for hearings, streamed meetings, public evaluations, social media analogues and institutional ceremonies.

### M5 — Audience incentives reshape institutional behavior
`AUDIENCE ATTENTION / EXPECTATION → INSTITUTIONAL INCENTIVE → RULE / ENVIRONMENT CHANGE → CHARACTER OPTION SPACE CHANGES`.

This is stronger than a static evil-institution model. The institution adapts to spectacle incentives.

### M6 — Domain-specific transferable competence
`ORDINARY SKILL → NEW CONTEXT → REFRAMED AFFORDANCE → ACTIONABLE ADVANTAGE`.

Examples of function: hunting becomes arena survival; camouflage becomes concealment; plant knowledge becomes food/toxin discrimination; strength becomes tactical utility.

Gate:
Competence must have boundaries. One strong domain does not imply universal intelligence or moral authority.

### M7 — Intelligent mistake / incomplete motive model
Katniss repeatedly interprets Peeta through incomplete hypotheses.

`OBSERVATION → MOTIVE HYPOTHESES → ACTION UNDER CURRENT MODEL → NEW EVIDENCE → MODEL UPDATE`.

Do not make YA protagonists stupid for plot. Let errors arise from reasonable but incomplete models.

### M8 — Performance can create authentic feeling without retroactive falsification
`STAGED BEHAVIOR → REPEATED EMBODIED INTERACTION → REAL RELATIONSHIP STATE CHANGE`.

But:
`LATER REAL FEELING ≠ EARLIER PERFORMANCE WAS SECRETLY ALL TRUE`.

This is crucial for romance/mystery braid and consent.

### M9 — Sponsor / mentor communication through constrained signals
Haymitch's gifts and withholding operate as compressed tactical communication.

`MENTOR OBSERVES → SIGNAL / RESOURCE TIMING → PROTAGONIST INFERS INTENT → TACTIC CHANGE`.

Useful where direct communication is impossible, but inference remains fallible.

### M10 — Complementary alliance competence
Katniss/Rue and Katniss/Peeta work because different competencies change the shared option space.

`AGENT A CAPABILITY + AGENT B CAPABILITY → MUTUAL DEPENDENCE → JOINT PLAN → RELATIONSHIP UPDATE`.

Do not create teams from role labels alone.

### M11 — Dependency attack instead of direct-force escalation
Destroying Careers' supply system changes opponent behavior more than attempting to overpower stronger opponents directly.

`OPPONENT STRENGTH → DEPENDENCY MAP → TARGET SUPPORT SYSTEM → OPPONENT OPTION SPACE SHRINKS`.

Strong for technical/institutional action design.

### M12 — Same setting, different affordance model
Characters read the same field/forest differently because of lived experience.

`SETTING → CHARACTER EXPERIENCE / NEED → PERCEIVED AFFORDANCES / RISKS → DIFFERENT CHOICE`.

Worldbuilding becomes character-specific rather than neutral description.

### M13 — Institution creates convergence by manipulating environment
The Gamemakers do not merely command combat; they remove water, create hazards, move resources and alter rules.

`INSTITUTIONAL GOAL → ENVIRONMENT / INCENTIVE CHANGE → CHARACTER ROUTES CONVERGE → CHOICE UNDER NEW CONSTRAINT`.

Use carefully: coercive architecture is a mechanism, not a default for all IVDIVO institutions.

### M14 — Symbolic action changes audience model
Rue's flowers and the berry move matter not only operationally but interpretively.

`ACTION → OBSERVED SYMBOLIC MEANING → AUDIENCE / INSTITUTION MODEL UPDATE → DOWNSTREAM CONSEQUENCE`.

Symbolic meaning is causal when observers act on it.

### M15 — Public narrative selection / omission
The Capitol edits the Games into a love story and omits evidence that supports rebellion.

`RAW EVENTS → SELECT / OMIT / ORDER → PUBLIC NARRATIVE → AUDIENCE MODEL`.

Critical firewall:
`PUBLIC NARRATIVE ≠ COMPLETE RECORD`.

Directly useful for mystery source provenance and institutional media.

### M16 — Surveillance changes self-presentation
`POSSIBLE OBSERVATION → SELF-MONITORING → SELECTIVE SPEECH / PERFORMANCE → AUDIENCE INFERENCE`.

A character under surveillance cannot be written as though private and public speech have identical stakes.

### M17 — Rule dependency can be attacked
The berry endgame succeeds because Katniss identifies what the institution itself requires: a victor.

`INSTITUTIONAL REQUIREMENT → HERO IDENTIFIES DEPENDENCY → CREDIBLE REFUSAL / COUNTERMOVE → INSTITUTION MUST UPDATE`.

This is not raw power; it is leverage through system logic.

### M18 — Procedural rule change can expose illegitimacy
The one-winner reversal reveals that rules serve spectacle rather than a stable moral contract.

Gate:
`FORMAL RULE ≠ LEGITIMATE RULE`.

Evaluate information, revocability, power, affected-agent consent and purpose.

### M19 — Benevolent rescue can violate consent
Katniss drugs Peeta to obtain medicine for him. The action is effective and life-preserving, but it overrides his explicit preference.

Gate:
`BENEFICIAL OUTCOME ≠ CONSENT`.

Do not let later success erase the autonomy question.

### M20 — Recovery does not reset relationship state
Katniss survives and Peeta survives, but post-arena discovery that parts of the romance were strategic causes real relational damage.

`SHARED CRISIS ENDS ≠ RELATIONSHIP RETURNS TO PRIOR STATE`.

### M21 — Public frame may become a survival constraint after the event
After the berries, Katniss must sustain the love interpretation because the institution treats rebellion as dangerous.

`PAST PUBLIC ACTION → POWERFUL AUDIENCE INTERPRETATION → REQUIRED FUTURE PERFORMANCE → IDENTITY / RELATIONSHIP COST`.

### M22 — Edited replay can reframe the protagonist's own memory
Katniss watches a selected narrative of events she lived. The edit changes which meanings are emphasized and exposes Peeta actions she did not witness.

`LIVED EXPERIENCE → EXTERNAL EDITED RECORD → NEW INFORMATION + FRAMING → SELF / RELATIONSHIP MODEL UPDATE`.

### M23 — Climax may be a refusal of the offered choice
The institutional frame says one must kill the other. The protagonists create a third option by threatening to invalidate the system's required output.

`FORCED BINARY → IDENTIFY SYSTEM DEPENDENCY → CREATE THIRD OPTION → FORCE NEW DECISION`.

Highly relevant to governance, YA autonomy and nonhuman personhood stories.

### M24 — Local victory can increase political danger
`SUCCESS → PUBLIC MEANING → AUTHORITY THREAT MODEL CHANGES → NEW SERIES PRESSURE`.

Success does not need to be rewritten as failure to create sequel propulsion.

## CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

### Collins + Brody
Brody provides YA developmental diagnostics; Collins demonstrates youth stakes through autonomy, responsibility, belonging and public identity without requiring a school-like template. Collins is the stronger fiction lab for institutional pressure and public/private self.

### Collins + Wells
Both support `PERSONHOOD / AGENCY > assigned role`. Wells is stronger on nonhuman consent/control provenance; Collins is stronger on public performance, surveillance and coercive audience systems.

### Collins + Crouch
Dark Matter adds decision-owner legitimacy; Collins adds forced-choice architecture and institutional dependence on a public outcome. Both support finding a third option outside an offered binary.

### Collins + Recursion
Recursion supplies intervention blast radius; Collins shows symbolic/public actions whose downstream interpretation creates new political consequences.

### Collins + Baxter / McKee Dialogue
Baxter and McKee help stage what is said/unsaid; Collins adds the audience layer: a scene may be performed for millions of observers and therefore have different public and private meanings.

### Collins + Zunshine / Herman
Strong convergence on motive uncertainty and source/model separation. Katniss's confident interpretations of Peeta are repeatedly revised by new evidence.

### Collins + Swain / Bell / Rosenfeld
The novel supports `success → new responsibility / new pressure`, not setback monoculture. Relationship processing and public-performance scenes change real state rather than serving as static aftermath.

## IVDIVO OPERATIONALIZATION

### A. Orbital Youth — youth stakes without money
Do not import Panem scarcity, sponsorship or consumer status.

Translate function into nonfungible stakes:
- trust;
- consent;
- qualification;
- mission role;
- access to a particular team/place/time;
- reputation;
- belonging;
- privacy;
- relationship;
- responsibility;
- mastery;
- unique experience.

Strong youth engine:
`ORDINARY COMPETENCE → PUBLIC / INSTITUTIONAL TEST → REAL SUCCESS → MORE VISIBILITY / TRUST / RESPONSIBILITY → HARDER CHOICE ABOUT SELF / GROUP / AUTHORITY`.

### B. Public/private identity gate
For any public youth role:

`PRIVATE WANT → REQUIRED PUBLIC ROLE → PERFORMANCE → AUDIENCE MODEL → MATERIAL CONSEQUENCE → PRIVATE COST / RELATIONSHIP CHANGE`.

The character's public mask should not be merely cosmetic; it must alter real options.

### C. Institution fairness audit
For every apparently universal rule:

`WHO ENTERS VOLUNTARILY? → WHO HAS MORE PRESSURE TO ENTER? → WHO CAN REFUSE? → WHO BEARS REPEATED COST? → WHO CAN APPEAL? → WHO BENEFITS FROM THE RULE?`

`SAME RULE ≠ SAME BURDEN`.

### D. Conscious robots / nonhuman persons
Do not turn public approval into personhood proof.

A nonhuman may be loved by audiences and still lack legitimate autonomy; or may be unpopular and still retain personhood.

Preserve:
`OWN WANT → OWN MODEL → OWN RELATIONSHIPS → CONSENT / REFUSAL → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE`.

### E. Relationship / romance
Use Collins for public/private relationship divergence:

`PUBLIC RELATIONSHIP PERFORMANCE → REAL EXTERNAL CONSEQUENCE → PRIVATE RELATIONSHIP PRESSURE → BOUNDARY / TRUST CHANGE`.

Firewalls:
- performance ≠ consent;
- attraction ≠ corroboration;
- sacrifice ≠ ownership;
- shared survival ≠ automatic couplehood;
- later genuine feeling does not erase earlier strategic ambiguity.

### F. Mystery / ROOM 917
The edited-highlights mechanism is directly useful for media/document analysis:

`RAW EVENT → WHO RECORDED → WHO EDITED → WHAT OMITTED → PUBLIC MODEL → WHAT ORIGINAL SOURCE CAN CORROBORATE`.

A promotional narrative, documentary reconstruction or emotionally convincing account cannot upgrade clue status.

### G. B07 — THE UNLISTED CITY
If an institution presents a binary decision about the distributed nonhuman life, test whether the binary is itself legitimate.

Use:
`OFFERED BINARY → DECISION OWNER? → SYSTEM DEPENDENCY → THIRD OPTION? → ENTITY CONSENT → CONSEQUENCE`.

Do not let humans choose between two preservation outcomes if the conscious entity can define a third path.

### H. Style / line editing
Do not import Collins' first-person-present thriller rhythm as house style.

`SUSPENSE ≠ SHORT-SENTENCE DENSITY`.

Maintain IVDIVO repair order:
`BOOK ENGINE → CHAPTER/SCENE NECESSITY → ORDER/DURATION/FREQUENCY → POV/EVIDENCE → SHOW/TELL → DIALOGUE/SUBTEXT → PARAGRAPH/READER-STATE → SENTENCE RHYTHM → WORDING`.
