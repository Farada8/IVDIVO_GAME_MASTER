# 2. Core mechanism — PRIOR CONSEQUENCE → INTENTION → COMPLICATION → RESULT → NEXT INTENTION

Rosenfeld's strongest transferable mechanism is not merely “every scene needs conflict.” It is that a character's immediate intention should grow from the story already in motion.

## IVDIVO recursion

`PRIOR CONSEQUENCE`
→ `WHAT THIS CHARACTER NOW WANTS TO DO`
→ `WHY THIS INTENTION BELONGS TO THIS CHARACTER`
→ `OPPOSITION / COMPLICATION / SUPPORT`
→ `ACTION`
→ `RESULT`
→ `REEVALUATION`
→ `NEXT INTENTION`

A scene becomes suspicious when its intention cannot be traced backward to an earlier consequence or forward to a later consequence.

## Failure mode: free-floating vignette

A scene may be witty, atmospheric or emotionally credible but still fail if:

- nobody wants anything specific;
- the desire is unrelated to the active story;
- the scene outcome changes nothing;
- the next scene would happen the same way if this scene vanished.

This does **not** mean all scenes require external confrontation. Contemplative, relational and epistemic scenes can still alter the next intention.

---

# 3. Plot-based intention vs scene-specific intention

A useful distinction:

### Plot-based intention
A durable goal generated by the story's central disturbance.

### Scene-specific intention
The present action a character chooses because of the latest consequences.

For IVDIVO:

`BOOK WANT / HUMAN PROBLEM`
→ `CURRENT STORY STATE`
→ `SCENE-SPECIFIC ACTION`

A subplot scene passes only if it either:

1. changes the A-story directly; or
2. changes a relationship, resource, belief, obligation or constraint that later collides with the A-story.

This protects ordinary life without allowing parallel filler.

# 4. Intention needs both opposition and support

Rosenfeld is useful because she does not reduce story to endless thwarting. If every intention is only blocked, the protagonist eventually stops moving and the book becomes artificially adversarial.

## IVDIVO pressure pattern

`INTENTION`
→ sometimes `BLOCKED`
→ sometimes `COMPLICATED`
→ sometimes `PARTIALLY ACHIEVED`
→ sometimes `SUPPORTED`
→ always `NEW STATE`

Reader energy comes from changing pressure, not permanent denial.

### Failure modes

- every scene ends in failure;
- every institution obstructs the teens;
- every adult is wrong;
- help always hides betrayal;
- a successful system is immediately revealed as evil.

For `Book Two`, Voss/Lattice must retain genuine utility. Otherwise Ethan's later decision has no moral cost.

# 5. Scene endings as operators

Rosenfeld's `zoom-in / zoom-out` distinction becomes useful when treated as an **ending operator**, not a template.

## ZOOM-IN ENDING
Leaves the reader close to:

- a decision;
- emotional pulse;
- private realization;
- relationship fracture;
- unresolved personal question.

## ZOOM-OUT ENDING
Widens to:

- systemic implication;
- new scope of danger;
- larger mystery;
- social consequence;
- future pressure.

## IVDIVO rule

A scene ending should usually perform at least one operation:

`DECISION | CONSEQUENCE | REVEAL | REFRAMING | RELATIONSHIP CHANGE | PRESSURE TRANSFER | QUESTION UPGRADE`

### Failure mode: end-of-scene restatement

Do not end by explaining what the scene has already demonstrated.

Bad pattern:

`behavior proves growth → narrator summarizes growth → short emphatic line repeats theme`.

This is one of the mechanisms that previously contributed to the synthetic/chopped cadence problem.

# 6. Transition economy — cross-source convergence

Rosenfeld's scene transitions align strongly with Gary Provost and James Scott Bell:

- most time/place movement should be quiet;
- uneventful transport/logistics should be compressed;
- a full scene is justified only when the transition itself changes plot, character, relationship, information or stakes.

## IVDIVO transition rule

If nothing load-bearing happens during a movement/process:

`ACKNOWLEDGE → ARRIVE → CONTINUE STORY`.

Do not dramatize:

- walking to every room;
- every security check;
- every train/car trip;
- every equipment setup;
- repeated orientation procedures;
- routine meals;
- repeated message exchanges.

Unless one of those creates:

`CHOICE | ACCESS CONFLICT | RELATIONSHIP TURN | EVIDENCE | COST | HUMOR WITH CHARACTER FUNCTION | WORLD RULE WITH CONSEQUENCE`.

### Why this matters to “обрубки”

Micro-dramatizing nonessential process tends to produce:

`action sentence → reaction fragment → dialogue → micro-thought → blank line → next micro-action`.

The cure is often **narrative selection / compression**, not merely joining short paragraphs.

# 7. Contemplative scenes — recovery, not parking

Contemplative scenes slow narrative time and allow a character to digest prior action and decide how to act next.

## Proper placement

`HIGH-PRESSURE EVENT`
→ `AFTERMATH / PROCESSING`
→ `CHANGED INTERPRETATION`
→ `NEW DECISION`
→ `RE-ENTRY INTO ACTION`

### Valid functions

- grief or fear becomes actionable;
- a relationship is reinterpreted;
- evidence changes meaning;
- a character decides what not to do;
- moral cost becomes legible.

### Failure mode: contemplative pileup

Two or more low-action reflective scenes in sequence require inspection. If each only rephrases the same dilemma, merge/compress or convert one into action/relationship pressure.

# 8. First-scene burden

The revised edition adds a useful opening discipline:

A first scene should efficiently establish:

- protagonist;
- ordinary world;
- an immediate problem / pressure;
- dramatic tension;
- inciting movement or imminent inciting movement;
- enough setting to orient, not an encyclopedia.

## IVDIVO opening gate

By the end of the opening scene the reader should be able to answer:

`WHO AM I FOLLOWING?`
`WHAT DO THEY WANT NOW?`
`WHAT IS ALREADY NOT WORKING?`
`WHY SHOULD I READ ONE MORE SCENE?`

Not necessarily:

`WHAT IS THE ENTIRE COSMOLOGY?`

# 9. Cross-source comparison

## Rosenfeld + Swain
Convergence: a scene should generate a changed state and new forward pressure. Swain formalizes stimulus/response and scene/sequel more tightly; Rosenfeld is more flexible on scene types.

## Rosenfeld + McKee
Convergence: scenes are active units that change value/pressure. McKee is stricter about turning/value change; Rosenfeld offers more practical scene-construction routing.

## Rosenfeld + Hodgins
Convergence: scene form depends on function; not every narrative unit requires conflict-as-war. Hodgins provides broader alternatives and whole-manuscript integrity checks.

## Rosenfeld + Provost + Bell
Strong convergence: do not dramatize uneventful transitions. Compression is a craft decision, not laziness.

## Limit
Rosenfeld's checklists can become formulaic if every scene is forced to display every element explicitly. IVDIVO uses them diagnostically, not mechanically.

# 10. New production gates

### Gate KE — Prior-Consequence Intention
A scene intention must be traceable to prior story consequences or a durable subplot pressure that will collide with A-story.

### Gate KF — A-Story Intention Link
The scene must alter the main mission or a load-bearing relationship/resource/belief that later alters the main mission.

### Gate KG — Intention Complication
The scene must meaningfully change the character's route: block, complicate, support, partially satisfy or redirect.

### Gate KH — Result Changes Next Intention
A scene result must alter what someone does next.

### Gate KI — Scene Ending Operator
Ending must perform a defined function: decision, consequence, reveal, reframing, relationship change, pressure transfer or question upgrade.

### Gate KJ — No End-of-Scene Restatement
Do not narratively repeat an emotional/theme conclusion already shown by behavior.

### Gate KK — Transition Economy
Uneventful time/place/process movement is compressed.

### Gate KL — No Logistics Dramatization
Routine logistics earn scene-time only when they produce choice, conflict, evidence, cost or character-relevant world pressure.

### Gate KM — Contemplative Recovery Placement
Reflection follows an event worth processing and must produce changed interpretation or next action.

### Gate KN — Contemplative Pileup Check
Consecutive reflective scenes require explicit justification and differentiated function.

### Gate KO — First-Scene Burden
Opening must establish protagonist, current want/problem, story pressure and forward curiosity without front-loading the world bible.

### Gate KP — Adjacent-Scene Integrity
Every scene is tested against both the previous and next scene: what arrives from before, and what it causes after.
