# 1. MCKEE — DIALOGUE

## 1.1 Dialogue is verbal action, not recorded conversation

McKee treats dialogue as purposeful behavior. Fictional talk is selected, compressed, layered, and designed to cause change; ordinary chatter copied literally is not automatically realistic or dramatic.

### IVDIVO DIALOGUE-ACTION TEST
For every meaningful line identify:
`SPEAKER WANT | TARGET | TACTIC | EXPECTED EFFECT | ACTUAL REACTION`.

If the line has no intended effect on another person, on the speaker, or on the situation, classify it as possible clutter, atmosphere, or deliberate pause rather than assuming it is dramatic dialogue.

## 1.2 Three simultaneous functions: exposition, characterization, action

A useful dialogue line may carry story facts, reveal the speaker, and perform an action. These functions should overlap where possible rather than appearing as separate authorial chores.

### IVDIVO MULTIFUNCTION DIALOGUE CARD
`ACTION FUNCTION | CHARACTER FUNCTION | INFORMATION FUNCTION | SUBTEXT FUNCTION | RELATIONSHIP/STATUS EFFECT`.

A line need not perform all five, but long exchanges in which most lines perform only information delivery are suspect.

## 1.3 Exposition should function as ammunition

Characters normally possess information about their world, history, and relationships. Instead of explaining shared facts for the reader, they can weaponize, conceal, trade, accuse with, bargain with, or resist those facts while pursuing goals.

### IVDIVO EXPOSITION-AS-AMMUNITION TEST
Before expositional dialogue ask:
1. Why does this speaker need to say this **now**?
2. What does the speaker want to achieve by saying it?
3. Why can the listener not simply reply, “I know”?
4. Does the fact change leverage, interpretation, trust, fear, or decision?
5. Could the information be withheld until the reader actively needs it?

## 1.4 Said / unsaid / unsayable

McKee distinguishes surface expression from consciously withheld thought/feeling and deeper drives that may remain outside the character's awareness. This creates a layered model of dialogue rather than a binary text/subtext model.

### IVDIVO THREE-LAYER DIALOGUE MAP
`SAID | CONSCIOUSLY UNSAID | NOT YET SAYABLE/KNOWN TO SELF`.

The third layer is optional and must not be used to invent arbitrary hidden motives after the fact. It is useful when established behavior repeatedly implies a pressure the character does not consciously articulate.

## 1.5 On-the-nose writing collapses character depth

If characters continuously state their fullest thoughts and emotions, scene interpretation becomes trivial. Direct honesty is still allowed when psychologically and situationally earned; the problem is universal transparency.

### IVDIVO DIRECTNESS TEST
For each important exchange choose intentionally among:
`DIRECT | PARTIAL | DEFLECTED | MASKED | MISREAD | SILENCED`.

Do not force subtext into every sentence. A sudden direct statement can become powerful precisely because preceding material was guarded.

## 1.6 Beat design: dialogue progresses through tactics and reactions

McKee's dialogue design ties each beat to action/reaction, tactic, story value, turning point, deep character, exposition, and character-specific verbal style.

### IVDIVO BEAT LEDGER
For high-value dialogue scenes:
`BEAT | SPEAKER ACTION | TACTIC | TARGET REACTION | VALUE CHARGE | NEW LEVERAGE/INFO | TACTIC CHANGE`.

The exchange must progress. Repetition of the same argument with synonyms is not escalation.

## 1.7 Character-specific dialogue requires limitation, not ornament

Different voices emerge from differences in knowledge, culture, vocabulary, habitual syntax, metaphor field, social strategy, emotional restraint, and what a character refuses or is unable to say. Adding slang mechanically is insufficient.

### IVDIVO VOICE DIFFERENTIATION TEST
For each core character define:
`KNOWLEDGE RANGE | VOCABULARY FIELD | DEFAULT SENTENCE SHAPE | DIRECTNESS | STATUS STRATEGY | HUMOR MODE | TABOO/AVOIDANCE | METAPHOR SOURCES | STRESS DEFORMATION`.

Under stress, voice may change, but the change should reveal pressure rather than erase identity.

## 1.8 Silence and pause can be actions

A pause is not empty when withholding, refusing, recalculating, punishing, protecting, inviting, or forcing another person to continue.

### IVDIVO SILENCE TEST
Annotate consequential silence with its function. If the silence has no effect and does not alter reader interpretation, it may be decorative stage business.

# 2. MCKEE — CHARACTER

## 2.1 Character is designed through contradiction

McKee's recurring principle is that dimensionality emerges from sustained, intelligible contradictions rather than a list of unrelated traits. The contradiction must recur under different pressures and remain psychologically legible.

### IVDIVO DIMENSION TEST
For each major character record 2–4 dimensions as tensions, not adjectives:
`QUALITY A ↔ QUALITY B | TRIGGER A | TRIGGER B | COST | CLIMACTIC TEST`.

Bad version: “kind, funny, brave, angry.”
Better version: “protective toward vulnerable people ↔ controlling when afraid of losing them.”

## 2.2 External events and inner character must recursively reshape each other

McKee describes a two-way design loop: events cause inner reactions/change; discoveries about the inner character cause the writer to redesign events that better expose or pressure that nature.

### IVDIVO CHARACTER-PLOT RECURSION
`EVENT PRESSURE → INNER INTERPRETATION → CHOICE → OUTER CONSEQUENCE → NEW SELF-KNOWLEDGE/DEFENSE → NEXT EVENT DESIGN`.

This prevents a separate “character arc track” from running beside plot without affecting it.

## 2.3 Cast design is not population; it is a revelation system

A supporting character earns space by drawing out a specific trait, contradiction, truth, desire, fear, or behavioral mode in another character. Relationships are therefore part of character architecture.

### IVDIVO CAST FUNCTION CARD
For every recurring cast member:
`RELATION TO MAIN ACTION | WHAT THEY WANT | WHAT THEY PROVOKE IN PROTAGONIST | WHAT PROTAGONIST PROVOKES IN THEM | UNIQUE CONFLICT AXES | UNIQUE STORY FUNCTION | REMOVE/MERGE TEST`.

If two supporting characters expose the same dimension, carry the same information, and create the same pressure, test whether they should be merged.

## 2.4 A cast should oppose on multiple axes

Simple one-axis opposition (“good vs evil”, “brave vs cowardly”) often flattens both characters. More compelling casts combine resemblance and difference across values, tactics, status, desire, history, and moral limits.

### IVDIVO CAST COUNTERPOINT MATRIX
Compare core cast across:
`GOAL | VALUE | METHOD | STATUS | FEAR | RELATIONSHIP NEED | MORAL LINE | WORLD MODEL | STRESS RESPONSE`.

Purpose: produce interactions that reveal rather than merely label differences.

## 2.5 No single relationship reveals a whole person

Different relationships expose different selves. A teen may be dominant with friends, deferential with a parent, defensive with a teacher, tender with a sibling, and competitive with a teammate without becoming inconsistent.

### IVDIVO RELATIONAL SELF TEST
For every core character define at least three relationship contexts and ask:
`WHAT PART OF SELF APPEARS HERE THAT CANNOT APPEAR ELSEWHERE?`

The variation must come from relational conditions, not random authorial switching.

# 3. SEGER — MAKING A GOOD SCRIPT GREAT

## 3.1 A simple main story can carry complex character life

Seger explicitly separates the directional main story from the dimensional work contributed by characters and subplots. Complexity need not mean multiple competing main plots.

### IVDIVO STORY-HIERARCHY TEST
State:
`A-STORY = concrete directional action`
`B-STORY = strongest relationship/character line`
`C-LINES = secondary human pressures`.

If the A-story cannot be told simply, diagnose whether the book has confused complexity with lack of hierarchy.

## 3.2 Subplots enrich, complicate, and reveal—but depend on a viable plotline

Seger emphasizes that subplots may reveal vulnerability, dreams, transformation, relationships, or create turns and unpredictability. Too many subplots reduce development time for the main story.

### IVDIVO SUBPLOT CONTRACT
Every B/C line must answer:
`WHAT DOES THIS REVEAL/CHANGE? | WHERE DOES IT COLLIDE WITH A-STORY? | WHAT MAIN DECISION DOES IT ALTER? | WHAT PAYOFF DOES IT EARN?`

A subplot that never changes action, interpretation, relationship, cost, or climax is a candidate for deletion/merger.

## 3.3 Motivation → goal → action forms a character spine

Seger distinguishes why a character starts acting, what outcome is pursued, and what actions follow. If one part is missing, direction weakens.

### IVDIVO CHARACTER SPINE
`MOTIVATING CHANGE → GOAL → SERIES OF ACTIONS → OBSTACLE → ADAPTATION → FINAL ACTION/RESULT`.

Cross-check with Truby/Stanislavski/Swain: the chain should remain causal and character-specific, not a static description of desire.

## 3.4 Conflict requires incompatible aims, but conflict can exist at several scales

Seger catalogues inner, relational, social, situational, cosmic, and group-level conflict. For IVDIVO the useful transfer is not the taxonomy itself but the need to externalize enough of the pressure to affect behavior.

### IVDIVO CONFLICT-LAYER MAP
For a major sequence record:
`INNER | RELATIONAL | INSTITUTIONAL/SOCIAL | SITUATIONAL/ENVIRONMENTAL | LARGE-SCALE`.

Do not force every layer into every scene. Across a long book, multiple layers can make the same main objective harder in different ways.

## 3.5 Act-two / middle movement requires changing action points

Seger's middle-act toolkit includes obstacles, complications, reversals, twists, and action/reaction movement. The transferable rule is that the middle must alter the problem, not repeat it.

### IVDIVO MIDDLE DIVERSITY TEST
Across consecutive major sequences, vary the dominant pressure:
`OBSTACLE | COMPLICATION | REVERSAL | REVELATION | RELATIONSHIP TURN | COST INCREASE | METHOD FAILURE | GOAL RECLASSIFICATION`.

Repeated “try again, fail again” scenes require a changed variable or compression.

## 3.6 Distinct voices should survive removal of character labels

Seger recommends reading dialogue aloud and testing whether speakers remain identifiable without names. This is a practical diagnostic rather than a literal requirement for every line.

### IVDIVO BLIND-DIALOGUE TEST
Sample 20–30 lines from a scene, remove names, and ask whether core speakers remain distinguishable through:
`word choice | syntax | knowledge | attitude | social strategy | rhythm | avoidance`.

If identification depends only on catchphrases, the differentiation is shallow.

# 4. CROSS-SOURCE SYNTHESIS

## 4.1 Dialogue should be generated from conflict, not decorated after scene design

McKee Dialogue + Seger + Stanislavski-derived work already in IVDIVO:

`CHARACTER WANT → OPPOSING WANT → TACTIC → VERBAL/NONVERBAL ACTION → REACTION → TACTIC CHANGE → TURN`.

Dialogue is the linguistic surface of this engine.

## 4.2 Exposition becomes strongest when it changes power

McKee's “ammunition” principle combines with IVDIVO mystery/reveal rules:

`FACT → WHO KNOWS IT → WHO NEEDS IT → WHY REVEAL NOW → LEVERAGE/INTERPRETATION CHANGE → DECISION/CONSEQUENCE`.

This converts lore delivery into action.

## 4.3 Character complexity belongs in the relationship web

McKee Character + Seger subplots + existing counterpoint work:

A character's contradictions should be **activated by different people and situations**, not described in a dossier and left dormant.

`DIMENSION → RELATIONSHIP TRIGGER → BEHAVIOR → OTHER PERSON'S REACTION → CONSEQUENCE`.

## 4.4 Supporting characters need independent wants and instrumental story function

Do not create “helper characters” who exist only to display the protagonist. Cast members need their own local desires, but their inclusion in this particular book must also affect the story/relationship system.

## 4.5 Directness and subtext are dynamic variables

Cross McKee Dialogue with Baxter/Seger:

The production rule is not “always use subtext.” It is:
`HOW MUCH CAN THIS PERSON SAY HERE, TO THIS PERSON, UNDER THIS PRESSURE?`

Changes in directness can themselves become relationship turns.

# 5. NEW PRODUCTION GATES — BI THROUGH BU

## GATE BI — VERBAL ACTION
Every substantial dialogue beat must have an intended effect or a deliberately chosen non-action function.

## GATE BJ — EXPOSITION AS LEVERAGE
Shared facts cannot be spoken merely for the reader; expositional dialogue must be motivated by conflict, need, discovery, teaching, bargaining, accusation, concealment, or another active function.

## GATE BK — THREE-LAYER SUBTEXT
Important scenes may track said / consciously unsaid / not-yet-sayable material without inventing arbitrary unconscious motives.

## GATE BL — TACTIC PROGRESSION
Dialogue conflict must progress through changed tactics/reactions; synonym repetition fails.

## GATE BM — CHARACTER-SPECIFIC LANGUAGE
Voice differentiation must come from worldview, knowledge, culture, syntax, social strategy, rhythm and avoidance—not decorative slang alone.

## GATE BN — FUNCTIONAL SILENCE
Pauses and silence must alter leverage, interpretation, pressure, or behavior when foregrounded.

## GATE BO — DIMENSIONAL CONTRADICTION
Major character dimensions must be recurring, context-triggered contradictions rather than unrelated adjective lists.

## GATE BP — CHARACTER/PLOT RECURSION
External events must alter inner state/decision, and character decisions must alter subsequent events.

## GATE BQ — CAST REVELATION FUNCTION
Every recurring character must expose or pressure something unique in the story/relationship system.

## GATE BR — CAST COUNTERPOINT
Core characters require multi-axis similarities and differences; one-axis moral labeling is insufficient.

## GATE BS — SUBPLOT CAUSAL COLLISION
A subplot must change main-story action, interpretation, cost, relationship, or climax; otherwise merge/cut/compress.

## GATE BT — MOTIVATION-GOAL-ACTION SPINE
A protagonist's initiating motivation, concrete goal, and significant actions must form a legible causal chain.

## GATE BU — BLIND-DIALOGUE DIFFERENTIATION
Core voices should remain substantially distinguishable in anonymized dialogue samples without reliance on catchphrases.

# 7. LIMITS / FAILURE MODES

1. McKee's dialogue framework is strongly dramaturgical; literary prose can intentionally include digression, banal talk, or non-goal speech for texture, comedy, alienation, realism, or thematic effect. Such passages need a conscious function rather than automatic deletion.
2. “Subtext under every line” should not become mechanical obscurity. Direct speech is often realistic and can be dramatically decisive.
3. Cast members should not be reduced to mere tools for the protagonist. IVDIVO keeps independent agency: every major supporting character needs their own credible wants and boundaries.
4. Seger's three-act/page-count discussion is screen-specific. IVDIVO transfers directional functions and turning logic, not fixed screenplay page ratios, to novels/audio fiction.
5. The “goal attained at the end” formulation is not universal; failure, transformed goals, tragic endings, and partial success remain valid. The operational requirement is a concrete resolution of the governing question.
6. Voice tests must avoid caricature. Dialect, accent, class markers, or cultural vocabulary are not substitutes for psychological and strategic differentiation.
