## 1. DISTURBANCE ≠ COMMITMENT

A disturbance changes attention. A commitment transition changes the available future.

**DISTURBANCE → OPTIONS STILL OPEN → COMMITMENT TRANSITION → NEW OBLIGATIONS/OPTIONS → SUSTAINED MAIN ENGINE**

### Gate B80-G01 — Disturbance Function
An early event must create a durable question, pressure, responsibility or changed expectation. It does not have to be violent.

### Gate B80-G02 — Commitment State Change
A major structural transition must change at least one of:
- available actions;
- accepted responsibility;
- relationship obligation;
- evidence status;
- institutional standing/authority;
- environmental constraints;
- cost of withdrawal.

A location change by itself does not qualify.

## 2. WALK-AWAY DIAGNOSTIC

Bell asks whether the protagonist can simply leave the plot.

IVDIVO expands this beyond physical entrapment.

### Gate B80-G03 — Earned Adhesive
If a focal agent remains engaged, the reason must be legible and character/world-specific.

Acceptable adhesive can include:
- responsibility voluntarily accepted;
- duty generated by a role already established;
- relationship or promise;
- evidence that makes inaction consequential;
- consent given to another party;
- bounded authority that cannot ethically be ignored;
- environmental condition;
- personal stake in a unique nonfungible opportunity.

### Gate B80-G04 — No Artificial Lock-In
Do not add kidnapping, death, arbitrary legal threat, debt, salary pressure or catastrophe merely to stop a character leaving.

### Gate B80-G05 — Post-Money Adhesive Firewall
In orbital cities, financial desperation is not the default adhesive. Use trust, role, time, access to a genuinely unique event, responsibility, relationship, reputation, consent or safety.

### Gate B80-G06 — Robot Independent Adhesive
A conscious robot remains in the causal engine for its own reasons. Human need cannot be the robot's sole binding force.

## 3. SECOND MAJOR TRANSITION = FINAL-PROBLEM BOUNDING

Bell frames a later doorway as clue/discovery/crisis/setback that pushes toward the ending.

IVDIVO generalizes the function:

**NEW EVIDENCE / SUCCESS / FAILURE / AUTHORIZATION / RELATIONSHIP CHANGE → OLD STRATEGY EXPIRES → FINAL PROBLEM BECOMES BOUNDED → DECISIVE ACTION BECOMES POSSIBLE**

### Gate B80-G07 — Endgame Enabler
The late major turn must narrow or redefine what must now be decided/done.

### Gate B80-G08 — Success May Turn the Story
A working repair, successful experiment, truthful disclosure or earned permission can create the endgame as legitimately as a setback.

### Gate B80-G09 — No Mandatory Disaster
Do not manufacture a crisis merely because a structural model expects one.

### Gate B80-G10 — Reveal-to-Action
If the late turn is a revelation, it must change plan, options, responsibility or interpretation strongly enough to alter action.

---

## 4. FUNCTIONAL BEGINNING / MIDDLE / END

Do not use Bell's approximate page markers as compliance targets.

### Gate B80-G11 — Beginning Function
Establish enough focal life/world/relationship context to make the durable story problem meaningful, while creating a credible reason to continue.

### Gate B80-G12 — Middle Function
Develop consequences, strategies, relationships, countermodels and changing information. Avoid repeated confrontation with the same geometry.

### Gate B80-G13 — Ending Function
Resolve the local causal question, establish cost/aftermath and changed state, then optionally open a saga hook.

## 5. FAILURE MODES

### FM-B80-01 — Scenery Doorway
New habitat/ring/school/ship is mistaken for a structural turn although options and obligations are unchanged.

### FM-B80-02 — Artificial Adhesive
Characters stay only because the author adds coercion that the story did not earn.

### FM-B80-03 — Percentage Retrofit
Strong material is displaced to satisfy 20%/75% markers.

### FM-B80-04 — Setback Monopoly
Every major transition is failure, producing misery-as-momentum and numbing the reader.

### FM-B80-05 — Death-Stakes Inflation
Trust, autonomy, consent or role stakes are escalated to injury/death without causal need.

### FM-B80-06 — Act Label Without State Change
A chapter is declared a new act while the causal engine remains functionally identical.

### FM-B80-07 — Human-Only Adhesive
A conscious robot is kept in the plot because humans need it, with no independent objective or consent.

### FM-B80-08 — Reveal Without Revectoring
A major clue expands lore but changes no decision, model or plan.

## 6. CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON

### Bell ↔ McKee
Both emphasize major reversals/transitions. McKee provides stronger flexibility on placement; Bell provides a useful practical walk-away question. IVDIVO retains function, rejects fixed timing.

### Bell ↔ Truby
Both seek causal unity. IVDIVO uses ONE DOMINANT CAUSAL ENGINE while allowing distributed objectives and distributed intelligence.

### Bell ↔ Rosenfeld / LaPlante
Bell is more confrontation-centric. Rosenfeld/LaPlante broaden causal movement to relationship, emotional and epistemic changes. IVDIVO therefore tracks multiple state types, not only victory/setback.

### Bell ↔ Vogler
Bell's doorways overlap threshold language. Both become optional diagnostics, not mandatory mythic stages.

## 7. IVDIVO TRANSITION AUDIT

For any proposed major turn, record:

1. PRE-STATE — what can each key agent do/leave/refuse?  
2. EVENT / CHOICE — what happens?  
3. NEW FACT / OBLIGATION — what is now true?  
4. OPTION CHANGE — what became possible/impossible/costlier?  
5. RELATIONSHIP CHANGE — who owes/trusts/fears/refuses whom differently?  
6. INFORMATION CHANGE — what model/evidence status changed?  
7. NEXT STRATEGY — what must someone now do differently?  
8. MAIN-ENGINE LINK — how does this alter ONE MAIN STORY?

If answers 3–7 are nearly empty, the beat is probably not a genuine structural transition.
