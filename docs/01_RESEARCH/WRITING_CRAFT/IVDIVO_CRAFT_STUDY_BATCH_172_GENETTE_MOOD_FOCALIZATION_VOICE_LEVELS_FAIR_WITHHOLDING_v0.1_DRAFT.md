## 1. Core distinction

The strongest production distinction from Genette is:

`WHO SPEAKS ≠ WHO PERCEIVES / KNOWS`.

Grammatical person, narrator identity, focalizer, knowledge access and reader inference are separate variables.

A scene may fail because one of those variables is wrong even when the underlying event is correct.

## 2. Distance / speech modes

Genette separates narrative of events from narrative of words and distinguishes several ways of presenting speech/thought:

- narratized speech/thought;
- transposed / indirect discourse;
- free indirect discourse;
- reported/direct discourse;
- immediate discourse / interior monologue.

Production rule:

`DIRECT WORDING IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY MORE TRUE OR MORE INTIMATE`.

Exact wording earns real-time space when the wording itself changes consent, evidence status, trust, authority, relationship terms, commitment or decision.

Otherwise indirect/narratized presentation is fully legitimate.

## 3. Show/tell firewall

Narrative cannot literally reproduce nonverbal reality; it always selects and mediates.

Therefore:

`SHOW ≠ GOOD / TELL ≠ BAD`.

The useful variables are:
- amount of information;
- directness / distance;
- narrator visibility;
- focal access;
- reader-time cost;
- evidentiary function.

A highly detailed scene may still have a very visible narrator. High immediacy and high narratorial presence can coexist.

## 4. Focalization

Genette's useful descriptive families:

- zero/nonfocalized;
- internal fixed;
- internal variable;
- internal multiple;
- external.

IVDIVO uses them as local diagnostics, not book-wide purity rules.

### Locality principle

A focalization rule may operate over a scene, paragraph or short passage rather than an entire novel.

`BOOK POV DESIGN ≠ EVERY SENTENCE MUST HAVE ONE IDENTICAL DISTANCE / ACCESS MODE`.

### Multiple focalization

The same event can be presented from more than one consciousness. This earns reader-time only if the second presentation changes:

`EVIDENCE / INTERPRETATION / RELATION / MORAL COST / ACTION`.

## 5. Alterations: paralipsis and paralepsis

### Paralipsis

Narrative withholds information that the active focalization/knowledge code would normally imply should be available.

Mystery danger:

`POV KNOWS X → NARRATION PRETENDS X IS NOT PRESENT → LATE SURPRISE`.

This is suspect unless a real discourse convention, memory condition, narrating-time distinction or other defensible mechanism explains the omission.

### Paralepsis

Narrative gives information beyond what the established focalization code authorizes: another mind, unseen event, unavailable fact.

Production test:

`WHO COULD KNOW THIS? → HOW? → AT WHAT TIME? → THROUGH WHICH SOURCE?`

If there is no answer, fix POV/access rather than decorating the line.

## 6. Perception vs interpretation

A focalizer can perceive a trace accurately and misunderstand it. The reader may infer more than the focalizer without narrator explanation.

Core evidence chain:

`WORLD FACT → OBSERVED TRACE → POV ACCESS → POV INTERPRETATION → READER INFERENCE → CORROBORATION STATUS`.

This is a high-value mystery mechanism because it allows fair information parity without forcing the investigator to understand every clue immediately.

## 7. Inner speech is sourced information

Proustian examples show that inward thought can be self-deceptive, incomplete, hypothetical or simply wrong.

Therefore:

`CHARACTER THOUGHT / SELF-REPORT ≠ MOTIVE FACT`.

This applies equally to human and nonhuman persons.

For conscious robots:

`SELF-REPORT → OWN MODEL/CLAIM`, not automatic metaphysical truth about the system or personhood.

## 8. Modalizing language

Words such as `perhaps / seemed / appeared / probably` can legitimately preserve hypothesis status.

But they can also become an author's covert way of smuggling privileged knowledge.

Audit:

`WHOSE UNCERTAINTY? → WHAT EVIDENCE EXISTS? → IS THIS POV HYPOTHESIS OR NARRATOR KNOWLEDGE?`

Multiple plausible hypotheses can be stronger than one covertly privileged explanation.

## 9. Voice — narrating instance

Narrator is not the same as author.

For fiction:

`AUTHOR → constructs NARRATING INSTANCE → tells STORY to NARRATEE/READER POSITION`.

A narrator may perform multiple weighted functions:
- narrative;
- directing/organizational;
- communication;
- testimonial/attestation;
- ideological/commentarial.

These functions are diagnostic, not a checklist.

## 10. Time of narrating

Genette distinguishes:
- subsequent;
- prior/predictive;
- simultaneous;
- interpolated narrating.

Narrating time affects knowledge.

In retrospective first-person, the narrator-now may know much more than hero-then. Choosing to stay limited to hero-then is a deliberate focalization choice, not a grammatical necessity.

Production passport:

`NARRATING NOW → HERO THEN → WHAT WAS KNOWN THEN → WHAT IS KNOWN NOW → WHAT IS DISCLOSED NOW → WHY`.

## 11. Narrative levels and source provenance

Levels include:
- extradiegetic;
- intradiegetic/diegetic;
- metadiegetic.

For IVDIVO the practical value is source lineage. A story told by a witness inside another story is not first-order evidence simply because narration renders it vividly.

Use:

`CURRENT CLAIM → ORIGINAL SOURCE → INTERMEDIARIES → EDIT/SELECTION → CURRENT NARRATOR → CORROBORATION`.

### Metadiegetic functions

An embedded story may:
1. explain how the current state came about;
2. create thematic analogy/contrast that influences a decision;
3. act causally through the act of telling itself.

Therefore `STORY TOLD INSIDE STORY` can be a real plot action.

## 12. Metalepsis firewall

Crossing narrative levels can be a deliberate metafictional/fantastic device. In ordinary mystery or YA prose, accidental level collapse is a continuity error.

`METALEPSIS MUST BE INTENTIONAL AND CONTRACT-COMPATIBLE`.

## 13. Person

Do not reduce narrator design to `first person / third person`.

Useful distinctions:
- `HETERODIEGETIC`: narrator absent as character from told story;
- `HOMODIEGETIC`: narrator present as character;
- `AUTODIEGETIC`: narrator is the central hero.

Grammatical person does not determine intimacy, reliability or information access.

## 14. Reader / narratee

Narratee and actual reader are not automatically identical.

Reader-model implication:

`WHO IS THE NARRATOR ADDRESSING? → WHAT DOES THAT RECIPIENT ALREADY KNOW? → WHAT MUST BE EXPLAINED?`

This is useful for avoiding exposition that exists only because the author wants the audience briefed.

## 15. IVDIVO gates

### G172-01 — SPEAKER/PERCEIVER SPLIT
Record narrator, focalizer and knowledge owner separately.

### G172-02 — LOCAL FOCALIZATION
Do not force whole-book purity where local variation is controlled and functional.

### G172-03 — PARALIPSIS FAIRNESS
If focal character knows critical X, hiding X requires a defensible narrative mechanism.

### G172-04 — PARALEPSIS ACCESS
Every extra-mind / unseen-event claim must have authorized access or be revised.

### G172-05 — INTERPRETATION STATUS
POV interpretation never auto-promotes to world fact.

### G172-06 — INNER SPEECH STATUS
Thought is a sourced self-model, not privileged truth.

### G172-07 — MODALIZATION OWNER
`perhaps/seemed/probably` must belong to a legible uncertainty owner.

### G172-08 — NARRATING-TIME PASSPORT
Separate hero-then knowledge from narrator-now knowledge.

### G172-09 — EMBEDDED SOURCE LINEAGE
Document/report/memory/story-within-story keeps original provenance.

### G172-10 — NARRATOR FUNCTION WEIGHT
Narrator commentary may exist, but its role and reader contract must be intentional.

### G172-11 — DIRECT SPEECH PAYMENT
Exact dialogue/thought only when exact wording pays.

### G172-12 — NO POV BY PRONOUN
Changing `I` to `he/she` does not repair focalization or knowledge defects.

## 16. Failure modes

## 17. Cross-source synthesis

### Genette × Zunshine

Focal access and source-monitoring combine into:

`TRACE → ACCESS OWNER → INTERPRETATION → SOURCE TAG → ALTERNATIVE MODEL → STATUS UPDATE`.

### Genette × le Carré

Embedded report is not independent corroboration merely because it is retold by another person. Maintain lineage.

### Genette × Kress / Card / Rosenfeld

POV depth, person and focalization are different controls. A close-third narrative can vary distance without changing grammatical person.

### Genette × Baxter / Chiarella / McKee Dialogue

Subtext and listener inference can exceed what a focal character consciously understands. But nonverbal cues remain inference material, not motive proof.

Relationship state may change access but never evidence truth.
