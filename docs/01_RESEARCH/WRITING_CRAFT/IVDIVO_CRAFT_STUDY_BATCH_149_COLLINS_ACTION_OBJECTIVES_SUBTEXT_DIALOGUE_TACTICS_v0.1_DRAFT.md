## Core synthesis

### A. Scene objective is a present directional force

Useful generalization:

`PRESENT WANT / ACTIONABLE QUESTION → TACTIC → RESPONSE / RESISTANCE / SUPPORT → UPDATE → OUTCOME → CHANGED STATE`.

The objective may be physical, verbal, cognitive, relational, evidentiary, or ethical. Agency is not synonymous with running, fighting, arguing, or pressing a button.

### B. Objective and tactic must be separated

A character can retain the same scene-level objective while changing how they pursue it. If the same tactic repeats under different dialogue, the scene may be spinning rather than progressing.

Production check:
`WHAT DO I WANT? → WHAT AM I TRYING NOW? → WHAT DID THE RESPONSE TEACH ME? → WHAT DO I TRY NEXT?`

### C. Shell-scene diagnosis, widened beyond Collins

Collins correctly attacks scenes whose characters exist mainly to carry backstory/exposition. IVDIVO widens the test so quiet reflection/setup may survive if it creates a meaningful state change.

`NO PRESENT CAUSAL FUNCTION + INFORMATION ONLY → SUMMARY / MERGE / REBUILD TEST`.

### D. Subtext is motivated non-equivalence

Subtext is not simply “words mean the opposite.” It emerges when the surface wording and the operative objective are not identical because the speaker:
- does not want to state the real objective;
- does not need to state it because the listener shares context;
- is protecting status, relationship, privacy, consent boundary, evidence, or self-image.

Mechanism:
`SURFACE UTTERANCE → UNSTATED OBJECTIVE / PRESSURE → LISTENER INFERENCE → RESPONSE → CHANGED TERMS / TACTIC`.

### E. Directness is a tool, not a defect

Collins importantly notes that major turning points may demand honesty. Therefore:

`ONGOING AVOIDANCE → SUBTEXT MAY CARRY PRESSURE`

but

`EARNED DECISION / BOUNDARY / CONFESSION / REFUSAL → DIRECT SPEECH MAY BE THE PAYOFF`.

No SUBTEXT-EVERYWHERE rule.

### F. Exposition must have a character owner

Dialogue cannot exist merely because the author wants the reader informed.

Gate:
`INFORMATION X → WHO WANTS/NEEDS TO SAY/ASK/CONCEAL IT? → WHY NOW? → WHY THIS PERSON? → WHAT CHANGES AFTERWARD?`

If the answers are weak, route to narration, summary, document, or a differently motivated scene.

### G. TIME channels: Thought / Inflection / Movement / Expression

These are optional delivery channels for subtext, not four boxes to tick after each line.

Use only the smallest combination required to keep reader inference legible.

Anti-pattern:
`LINE → LOOK → HAND → THOUGHT → LINE → FACIAL EXPRESSION`.

That produces MICROGESTURE CONFETTI and often worsens choppy prose.

### H. Listener model matters

Subtext is relational. The same line means different things depending on what the listener knows, expects, fears, or refuses to acknowledge.

`SPEAKER OBJECTIVE + LISTENER MODEL + SHARED HISTORY → ACTUAL COMMUNICATION`.

### I. Evidence firewall

Subtext is a reader/POV inference mechanism, not an evidence upgrade mechanism.

`PAUSE / INFLECTION / EVASION / ATTRACTION / SILENCE → INTERPRETATION`.

It does NOT automatically yield:
`MOTIVE FACT / GUILT / PERSONHOOD / ONTOLOGY / CORROBORATION`.

## Where Collins is too rigid for IVDIVO

1. Four Ds remain one optional plotting lens; no mandatory Denial or Devastation.
2. A scene can have quiet causal value without overt conflict.
3. Not every conversation needs subtext.
4. TIME must not become a body-language checklist.
5. Collins's strong show-don't-tell bias is moderated by LaPlante, Rosenfeld, Prose, Lodge, Scofield and Swain: summary/indirect narration are legitimate modes.
6. A private authorial motive model is not automatically objective truth about the character or world.

## Cross-source synthesis

### McKee Dialogue
Converges on dialogue as verbal action/tactic. IVDIVO rejects screenplay-specific pressure toward constant compression/visual substitution when novelistic interiority or syntax earns reader time.

### Baxter — The Art of Subtext
Adds inflection, staging, non-listening, social language and tonal contradiction. Baxter strengthens the warning that subtext is carried by the whole staging, not merely hidden definitions under each sentence.

### Bernays/Painter — What If?
Fictional dialogue is selected and shaped, not a transcript. Subtext can imply what is not said; exposition-heavy dialogue sounds stagey.

### Scofield
Scene weight and admission precede line craft. Direct dialogue is expensive reader-time and should be protected where exact words change state.

### Zunshine / le Carré / Fludernik
All strengthen the evidence firewall: what a speaker implies, what a POV infers, what a source claims, and what the world establishes remain separate levels.

## Production gates

1. OBJECTIVE LEGIBILITY — identify what the scene owner is trying to alter now.
2. OBJECTIVE ≠ TACTIC — track tactical changes separately.
3. SHELL-SCENE TEST — information-only scenes need strong admission evidence.
4. SUBTEXT MOTIVATION — why is direct speech avoided or unnecessary?
5. HONESTY PAYOFF — direct speech may be the earned turn.
6. EXPOSITION OWNER — information in dialogue needs an in-story causal owner.
7. TIME MINIMUM — use only the channels necessary for legibility.
8. NO MICROGESTURE PATCH — do not repair flat dialogue by adding physical business after every line.
9. LISTENER MODEL — track what each listener actually knows/hears/infers.
10. RELATIONSHIP-SPECIFIC SELF — the same character should communicate differently across relationships because objectives and risk differ.
11. EVIDENCE FIREWALL — subtext does not corroborate clues.
12. PERSONHOOD FIREWALL — nonhuman depth is not measured by human-like gestures.
13. DIRECT/INDIRECT ROUTING — if exact words do not change state, test indirect dialogue or summary.
14. NO CHOPPY SUBTEXT — analytic beats do not dictate sentence/paragraph boundaries.

## IVDIVO implications

### ONE MAIN STORY
A dialogue scene belongs when its verbal actions change the dominant causal engine directly or through evidence, relationship, consent, authority, commitment, strategy, or future options.

### YA
Youth voice/subtext comes from status, intimacy, embarrassment, autonomy, peer belonging, privacy, competence and future-cultural norms—not a current-Earth slang costume.

### Post-money orbital society
Do not make young characters discuss money-equivalent concepts just to create familiar dialogue stakes. They can speak indirectly about role, access, trust, time, consent, qualification, reputation, belonging, intimacy or responsibility.

### Conscious robots
The robot's surface utterance and human interpretation must remain distinct. Silence/compliance may be tactical, constrained, uncertain or consensual; do not infer motive without evidence.

### Mystery / ROOM 917
Relationship state can change access and investigative behavior but never replaces corroboration. `TRUST / ATTRACTION / CONFESSION / INFLECTION ≠ EVIDENCE`.

### B07 / nonhuman distributed personhood
Once personhood is independently established, dialogue about the entity cannot substitute for the entity's own decision. Its consent/refusal must be part of the option architecture.

### Prose rhythm
Before adding gestures/thoughts around dialogue:
`SCENE NECESSITY → DIALOGUE MODE → PARAGRAPH INTENT → READER-STATE CONTINUITY → SENTENCE RHYTHM`.
