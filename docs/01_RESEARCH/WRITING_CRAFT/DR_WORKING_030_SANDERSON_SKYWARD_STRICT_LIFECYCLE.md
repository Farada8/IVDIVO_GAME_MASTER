## PROVISIONAL MECHANISMS — DO NOT TREAT AS FINAL BOOK SYNTHESIS

### 1. Public ideal / actual access gap
`PUBLIC VALUE CLAIM → FORMAL RULE → INFORMAL EXCEPTION → WHO BENEFITS / WHO BEARS COST → APPEAL PATH`.

The institution can sincerely celebrate equal contribution while simultaneously embedding unequal access, inherited privilege or stigma. The discrepancy itself is story material.

### 2. Stigma as a causal public model
`PAST PUBLIC NARRATIVE → AUDIENCE / INSTITUTION MODEL → ACCESS / REPUTATION CONSEQUENCE → CHARACTER RESPONSE`.

A family or person can be governed by a public interpretation before individual behavior is examined. Public narrative is not evidence of individual motive.

### 3. Privilege / competence separation
`UNFAIR ACCESS ≠ INCOMPETENCE`.
`COMPETENCE ≠ LEGITIMACY`.

A privileged cadet may be genuinely excellent; an unfair institution becomes more credible when privilege and competence are not collapsed into one moral variable.

### 4. Theory / embodied competence separation
`THEORETICAL KNOWLEDGE → LIVE TEST → EMBODIED LIMIT → PRACTICE → UPDATED SELF-MODEL`.

Knowing procedures, reading manuals or scoring well does not equal physical execution under load.

### 5. Prior-skill transfer
`OLD DOMAIN SKILL → NEW CONTEXT → REFRAMED AFFORDANCE → SPECIFIC ADVANTAGE`.

Transfer must be specific and bounded. One successful transfer does not make a character universally competent.

### 6. Dream ownership
`SHARED HISTORY / PREPARATION ≠ SHARED LIFE GOAL`.

A friend may help train for a prestigious path and later discover that the path was never truly theirs.

### 7. Success without obligation
`QUALIFICATION / SUCCESS ≠ DUTY TO CONTINUE`.

The right to withdraw after informed experience is a youth-agency mechanism, not cowardice by default.

### 8. Partial inclusion
`FORMAL ROLE ≠ SOCIAL / LOGISTICAL BELONGING`.

A person may gain the title or qualification while remaining excluded from infrastructure, status, community or ordinary privileges of participation.

### 9. Official safety rule / informal status incentive
`WRITTEN POLICY → PEER STATUS SIGNAL → INFORMAL NORM → ACTUAL BEHAVIOR`.

Formal institutional policy may be sensible while subculture rewards behavior that undermines it.

### 10. World wonder / embodied discomfort
`DESIRED WORLD → ACTUAL SENSORIUM / BODY RESPONSE → MIXED EMOTION → ACTION`.

A longed-for environment can be beautiful and physiologically/socially uncomfortable at the same time.

### 11. World rule becomes tactic
`WORLD TECHNOLOGY / PHYSICS → CONSTRAINT + AFFORDANCE → TEAM TACTIC → CONSEQUENCE`.

Worldbuilding earns page space when it changes what characters can do.

### 12. Language / silence motive firewall
`OBSERVED SILENCE / SHORT SPEECH → MULTIPLE HYPOTHESES → CONTEXT → MODEL UPDATE`.

Limited language, accent, unfamiliarity or attention can be mistaken for hostility or coldness. Behavior is evidence, not motive.

### 13. Success → responsibility
`COMPETENT ACTION → REAL SUCCESS → MORE TRUST / ACCESS / RESPONSIBILITY → HARDER NEXT CHOICE`.

Do not create setback monoculture merely to preserve tension.

### 14. Team complementarity
Distinct cadets require distinct competence fingerprints, goals, embodied limits, humor registers and risk responses. Ensemble differentiation cannot rest on cosmetic dialogue alone.

### 15. Public/defensive want vs deeper positive want
A character may defend a socially legible goal (redeem a family name, prove critics wrong) while a more affirmative desire (flight, wonder, mastery) also exists. Do not force every difference into trauma/hidden-wound doctrine.
