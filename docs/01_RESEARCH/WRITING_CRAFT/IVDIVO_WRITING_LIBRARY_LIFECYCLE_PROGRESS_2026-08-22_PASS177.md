## Highest-value findings
1. Separate generation from evaluation; the “ferocious editor” belongs after material exists.
2. Humor should be character-owned through worldview, attention, relationship and present want.
3. Comic premise can be modeled as a gap between incompatible expectations/models.
4. Tools/categories are diagnostic/generative aids, not pigeonholes.
5. Setup/payoff requires enough shared information for audience decoding.
6. Rule of three is optional and can become forced; even Vorhaus says so.
7. Placeholder weak jokes can mark function in discovery and be rewritten later.
8. Running gags require new source/context/stakes/meaning; pure repetition decays.
9. Callback can close a loop when earlier material returns with new meaning.
10. Micropressure can echo the macro question/theme without forcing overt conflict.
11. Wordplay/alliteration/puns are polish layers; they cannot fix a broken scene.
12. Details must pay in POV, character, humor or causality; cleverness alone is not enough.
13. `LOCAL FIX → UNINTENDED CONSEQUENCE → NEW FIX → GREATER COUPLING / COST` is a strong causal escalation model.
14. Secondary comedy strands must pay into ONE MAIN STORY.
15. Plot logic and character logic must intersect; dynamic spectacle never outranks causal integrity.
16. Beta-reader effect is evidence; proposed repair is a hypothesis.
17. Good unused humor belongs in an idea bank rather than being forced into the current book.
