## 1. Ordinary-Life Relationship Competition

Purpose: protagonist price must damage a life that existed before anomaly. Relationship cannot be hostage function or a person whose only purpose is to say “don’t risk your career.”

### OPTION R1 — SHARED-HOUSING PARTNER / CLOSE FRIEND — WORKING WINNER

**Relationship type:** close friend / roommate / planned co-housing partner. Exact friendship/romance status remains UNKNOWN until B02–B07 continuity makes it earned.

**Why orbital-native:** certification changes shift eligibility, commute geometry, residence priority or affordability. Two young adults have planned their next housing step around both work routes and hero’s expected permanent operations placement.

Before crisis:
- they already have separate jobs/lives;
- they are not constantly together;
- they have been comparing housing options, commute time and costs;
- friend worries hero treats certification as proof of human worth rather than one career step;
- hero thinks friend does not understand what permanent placement would finally mean.

**Concrete shared WANT:** move into a better-located or more stable shared unit after hero’s certification becomes permanent.

**Relationship contradiction:** friend benefits from hero succeeding professionally, but dislikes how hero is narrowing life around sponsor/director and one institutional track.

During crisis friend is **NOT trapped** in sealed sector by default. They are safe enough to remain a relationship actor rather than hostage. Their own work/transport disruption gives ordinary civilian perspective on lockdown.

When hero begins risking certification, friend does not automatically celebrate rebellion. They may argue that one disputed old Annex is not worth throwing away years of work.

Aftermath: suspension/placement loss makes planned housing move impossible or materially delayed. Friend does not abandon hero, but shared future must be redesigned.

**Changed future:** relationship survives only if both stop treating original plan as relationship itself.

Strengths:
- connects job → money → transport → housing → friendship;
- does not require romance if later continuity does not support it;
- gives price after physical danger is over;
- strongly orbital rather than generic family stakes;
- friend remains independent civilian.

Risk: housing system must be established naturally; do not invent a punitive caste allocation just to raise stakes.

**Score:** 47/50 provisional.

## 2. R1 Relationship Arc

**START — INDEPENDENT LIVES:** hero and friend have different jobs/shifts and already need effort to see each other. Closeness is chosen, not automatic cohort proximity.

**AFFILIATION:** jointly planning next housing/commute step; ordinary conflict over routes, shifts, food, money and what “near” means in orbital geography.

**VULNERABILITY:** hero indirectly reveals that permanent operations placement means no longer being just the local apprentice people like, but someone trusted with real responsibility.

**EXTERNAL COST:** sponsor/director controls path to certification; work choices eat shared time.

**DEFENSE:** friend criticizes hero’s tunnel vision; hero hears lack of respect for career.

**CRISIS PRESSURE:** after hard seal worsens conditions, hero could protect career by staying inside command. Friend initially wants exactly that: do not throw away future over Smith’s disputed document and one cadet model.

**TURN:** as evidence changes, friend does not tell hero what to do. Relationship forces the emotional question: if hero keeps career by transferring responsibility for a crisis they helped worsen, what has career proved? Do not write this as polished thesis dialogue.

**CLIMAX CONSEQUENCE:** hero takes responsibility and loses clean placement path.

**AFTERMATH:** planned move/housing application must be withdrawn, delayed or changed. Friend may be angry about material consequence while accepting hero’s choice.

**RESOLUTION:** they build a smaller/less convenient next plan rather than restoring original one.

## 3. Annex Historical Mini-Chain — Three Options

History must produce present evidence and moral complication. It must not become a novella inside the novel.

### OPTION H1 — THE FIRST STATIC SEAL — WORKING WINNER

**Historical era:** early human orbital expansion, before current orbital institutions matured and before formal Confederation integration. Exact year UNKNOWN.

**Past event:** orbital transfer/work site records same broad class of multi-system sequence disagreement. Old Earth Security interprets through then-existing containment model and orders total static seal.

**Old wrong model:** unknown factor assumed to behave like a thing that can cross boundary with people/material; tighter isolation appears safer.

**What actually happens:** after full seal, control discrepancies accelerate. Local workers report brief unsynchronized access cycles temporarily reduce some failures, but central command treats reports as contamination risk / unreliable field improvisation.

**Historical price:** OES keeps site sealed too long while protecting secrecy and trying to determine what is inside. People die or suffer permanent harm that earlier evacuation likely would have reduced. Exact casualty number UNKNOWN and should remain modest enough for plausible compartmentalization but serious enough to stain institution.

**Critical human action:** local rescue/operations lead creates a moving segmented passage against or at edge of containment order. Passage reduces control disagreement and enables evacuation.

**What OES learns:** not ontology. Only empirical relation:
`STATIC COMPLETE ISOLATION + THIS SIGNATURE CLASS = WORSENING`  
`CONTROLLED DYNAMIC PASSAGE = REDUCTION / ESCAPE WINDOW`

**Institutional failure/crime:** after-action record compartmentalized because it reveals:
- initial OES order materially worsened event;
- local operators had useful evidence before central command accepted it;
- secrecy delayed rescue;
- institution later presented outcome as successful containment.

**Why Annex is written:** minority inside OES refuses to let practical rescue lesson vanish. They encode narrow emergency exception while politically damaging full case remains restricted.

**Present Smith choice:** disclose compromised history. This costs bargaining/status credibility with orbital director but gives hero complete evidence chain rather than requiring trust in OES authority.

## 6. Story Clock — Relative Engineering v0.1

**Status:** WORKING STORY CLOCK / TO ENGINEERING LOCK. No exact physics numbers are canon.

Three competing clocks:
A. occupancy/control degradation;  
B. exterior evacuation throughput;  
C. safe detachment deadline.

Conflict is strong because no conservative option clears everyone before C.

### 6.1 Clock Shape
- **T0 — INITIAL SIGNATURE / HARD SEAL:** hero executes full static isolation.
- **T1 — EARLY DRIFT:** small timing/order mismatches become visible; multiple hypotheses remain.
- **T2 — E1 COORDINATION DRIFT:** manual reconciliation required; director authorizes investigation/limited exterior options.
- **T3 — EXTERIOR EXTRACTION STARTS:** tenders/lifeboats remove easiest/highest-risk groups, proving director is attempting rescue.
- **T4 — E2 ACTUATOR CONFLICT:** safe occupancy window shortens; Annex relevant but disputed.
- **T5 — MIDPOINT TEST:** cadet boundary-cycle prediction succeeds; isolation is now part of problem.
- **T6 — DETACHMENT PREPARATION DEADLINE:** later separation may endanger main habitat; director begins irreversible prep.
- **T7 — LAST FULL CONSERVATIVE CHOICE:** exterior extraction cannot clear enough people; director chooses whole-habitat survival absent validated corridor; hero gets career-saving stand-down.
- **T8 — LAST OVERRIDE / FIRST SEGMENT:** hero performs disputed moving-corridor segment.
- **T9 — EVIDENCE TURN:** isolated mismatch falls; receiving sector remains clean under defined sensors; director revises model and becomes rescue authority.
- **T10 — FULL EVACUATION RACE:** moving corridor clears remaining population before E3/E4.
- **T11 — PHYSICAL RESOLUTION:** static sealed-state condition ends; trapped population out; main emergency closed.

### 6.2 Working Ratios — NOT FINAL NUMBERS
- Total trapped = `P`.
- By midpoint, exterior extraction removes roughly `10–20% of P`.
- By safe detachment commit, exterior methods remove at most roughly `30–40% of P`.
- Therefore `60–70%` remain when director must protect larger habitat.
- Moving segmented corridor, once validated/supported, has roughly `4–6×` exterior throughput because it uses designed internal transit rather than vehicle-by-vehicle docking.
- Initial hero test is tiny: one segment / one small convoy, enough to create evidence but not solve rescue alone.

### 6.3 Working Time Fractions
Define total safe-control window T0 → projected E3 as 100%.

- T0 seal = 0%.
- T2 clear drift = 15–20%.
- T3 exterior extraction = 25%.
- T4 E2 = 35–40%.
- T5 midpoint test = 50%.
- T6 detachment prep = 62–68%.
- T7 detachment commit decision = 75–80%.
- T8 first override segment = 82–86%.
- T9 proof = 86–90%.
- T10 rescue expansion = 90–98%.
- T11 resolution before E3/E4 = under 100%.

Story rhythm model, not technical timing spec.

### 6.4 Why Clock Cannot Be Reset Easily
No false “power-cycle it” solution.

Working constraints:
- affected state spans multiple independently powered control domains;
- full shutdown removes life-safety control occupants still need;
- restarting under same static boundary recreates condition;
- evacuation/detachment must happen while enough control coherence remains.
