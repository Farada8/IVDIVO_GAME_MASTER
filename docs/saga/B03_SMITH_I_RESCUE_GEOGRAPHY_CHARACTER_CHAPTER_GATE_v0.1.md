## 1. FACTUAL NORMALIZATION — SLOVENIAN RESCUE FRAME

Public-source grounding used only to normalize functions, not to copy real incidents.

Current Slovenia has a 24/7 protection-and-rescue system with a national notification centre and 13 regional notification centres handling 112 calls; regional centres dispatch and coordinate multiple rescue services including mountain rescue. Civil Protection is multi-level rather than one all-powerful rescue service. Helicopter mountain rescue is highly useful but operationally dependent on weather and other conditions.

**Story consequence:** Jana should not be written as “commander of every rescuer in the valley.”

### Jana revised working role

**Incident Operations Lead / Regional Civil Protection operational commander for the Koren emergency**, with earlier field experience as a mountain-rescue medic.

She can:
- coordinate the incident picture;
- allocate regional rescue resources within her remit;
- interface with 112 dispatch / rescue services / municipal emergency actors;
- set shared incident objectives and safety thresholds;
- negotiate access and information rules with OES.

She cannot:
- personally dictate every field tactic to mountain-rescue teams;
- command police/military/medical systems outside applicable incident arrangements;
- overrule OES anomalous containment inside OES’s narrow lawful remit by fiat.

This makes the climax stronger: distributed professional judgment already exists institutionally and Jana must stop over-centralizing it in herself.

## 2. WORKING PHYSICAL GEOGRAPHY PASSPORT

The valley remains fictional. Exact Slovenian location/names are not copied from a real disaster.

### G0 — Koren Regional Emergency Centre

Functions:
- receives/records 112-type calls;
- displays incident status;
- dispatches conventional services;
- houses the shared incident picture;
- becomes the first place where precursor calls appear as impossible but properly recorded emergency traffic.

Story use:
- reader sees normal procedure before anomaly;
- OES suppression has a physical/institutional target;
- dispatcher Nika’s later forwarding has a believable source.

### G1 — Upper Gorge Road

Narrow mountain access with rockfall galleries and limited turning/parking points.

Story use:
- moving rescue vehicles changes casualty geometry;
- closure can protect one zone while displacing traffic/resources into another;
- weather can remove the route naturally.

### G2 — Hydro Intake / Service Gallery

Not a giant dam-catastrophe story. A modest hydro/water-management system creates workers, service passages, gates and an independently real flood/debris risk.

Story use:
- supplies a genuine non-anomalous emergency;
- provides trapped workers and technical choices;
- prevents the climax from becoming “only radio theory.”

### G3 — Lower Gorge Bridge / Choke Point

Single important crossing used by residents and emergency traffic.

Story use:
- a prediction-driven closure can itself create congestion/exposure;
- local team may later find/use a smaller service crossing unavailable to central planning.

### G4 — Settlement A

Small inhabited settlement below a possible debris-dam/flash-flood path.

Story use:
- gives meso-scale human stakes;
- avoid “millions will die” inflation.

### G5 — Mountain Communications Relay

A redundant emergency communications repeater with automatic failover links between valley sectors.

Important rule:
This is ordinary resilience engineering. Under normal disaster conditions automatic reconnection is good. During the precursor loop, automatic reconnection keeps rebuilding one centralized prediction-driven response network.

Climax use:
Smith must physically isolate cross-links after software/remote commands keep restoring failover. No secret OES magic button.

### G6 — Helipad / Air Rescue Staging

Helicopter is an available but weather-limited resource, not guaranteed transport.

Story law:
- use early enough that reader knows capability;
- worsening weather progressively removes reliability;
- never cancel helicopter “because plot needs it” without established weather/visibility/wind condition.

No arbitrary countdown.

Natural clock:

- helicopter windows narrow;

Working progression, not final timestamps:

## 4. JANA KOVAČ — CHARACTER / VOICE PASSPORT v1.1

### Goal
Keep people alive and maintain a rescue system that responders can trust.

### Private desire
Leave behind competent successors who can make hard calls without waiting for her personal permission.

### Fear
A defensible decision of hers will kill another rescuer.

### Contradiction
She preaches local judgment but centralizes the most dangerous calls in herself.

### Social/ordinary life
WORKING, deliberately modest:
- long separated/divorced;
- sibling relationship strained by uneven care for an older parent;
- family pressure appears through logistics/time, not hostage melodrama;
- she has people who know her as a tired daughter/sister, not only as commander.

### Money/status
Not a wealth subplot. Her pressure is public-service responsibility, staffing and the future credibility of the rescue system after impossible warnings.

### Humour
Dry operational humour. She mocks abstractions that ignore terrain, weather or actual people, but does not produce constant one-liners.

### Voice DNA
Vocabulary: road, team, access, weather, patient, route, who-is-there, what-can-they-see.
Sentence shape: concise under pressure; longer when challenging policy.
Interruptions: cuts off speculative ontology if it does not change current rescue action.
Status behaviour: gives field specialists room when she trusts them; becomes controlling when casualty risk rises.
Lie/avoidance pattern: understates fatigue and personal fear; does not falsify evidence.
Silence pattern: becomes quiet when people turn casualties into percentages.
Stress tell: asks for the name/location of the actual person behind an abstract casualty count.

### Arc
Beginning: “Central coordination exists so somebody owns the whole picture.”
End: “Central coordination must know when the whole picture is precisely what no one can safely own.”

Her climax change is operational: she issues objectives/limits and lets local commanders choose tactics rather than micromanaging a predicted script.

## 5. SMITH — VOICE / FIELD BEHAVIOUR PASS

Smith must not sound like Enia.

### Voice DNA
Vocabulary: evidence, sequence, threshold, access, known/unknown, before/after, who observed it, what changed because we acted.
Sentence shape: controlled and economical; questions often narrower than other people want.
Humour: understated, usually procedural irony rather than wit-performance.
Conflict tactic: separate observation from interpretation.
Stress behaviour: reduces variables, shortens language, assigns responsibilities.
Withheld information: speculative ontology and unverified archive material; not material safety evidence.
Error pattern: assumes that making a system more observable/controlled necessarily makes intervention safer.

### Development rule
After midpoint he does not become vague or philosophical. His questions become more relational while remaining operational:
- “What happened before the warning?” becomes “What did the warning make us do?”
- “Is the signal true?” becomes “Which parts remain true after we act on it?”
- “How do we suppress it?” becomes “Which response relation is sustaining it?”

## 6. NIKA — DISPATCHER FUNCTION / VOICE

**WORKING name.** Late 20s / early 30s, experienced emergency communications operator, not a naive junior.

### Want
Never be the person who had a usable warning and watched someone die because a distant rule said not to pass it on.

### Fear
That she cannot tell moral courage from dangerous self-importance.

### Mistake
Forwards one suppressed precursor warning to a trusted field contact.

### Result
The warning genuinely helps save someone, validating her morally — and the resulting response becomes part of the next precursor trace, validating Smith causally.

### Voice
Fast, highly structured call language at work; ordinary speech becomes messier after guilt/defensiveness enters. When hiding the forwarding she overexplains procedure rather than lying cleanly.

Do not turn her into whistleblower archetype or conspiracy exposition source.

## 7. OES SUPERVISOR FUNCTION

No cartoon secret-service boss.

Want: prevent Smith from improvising with a phenomenon whose previous response cascade killed people.

Right about:
- first direct responses can create casualties;
- warning content cannot be treated as benevolent intelligence;
- experimentation during live rescue has ethical limits.

Wrong/incomplete about:
- historical blackout doctrine is treated as if it were context-free;
- institutional secrecy prevented OES from learning the full moral/causal structure of its own success.

Tactic changes when evidence changes. If the supervisor simply repeats “blackout” after midpoint without processing data, rewrite.

## 8. CAUSAL CHAPTER MAP v0.2

This is now a proposed 29-chapter structure. Count remains WORKING; causality is the lock target.

### PART I — THE CALL BEFORE THE FALL

**CH1 — WRONG ADDRESS**
POV: Nika/Jana-limited local opening.
First 112-type distress call gives precise location. Team arrives: no incident. Quiet dread comes from a normal recording that should not exist.
Exit: hours later a related real accident occurs.

**CH2 — THE CALL THAT WORKS**
Second precursor call predicts a different accident. Jana sends a bounded response; civilians are saved.
Exit: impossible information now has moral value.

**CH3 — THE THIRD DEPLOYMENT**
Warning moves resources/vehicles. Secondary rockfall/incident kills a rescuer Jana trained.
Exit: warning may be hazard as well as aid.

**CH4 — DUBLIN**
Smith ordinary-life/Enia seed + OES signature match. He verifies jurisdiction/evidence before deployment.
Exit: he accepts case.

**CH5 — WHAT HAPPENED BEFORE THE CALL?**
Smith arrives, meets Jana/Nika, separates observations from interpretations, authenticates recordings.
Exit: historical precursor class plausible.

**CH6 — FIRST PRECURSOR DOCTRINE**
Smith imposes bounded information suppression / response restrictions.
Warnings stop. No new false deployments.
Exit: false victory.

### PART II — THE COST OF SILENCE

**CH7 — OUT OF THE SYSTEM**
Smith briefly returns Dublin because case appears contained. Enia ordinary interaction pressures his assumption that removed relationship = unchanged system.

**CH8 — THE BLOCKED CALL**
Koren receives another precursor warning. OES rule prevents distribution.
Exit: nothing happens immediately.

**CH9 — WHAT SILENCE COST**
A real incident later matches enough warning details; people die who might have received earlier attention.
Smith returns.
Exit: his correct procedure has a direct human price.

**CH10 — JANA’S LINE**
Jana challenges Smith. Local rescue still has to work while OES studies causality.
Exit: trust is damaged but both remain professionally necessary.

**CH11 — THE FORWARDED WARNING**
Nika secretly passes a later warning to a field contact. The information helps save a person/team.
Exit: morally useful unauthorized action.

**CH12 — THE DETAIL THAT SHOULD NOT EXIST**
Smith finds response-created content in the next trace. Nika’s action also deepened the loop.
Exit: anomaly is tracking response architecture.

### PART III — THE RESCUE INSIDE THE WARNING

**CH13 — CALLSIGN**
A helicopter callsign / dispatcher phrase appears in a warning before assignment/use.
Exit: independent-future model seriously damaged.

**CH14 — TEST, NOT THEORY**
Smith designs a bounded dummy response architecture with Jana/OES oversight. No dangerous field deployment.

**CH15 — MIDPOINT: WE ARE IN THE RECORDING**
New precursor content incorporates information created by the response plan itself.
Mission changes: warnings include probable response to warnings.

**CH16 — MORGEN PASS**
Smith requests deeper archive layer; access resistance is institutional/legal, not a puzzle-box gimmick.

**CH17 — THE PEOPLE REMOVED FROM THE REPORT**
Full historical case shows OES success plus deliberate concealment of the group denied warning.
Smith discloses material truth to present decision-makers rather than protecting institutional reputation.

**CH18 — TWO FUTURES**
Two proposed response architectures are followed by two differing credible precursor states, each with different casualties.
Exit / point of no return: no single correct prophecy can be chosen.

### PART IV — NO SAFE OUTSIDE POSITION

**CH19 — THE REAL STORM**
Independent conventional evidence confirms slope/water emergency. The anomaly is no longer the only danger.

**CH20 — BLACKOUT**
OES argues for full information blackout. Jana demonstrates rescue cannot operate safely blind during the developing real disaster.

**CH21 — OBJECTIVES, NOT SCRIPTS**
Smith/Jana build distributed response: local teams get verified present hazards, objectives, limits; precursor detail is quarantined.

**CH22 — FIRST DISTRIBUTED RUN**
The model reduces prediction alignment but costs speed and coordination. A patient/route suffers a real delay. New strategy has price.

**CH23 — FAILOVER**
Emergency network automatically reconnects relay sectors for resilience. Precursor specificity strengthens again.
Exit: system itself keeps rebuilding central predictability.

**CH24 — LOW POINT / GO TO THE RELAY**
Latest precursor states show severe casualties under both known centralized plans. Smith chooses physical relay segmentation while Jana must delegate local command.

### PART V — THE EMPTY RESCUE

**CH25 — CASCADE**
Slope/road/hydro events become real. Multiple local teams operate under objectives rather than one valley-wide script.

**CH26 — THE RELAY**
Smith + technical support reach mountain relay under worsening access and physically isolate automatic cross-links. Established geography/equipment only.

**CH27 — THE BEST WARNING YET**
A new precursor warning offers an apparently cleaner centralized route to a trapped group. The temptation is strongest because it is specific and plausible.
Smith chooses not to rebuild unified command around it.

**CH28 — THE THIRD ROUTE**
Local field team discovers an option not present in any warning. Rescue succeeds partially; another loss remains irreversible. Precursor coherence degrades as response becomes less centrally predictable.
Main physical emergency is contained by ordinary rescue work.

**CH29 — WHAT WE DID WITH THE WARNING**
Local case resolution: casualties accounted, Nika/Jana consequences, OES record correction/review, ordinary authority restored. Only after case closure: Dublin/Enia aftermath where Smith articulates changed operational model. No sequel hook required.

## 9. CHAPTER CAUSALITY TEST

Strong chain:
first call -> response changes state -> second call gains credibility -> third response kills rescuer -> OES suppression -> false victory -> genuine blocked warning -> preventable loss -> unauthorized use saves life -> response-created clue -> controlled test -> feedback model -> historical crime -> competing warning states -> real storm eliminates “do nothing” -> distributed model -> failover rebuilds problem -> physical segmentation -> seductive final warning -> Smith refuses script -> local third route -> loop decays -> case closes.

Major chapters cannot be freely reordered without breaking causality.
