## Purpose

Build the novel’s causal spine without chapter count or prose. Each turn must change action, information, relationship, risk, authority or future option. No scene-level polish. No franchise hook before resolution.

## Reference-Mechanism Routing — REFERENCE ONLY

Use:
- ordinary life/current pressure → immediate want → why now → attempt → resistance → turn → consequence;
- wrong/incomplete model → action test → cost → narrowing options → reclassification → crisis choice → tactical climax → local resolution → aftermath;
- relationship before ontology;
- wrong model causes action and price;
- aftermath returns to relationships.

## 1. Why Now / Initial Trigger Competition

Initial trigger must be routine civilization, not villain pushing a red button.

### Trigger A — RECOMMISSIONED HIGH-THROUGHPUT TRANSFER SPINE — WORKING WINNER

An older outer service/transfer spine has been rebuilt, extended or recommissioned as part of normal orbital growth and now connects mature residential/work zones to newer expansion infrastructure.

A new high-throughput operating pattern brings several previously semi-independent route/control domains into sustained coordinated use. During normal traffic, the spine crosses an operating condition under which a dormant/unknown legacy-class signature becomes observable.

Important: new system does **not** simply malfunction and create anomaly. It exposes a condition older OES records encountered under different technology.

Reader-level first effect: small impossible-looking sequence mismatches across physically independent local systems during ordinary transit.

Story value:
- world reveals expansion through work/commute;
- hero naturally belongs there;
- old/new infrastructure collision is thematic without lore dump;
- no special cargo/person MacGuffin;
- crisis grows from civilization functioning normally.

Risk: do not make this Book 2 Ring Six again. Problem is not optimization/control-loop engineering. Independent systems disagree about event order/state in a way ordinary control failure cannot explain; static boundary then causally changes phenomenon.

**Score:** 48/50 provisional.

### Trigger B — Returning Service Convoy
Concrete movement but drifts toward contaminated cargo/people and quarantine-vehicle structure. **40/50.**

### Trigger C — External Debris / Solar Disturbance
Physically clear but coincidence-driven and weakly tied to crossing engine. **34/50.**

**WORKING DECISION:** Trigger A. Exact engineering/recommissioning meaning remains TO WORLD/TECH LOCK.

## 2. Pre-Crisis Ordinary-Life Baseline

### Turn 1 — A Future Almost Affordable
Hero and R1 close friend/shared-housing partner compare a realistic housing/commute option tied to hero’s expected permanent operations placement.

Hero wants friend to commit because certification feels close enough to plan around. Friend wants a plan that does not assume hero’s entire life belongs to one career track.

**Change:** shared future becomes concrete — route, cost, schedule, ordinary inconvenience. Reader knows what losing placement later means.

### Turn 2 — Real Work, Not Symbolic Youth Participation
Hero begins normal operations shift in recommissioned transfer spine. Maintenance background appears through boring competence: local panel, service route, actual traffic habits.

Director/sponsor gives bounded real responsibility.

**Change:** private WANT and director relationship enter action.

### Turn 3 — Local Status / Humour / Fatigue
Small ordinary problem — route delay, coworker teasing, food, schedule collision, housing message — proves hero is not living inside an emergency novel yet.

Hero chooses work over a non-urgent shared-life obligation.

**Change:** small version of coming imbalance.

## 3. Inciting Incident / Hero’s Causal Error

### Turn 4 — Precursor
During routine high-throughput operation, two independent systems disagree about sequence/order in a way not immediately reconciled. Hero logs it. Local maintenance explanation remains plausible.

**Change:** question, not crisis.

### Turn 5 — Second Independent Mismatch
Another controller/physical state disagrees. Current protocol crosses unknown-system security threshold. Director has enough evidence to order hard seal.

**Change:** ordinary operations → security incident.

### Turn 6 — The Hero Does the Right Thing Wrong
Hero executes complete static isolation quickly and correctly. WANT matters: this is exactly the consequential shift on which permanent trust is built.

During sequence, hero sees one local timing irregularity that could justify pausing last sealing step, but classifies it as transition noise inside allowed procedure rather than escalating weak anomaly against clear order.

No sabotage. No idiocy.

**Change:** full static boundary completes; hidden amplification condition exists because of protagonist action.

### Turn 7 — False Containment Success
Outside sector, mismatches stop appearing in receiving systems. Security believes seal worked. Hero receives professional approval. Inside sector, people shelter temporarily.

**Change:** wrong strategy rewarded before it becomes costly.

**ACT I EXIT:** first internal control mismatch grows after seal rather than fading.

## 4. Act II-A — Containment Becomes a Problem

### Turn 8 — E1 Coordination Drift
Inside sector, doors, route states and command timestamps disagree enough for human reconciliation. Hero argues first for ordinary fault isolation because it protects current model and successful seal.

**Change:** hero becomes invested in wrong model.

### Turn 9 — Civilian Consequences Enter
Shelter runs long. Missed work, medication, childcare, deliveries and cross-sector family contact become practical problems. R1 friend outside remains safe but disrupted.

**Change:** crisis stops being abstract infrastructure.

### Turn 10 — Current Authorities Try Safe Option
Director authorizes exterior extraction of highest-priority groups and remote service attempts. Partially works.

**Change:** director earns reader credibility; containment is not abandonment.

### Turn 11 — Legacy Match Routes Smith into Story
Current orbital unknown-hazard query/archive cross-check returns restricted legacy OES match. Smith is involved because present system asks for missing historical evidence, not because franchise characters randomly meet.

Smith identifies narrow Emergency Annex but cannot establish applicability.

**Change:** new evidence source; authority conflict begins.

### Turn 12 — Annex Is Real and Uncomfortable
Provenance shows Annex genuine and once incorporated by reference into early orbital life-safety rules. Director refuses to change quarantine because authenticity ≠ present validity and public history says successful containment.

Hero sides with director more strongly than necessary.

**Change:** Smith has knowledge, not command; hero/director relationship temporarily strengthens around local legitimacy.

### Turn 13 — Exterior Extraction Hits Throughput Limit
Vehicle-by-vehicle evacuation removes some people but sequence drift slows docking/release. Projected clearance falls behind safe-control clock.

**Change:** conservative rescue real but insufficient.

### Turn 14 — E2 Actuator Conflict
Independent safety systems issue incompatible action order. Life-safety margin visibly shrinks.

Hero notices brief local manual boundary cycling during extraction temporarily improves one mismatch. Logs it but does not yet interpret it.

**Change:** routine clue grid contradicts static containment model.

**ACT II-A EXIT:** director begins formal detachment contingency planning.

## 5. Midpoint — Problem Reclassified

### Turn 15 — Limited Confederation Technical Query
Human/orbital actors use already permitted Contact technical-advisory channel because current models cannot explain cross-system ordering effect. Cadet/young representative with relevant knowledge joins bounded analysis.

No Confederation command enters habitat.

### Turn 16 — Cadet Does Not Solve It; Cadet Predicts
Cadet proposes falsifiable test: if static boundary is causally coupled, brief controlled change in one isolated local boundary should reduce a specific independent mismatch.

Director authorizes only tiny test. Hero executes/coordinates under current authority.

### Turn 17 — Prediction Works
Measured mismatch drops during controlled change, then begins returning under restored static isolation.

Does not prove city-safe corridor. Proves isolation is not neutral.

**Change:** core model flips.

### Turn 18 — Smith Reveals What OES Hid
Smith discloses restricted after-action evidence: OES static seal worsened old case; local operator evidence discounted; people harmed before moving passage worked; clean “successful containment” summary concealed institutional error.

Smith could offer operational rule without damaging context but chooses full disclosure.

**Change:** OES loses moral/negotiating authority while empirical evidence becomes more trustworthy.

**MIDPOINT VALUE TURN:** before, `OPENING = DANGER / ISOLATION = SAFETY`; after, `OPENING MAY BE DANGER / ISOLATION IS ALSO ACTIVE DANGER`.

No option is safe.

## 6. Act II-B — Safe Choice Starts Killing

### Turn 19 — Director Refuses False Binary
Director accepts midpoint evidence but rejects immediate full corridor because it does not prove dynamic passage cannot propagate hazard to city.

**Change:** opponent remains rational.

### Turn 20 — Hero’s Status Falls
Hero pushes for more boundary testing and compares maintenance logs beyond assigned analytical role. Director narrows credentials because hero is now operator + advocate.

**Change:** private WANT starts being lost before climax.

### Turn 21 — R1 Relationship Resists Heroic Self-Destruction
Friend/roommate learns hero may lose placement and argues for standing down because senior adults are responsible and evidence uncertain.

Hero reacts badly because friend has always questioned certification tunnel vision.

**Change:** ordinary relationship becomes operational pressure.

### Turn 22 — Hero Finds Modern Difference
Using legitimate maintenance knowledge, hero maps how old “moving passage” principle could translate to current infrastructure via segmented local life-safety controls and current route topology.

Hero also confirms exterior assets cannot clear enough occupants before detachment threshold.

**Change:** new action physically imaginable but unauthorized.

### Turn 23 — Detachment Preparation Becomes Visible
Director starts visible preparation: route clearing, resource reallocation, structural/control setup.

Civilian status changes from delayed evacuation to possible cut-away.

### Turn 24 — Annex Activation Conditions Converge
Smith authenticates pattern match. Two current channels confirm isolation-linked degradation. Cadet test corroborates. Current procedure has no validated solution inside remaining window.

One condition remains disputed: whether local operator can invoke old life-safety passage defense against standing quarantine.

**Change:** mechanism actionable but legally dangerous.

### Turn 25 — Director’s Strongest Move
Director reaches D3 logic: absent validated corridor, commit to detachment soon enough to protect main habitat.

Offers hero clean professional exit: stand down; senior command owns sacrifice; earlier hard seal was correct under information then available.

This is tempting because substantially true.

**ACT II EXIT / CRISIS:** hero realizes stand-down preserves career by allowing institution to own consequences of boundary state hero completed and now has evidence may be reversible.

## 7. Act III — The Last Override

### Turn 26 — Hero Takes Responsibility, Not Command
Hero cites narrow Annex life-safety basis, accepts named audit/liability and chooses one local passage action physically inside local emergency panel capability.

Director refuses normal authorization and revokes standard network credentials.

**Change:** clean professional future lost before outcome known.

### Turn 27 — Physical Movement to Local Control
Hero must reach/use local panel because central credentials locked. Maintenance background pays off through established service access, physical interfaces and manual passage sequence.

Not a hacking scene.

### Turn 28 — First Segment / Point of No Return
Hero begins tiny moving segmented corridor with small group already positioned for local life-safety transfer. Downstream closes before upstream exposure exceeds limit. No unrestricted city path.

If wrong, hero may create propagation path director feared.

### Turn 29 — First Result Briefly Ambiguous
One sensor improves; another lags. Receiving side no immediate matching signature but confirmation not instant. Hero must complete started local segment correctly rather than freeze halfway.

**Change:** skill matters after moral choice.

### Turn 30 — Evidence Crosses D4
Independent isolated mismatches drop as predicted. Receiving-sector sensors stay below propagation threshold. Movement of people does not reproduce effect; boundary-state change tracks improvement.

**Change:** director risk model must change.

### Turn 31 — Opponent Becomes Rescue Authority
Director does not apologize/surrender jurisdiction. Uses legitimate authority to halt detachment, allocate teams, open approved downstream resources, expand monitored corridor and coordinate exterior backup.

Hero remains local route operator, not city commander.

### Turn 32 — Full Evacuation Race
Execution challenge:
- sequence segments;
- manage crowd flow;
- react to residual control drift;
- protect receiving side;
- keep corridor dynamic enough not to recreate static state;
- clear vulnerable groups/workers before E3/E4.

Smith/cadet cannot solve operationally.

### Turn 33 — Last Group / System Reconfiguration
Final occupants clear. Sector transitions out of dangerous full-static configuration under controlled state. Mismatch collapses/falls to recoverable residual. Main habitat remains clean under available evidence.

**Change:** physical central conflict CLOSED.

## 8. Resolution / Aftermath

### Turn 34 — Immediate Professional Price
Director relieves/suspends hero and preserves logs for review. No vindictive speech. Hero knew this was part of act.

### Turn 35 — Director Relationship Resolves
Director report records both violation/disputed authority and that action produced evidence preventing worse outcome. Sponsorship ends/transforms; mutual respect survives without restoring old relationship.

### Turn 36 — R1 Shared Future Changes
Hero/friend confront housing/commute plan that cannot happen as designed. Friend may be materially angry. No instant elite replacement job. They choose smaller, delayed or different next arrangement.

### Turn 37 — Smith Aftermath
Smith ensures complete old case — useful rule + institutional wrongdoing — enters appropriate current record rather than another clean myth. OES remains active. No permanent team forms.

### Turn 38 — Cadet Aftermath
Cadet returns to limited Contact role with changed human relationship/question; no jurisdictional expansion granted as reward.

### Turn 39 — Hero End State
Hero no longer equates being trusted with procedure carrying responsibility. Behavior now reflects:
- evidence may come from institutions lacking authority;
- legitimate authority may operate on wrong model;
- accepting responsibility includes audit/cost;
- work is part of life, not proof of worth.

Show in action, not thematic speech.

### Turn 40 — Book Closed
People safe. Emergency over. Old future not restored. Relationships/institutions permanently changed.
