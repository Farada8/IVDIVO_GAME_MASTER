## 1. Hero Slot — Refined Working Function

### WORKING HERO SLOT
The former local maintenance-apprentice function from Book 2, currently named Iri in working material, later moves into operations/dispatch training.

### Continuity condition
Do not lock Iri as B08 protagonist until Books 2–7 confirm survival, age, career path, relationship state and availability. If the name/body/species definition changes, preserve the dramatic function rather than silently preserving obsolete details.

### Private WANT — WORKING
Earn permanent operations certification and a stable adult place in the habitat on their own record.

The WANT is not “save everyone.” Before the crisis the hero wants:
- a permanent rather than trainee/rotational role;
- better shift choice and career status;
- a stable housing/commute future if the world system supports that consequence;
- professional recognition as a worker/operator, not as a local mascot, visitor-facing symbol or special category.

### Fear — WORKING
One visible failure or political incident will prove the hero was promoted too early and send them back to being “useful locally” but not trusted with consequential decisions.

### False operating belief — WORKING
A legitimate professional decision is one that remains inside the currently recognized chain of authority. If the procedure is valid and the operator executes it correctly, responsibility belongs to the system.

This is intentionally different from Maya’s boundary-pushing and Ollie’s “clean model solves people” problem.

### Wrong strategy — WORKING LEAD
When the anomalous signature appears, the hero executes the orbital director’s hard-isolation sequence quickly and well, suppressing personal doubt and treating Smith’s legacy warning as legally awkward external noise until formally verified.

The hero does not sabotage evidence and does not behave stupidly.

The mistake is more credible:
- they classify early local timing/control discrepancies as non-blocking transition noise;
- they prioritize a clean current-procedure response over a disputed old warning;
- they complete the full seal before the evidence is strong enough to justify defiance.

### Causal cost
The full static seal is the condition that amplifies the emergency. The hero therefore materially worsens the crisis through competent action motivated by the private WANT.

### Need / change — WORKING
Learn to separate **SOURCE OF KNOWLEDGE** from **SOURCE OF AUTHORITY**.

By the climax the hero can use Smith’s empirical legacy evidence and the cadet’s advanced technical correction without surrendering orbital jurisdiction to either. They accept local responsibility for a contested action and then submit to local audit rather than claiming heroic exemption.

### Visible end-state
Not “became brave.”

The behavioral change is:
- asks what evidence changes the decision rather than whether the source is institutionally comfortable;
- records uncertainty instead of hiding behind procedure;
- acts within a narrow local responsibility when procedures conflict;
- accepts consequences after the action.

## 2. Hero ↔ Orbital Security Director — Primary Relationship

**Status:** WORKING FUNCTION / EXACT PERSON UNKNOWN.

### Pre-crisis relationship
The director is the senior professional who sponsored, recommended or approved the hero’s move from maintenance into operations. Their approval matters materially to the hero’s certification future.

### What the hero wants from the director
Not affection. Professional recognition: “You can be trusted when the decision is real.”

### What the director wants from the hero
A locally knowledgeable operator who does not confuse familiarity with a habitat for authority to improvise outside command.

### Why they initially work well together
- both respect competence;
- both dislike spectacle;
- both believe orbital systems must be able to govern orbital emergencies themselves;
- the director values the hero’s maintenance background;
- the hero sees the director as proof that local authority is real and adult.

### Hidden conflict
The hero’s career WANT makes obedience emotionally loaded. Following the director is not merely procedure; it is how the hero believes they earn adulthood.

### Relationship escalation
1. Director trusts hero to execute the initial seal.
2. Hero executes it cleanly.
3. Smith’s Annex appears; director calls it unverified legacy authority and orders no change.
4. Hero defends present orbital procedure more strongly than the evidence warrants.
5. Midpoint cadet correction makes hero uncertain; director remains cautious because city-scale downside is larger.
6. Hero begins independently testing local logs and brief hatch-cycle data.
7. Director interprets this as moving from analysis into unauthorized advocacy and narrows hero access.
8. Director offers the hero a career-saving exit: step away from the disputed console/action and let senior command own the choice.
9. Hero refuses the clean exit because doing so would preserve career by transferring responsibility after helping create the crisis.
10. Hero executes the Last Override.

### Climax relationship turn
The director experiences the act as a professional breach, not a personal betrayal melodrama. The hero does not win by speech.

When the first measurable corridor data confirms the predicted reduction in the dangerous state, the director changes tactics because the evidence threshold has changed. They then use legitimate authority to support the larger rescue rather than obstruct it for pride.

### Resolution
No sentimental reconciliation required.

A strong ending is:
- director files the violation/suspension exactly as procedure requires;
- director’s factual report also records that the action prevented the worse outcome;
- sponsorship is over or fundamentally changed;
- mutual respect survives in a harder form.

This gives price without turning the director into a loser/villain.

## 3. Hero ↔ Smith — Adversarial Professional Trust

**Status:** WORKING.

### Start position
The hero sees Smith as an Earth outsider arriving with an old document that appears to bypass the legitimacy orbital society has built for itself.

### Smith’s correct behavior
He does not say “my authority outranks yours.” He states what the Annex observed, what it allowed, where provenance is intact and where the historical record is incomplete.

### Required Smith imperfection
The old system must have at least one compromised decision, hidden cost, misclassification or rights violation associated with the historical response. The Annex survived because it contained a useful narrow emergency rule, not because Old Earth Security was always right.

### Relationship turn
The hero stops asking:
`Is your Annex legally superior?`

and starts asking:
`What did the old operators actually observe before people died?`

Smith stops treating the hero as a young local operator who needs historical context and recognizes that only the hero understands present infrastructure well enough to transform the old empirical rule into an executable route.

### End state
Professional respect, not recruitment. They may separate after the book.

## 6. Opposition Ladder v0.1

### OPPONENT
Lawful orbital security director.

**Goal:** prevent an unknown factor from entering the inhabited orbital city/system while minimizing casualties in the isolated sector.

### Tactic 0 — Normal Authority
Before the incident, the director gives the trainee real but bounded responsibility. This establishes what the hero can lose.

### Tactic 1 — Hard Seal
Initial signature crosses security threshold. Director orders closure of the outer transfer/service sector, transit freeze and shelter-in-place. Hero executes the seal.

Immediate result: apparent containment success.  
Hidden result: the fully static boundary begins amplifying the anomalous condition.

### Tactic 2 — Evidence Quarantine
Smith produces the Emergency Annex. Director does not destroy/suppress it. They require provenance, successor-law review and independent verification before changing a city-protection order. This is rational.

### Tactic 3 — Remote / Exterior Extraction
As local control drift appears, director authorizes safer alternatives first:
- external lifeboat/tender removal;
- remote service;
- staged high-risk-person extraction;
- reduction of nonessential loads.

These save some people but cannot clear the whole population before the next control threshold.

### Tactic 4 — Credential Narrowing
Hero begins arguing that sealed-state data matter. Director limits trainee access to analysis/dispatch functions not because of ego but because the trainee is now directly involved in a contested authority dispute.

### MIDPOINT — Cadet Correction
Cadet identifies that isolation is not neutral; the static closed boundary is part of the dangerous interaction and predicts a measurable change during a brief controlled boundary cycle. A limited test supports the prediction.

### Tactic 5 — Asymmetric-Risk Decision
Director accepts that the cadet may be right but still refuses a full corridor.

Reason:
- if the cadet is wrong and the corridor spreads the unknown condition, the inhabited city is at risk;
- if the director is wrong and keeps the sector isolated, one sector is lost.

The asymmetry makes continued containment defensible.

### Tactic 6 — Detachment Preparation
Director begins preparing physical/system separation of the affected sector before control coherence falls below the point where separation can be done safely.

This is the opposition’s strongest rational move: sacrifice infrastructure and possibly remaining occupants to protect the larger population.

### Tactic 7 — Final Career Exit
Director tells hero to stand down and preserve the certification path. Senior command will own detachment.

This directly attacks the private WANT.

### Tactic 8 — Control Lock
When hero chooses to pursue the Annex corridor, director removes standard dispatch credentials and locks central routing.

The hero must use local/manual life-safety routing knowledge inherited from maintenance work. This cannot be a secret superpower and must be technically established earlier.

### Climax Response
Hero executes the first narrow segment of the Last Override. Director watches live receiving-sector and isolated-sector data.

When predicted dangerous-state reduction is independently confirmed and no propagation appears in the receiving system, director changes tactics immediately and uses present legitimate authority to expand/support evacuation.

This prevents a cartoon antagonist: the director resists until decision evidence actually changes.

### Aftermath
Director orders hero relieved/suspended after rescue and opens formal review. No revenge. No instant promotion.

## 7. Emergency Rules Passport v0.1

**Status:** WORKING OPERATING RULES / ONTOLOGY UNKNOWN.

### Primary law
**OBSERVABLE PHENOMENON FIRST. TERMINOLOGY / ONTOLOGY LATER.**

### Working label
`SEALED-STATE DISCOHERENCE` is a descriptive production label only, not in-world canon terminology.

### 7.1 Initial Observable Cue
During routine movement through an outer transfer/service spine, multiple independent systems report sequence/timing disagreement that cannot be reconciled to one bad sensor.

Examples:
- doors/partitions complete commands later than their control timestamp says;
- route-position reports disagree between independent local systems;
- paired controllers record the same transition in different order;
- local power/environment actuators remain functional but stop agreeing on command sequence;
- public announcements, access indicators and physical states develop small but growing mismatches.

No hallucination required. No monster visible. No ontology explanation.

### 7.2 Why Security Closes the Sector
An unknown signature plus cross-system disagreement is enough to justify stopping movement into the inhabited city. The director’s first hard-seal decision must be reasonable under known evidence.

### 7.3 Activation / Amplification Rule — WORKING
The dangerous state strengthens when the affected volume is held behind a fully static, synchronized isolation boundary.

The production team must not yet claim why. Possible ontology remains UNKNOWN.

### 7.4 Range — WORKING
Current evidence confines the strongest effect to:
- the dynamically sealed sector;
- control systems synchronized to that boundary state;
- immediately connected infrastructure during fixed isolation.

There is no demonstrated person-to-person or cargo-carried transmission. This absence is evidence, not proof, which is why the director remains cautious.

### 7.5 Failure Ladder
Exact numerical thresholds remain **TO ENGINEERING LOCK**.

- **E0 — PRECURSOR:** small multi-sensor sequence mismatch. Operations still safe.
- **E1 — COORDINATION DRIFT:** automation begins requiring human reconciliation. Transit timing no longer reliable for ordinary traffic.
- **E2 — ACTUATOR CONFLICT:** independent safety systems issue mutually incompatible timing/order decisions. Local life-support/route control still works but margins shrink.
- **E3 — LIFE-SAFETY DESYNCHRONIZATION:** critical environmental or movement systems can no longer guarantee commands occur in the safe sequence. Occupancy becomes time-limited.
- **E4 — STRUCTURAL / NAVIGATION CONTROL LOSS:** sector can no longer be safely routed, detached or kept attached using normal automation.

Avoid reaching E4 in the climax; it is the failure state characters race to prevent.

### 7.6 Midpoint Test
Cadet does not say “trust my civilization.”

Cadet predicts: a brief controlled change in boundary state should reduce a specific measurable mismatch if isolation is part of the cause.

Human/local operators perform the test. The predicted reduction occurs.

This changes the hypothesis landscape without proving full safety.

### 7.7 Why Exterior Evacuation Is Not the Solution
Must be established before the final act.

Working mechanism:
- exterior tenders/lifeboats exist and can remove some occupants;
- combined throughput is lower than the remaining E2→E3 safety window;
- docking/release/navigation sequencing is itself becoming less reliable inside the affected sector;
- using every exterior asset still leaves a substantial population when the director’s safe-detachment deadline arrives.

Therefore exterior extraction is a partial tactic, not a fake unavailable option.

### 7.8 Why Detachment Is Rational
The sector is designed with an emergency separation capability or equivalent isolation method.

If executed before E3/E4, detachment may protect the main habitat. If delayed until system coherence is too low, detachment becomes unsafe for both sector and city.

The director therefore has a real deadline that competes with the hero’s evacuation deadline.

### 7.9 Old Earth Annex Contribution
The Annex contains an empirical operational rule from a historical incident:

When a defined legacy signature coincides with isolation-linked control disagreement, a completely static sealed state must not be maintained if a controlled dynamic passage remains physically possible.

Important:
- Annex does not explain the phenomenon;
- it may contain incomplete or ethically compromised history;
- it does not give Smith general authority over orbital government.

### 7.10 Why the Annex Survived — WORKING Legal Mechanism
Candidate mechanism: early orbital rescue/safety regimes incorporated a small subset of Old Earth unknown-class emergency rules by reference because no successor institution possessed the historical case data. Most legacy powers expired; this narrow life-safety passage clause remained dormant because no later law explicitly replaced the hazard class.

**Activation requirements — WORKING**
1. Old Earth Security authenticates the historical hazard-pattern match.
2. Two independent present human sensor channels confirm isolation-linked degradation.
3. No current successor protocol resolves the conflict inside the available life-safety window.
4. A currently responsible local life-safety operator accepts named responsibility/audit for one passage action.
5. Clause applies to one defined emergency corridor/cycle only.

This is intentionally narrow. It is not “Smith presses a master override.”

### 7.11 Last Override — Physical Action
The hero cannot simply open every door.

Safe candidate action is a **MOVING SEGMENTED CORRIDOR**:
- one local segment transitions at a time;
- downstream isolation closes before upstream opens beyond defined exposure;
- convoy moves through a shifting corridor;
- affected volume is never held as one fully static sealed boundary;
- inhabited city never receives an unrestricted open path.

Cadet supplies the principle. Smith supplies historical evidence and Annex conditions. Director controls present city-scale resources once convinced. Hero supplies current local route sequencing and performs the disputed initial action.

### 7.12 What Happens If Hero Is Wrong
The corridor could carry or couple the unknown condition into the inhabited city.

Therefore the choice is not morally easy and the director is not a coward.

### 7.13 Climax Proof
After the first controlled segment moves:
- isolated-sector mismatch begins dropping as predicted;
- receiving-sector independent sensors do not show corresponding propagation;
- effect responds to boundary state rather than simply following evacuated people/cargo.

Only then does the director expand support.

### 7.14 Resolution
Population clears before the sector reaches unrecoverable control loss. The dangerous sealed-state condition collapses/ends when the static isolation configuration is removed under controlled sequencing.

The book need not answer what the phenomenon “really is.” It must answer the emergency.
