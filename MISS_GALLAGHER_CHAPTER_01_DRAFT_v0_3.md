It was raining in Dublin, and Smith entered the city through a man.

The man stood beneath the hotel awning with an umbrella in one hand and a staff badge clipped to his belt. Greying temples. Wedding ring. Damp cuff. Too much coffee. A headache beginning behind the left eye.

Smith let the rest go past him.

Ordinary people leaked detail. A wife’s irritated voice. A daughter’s school photograph. A rota changed without warning. The private humiliations of a life that had nothing to do with the work. If you were careless, you came out carrying pieces of them.

Smith was not careless.

He moved the clerk through the revolving door, behind the front desk, and into the rhythm the body already knew. Two calls in another man’s voice. One look at the conference register. A question asked with exactly the degree of boredom expected from hotel staff.

Enya Gallagher.

Irish passport. American address. Room on the fourth floor. Registered for the Philosophy of Synthesis Conference. Closed evening session at half seven.

No security notation.

No companion.

Nothing on paper that explained why her name had crossed half the department and landed with Smith.

The order itself had been almost insulting in its simplicity.

**Gallagher. Dublin. Confirm. Take her before evening.**

Smith withdrew.

The clerk blinked and looked down at the desk as though he had misplaced a thought.

By the time the man remembered what he had been doing, Smith was already checking the target.

Not her thoughts.

Thought was the last thing a competent operator trusted first. Thought lied, wandered, rehearsed arguments, remembered songs, invented motives after the body had already chosen. Smith looked beneath it for the relation he expected every ordinary subject to carry.

There was always some point of purchase.

Clean or damaged. Recent or old. Buried, layered, contested, sometimes almost erased. But present.

He found Gallagher’s room.

Found the living body inside it.

Found nothing else.

Smith held the read for three seconds longer.

Nothing.

He released it.

The result did not frighten him.

It annoyed him.

The clerk was a bad instrument for precision. Too much ordinary noise, too little preparation. Smith had used such bodies for speech, motion, access, never for a final classification if he had a choice.

So he did not classify.

He verified.

Minutes later he crossed the same lobby in his own body, carrying a small case and wearing the expression of a man who had arrived in Dublin for reasons nobody would remember. The receptionist gave him a room beside Gallagher’s without difficulty.

The lift rose.

Rain crawled down the glass beyond the landing.

In the corridor Smith stopped once outside his door and listened to the building itself: plumbing, old stone under recent renovation, electricity in the walls, footsteps two floors above, a television behind a door farther down.

Then Gallagher moved in the room beside his.

Ordinary weight on carpet.

He went inside.

His room was narrow and expensive, designed to be forgotten. Grey carpet, pale desk, two prints on the wall that might have shown Dublin or any other wet European city. He placed his case on the chair and stood facing the wall he shared with Enya Gallagher.

This time there was no clerk between them.

He checked again.

The second method was not stronger. Strength was what people used when they had run out of questions.

Smith changed the question.

A hidden relation still distorted something around it. A defended subject produced pressure against the defense. Severance left damage. Transfer left discontinuity. Even a false record could usually be found by the effort required to maintain it.

He searched for consequence rather than connection.

Nothing.

Not blankness.

Blankness was a result.

This was absence in the place where the category itself should have begun.

Smith opened his eyes.

For several seconds he looked at the wallpaper.

Then at the clock.

18:12.

His report window was 19:40.

That gave him less than an hour and a half to decide whether the department had sent him after a concealed subject, an unknown category, or something for which the order itself had been written incorrectly.

The last possibility was the one he disliked most.

Orders could be wrong in detail. Names, locations, timing, local conditions. That was why field operators existed.

An order wrong in category was different.

Smith had seen what happened when an operation ceased to be only an operation and became evidence about the people who had authorized it.

He had also been the person sent to close such evidence.

His phone vibrated once.

No number.

**STATUS.**

Smith typed:

**SUBJECT LOCATED. CLASSIFICATION PENDING.**

He considered the sentence.

It was exact.

He sent it.

The reply arrived before he put the phone down.

**ORDER UNCHANGED.**

Smith read it once.

Then turned the phone face down on the desk.

On the far side of the wall a wardrobe door opened.

He sat.

If Gallagher had a defense, attacking it blindly would be stupidity. If she had no defense and belonged to a category he did not know, attacking it blindly would be worse.

So he listened at the lowest level that still produced information.

The brain was flesh. Flesh carried current. Attention altered breathing, muscle tone, movement. The surface of a person could be read without pretending the surface was the whole person.

A dress came out of the wardrobe.

Pause.

Back in.

Another.

A small flare of annoyance.

Shoes.

Rejection.

Something about rain.

Smith waited for strategy beneath the domestic clutter.

There was none.

Gallagher was getting ready to go downstairs.

That, more than a visible shield would have done, disturbed him.

He had met people who knew how to resist.

One man, years ago, had known Smith was coming before Smith entered the country. He had layered his house with defenses and, when those failed, tried to buy the continuation of his own life with money, names, access, promises. Smith listened to every offer and completed the order anyway.

Resistance was legible.

Fear was legible.

Greed was legible.

An ordinary woman choosing between two dresses while occupying a place in reality Smith could not read was not.

A drawer opened.

Paper moved.

Gallagher crossed the room, then crossed it again. Her phone rang.

Smith did not reach into the call. He heard only her side when she moved closer to the shared wall.

“Yes.”

Pause.

“No, I have it.”

She listened.

“I was told it had to be handed in personally.”

Another pause.

“I know the session is closed.”

She checked the time. Smith heard the small movement of a watch or phone against the desk.

“I’ll be there before half seven.”

The call ended.

Smith looked at the wall.

So she had a task.

Good.

Tasks created routes. Routes created decisions. Decisions exposed structure.

He narrowed his attention to the immediate physical line around what she had just handled: breath, fingers, movement, paper.

For less than a second he caught the pressure of a narrow folder or envelope under her hand.

Then the line vanished.

Not faded.

Not blocked.

Gone.

Smith stood.

On the other side of the wall, Gallagher stopped moving.

Silence settled between the rooms.

He waited.

Nothing.

He almost released the contact.

Then something moved inside his own body.

A wave rose from the base of his spine toward the back of his skull.

Smith froze.

For years movement had gone in the other direction.

Outward.

Into carriers. Across thresholds. Toward targets.

Nothing entered him.

That certainty was older than most of the faces he remembered wearing.

The wave reached the back of his neck and disappeared.

No pain.

No pressure.

Only a brief cold recognition with no object attached to it.

Smith waited.

His first explanation was contamination from the clerk.

He discarded it immediately. Wrong location. Wrong texture.

His second was a defensive return from Gallagher.

Possible.

His third never became a full thought.

A room full of light.

A voice.

Someone telling him something he had once understood.

The image vanished before he could turn toward it.

Smith’s hand had gone to the wall.

He did not remember raising it.

His palm rested flat against paper and plaster.

Across from it, on the other side, no more than a few inches and several categories of reality away, Enya Gallagher took one step.

Then another.

She came closer to the shared wall.

Smith did not breathe.

The room was suddenly too quiet. He could hear the heating system, a lift bell in the corridor, the rain touching the window.

Gallagher stopped.

Exactly opposite him.

There was no reason she could know where he stood.

No direct line of sight.

No sound he had made.

Nothing in his method that should have announced a physical position to an untrained subject.

For the first time in years Smith was unable to decide whether the next movement belonged to the target or to him.

On the other side of the wall, Gallagher turned her face toward the place where his hand rested.

And smiled.

A small smile.

Not triumph.

Not fear.

Recognition.

The way someone might smile when a guest expected for a long time had finally arrived.

Smith pulled his hand away.

His phone vibrated on the desk.

He did not look at it.

Gallagher moved again. A zip closed. Shoes crossed the carpet. The hotel-room door opened.

Only then did Smith pick up the phone.

Two messages.

**ORDER UNCHANGED.**

Below it:

**PREPARE TRANSFER.**

He stared at the words.

The correct sequence was obvious. Leave the room. Intercept before the closed session. Complete the transfer. File the discrepancy afterward.

Smith had followed more difficult sequences while bleeding through other people’s bodies.

He put the phone in his hand and stood at the door.

The wave was gone.

The room of light was gone.

Gallagher’s footsteps were already moving toward the lift.

He had no evidence the department would accept that anything had happened at all.

Only an impossible absence.

A smile through a wall.

And a thought he could not remember ever having been taught:

If he completed the order exactly as given, he might destroy the only evidence that the order was wrong.

The lift bell sounded.

Smith opened his door.
