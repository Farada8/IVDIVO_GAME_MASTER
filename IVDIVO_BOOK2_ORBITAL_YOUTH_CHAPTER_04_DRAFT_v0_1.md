# ORBITAL YOUTH
## BOOK TWO
### CHAPTER FOUR — FIVE ADDRESSES

**Status:** WORKING DRAFT v0.1 / NOT CANON

Ethan's first dinner in orbit required three calendars, two transit forecasts and one lie.

The lie was that dinner was happening at nineteen hundred.

He had put it into the shared calendar anyway.

DINNER — ALL FIVE
19:00
COMMON FOOD LEVEL / SPINE C

Aoife had renamed it within forty seconds.

ETHAN ATTEMPTS SOCIAL CONTROL THROUGH FOOD

Samir had accepted.

Maya had selected MAYBE.

Ollie had marked TENTATIVE.

Aoife had done nothing at all, which Ethan found more aggressive than declining.

He stood in the small common space of his host residence, staring at the calendar while a wall display showed Vela's transit state in slow-moving bands.

His host, Davin, was making tea behind him.

"If you look at it longer," Davin said, "will the other people become available?"

Ethan did not turn around.

"Possibly."

"Useful skill."

"It's still developing."

Davin set a cup on the table.

Ethan's residence was in a medium-gravity family sector on the inner side of Residential Ring Two. It had a good transfer connection to the central spine, terrible access to Samir's sport orientation and, according to Aoife, "the emotional atmosphere of an airport lounge designed by librarians."

Ethan thought it was fine.

The room was clean. Quiet. The exterior view showed part of another ring crossing the black at an angle. Davin and his partner worked different contribution cycles and had arranged the apartment around that fact: one sleeping room could isolate acoustically from the rest of the residence, kitchen lighting changed by household schedule, and the common wall displayed only information everyone had agreed could remain public.

None of that helped with dinner.

Ethan opened the group thread.

ETHAN: final check 19:00 spine C

SAMIR: yes

MAYA: maybe remains maybe

OLLIE: handover may run late

Aoife's response arrived last.

AOIFE: source orientation at 18:40

ETHAN: how long

AOIFE: unknown

ETHAN: estimate

AOIFE: no

Ethan stared at the screen.

ETHAN: because you don't know or because I asked

AOIFE: healthy question

Davin slid the tea toward him.

"Friend problem?"

"Scheduling."

"Same thing at your age."

"It's not a problem."

Davin sat down across from him.

Ethan moved Ollie's maintenance handover on his mental map.

If Ollie finished at eighteen thirty, he needed approximately nineteen minutes to reach Spine C under current transfer timing. Samir's sport orientation ended at eighteen twenty-five but Outer Ring Four had a bad alignment window at eighteen forty-two. Maya was the easiest geographically, assuming she actually left when she said she would. Aoife—

No.

Aoife was not a transport variable.

He deleted the sentence he had started typing.

Then opened the transit overlay again.

"You know," Davin said, "the station has a route planner."

"I know."

"It is fairly good."

"I know."

"It enjoys being used."

Ethan looked at him.

Davin drank his tea.

Ethan tried a different approach.

ETHAN: if 19:00 breaks, 19:45?

SAMIR: can

MAYA: probably

OLLIE: worse

AOIFE: impossible

He closed his eyes.

The five of them had spent months in Northern Virginia fitting school, futsal, maker work, family obligations, Contact sessions, documentary recording and whatever Maya refused to put into the calendar around one another.

They had never needed orbital mechanics.

That seemed unreasonable.

A private message appeared.

OLLIE: don't move it for me

Ethan answered immediately.

ETHAN: it's dinner

OLLIE: exactly

ETHAN: that's not an argument

OLLIE: first handover. if i leave early someone else stays to finish

Ethan looked at the transit board.

A simple solution presented itself.

Ollie could skip one handover. Nobody would die. He had just arrived. There would be more shifts.

Ethan typed:

ETHAN: you don't need to—

He stopped.

Deleted it.

Started again.

ETHAN: okay

Ollie reacted with a thumbs-up.

Ethan hated the thumbs-up.

Not personally.

Structurally.

It closed the conversation before he had solved anything.

Davin watched him for a moment.

"Bad news?"

"No."

"You look offended."

"Ollie is missing dinner."

"Ah."

"That's not an ah."

"What is it?"

"Temporary."

Davin nodded.

"Then eat with four people."

Ethan stared at him.

The answer was so obvious he disliked it immediately.

---

At eighteen twelve Ethan left his residence carrying a strap that belonged to Samir.

This was not an excuse to visit.

The strap had been misrouted into Ethan's luggage during the Dublin transfer and Samir needed it for a sport evaluation the next morning.

It was therefore a logistics task.

The fact that Ethan had checked Samir's host zone and discovered it lay on the way to Spine C was irrelevant.

Mostly.

The route took him from Residential Ring Two into the central spine, through a reduced-gravity transfer section and out toward Outer Ring Four.

By the second transition, Ethan understood why Vela's route planner refused to summarize distance in kilometers.

The station was not large in the way a city on Earth was large.

It was large in delay.

A ring could be visible through a viewport and still be twenty-two minutes away because the nearest transfer point had passed alignment. Two residences separated by what looked like a few hundred meters across open structure could belong to completely different movement schedules.

At one junction, the system suggested Ethan wait ninety seconds for the next synchronized crossing.

He looked at the visible connector.

Then at the countdown.

He considered Maya.

He waited.

The fact that this counted as character growth was humiliating.

Samir's host residence sat near a sports and education cluster where the local gravity was slightly lower than Ethan's.

He noticed it in his first step after leaving the transfer.

Not enough to float. Enough to make his body optimistic.

The corridor walls were covered with team schedules, youth event notices and a set of colored route symbols Ethan had not seen in his own sector.

Several teenagers passed carrying curved sports frames that looked like somebody had taken lacrosse equipment apart and rebuilt it under different physics.

Samir opened the residence door in socks.

"Why are you here?"

Ethan held up the strap.

Samir looked at it.

"Oh."

"Dublin put it in my case."

"I thought I lost it."

"You did. Geographically."

Samir took the strap.

"Thanks."

Behind him, the residence was louder than Ethan's.

Two younger children were arguing over a flexible wall display. Someone in the kitchen was speaking in a language Ethan did not recognize. A sports bag lay open beside the entry bench, half-filled with equipment.

The main room had no exterior view at all.

Instead one wall opened into a shared interior garden running through several connected residences.

Samir followed Ethan's eyes.

"It's better than a window."

"I didn't say anything."

"You did a face."

Ethan hated that this phrase had become communal property.

A woman appeared from the kitchen and introduced herself as Samir's host, Hana. She offered Ethan food before asking who he was, then told Samir his equipment inspection slot had moved ten minutes earlier.

Samir looked at his wrist.

"That kills dinner."

Ethan felt something inside him tighten.

"How long is inspection?"

"Not long. But practice orientation is on the next ring and the transfer window after nineteen is bad."

"You could come late."

"Maybe."

"How late?"

Samir looked at him.

Not irritated yet.

Just watching.

Ethan heard himself before he continued.

He stopped.

"Never mind."

Samir lifted one eyebrow.

"That looked painful."

"It was fine."

"You almost scheduled me."

"I asked a question."

"You asked the beginning of six questions."

Hana moved past them with a basket of folded clothes and did not get involved.

Ethan was grateful.

"Come if you can," he said.

Samir clipped the recovered strap onto his bag.

"I want to."

The answer landed differently.

Not I can.

Not I will.

I want to.

Ethan nodded.

"Okay."

Samir glanced toward the shared garden.

"You want to see something?"

"Depends."

"Dangerous answer."

Samir led him through the residence.

The interior garden was not large. Maybe twenty meters long, curved with the ring, shared by four households through transparent partitions that could become opaque.

There were plants, low seating and a narrow strip where two children were pushing a ball back and forth with far too much force for an indoor space.

The ball moved strangely in the lower gravity.

One child anticipated the bounce correctly.

The other did not.

It hit the ceiling.

Nobody screamed.

"The sport center is two minutes that way," Samir said. "My host says this is why nobody practices inside."

The ball hit the ceiling again.

"Effective policy."

Samir smiled.

"Also my room is tiny."

"How tiny?"

"If I sit on the bed and put my feet down, I'm technically in storage."

"Good commute?"

"Seven minutes to the courts."

Ethan looked back at the garden.

His own room was larger. His view was better. Samir had almost no private space and could reach practice faster than Ethan could reach a food level.

He opened the housing map on his wrist.

Samir saw.

"Don't."

"I'm not doing anything."

"You're comparing."

"I'm curious."

"You're ranking."

Ethan closed the map.

Samir smiled wider.

"Growth."

"Everyone needs a new word."

Samir's equipment notice chimed.

He checked it.

"I have to go."

Ethan looked at the time.

If he left now he could reach Spine C at eighteen forty-three.

Aoife's source orientation might end by nineteen ten if it was short. Maya had said maybe. Ollie was gone.

Dinner was becoming an increasingly theoretical object.

Samir shouldered the sports bag.

"Tell them I'll try."

"Tell them yourself."

"You're the dinner government."

Ethan opened the door.

"This is why civilizations fail."

Samir laughed and went the other direction.

---

By nineteen six, Ethan had a table for four and one person.

The one person was him.

He had chosen a common food level near the central spine because it sat within roughly equal inconvenience of three host sectors.

This had turned out to mean it was convenient for nobody.

The space was busy without being crowded. Long tables ran beside smaller booths that adjusted their atmosphere and acoustic isolation within a limited range. Food services clustered along one wall: local staples, planetary imports, mixed-environment preparation stations and several things Ethan could not identify without opening descriptions.

He did not open descriptions.

He was not here to study food.

He checked the group thread.

OLLIE: still in handover

SAMIR: inspection ran late. go without me

MAYA: coming maybe 15

AOIFE: source ran over. don't wait

Ethan looked at the empty seats.

He had reserved four because Ollie had dropped out first.

Then Samir.

Then Aoife.

Maya was maybe fifteen minutes away, which on Vela meant she could arrive in eight or thirty.

A family asked if the three empty seats were free.

Ethan looked at them.

"Yes."

They sat.

He moved to the end of the table.

One of the children had a translucent food container with something bright orange in it and kept trying to make it wobble under reduced local gravity.

Ethan ordered without waiting for anyone else.

This felt like defeat.

His wrist buzzed.

Private message from Aoife.

AOIFE: are you actually sitting there alone

ETHAN: no

AOIFE: who came

Ethan looked at the family occupying the other seats.

ETHAN: three people

There was a pause.

AOIFE: names

ETHAN: unknown

AOIFE: excellent dinner

He almost smiled.

ETHAN: source okay?

The typing indicator appeared.

Disappeared.

Appeared again.

AOIFE: yes. complicated. good.

ETHAN: want help

Another pause.

AOIFE: not yet

Ethan read the two words twice.

Not no.

Not yes.

Not yet.

He typed:

ETHAN: okay

Then, because apparently he had learned nothing:

ETHAN: how long do you think

The response came immediately.

AOIFE: there he is

Ethan put the wrist face down on the table.

The orange food hit the inside of the child's container and folded in on itself.

The child laughed.

Ethan's meal arrived.

He ate three bites.

Maya appeared.

She came in fast, looked around, saw him at the end of a table with strangers and stopped.

"This is depressing."

"Hello to you too."

She sat across from him.

"Where is everyone?"

"Living their lives, apparently."

Maya looked at him.

"You sound eighty."

"Ollie has handover. Samir got moved. Aoife's source ran late."

"So you still came?"

"I made a reservation."

Maya stared.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You did a face."

"No. I was just wondering if Vela lets you marry calendars."

The child with the orange food looked over.

Ethan lowered his voice.

"I hate all of you."

Maya ordered something without reading most of the options.

"My host's son says this level is for people who couldn't book the good one."

"There is a good food level?"

"Apparently several."

"Why didn't anyone say that?"

"Because you're new and annoying."

Ethan looked at her.

She smiled.

"I am also new. Different category."

"How was arrival?"

"Perfect."

"Samir says you took a bad route."

Maya's expression changed.

"Samir is dead to me."

"So bad route."

"It was shorter."

"But slower."

"Why is everybody obsessed with this?"

Ethan laughed.

For the first time all day, dinner felt almost like dinner.

Maya's food arrived. She took one bite, frowned at it, took another and decided it was acceptable.

"I'm going out later," she said.

"With who?"

She looked at him.

Ethan stopped.

"Sorry."

"That was fast."

"I'm adapting."

"Host kid and some locals."

"Good."

"You can ask who."

"I don't need to."

"Now you're being weird."

"I am trying not to schedule you."

Maya leaned back.

"You know there is a middle between schedule me and pretend you have no questions."

Ethan considered that.

"Who?"

"Kellan. His friends. Maybe some people from the courts."

"Which courts?"

Maya pointed at him with her fork.

"Careful."

"That is a normal question."

"Barely."

His wrist buzzed.

Samir had sent a photograph from a sports hall.

The court curved.

Not metaphorically. The playing surface continued up two walls into a shallow bowl. A set of illuminated bands marked active zones at different heights.

SAMIR: apparently I have been running incorrectly my entire life

Maya looked over Ethan's wrist.

"That's good. We can be humiliated together."

ETHAN: dinner status: 2/5

OLLIE reacted with a wrench.

AOIFE reacted with a microphone.

SAMIR sent:

SAMIR: spiritually present

Maya typed from her own wrist:

MAYA: do not encourage him

The family beside them got up to leave.

The child carrying the orange food waved at Ethan.

He waved back.

Three empty seats returned.

Maya noticed him looking at them.

"Stop counting."

"I'm not."

"You are literally looking at chairs."

"Chairs exist independently of counting."

"That's probably what Ollie says about sensors."

Ethan smiled despite himself.

Then Ollie sent a private message.

OLLIE: handover done in 20. don't wait.

Ethan started typing.

ETHAN: we can—

He deleted it.

Maya watched.

"What did you almost do?"

"Nothing."

"That was definitely something."

Ethan put the wrist down.

"I was going to move dinner."

Maya looked around at the half-eaten meals.

"To when?"

"Later."

"Aoife can't. Samir can't. I'm leaving."

"I know."

"Then why?"

He did not have an answer he liked.

Because this was supposed to be their first dinner in orbit.

Because there were five of them.

Because for months, if one person could not make something, they changed it until they could.

Because being in five places at once felt like an error.

He picked up his cup.

"Habit."

Maya looked at him for a second.

She did not make a joke.

"Yeah," she said.

That was worse somehow.

---

Ollie joined the group call at twenty-one fourteen from a service corridor.

Not because Ethan scheduled it.

Ethan wanted that noted.

He had returned to his host residence. Maya was somewhere on the inner social level. Samir was eating after sport orientation. Aoife was editing in a public media room because her host residence was apparently too loud. Ollie had finished the handover and was waiting for a local transit connection.

Five small windows appeared on Ethan's wall.

Maya's image moved constantly because she was walking.

"Do not show my host this," she said.

"Show your host what?" Ethan asked.

"Nothing."

"Excellent."

Samir was eating something from a bowl.

Aoife wore headphones around her neck.

Ollie had grease or sealant on one sleeve.

"You missed dinner," Ethan told him.

"I was informed."

"It was historic."

Maya said, "There were strangers."

"Three," Ethan said.

Aoife looked up from her screen.

"Did you learn their names?"

"No."

"Then I reject the historical significance."

Ollie smiled tiredly.

"How bad was it?"

"Two of five," Maya said.

Samir raised his spoon.

"Spiritually present."

Ethan leaned back.

There they were.

All five.

Technically.

"Tomorrow," he said, "nineteen hundred—"

Four voices reacted at once.

Not words at first.

Just sound.

Maya groaned.

Aoife took off one headphone.

Samir lowered the spoon.

Ollie closed his eyes.

Ethan stopped.

"Fine."

There was a suspicious silence.

"Fine what?" Aoife asked.

"No schedule."

Maya slowed her walking.

"Are you ill?"

"Tomorrow, if people are free, we meet. If not, we don't."

Samir looked genuinely concerned now.

"Ethan."

"What?"

"Blink twice if someone has access to your account."

"I regret this already."

Aoife smiled.

Then her expression changed as she looked at something off-screen.

"I have to go."

"Everything okay?" Ethan asked.

"Yes. Source sent another note."

"Do you need—"

He stopped before finishing.

Aoife waited.

Ethan changed the question.

"Are you calling back later?"

"Maybe."

Maya said, "Maybe means maybe."

Aoife looked at her.

"Thank you."

She disconnected.

Her window vanished.

A few seconds later Samir's host called him from another room.

"I have to help with something," he said. "Night."

His window vanished too.

Maya reached a louder section of the station. Music entered her audio.

She glanced over her shoulder.

"They're here. I'm going."

"Who?" Ethan asked automatically.

Maya grinned.

"Good night, Ethan."

Her window disappeared.

Only Ollie remained.

The service corridor behind him was nearly empty.

Ethan looked at the two windows on the wall.

"You good?"

Ollie shrugged.

"Tired. Good."

"How was handover?"

"Real."

The word carried more weight than Ethan expected.

Ollie looked away from the camera as a transit status changed overhead.

"They don't let you touch anything important at first."

"That seems responsible."

"It is."

"You sound disappointed."

"I am responsible and disappointed."

Ethan smiled.

"Tomorrow?"

Ollie looked back.

Ethan did not say nineteen hundred.

"If you're free."

Ollie nodded.

"If I'm free."

They disconnected.

The wall went dark.

Ethan sat alone in the common room.

His tea from earlier was still on the table, cold now.

He opened the group calendar.

Five names.

Five host addresses.

Five sets of commitments beginning to fill the week.

He could still see all of them.

That was not the same as being able to put them in one place.

A private message appeared from Aoife.

AOIFE: source okay. complicated but okay.

Then:

AOIFE: also your dinner looked tragic

Ethan typed:

ETHAN: it had structure

AOIFE: exactly

He stared at that for a second.

Then wrote:

ETHAN: good night

Aoife reacted with a small moon icon.

No joke.

Ethan closed the calendar.

Outside the residence, the ring kept turning whether any of them were together or not.
