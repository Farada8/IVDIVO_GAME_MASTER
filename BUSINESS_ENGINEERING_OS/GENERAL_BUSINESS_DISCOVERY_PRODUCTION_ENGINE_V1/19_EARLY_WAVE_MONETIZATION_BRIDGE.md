## Purpose
The Business Engine must keep two questions separate:

1. **WHAT market/problem/wave are we entering?**
2. **HOW does money flow to us?**

A promising technology/regulation is not a business model. A money mechanism such as SaaS, consulting, audit, brokerage or subscription is not a market thesis.

Canonical bridge:

`OPPORTUNITY -> CONCRETE DELTA -> BUYER ROLE -> FIXED-SCOPE DIAGNOSTIC -> PAID EVIDENCE -> IMPLEMENTATION -> RECURRING NEED -> SOFTWARE CANDIDATE`

Do not skip stages because software is easy to build.

## Monetization ladder

### M0 — INTERNAL PROOF ARTIFACT
Goal: prove we can generate a non-trivial technical/decision delta on a real or synthetic fixture.
Money claim: none.

### M1 — FIXED-SCOPE DIAGNOSTIC SPEC
A bounded deliverable with inputs, outputs, exclusions, acceptance criteria and evidence pack.
Money claim: price remains a hypothesis; WTP remains UNKNOWN until a real buyer pays/commits admissibly.

### M2 — PAID DIAGNOSTIC VALIDATED
At least one real paid diagnostic/deposit/contract within the bounded scope.
What this proves: some buyer WTP for the diagnostic.
What it does not prove: implementation demand, repeatability, scalable CAC or SaaS demand.

### M3 — IMPLEMENTATION PROJECT VALIDATED
At least one paid implementation following a diagnostic or equivalent scoped need.
What this proves: implementation can be sold/delivered once.
What it does not prove: recurring revenue.

### M4 — RECURRING MONITORING VALIDATED
Observed reason for repeat work plus at least two paid recurring/renewal cycles or equivalent repeated paid use.
What this proves: a recurring problem exists for at least one buyer context.
What it does not prove: broad software demand.

### M5 — SOFTWARE CANDIDATE
At least three independent customer contexts repeatedly require materially the same workflow/rules/data transformation, with enough standardisation to reduce marginal delivery cost.
Disposition is `SOFTWARE_CANDIDATE`, not `SAAS_PROVEN`.

### M6 — SOFTWARE BUSINESS VALIDATION
Requires actual product usage, paid retention, support burden, acquisition economics and churn/renewal evidence. Outside current internal scope.

### Evidence now

- generic-advice outputs = 0.

- buyer demand/WTP/transaction/profitability remain unproven.

### Current monetization route
`M1_FIXED_SCOPE_DIAGNOSTIC_SPEC_READY_NOT_WTP_PROVEN`

### M1 offer specification — Agent Commerce Readiness Diagnostic
**Potential buyer roles to test later:** ecommerce owner/operator, head of ecommerce, ecommerce engineering/integration lead, agency managing ecommerce infrastructure. These are hypotheses, not observed buyers.

**Inputs:** merchant domain; platform/integration inventory if voluntarily supplied; public UCP/profile evidence; product-feed sample if buyer supplies it; checkout/fulfilment/returns policy metadata.

**Deliverables:**
1. machine-readable product/feed completeness report;
2. public agent-discovery/UCP evidence report;
3. PASS / FAIL / UNKNOWN findings by rule, not a magic score;
4. top deterministic blockers separated from internal-state UNKNOWNs;
5. remediation backlog by owner/system;
6. protocol/version assumptions;
7. post-change regression checklist.

**Explicit exclusions:** platform approval; payment/security certification; legal opinion; transaction testing without separate authorization; inferred private feed/backend state.

### M2/M3 possible only after paid evidence
If M1 gets real paid demand, implementation may include bounded feed normalization, public UCP/profile repair, policy/data mapping, or connector/adaptor work. Do not promise a protocol that the merchant/platform does not actually use.

### M4 recurring hypothesis
Protocol/version drift, catalogue/schema regressions and deployment changes may create periodic monitoring. This is a hypothesis until repeated paid need is observed.

### M5 software hypothesis
Scanner/CI/API only becomes a software candidate after materially the same checks and remediation workflow recur across >=3 independent customer contexts.
