## Current facts encoded
- Article 50 transparency obligations generally apply from **2 August 2026**.
- **Binding transition:** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 adds a narrow transition only for providers of AI systems (including GPAI systems) generating synthetic audio/image/video/text that were **placed on the market before 2 August 2026**. Those providers must take the necessary steps to comply with **Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026**.
- The transition is not an exemption, not a blanket Article 50 delay, and does not defer Article 50(1), (3), or (4).
- Providers: direct-interaction notification under 50(1); machine-readable marking/detectability for synthetic audio/image/video/text under 50(2).
- Deployers: emotion-recognition/biometric-categorisation exposure notice under 50(3); deepfake and certain public-interest text disclosure under 50(4).
- Direct-interaction “obviousness” is an exception to be interpreted restrictively, not a default shortcut.
- Machine-readable provider marking and human-facing deployer disclosure are distinct controls.
- Creative/artistic/satirical/fictional deepfake content has a special manner-of-disclosure regime, not a blanket disappearance of the disclosure obligation.
- Public-interest AI text may fall outside the 50(4) deployer disclosure requirement where human review/editorial control occurred and a natural/legal person holds editorial responsibility.
- Commission EU icons are optional; clear labelling is mandatory where Article 50(4) applies.
- The Transparency Code is voluntary and can support demonstration of compliance; it does not automatically certify compliance.

## Transition evidence rule
A case may use the Article 50(2) transition only if it evidences:
1. provider role;
2. in-scope synthetic-content generation;
3. system placed on market before 2026-08-02;
4. assessment date before 2026-12-02;
5. remediation/evidence plan targeting the 2026-12-02 deadline.

Missing transition evidence must not silently defer the ordinary Article 50(2) route.
