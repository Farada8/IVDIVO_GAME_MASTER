# IVDIVO — BOOK 2 ORBITAL YOUTH — RED TEAM v0.1

**Status:** WORKING DEVELOPMENT AUDIT  
**Audited:** Story Core v0.1 + Technical Passport v0.1 + Causal Chapter Map v0.1  
**Purpose:** attack the current architecture before scene cards/prose.

---

# VERDICT

**NO FATAL STORY BREAK FOUND.**

The book has a real hero, want, opposition, midpoint, climax choice, price and resolution.

However it is **NOT YET READY FOR PROSE**.

Current status: `YELLOW/GREEN — REPAIR BEFORE SCENE CARDS`.

The main risk is exactly the one the Founder has repeatedly rejected: an intelligent, coherent future-system book that becomes homework because engineering, policy and world design displace youth life and emotional urgency.

# FATAL

None currently.

# MAJOR 1 — OLLIE’S WANT IS TOO PROFESSIONAL

Current:
“Ollie wants to do real orbital work and prove the sacrifices were worth it.”

This is functional but not yet enough for a YA protagonist.

## Problem
A teenager can want a real job, but the book needs a desire that hurts outside résumé logic.

## Repair
Add private desire:

> Ollie wants to become the person people call when something real breaks — because he is tired of being the friend whose competence lives in workshops, models and youth programs while Ethan naturally occupies the visible leadership space.

This introduces:
- status inside the friend group;
- envy he dislikes in himself;
- hunger for adult respect;
- reason he overcommits to the ring;
- emotional cost when the ring fails.

He must never say this as a thematic speech.

---

# MAJOR 2 — MIDPOINT CAUSAL RESPONSIBILITY MUST BE SHARPER

Current map says Ollie supports limited opening based on incomplete model.

## Problem
If adults would have opened anyway, his failure is emotionally decorative.

## Repair
Ollie’s analysis must answer one disputed commissioning question.

Example function:
- Safety team has two plausible models.
- Ollie’s timing analysis appears to falsify the more conservative one.
- His result is not the sole reason to open, but it materially removes one reason to delay.

Then midpoint failure forces him to confront:

> I did not cause the system defect, but my incomplete interpretation changed the decision environment.

This is stronger than guilt-by-proximity.

---

# MAJOR 3 — CLIMAX NEEDS PHYSICAL ACTION, NOT ONLY A GOOD REPORT

Current climax contains an honest recommendation and final report.

## Problem
The moral choice is good, but the action peak may feel administrative.

## Repair
During migration stress, Ollie has a **local, pre-authorized emergency action** available:
- isolate one residential segment;
- halt a transit feed into that segment;
- dump a local zone into safe-state configuration;
- physically confirm / lock one boundary when automation still shows recoverable conditions.

The action:
- is inside his trained role;
- immediately harms throughput / displaces people;
- looks premature to anyone watching fixed sensors;
- is justified by the new lag model and resident embodied warning;
- prevents the oscillation from propagating.

Adults retain command of the wider emergency.

The later report is the emotional/moral completion of the physical choice.

---

# MAJOR 4 — AOIFE’S VIRAL CLIP IS TOO EASY TO WRITE BADLY

## Risk
If she simply breaks a promise and uploads private material, she becomes careless in a way that ignores Book One growth.

## Repair
Her error should be subtler:
- source approves the excerpt itself;
- Aoife respects the literal agreement;
- she fails to see that location, body-service detail, background sound/image or context allows outsiders to infer something the source did not expect to become legible;
- a third party clips/reframes the legal publication.

Aoife’s responsibility remains real because she understands media better than the source and should have anticipated inferability.

Theme:
`consent to a sentence ≠ consent to every inference an audience can make from it`.

This is development, not regression.

---

# MAJOR 5 — SYNTHETIC-BODY CHARACTER IS CURRENTLY AN UNFUNDED PROMISE

Iri’s full synthetic body is currently OPTION.

## Problem
If retained, it opens major questions:
- consciousness continuity;
- body ownership;
- maintenance;
- replacement;
- privacy;
- sensory range;
- legal identity;
- vulnerability;
- attraction / touch / sexuality;
- whether this technology is routine or exceptional.

Those questions are larger than a decorative supporting-character trait.

## Decision
**DO NOT LOCK IRI AS SYNTHETIC-BODIED YET.**

For scene architecture use:
`IRI = LOCAL ORBITAL YOUTH WITH A BODY/ENVIRONMENT INTERFACE CLASS THAT DIFFERS FROM HUMAN DEFAULT — exact ontology pending passport.`

If full synthetic embodiment is approved, it must materially affect Iri’s life and at least one Book-Two relationship.

Otherwise use a less ontologically expensive mixed-species / engineered-body case.

# MAJOR 6 — B-LINE NEEDS MORE THAN FOUR “YOUTH CHAPTERS”

The current map includes sport, party, housing, documentary and romance, but middle chapters 18–28 risk becoming engineering/procedure-heavy.

## Repair rule
Every two technical chapters must contain at least one scene where the main pressure is:
- friendship;
- attraction / jealousy;
- family;
- money/allocation;
- job hierarchy;
- sport;
- boredom;
- embarrassment;
- status;
- housing/privacy;
- entertainment.

Not a “break from plot.” The ordinary-life pressure must alter A-line choices.

Examples:
- Ollie misses a group event because he is chasing model data; local trust gained, friend trust lost.
- Samir’s sport team gives him access to movement patterns nobody on commissioning staff sees.
- Maya’s social route reveals how residents actually cross zones at night, invalidating daytime occupancy assumptions.
- Aoife/Ethan jealousy changes who tells whom important information first.

# MAJOR 7 — MAYA RISKS REPEATING BOOK ONE

Current:
Maya crosses a work boundary, gets mobility restriction, later earns it back.

This is dangerously close to Book One’s “technically right but lacked authority” beat.

## Repair
Change the Book-Two mistake.

Better:
Maya receives genuine new freedom and uses it competently — but lies about **where she will sleep / who she is with / whether she will return before a transit closure** because she wants one normal night without being tracked as Youth Cohort.

The problem is not technical authority.
It is relational trust and orbital logistics: when a transport/environmental closure occurs, other people must search/plan around false information.

Her Book-Two lesson becomes:
`privacy and independence require truthful boundaries, not disappearance`.

This is new.

---

# MAJOR 8 — ETHAN’S BIRTHDAY CAN STEAL THE BOOK

Ethan turning 18 is continuity-rich but high-gravity story material.

## Repair
Limit adult-track decision to:
- one setup scene;
- one pressure scene;
- one decision scene.

Do not build another appeals / eligibility / institutional sequence.

His new adult status should mainly alter relationships:
- younger friends react differently;
- Ethan catches himself using “I’m legally allowed” as emotional leverage;
- Aoife refuses to treat birthday as automatic maturity.

# MAJOR 9 — THE RING CURRENTLY HAS NO FACE

“Residents waiting to move” is a population, not a character.

## Repair
Before scene cards create at least three resident anchors:
1. local youth apprentice / peer tied to maintenance;
2. a household waiting for compatible housing;
3. a worker/service person who will pay the commute/job price if opening delays.

They must disagree with each other.

No resident should exist merely to confirm the protagonists are morally correct.

# MEDIUM 1 — OPPOSITION IS DIFFUSE

No villain is a strength, but Ollie needs a recurring human resistance relationship.

## Repair
Create one adult commissioning supervisor / engineer whose model of professionalism conflicts with Ollie’s.

Required properties:
- competent;
- not hostile to youth;
- wants Ollie to succeed;
- legitimately values certified evidence and deadline discipline;
- is partly right when Ollie is wrong;
- later changes only because evidence changes.

This person becomes Ollie’s pressure relationship, not a mentor vending machine.

---

# MEDIUM 2 — MONEY/ALLOCATION IS STILL ONLY A WORLD NOTE

Book Two canon function includes money / resources / first jobs.

## Repair
Before scene cards define only the **minimum lived economic loop** needed for the characters:
- what Ollie receives for apprentice work;
- what he can choose to spend/request/access with it;
- what remains scarce even in advanced orbit;
- how housing and transit capacity are allocated.

Do not design the entire orbital economy.

---

# MEDIUM 3 — THE ORBITAL CITY NEEDS CULTURAL IDENTITY

Current city is intentionally unnamed/constitutionally undefined.

Good for canon safety, bad for prose if left this way.

## Repair
Before Chapter 1 prose define:
- working city name;
- one founding history;
- one architectural signature;
- one local youth stereotype/joke about planet-born people;
- one sport/entertainment form;
- one food/social ritual;
- one political tension;
- one thing residents complain about despite living in an extraordinary place.

No encyclopedia.

---

# MEDIUM 4 — CLIMAX MIGRATION PRESSURE MUST BE SEEDED FROM CHAPTER 6

The older-sector overhaul cannot appear as convenient third-act pressure.

## Repair
From first ring tour onward show:
- moving dates;
- boxes/storage;
- temporary assignments;
- workers preparing transfer;
- school/team schedule consequences;
- arguments about whether opening date will hold.

Then migration is consequence, not plot machinery.

---

# MEDIUM 5 — BOOK TWO NEEDS ONE LARGE POSITIVE SOCIAL WONDER EVENT

Book One already established wonder beats.
Book Two should have at least one event that readers simply want to attend.

Candidates (function only):
- gravity-variable youth sport final;
- habitat-wide night market across several environment zones;
- moving music/performance event through transit spine;
- mixed-species holiday / local civic festival.

The event must also carry relationships/status, not become a travel brochure.

# POLISH / PROSE RISKS FOR LATER

- overuse of technical nouns;
- characters explaining systems they all know;
- every local teenager being worldly and cool;
- Earth kids constantly amazed;
- nonhuman culture used as quirky decoration;
- AI interface delivering convenient exposition;
- repeated “Ollie looked at the data and realized...” construction;
- everyone speaking in clean policy arguments.

---

# REPAIRED STORY CORE — ONE SENTENCE

> On the first orbital residency of the new Youth Cohort, Ollie finally gets the real maintenance work he has wanted, but his desire to prove himself helps validate an incomplete model for a residential ring that must open before thousands of people are relocated; after the partial opening fails, he has to rebuild the model from the lived behavior of the city itself and choose, under migration pressure, to sacrifice the clean launch and his own recognition in order to open only what is actually safe.

This is stronger than v0.1 because:
- Ollie materially affects the midpoint;
- residents have concrete urgency;
- climax has action + moral choice;
- success remains partial;
- ordinary life can feed evidence rather than decorate it.

---

# DECISION

**REPAIR, DO NOT REBUILD.**

Architecture is viable.

Required before scene cards:
1. add Ollie private status desire;
2. define his causal contribution to opening decision;
3. upgrade climax to local authorized physical action;
4. repair Aoife inference-consent mistake;
5. keep synthetic embodiment optional until passport;
6. replace Maya repeated authority mistake;
7. create three resident anchors + one adult engineering opponent;
8. define minimum economic loop + city cultural identity;
9. seed migration pressure from Act I.

After these repairs: rerun Story Gate and proceed to scene architecture.
