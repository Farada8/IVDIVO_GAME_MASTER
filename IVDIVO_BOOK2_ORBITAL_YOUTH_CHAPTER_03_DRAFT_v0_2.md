# ORBITAL YOUTH
## BOOK TWO
### CHAPTER THREE — ARRIVAL WITHOUT A FLOOR

**Status:** WORKING DRAFT v0.2 / RED-TEAM PATCHED / NOT CANON

Maya's first mistake in orbit lasted less than three seconds.

Unfortunately, twelve people saw it.

The transfer corridor from Dublin opened into an arrival chamber that seemed designed by somebody who had never agreed that floors should remain beneath people.

There was a floor. Mostly.

A pale traffic band curved up along the far side of the chamber until Maya's body insisted it had become a wall, even though people kept walking across it.

Families, commuters and freight staff moved through the curve without looking down.

Maya stopped just beyond the gate.

Her stomach arrived a fraction later.

The sign over the lane shifted into English.

ROTATIONAL ENTRY — BLUE BAND

MATCH WALKING PACE

DO NOT STOP IN TRANSITION

The last instruction appeared while Maya was already stopped.

Someone behind her said, "Seriously?"

She moved.

Too fast.

Her left foot came down where she expected. Her right corrected for a pull that was not exactly sideways. She crossed the edge of the blue lane and made an older man with a shopping carrier twist around her.

His carrier corrected better than she did.

A girl coming the other way caught Maya by the forearm.

"Blue means keep pace."

Maya straightened.

"I know."

The girl glanced at Maya's feet.

"Right."

She let go.

Maya felt heat climb into her face.

Everyone else had already continued.

That should have helped.

It did not.

The girl was about Maya's age, maybe a little older. Dark hair cut at the jaw. Gray trousers. A jacket with three narrow reflective strips at one shoulder and a transit patch Maya did not recognize.

"First day?" she asked.

Maya looked back at the transfer gate.

"What gave it away?"

The girl pointed once at the blue lane.

Then she stepped into the curve and kept walking.

Four paces later she was moving at an angle Maya's inner ear still rejected.

Maya stood very carefully inside the band.

Her wrist buzzed.

ETHAN: Transfer complete?

MAYA: obviously

AOIFE: define complete

OLLIE: same

SAMIR: where

Maya looked around.

Seven exits. Three visible levels. Two lanes that became walls. A central shaft where passenger capsules moved in directions her brain had not yet assigned names to.

MAYA: somewhere extremely intuitive

ETHAN: send route tag

Maya did not.

She had been on Vela for maybe four minutes. The thought of Ethan locating her through a route tag already felt unbearable.

She opened the city map instead.

VELA TRANSFER HUB — ARRIVAL RING

HOST RESIDENCE: EAST RESIDENTIAL 4

RECOMMENDED ROUTE: SPINE C / TRANSFER 3 / ER4 LOCAL

ESTIMATED TIME: 31 MIN

The route went away from East Residential 4 before returning toward it.

Maya zoomed out.

A shorter connection was visible through a neighboring transfer hub.

The system marked it:

NOT RECOMMENDED FOR CURRENT ADAPTATION PROFILE

Maya read the wording twice.

Not forbidden.

That was enough.

---

The shorter route worked beautifully for the first six minutes.

Then the cabin crossed the central spine.

Pressure through Maya's feet faded. For several seconds the floor stopped making a convincing argument that it was down.

A boy in school clothes hooked one shoe under a rail without interrupting his food. A woman on a call shifted half a step. A maintenance worker kept his eyes closed.

Maya gripped the handle harder than any of them.

Weight returned differently on the other side.

She stepped out too quickly.

Her wrist flashed amber.

ADAPTATION CHECK

PLEASE PAUSE IN MARKED ZONE

A rectangle lit beside the lane.

Maya stepped into it.

The countdown appeared.

03:40

She stared.

"No."

The system did not revise its position.

A group of local students came through the corridor and walked directly past the adaptation zone.

The girl from the blue band was with them.

Maya looked away, then back because pretending not to see her was worse.

The girl noticed the red boundary around Maya's feet.

"You took Three?"

"Yes."

"From arrival?"

"Yes."

The girl looked at the route board.

"Why?"

"It's shorter."

A tiny pause.

"Geometrically."

"That's usually what shorter means."

"Not here."

The girl kept walking.

Maya watched her go.

The countdown read 02:47.

Her wrist buzzed again.

ETHAN: We are trying to regroup before host dispersal.

MAYA: busy

ETHAN: with what

MAYA: local culture

Aoife sent:

AOIFE: record it

MAYA: absolutely not

When the adaptation zone finally released her, the route system recalculated.

ESTIMATED ARRIVAL: 36 MIN

The recommended route from the first hub had been thirty-one.

Maya had spent eleven minutes proving that nine minutes was less than fourteen.

Mathematics had become personal.

---

By the time Maya reached East Residential 4, Vela had stopped feeling like one place.

It felt like timing.

Doors became useful only when the right ring reached the right phase. A route could be physically near and operationally stupid. People said "two transfers" the way people on Earth said "five minutes," then complained when one transfer was out of sequence.

At the residence intake desk, a small boy stared at the Earth mark on Maya's luggage.

"Does the ocean really move all the time?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Gravity."

He looked disappointed and went back to his game.

That was the only person who cared she had come from Earth.

In Northern Virginia, Contact had made the five visible without asking them. Here, Maya was just another arrival standing in the wrong place.

She had not decided whether that was insulting or wonderful when her host opened the door.

"You're late," the woman said.

Maya checked her wrist.

Eleven minutes.

"The route changed."

The woman looked at the permitted arrival record between them.

"You changed it."

Maya took off her jacket.

"Technically."

The woman stepped aside.

"I'm Nara. Come in."

The residence was smaller than the pictures had suggested, but every surface seemed to have more than one job. The entry wall held shoes, outer layers, emergency masks and two folding seats. A narrow table extended from a partition only when Nara touched it.

Plants grew along one wall.

"Food garden?" Maya asked.

"Humidity buffer. Basil too."

A second voice called from deeper inside.

"She took Three, didn't she?"

Maya closed her eyes.

Nara's mouth moved by maybe a millimeter.

"My son. Kellan. He finds planet-born routing choices entertaining."

"He can stop."

A boy appeared with a sports bag over one shoulder.

He looked at Maya, then at Nara.

"Three?"

"Go away."

He nodded once, as though this was a reasonable boundary.

"Kellan."

"Still go away."

"All right."

He did.

That annoyed Maya slightly. She had expected him to argue.

Nara pointed down the corridor.

"Last room. Bathroom is shared with Kellan. Kitchen is shared with everyone. Quiet cycle starts at twenty-three thirty unless we agree otherwise. If your sleep schedule changes, tell the house system."

Maya stopped at the bedroom door.

"What do I have to report?"

"To me?"

"Start there."

Nara leaned one shoulder against the wall.

"What do you want to do?"

"Go out."

"Yes."

"Late."

"Probably."

"Without asking permission every time I change locations."

"Fine."

Maya waited.

"That's it?"

"No continuous tracking," Nara said. "If your return changes by more than an hour, send a message. If you sleep somewhere else, tell me where. If transit closes or a zone changes state, don't give me a fake location to avoid an argument."

Maya folded her arms.

"My parents agreed to that?"

"Eventually."

"How eventually?"

"Forty-eight messages."

Maya stared at her.

Nara looked toward the kitchen.

"Your father wanted a twenty-two thirty return. I sent him the public transit schedule."

"You didn't."

"I did."

Maya laughed despite herself.

The bedroom door opened to her host profile.

The room was narrow but private. Bed partly folded into the wall. Storage inside the curve. One section of the ceiling showed a live exterior view.

Stars.

No Earth.

Maya put her bag down and looked longer than she meant to.

Nara did not say anything about it.

That made Maya like her more.

"Anything else?" Maya asked.

"If you say you're coming back, come back or change the plan. That's enough for tonight."

No lecture about being sixteen.

No demand to know who she talked to.

No live dot on somebody else's screen.

Maya had spent months earning back ordinary trust after Book One. She had expected to arrive in orbit carrying a permanent warning label.

Instead Nara had given her more freedom in five minutes than Maya knew what to do with.

That was almost worse.

---

Her wrist showed twenty-one group messages by the time she sat on the bed.

Ethan had finally mapped everyone's host zones.

OLLIE: mine is not near any of you

AOIFE: define near

SAMIR: practice orientation outer ring

ETHAN: dinner tomorrow 19:00 looks possible

Maya had nothing scheduled yet.

That bothered her.

A knock sounded on the open doorframe.

Kellan stood there, sports bag gone now.

"Some people are going to the inner social level after training," he said. "My mother says I should tell you."

"Did she tell you to invite me?"

"More or less."

"Good."

He shrugged.

"You can come or not."

This was a terrible invitation.

Maya immediately wanted to go.

"What's there?"

"Food. Music. Courts. Festival booking opens tonight."

"What kind of courts?"

"Depends which section is free."

Maya glanced at the shared chat.

ETHAN: 19:00 tomorrow — hold unless work/sport changes

AOIFE: do not say hold like we're negotiating a ceasefire

OLLIE: depends on handover

SAMIR: practice until 18:30 outer

Maya typed:

MAYA: maybe

Ethan answered at once.

ETHAN: What does maybe mean?

Maya muted the group for an hour.

"When do you leave?" she asked Kellan.

"Twelve minutes."

"I'll be ready."

He nodded and went away.

No joke. No comment about Transfer Three.

Maya was beginning to suspect local teenagers were less interested in her than she had planned for.

---

Dinner was noodles.

This disappointed every documentary Maya had ever seen about life in orbit.

Nara cooked. Kellan burned part of the sauce while arguing through an audio channel about a team reservation. Maya cut vegetables. The basil from the humidity wall went into the food.

The kitchen system warned that one pan was heating unevenly.

Nara moved it three centimeters and continued cooking.

Maya's wrist buzzed against her leg.

She ignored it.

It buzzed again.

Kellan glanced over.

"Important?"

"Ethan."

Kellan nodded as though this answered something.

Maya checked the message anyway.

ETHAN: You okay? You vanished after arrival.

Her irritation weakened.

She hated when he did that.

Not the checking. The part where sometimes it was simply about whether she was okay.

MAYA: host family normal. station annoying. alive.

She almost left it there.

Then typed:

MAYA: took a stupid route

Ethan took longer to answer.

ETHAN: you chose it yourself didn't you

MAYA: irrelevant

ETHAN: glad you're alive

Maya put the wrist down.

Kellan was looking at her.

"What?"

"Nothing."

He returned to his food.

That was somehow more irritating than a joke.

After a minute Maya said, "A girl at arrival told me I was standing wrong."

Nara kept eating.

Kellan looked up.

"Which girl?"

Maya immediately regretted speaking.

"Nobody."

"Okay."

Again, no interrogation.

Maya stabbed a piece of vegetable harder than necessary.

---

Later, alone in her room, Maya turned on the exterior view.

She had expected stars to look different from orbit.

They did not.

The black around them did.

No atmosphere softened it. No horizon sat at the bottom of the image telling her where home ended.

A dark piece of Vela's structure crossed one corner of the view.

Her wrist displayed the host agreement for acknowledgement.

FIRST-WEEK RETURN CONDITION:

— no continuous location feed required

— zone update if return changes beyond agreed window

— sleeping location must remain truthful

— route condition must remain truthful during service interruption

Maya read it twice.

Then approved it.

A message arrived from Kellan.

KELLAN: leaving in 12

A second one followed.

KELLAN: blue lane

Maya stared at the screen.

MAYA: die

He reacted with a heart and nothing else.

She changed clothes and opened the group chat.

Ethan had added tomorrow's dinner to the shared calendar.

Aoife had renamed it:

ETHAN ATTEMPTS SOCIAL CONTROL THROUGH FOOD

Samir had accepted.

Ollie had marked tentative.

Maya hovered over ACCEPT.

Then selected MAYBE.

She put on her jacket.

At the end of the corridor, Nara called from the kitchen.

"Zone?"

"Inner social level. Back before one unless I message. Sleeping here."

"Good."

That was all.

Maya stepped into the corridor.

The station curved away in both directions.

Somewhere inside it, four friends were unpacking into four other homes.

Somewhere below—or inward, or whatever word Vela used when directions stopped cooperating—Kellan was waiting.

And somewhere between the arrival hub and Transfer Three was a girl who had corrected Maya twice and probably had not thought about her since.

Maya found this intolerable.

She started walking.

When the floor markings changed, she watched what the locals did first.

Then she chose her own lane.
