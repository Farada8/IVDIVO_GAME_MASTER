## T1 — No-Free-Generativity under induced operations

Let A and B be algebras of the same signature, h:A→B a surjective homomorphism, and X⊆A. Let Term_A(X) be the subalgebra/term closure generated by X.

Then:

`Term_B(h(X)) = h(Term_A(X))`.

### Proof
Every B-term over h(X) has the form `t^B(h(x1),...,h(xn))`. By homomorphism compatibility this equals `h(t^A(x1,...,xn))`, so it lies in `h(Term_A(X))`. Conversely every `h(t^A(...))` equals `t^B(h(...))`, giving the reverse inclusion.

### Consequence
A pure quotient/homomorphic abstraction whose operations are all induced from the lower algebra cannot create an abstract term-output outside the image of lower term closure. It may still shorten descriptions, improve resource-bounded reachability, expose invariants or support new interpretations. The theorem rules out only free extensional generativity from pure quotient.

## T2 — Composition-closed admissibility collapses endpoint depth

Let objects be typed and A a class of admissible well-typed transformations. Assume identities where needed and closure under well-typed composition: if `f:x→y` and `g:y→z` are admissible, then `g∘f:x→z` is admissible.

Define `Ord_A(x,y)` as the minimum number of admissible arrows in a path from x to y.

Then:
- `Ord_A(x,x)=0`;
- if y is reachable from x and y≠x, `Ord_A(x,y)=1`;
- if y is unreachable, `Ord_A(x,y)=∞`.

### Proof
Any finite admissible path composes, by repeated closure, to one admissible arrow from x to y.

### Consequence
Any nontrivial finite depth greater than one must arise because admissibility is not closed under composition, or because resource/type annotations change in a way not represented by the endpoint-only definition.

## C1 — Compression-memory monotonicity is false in general

Using exact lumpability defect as a finite-Markov closure/memory criterion, explicit controls exhibit all three possibilities under stronger compression:
- defect increases: `0 → 0.05`;
- defect is unchanged: `0 → 0`;
- defect decreases: `0.10 → 0`.

Therefore no universal theorem `more compression => more memory` is possible without substantial extra assumptions.
