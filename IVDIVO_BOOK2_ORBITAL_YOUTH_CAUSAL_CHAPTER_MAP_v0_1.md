# IVDIVO — BOOK 2 ORBITAL YOUTH — CAUSAL CHAPTER MAP v0.1

**Status:** WORKING ARCHITECTURE / NOT CANON  
**Target:** complete novel; 32 chapters + optional short epilogue only after main resolution.  
**Primary hero:** Ollie.  
**Ensemble:** Ethan, Aoife, Samir, Maya + orbital peers.  
**Working chronology:** January–February 2033. Exact dates remain continuity review items except Ethan DOB 22 Jan 2015.

Primary story engine:
`arrival -> first real work -> clean model -> social/technical pressure -> premature partial opening -> coupled failure -> loss of trust -> new evidence model -> constrained migration -> honest partial solution -> changed life`.

---

# PART I — UP

## CHAPTER 1 — THE LAST GROUND
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** leave Earth feeling like this is the beginning of real work, not another youth program.  
**Action:** the five depart Northern Virginia; ordinary family/school/maker-team obligations follow them into travel.  
**Conflict:** Ollie’s old rescue-rover team has a new build milestone while he will be away.  
**World reveal:** orbital residency requires luggage/health/work compatibility planning, not tourist packing.  
**Turn:** Ollie chooses the orbital maintenance placement over a safer media/education placement.  
**Change:** his Book-One sacrifice becomes a renewed commitment.

## CHAPTER 2 — DUBLIN VERTICAL
**POV:** Aoife.  
**Want:** record the transition without making it a tourist reel.  
**Obstacle:** historic Dublin, deep/vertical transfer infrastructure and orbital traffic are too visually overwhelming; Earth media passengers perform for cameras.  
**World reveal:** Ireland as planetary-spaceport civilization; old city and orbital-derived infrastructure coexist.  
**Relationship:** Aoife refuses Ethan’s suggestion for how to structure her documentary.  
**Turn:** orbital transfer separates the five into different operational routes / seats / prep groups.  
**Change:** first physical sign that their lives will not stay synchronized.

## CHAPTER 3 — ARRIVAL WITHOUT A FLOOR
**POV:** Maya.  
**Want:** look competent and local immediately.  
**Obstacle:** gravity transitions, orientation rules, transit etiquette and host arrangements expose how planetary she is.  
**World reveal:** city transport, rotational zones, mixed-species flows, AI mediation, ordinary commuting.  
**Turn:** Maya ignores one local movement norm, inconveniences people and is corrected by a teenager, not an adult official.  
**Change:** wonder and embarrassment arrive together.

## CHAPTER 4 — FIVE ADDRESSES
**POV:** Ethan.  
**Want:** keep the group on one schedule.  
**Obstacle:** they have different host housing, work placements, training blocks and social obligations.  
**World reveal:** orbital housing is differentiated by gravity, atmosphere compatibility, household type and transit access.  
**Turn:** Ethan discovers he cannot even organize dinner without negotiating five incompatible routes.  
**Relationship:** Aoife enjoys that he cannot control the city.  
**Change:** group becomes a network, not a room.

## CHAPTER 5 — FIRST SHIFT
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** impress the ring commissioning crew.  
**Obstacle:** adults/local apprentices give him boring real tasks: inspection, tagging, cleaning, calibration support.  
**World reveal:** maintenance labor, safety culture, tool custody, shift hierarchy.  
**Turn:** Ollie catches a minor practical issue because he actually does the boring task carefully.  
**Payoff:** crew gives him a more meaningful diagnostic role.  
**Change:** respect begins through work.

## CHAPTER 6 — THE RING PEOPLE WANT
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** understand why everyone is obsessed with opening deadline.  
**Action:** supervised tour of finished residential sections with future residents / local youth.  
**World reveal:** homes, community spaces, mixed-environment zones, views, schools, first-job storefronts, sport access.  
**Human stakes:** families are already living in temporary arrangements awaiting the ring.  
**Turn:** a commissioning occupancy run shows a small unexplained environmental settling delay.  
**Change:** first anomaly, but no crisis.

# PART II — WORK

## CHAPTER 7 — STATIC SAFE
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** prove anomaly is instrumentation noise.  
**Obstacle:** all certified fixed sensors look normal.  
**Action:** he helps compare logs; adults correctly refuse speculation.  
**Turn:** same anomaly appears in a different zone under a different occupancy mix.  
**Change:** random-glitch model weakens.

## CHAPTER 8 — LOCAL RULES
**POV:** Samir.  
**Want:** join an orbital sport / social activity without becoming a Contact curiosity.  
**World reveal:** sport altered by gravity architecture; team status and neighborhood identity.  
**Obstacle:** someone recognizes him from Book-One publicity.  
**Choice:** Samir sets a boundary instead of leaving.  
**Turn:** later, while commuting, he notices anomaly timing tracks movement between zones, not absolute environmental values.  
**Change:** ordinary life generates useful observation.

## CHAPTER 9 — THE APPRENTICES
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** be accepted by local youth maintenance crew.  
**Obstacle:** they see Earth cohort as prestige visitors who leave before consequences arrive.  
**New character:** Iri / local apprentice slot enters through work, not exposition.  
**Turn:** Ollie makes a useful suggestion but misreads a resident/body-status warning as low-confidence telemetry.  
**Change:** competence and blind spot established together.

## CHAPTER 10 — A CITY AFTER MIDNIGHT
**POV:** Maya.  
**Want:** attend a late youth event without escort restrictions.  
**Obstacle:** host/parent agreement and transit-zone permissions.  
**Action:** she negotiates, partly wins, then pushes one boundary farther than agreed.  
**World reveal:** entertainment, late transit, status, mixed peer groups.  
**Turn:** she gets home safely but creates a trust problem because another person had to cover for her.  
**Change:** independence costs social credit in the human sense — trust, not a numeric score.

## CHAPTER 11 — WHOSE STORY
**POV:** Aoife.  
**Want:** record an orbital-youth piece that feels intimate and true.  
**Obstacle:** locals are tired of Earth media asking what it is “like to live in space.”  
**Action:** she earns access by recording work, boredom, arguments and commute rather than spectacle.  
**Turn:** Iri/local peer allows one private recording under specific context boundaries.  
**Change:** trust asset created.

## CHAPTER 12 — THE MODEL
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** connect anomalies into a technical explanation.  
**Action:** fixed instrumentation + maintenance logs; Samir contributes timing; Maya contributes route geometry.  
**Obstacle:** data does not produce one failed component.  
**Wrong strategy:** Ollie filters resident/body reports as anecdotal because they are not certified instrumentation.  
**Turn:** model predicts next run will settle normally.  
**Exit:** a limited occupancy test is scheduled.

## CHAPTER 13 — ALMOST LOCAL
**POV:** ensemble / Ethan dominant.  
**Want:** group finally spend one evening together.  
**Obstacle:** work shifts, sport, documentary, hosts, Maya’s plans.  
**Relationship:** Ethan/Aoife attraction advances through jealousy and scheduling friction, not confession.  
**Ethan pressure:** adult-track information appears because his 18th birthday is approaching.  
**Turn:** he hides how tempting the separate adult path is.  
**Change:** friendship is now something they must arrange.

---

# PART III — THE WRONG SUCCESS

## CHAPTER 14 — LIMITED OPEN
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** see his model survive a realistic test.  
**Obstacle:** high-density movement creates more variability than simulation.  
**Action:** commissioning begins with real future residents and service crews under controlled conditions.  
**Turn:** system settles inside allowed metrics.  
**False victory:** Ollie believes the anomaly is solved / operationally negligible.  
**Change:** pressure to proceed increases.

## CHAPTER 15 — THE CLIP
**POV:** Aoife.  
**Want:** publish a short piece showing the ring as lived community, not engineering spectacle.  
**Mistake:** a private-context detail remains inferable in edit / preview.  
**External event:** a clipped excerpt circulates outside her intended audience.  
**Turn:** Iri/local peer becomes a symbol in Earth debates about orbital bodies / rights / technology access.  
**Price:** source trust breaks; Aoife loses informal access.

## CHAPTER 16 — PLANET PEOPLE
**POV:** Maya.  
**Want:** defend the five against local resentment.  
**Obstacle:** locals have legitimate reasons to resent visitors whose mistakes become global media before local residents finish their shifts.  
**Action:** argument at youth social venue escalates.  
**Turn:** Maya discovers one waiting family may be split if ring misses deadline.  
**Change:** “delay for safety” gains human cost.

## CHAPTER 17 — EIGHTEEN
**POV:** Ethan.  
**Date function:** 22 January 2033 if chronology retained.  
**Want:** enjoy birthday without making it a policy event.  
**Obstacle:** adult-status eligibility changes in background immediately.  
**Action:** messages/offers arrive; friends joke; Aoife sees he is torn.  
**Turn:** Ethan accepts an informational adult-track meeting but does not leave group.  
**Change:** adulthood becomes temptation, not prize.

## CHAPTER 18 — GO / NO-GO
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** support partial opening approval.  
**Obstacle:** safety authority wants a lower-density repeat; relocation office argues schedule is collapsing.  
**Action:** Ollie presents clean model and admits residual uncertainty, but weights certified sensors over resident reports.  
**Turn:** limited partial habitation is authorized under restrictions.  
**Choice:** Ollie publicly supports it.  
**Exit:** real occupancy begins.

## CHAPTER 19 — OSCILLATION
**POV:** rapid controlled ensemble; Ollie anchor.  
**Want:** complete partial opening day safely.  
**Failure:** coupled occupancy oscillation emerges.  
**Adults:** detect problem, order isolation/evacuation, perform competently.  
**Youth role:** immediate local compliance / communication / assisting within permissions.  
**Turn:** one late-isolating compartment creates real injury/equipment damage.  
**Key evidence:** low-trust body/resident telemetry warned earlier.  
**Change:** calibration-glitch model dies.

## CHAPTER 20 — SHUT
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** understand what he missed.  
**Obstacle:** ring closed; access restricted; crew angry; residents displaced again.  
**Price:** Ollie’s credibility collapses because he argued for opening.  
**Turn:** Iri tells him the problem was felt before it was measured — and that he heard the report.  
**Change:** failure becomes personal.

# PART IV — WHAT COUNTS AS DATA

## CHAPTER 21 — AFTER THE CAMERA
**POV:** Aoife.  
**Want:** apologize / repair source trust.  
**Obstacle:** apology does not undo circulation.  
**Action:** she removes/withholds material even though it hurts her project.  
**Turn:** source does not forgive instantly but gives one factual correction that changes the technical timeline.  
**Change:** ethical choice reopens a narrow information channel.

## CHAPTER 22 — NO ACCESS
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** get back on commissioning team.  
**Obstacle:** adults correctly say no until review.  
**Action:** he takes maintenance support shifts elsewhere instead of campaigning.  
**World reveal:** waste, repair economy, replacement parts, service corridors, mundane orbital labor.  
**Turn:** maintenance worker shows recurring “settling” complaints from occupied older modules.  
**Change:** clue broadens without conspiracy.

## CHAPTER 23 — SAMIR SAYS NO
**POV:** Samir.  
**Want:** contribute pattern without becoming public face.  
**Obstacle:** media/official interest in “the kid who noticed.”  
**Action:** he negotiates how his observation may be used and refuses interview.  
**Turn:** his timing map shows failure begins after a specific sequence of occupancy transitions.  
**Change:** selective visibility works.

## CHAPTER 24 — MAYA’S SHORTCUT
**POV:** Maya.  
**Want:** verify route geometry / zone sequence physically.  
**Obstacle:** work-zone restriction.  
**Wrong action:** she enters beyond agreed boundary with local peers.  
**Consequence:** no disaster, but she compromises traceability and is caught.  
**Turn:** mobility privileges reduced.  
**Change:** being right about geometry does not excuse authority breach — Book-One lesson tested, not repeated abstractly.

## CHAPTER 25 — THE SECOND MODEL
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** build a model that includes time-lag and lived data.  
**Action:** certified sensors + privacy-safe resident telemetry + route timing + partition state.  
**Turn:** difference between resident response time and building response predicts failure.  
**Choice:** Ollie asks local users what his model is missing before finalizing it.  
**Change:** character strategy shifts.

## CHAPTER 26 — SMALL TEST
**POV:** Ollie / Samir.  
**Want:** reproduce condition safely.  
**Adults:** design and authorize controlled low-density test.  
**Result:** oscillation reproduced at small scale, then stopped.  
**Discovery:** no single component is broken; the coupling is real.  
**Closure:** mystery of failure substantially solved.  
**New problem:** full ring cannot be fixed before relocation deadline.

## CHAPTER 27 — TWO BAD OPTIONS
**POV:** Ethan.  
**Want:** help find decision everyone can live with.  
**Options presented:** full delay with severe relocation cost vs broad opening with exclusion/restriction.  
**Obstacle:** Ethan starts solving other people’s priorities for them.  
**Turn:** Samir/Aoife call him on it; he steps back.  
**Change:** third option must be negotiated by residents, engineers and safety authority together.

# PART V — PARTIAL HOME

## CHAPTER 28 — SEGMENTS
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** find a technically honest partial-opening plan.  
**Action:** divide ring into occupancy-certified zones with different density/transition limits.  
**Obstacle:** segmentation is socially ugly and operationally inefficient.  
**Turn:** some families can move; others still cannot.  
**Change:** solution becomes possible but costly.

## CHAPTER 29 — WHO WAITS
**POV:** Aoife.  
**Want:** understand consequences without turning families into content.  
**World reveal:** temporary housing, commute strain, school shifts, relationship disruption.  
**Action:** she works without recorder for part of chapter.  
**Turn:** one previously angry resident supports partial opening because waiting everyone is worse.  
**Change:** moral binary dissolves.

## CHAPTER 30 — THE ADULT DOOR
**POV:** Ethan.  
**Want:** decide whether to transfer immediately to adult professional path.  
**Obstacle:** opportunity is real and deserved; staying is not automatically noble.  
**Choice:** he postpones transfer only until current ring commitment closes, not forever.  
**Price:** gives up first available slot / status timing.  
**Change:** leadership becomes commitment rather than possession.

## CHAPTER 31 — MAYA ASKS
**POV:** Maya.  
**Want:** regain enough mobility to participate in relocation support.  
**Action:** instead of bypassing rule, she builds a specific responsibility case with host/parent/safety staff.  
**Turn:** limited permission restored.  
**Change:** autonomy is earned through shared-risk clarity.

# PART VI — MOVE DAY

## CHAPTER 32 — MIGRATION
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** see segmented ring function under actual relocation load.  
**World reveal:** moving day in orbit — freight, families, pets/organisms, body compatibility, school routes, service workers, emotional mess.  
**Obstacle:** real behavior differs from plans immediately.  
**Turn:** capacity margins tighten but remain safe.  
**Change:** model meets life.

## CHAPTER 33 — THE KNOWN HAZARD
**POV:** ensemble.  
**Setup from earlier chapters pays:** scheduled structural/shielding work and migration create a known period of reduced redundancy.  
**Event:** a predicted operational stress / service interruption occurs inside planned range but coincides with crowd movement.  
**Adults:** activate emergency protocol.  
**Youth:** confined to local responsibilities.  
**Turn:** one zone begins trending toward the same coupled oscillation.

## CHAPTER 34 — LISTEN
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** keep local segment open to preserve migration flow.  
**Obstacle:** Iri/residents report early warning while fixed sensors still predict recoverable state.  
**Climax choice begins:** trust the corrected causal model and embodied lag signal, or preserve throughput.  
**Action:** Ollie recommends slowing migration and isolating a section before formal threshold breach.  
**Price:** creates visible bottleneck / forces families back out temporarily.  
**Turn:** isolation prevents oscillation escalation.

## CHAPTER 35 — NOT OPEN
**POV:** Ollie.  
**Want:** finish commissioning review honestly.  
**Obstacle:** institution can truthfully describe relocation as successful and would like a positive opening statement.  
**Action:** Ollie refuses language implying full-ring commissioning succeeded.  
**Climax:** supports partial habitation + continued closure/redesign of unsafe sections.  
**Price:** no clean triumph / no simple young-engineer recognition.  
**Payoff:** maintenance/safety adults treat him as trustworthy under uncertainty.  
**Main conflict closes.**

# PART VII — LIFE CONTINUES

## CHAPTER 36 — A HOME THAT ISN’T FINISHED
**POV:** controlled ensemble, Ollie anchor.  
**Resolution:** inhabited safe zones feel ordinary within days: food deliveries, arguments, school bags, laundry, first shifts, parties, complaints.  
**Ollie:** returns to his placement knowing incomplete is not failure if limits are honest.  
**Aoife:** produces a smaller documentary with consent boundaries; loses some viral reach, gains trust.  
**Samir:** makes one public contribution on his terms, then goes to sport / friends.  
**Maya:** uses restored mobility responsibly and immediately complains about the curfew anyway.  
**Ethan/Aoife:** attraction remains active, complicated by different futures; no forced couple lock.  
**Group:** they miss one planned gathering because their lives genuinely diverge — then reschedule.  
**Book change:** orbital civilization has stopped being destination and become ordinary life.

## Opposition
GREEN — engineering + deadline + residents + safety + media + internal flaws; no cardboard villain.

## Midpoint
GREEN — partial opening failure changes the question.

## Climax
GREEN — decision under pressure uses discovered rule and costs original success metric.

## Resolution
GREEN — main housing/commissioning conflict closes with permanent consequence.

## Ordinary youth life
YELLOW/GREEN — architecture contains sport, party, housing, family, romance, work and status, but scene pass must ensure they occupy enough page-time rather than becoming garnish.
